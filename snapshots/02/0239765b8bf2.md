# Relative of Cambodia's second PM murdered

By ANDREW NETTE

[![Image 2: Add as preferred source on Google](https://www.upi.com/img/google_preferred_source.png)](https://google.com/preferences/source?q=upi.com "Add as preferred source on Google")[![Image 3: Share with X](https://www.upi.com/img/x_share.webp)](https://x.com/intent/post?url=https%3A%2F%2Fupi.com%2Far%2F7068848379600&text=Relative+of+Cambodia%27s+second+PM+murdered&via=upi "Share with X")

PHNOM PENH, Nov. 19 --  A close relative of Cambodia's second prime minister Hun Sen was shot dead in Phnom Penh Tuesday morning in what police described as a political murder. Kov Samuth, the deputy chief of the economic department at the Interior Ministry and Hun Sen's brother-in-law, was shot four times at 8:45 a.m. as he left a restaurant near the Independence Monument in central Phnom Penh. He was dead on arrival at the city's Calmett hospital. Police described the killing as a professional hit, and said it was carried out by two men in civilian clothes who fledthe scene on a black Honda motorcycle. 'We suspect the killing was politically motivated,' said one police official who did not want to be named. Senior officials of Hun Sen's formerly communist Cambodian People's Party were less equivocal. 'It is a political assassination and a warning to the second prime minister Hun Sen,' said Om Yentieng, a senior advisor to the second prime minister. Yentieng said it was carried out by '...a group of terrorists backed by a political party.' 'If things keep going like this we cannot be patient and calm and let them escape,' he warned. The killing of a 41-year-old relative of one of the country's senior leaders worsens political tensions in Phnom Penh, already running high following accusations on the weekend that the nation's most prominent opposition politician, Sam Rainsy, and his Khmer Nation Party, were fronts for the Khmer Rouge.

The claim was made Saturday at the home of Hun Sen by ten men who claimed to be leaders of a nationwide network of Khmer Rouge urban terrorist squads. The men released a detailed statement in which they claimed Rainsy had met with senior Khmer Rouge leaders, and taken money assistance from the guerrilla movement. Rainsy has been one a vocal critic of corruption and human rights abuses by the government, especially the formerly communist Cambodian People's Party led by Hun Sen. The ten also claimed that the Royalist FUNCINPEC party, led by first prime minister Prince Norodom Ranariddh, had tried to prevent the link from becoming public. Ranariddh, who left on a visit to Hong Kong on Tuesday, completely denied the accusation and threatened to being legal action against the men who made the charges. A senior advisor to Hun Sen labeled Samuth's death 'a political murder.' 'I think it comes from the source of the threat to the new defectors,' he said. 'If this kind of action continues...we will not stay calm for long,' he said.
