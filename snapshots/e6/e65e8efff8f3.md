Okinawa Island

**Okinawa Island** ( [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 沖縄島, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization "Hepburn romanization"): _Okinawa-jima_; [Okinawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_language "Okinawan language"): 沖縄 / うちなー, romanized:_Uchinā_;[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-4) [Kunigami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunigami_language "Kunigami language"): ふちなー, romanized:_Fuchináa_), also known as **Okinawa Main Island** (沖縄本島, _Okinawa-hontō_),[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-5) is the largest of the [Okinawa Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Islands "Okinawa Islands") and the [Ryukyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyu_Islands "Ryukyu Islands") ( _Nansei_) Islands of Japan in the [Kyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu "Kyushu") region. The island is approximately 106 kilometres (66mi) long, an average 11 kilometres (7mi) wide,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-6) and has an area of 1,206.98 square kilometers (466.02sqmi). It is roughly 640 kilometres (350nmi; 400mi) south of the main island of [Kyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu "Kyushu") and the rest of Japan. It is 500km (270nmi; 310mi) northeast of [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"). The total population of Okinawa Island was 1,384,762 in 2009.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-okinawa-population-3) The greater [Naha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha "Naha") area has roughly 800,000 residents, while the city itself has about 320,000 people. Naha is the seat of [Okinawa Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture") on the southwestern part of Okinawa Island. Okinawa has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate "Humid subtropical climate").

Okinawa has been a strategic location for the [United States Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces") since the [Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa "Battle of Okinawa") and the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). The island was formally controlled by the [United States Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Civil_Administration_of_the_Ryukyu_Islands "United States Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands") until 1972, with around 26,000 U.S. military personnel stationed on Okinawa today, comprising about half of the total complement of the [United States Forces Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Japan "United States Forces Japan"), spread among 31 areas, across 13 bases and 48 training sites. United States military installations cover approximately 25% of the island and have been a point of contention among locals. Crimes committed by US military personnel, notably the [1995 Okinawa rape incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Okinawa_rape_incident "1995 Okinawa rape incident"), have caused protests against the US military presence in Okinawa.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

Main articles: [Ryukyuan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_people "Ryukyuan people") and [History of the Ryukyu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Ryukyu_Islands "History of the Ryukyu Islands")

### Shell mound eras

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Shell mound eras")\]

Early Okinawan history is defined by [midden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midden "Midden") or shell heap culture and is divided into Early, Middle, and Late Shell Mound periods. The Early Shell Mound period was a [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer") society, with the wave-like opening [Jōmon pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_pottery "Jōmon pottery"). In the latter part of this period, [archaeological sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site "Archaeological site") moved near the seashore, suggesting the engagement of people in fishing. On Okinawa, rice was not cultivated until the Middle Shell Mound period. Shell rings for arms made of shells obtained in the [Sakishima Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakishima_Islands "Sakishima Islands"), namely [Miyakojima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyakojima "Miyakojima") and [Yaeyama islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaeyama_Islands "Yaeyama Islands"), were imported by Japan. In these islands, the presence of shell axes from 2,500 years ago suggests the influence of a southeastern-Pacific culture.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-8)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/First_Ryukyan_mission_to_Edo.JPG/250px-First_Ryukyan_mission_to_Edo.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:First_Ryukyan_mission_to_Edo.JPG) The first of the [Ryukyuan missions to Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_missions_to_Edo "Ryukyuan missions to Edo"), the capital of [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate "Tokugawa shogunate")

After the late Shell Mound period, agriculture started about the 12th century, and the population center moved from the seashore to higher places. This period is called the Gusuku period. _[Gusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusuku "Gusuku")_ is a term used for the distinctive Ryukyuan form of castles or fortresses. Many _gusuku_ and related cultural remains in the Ryukyu Islands have been listed by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") as [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites "World Heritage Sites"). There are three perspectives regarding the nature of _gusuku_: that they were holy places, dwellings encircled by stones, and castles of the rulers. In this period, [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain "Porcelain") trade between Okinawa and other countries became commonplace, and Okinawa was an important relay point in eastern-Asian trade. Ryukyuan kings, such as [Shunten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunten "Shunten") and [Eiso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiso_(king) "Eiso (king)"), were important rulers. An attempted Mongolian invasion in 1291 during the Eiso dynasty failed. [Hiragana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragana "Hiragana") was imported from Japan by [Ganjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jianzhen "Jianzhen") in 1265. _[Noro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noro_(priestess) "Noro (priestess)")_, village priestesses of the [Ryukyuan religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryukyuan_religion "Ryukyuan religion"), appeared.

### Sanzan era and Ryūkyū Kingdom

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Sanzan era and Ryūkyū Kingdom")\]

The [Sanzan period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanzan_period "Sanzan period") began in 1314 when the kingdoms of [Hokuzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuzan "Hokuzan") and [Nanzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzan "Nanzan") declared independence from [Chūzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABzan "Chūzan"). The three kingdoms competed with one another for recognition as a vassal state of [Ming China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-9) King [Satto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satto "Satto"), leading Chūzan, was very successful, establishing relations with Korea and Southeast Asia as well as China. The [Hongwu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongwu_Emperor "Hongwu Emperor") of China sent a large population of Chinese migrants, primarily consisting of 36 large families from [Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian "Fujian") Province, in 1392 at the request of the Ryukyuan king. The purpose of the migration was to increase the population of the Ryukyuan kingdom as well as enhance maritime relations between the Chinese empire and the Ryukyuan kingdom. The Chinese migrants assisted the Ryukyuans in developing their technology and diplomatic relations. In 1407, however, a man named Hashi overthrew Satto's descendant, King [Bunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunei "Bunei"), and installed his father, [Shishō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Shish%C5%8D "Shō Shishō"), as king of Chūzan.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Sho_Tai.jpg/250px-Sho_Tai.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sho_Tai.jpg) The last King [Shō Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Tai "Shō Tai")

In 1429, King [Shō Hashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Hashi "Shō Hashi") completed the unification of the three kingdoms and founded the Ryūkyū Kingdom with its capital at [Shuri Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle "Shuri Castle"). His descendants conquered the [Amami Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amami_Islands "Amami Islands"). In 1469, King [Shō Taikyū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Taiky%C5%AB "Shō Taikyū") died, so the royal government chose a man named Kanemaru as the new king, who chose the name [Shō En](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_En "Shō En") and established the Second Shō dynasty. His son [Shō Shin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Shin "Shō Shin") conquered the Sakishima Islands and centralized the royal government, the military, and the _noro_ priestesses.

### Satsuma Domain

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Satsuma Domain")\]

In 1609, the Japanese [Satsuma Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Domain "Satsuma Domain") launched the [invasion of Ryukyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Ryukyu "Invasion of Ryukyu"), ultimately capturing the king and his capital after a long struggle. Ryukyu was forced to cede the Amami Islands and become a vassal of Satsuma. The kingdom became both a tributary of China and a tributary of Japan. Because China would not make a formal trade agreement unless a country was a tributary state, the kingdom was a convenient loophole for Japanese trade with China. When Japan officially closed off trade with European nations except for the Dutch, [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki "Nagasaki"), [Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima,_Nagasaki "Tsushima, Nagasaki"), and [Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima "Kagoshima") became the only Japanese trading ports offering connections with the outside world.

### 18th and 19th centuries

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 18th and 19th centuries")\]

Several Europeans visited Ryukyu starting in the late 18th century. The most important visits to Okinawa were from Captain [Basil Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_Hall_(naval_officer) "Basil Hall (naval officer)") in 1816 and Commodore [Matthew C. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_C._Perry "Matthew C. Perry") in 1852. A Christian missionary, [Bernard Jean Bettelheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Jean_Bettelheim "Bernard Jean Bettelheim"), lived in the [Gokoku-ji temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokoku-ji_(Okinawa) "Gokoku-ji (Okinawa)") in Naha from 1846 to 1854.

In 1879, Japan annexed the entire Ryukyu archipelago.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-10) The [Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_period "Meiji period") government then established [Okinawa Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture"). The monarchy in [Shuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri,_Okinawa "Shuri, Okinawa") was abolished, and the deposed King [Shō Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_Tai "Shō Tai") was forced to relocate to [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo").

### Pacific War

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Pacific War")\]

Okinawa Island had [the bloodiest ground battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa "Battle of Okinawa") of the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War "Pacific War") from 1 April to 22 June 1945. During this 82-day-long battle, about 95,000 [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army "Imperial Japanese Army") troops and 20,195 Americans were killed. The [Cornerstone of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornerstone_of_Peace "Cornerstone of Peace") at the Peace Memorial Park in [Itoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itoman "Itoman") lists 149,193 persons from Okinawa—approximately one quarter of the civilian population—were either killed or committed suicide during the [Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa "Battle of Okinawa") and the Pacific War.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-11) Very few Japanese ended up in [POW camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW_camps "POW camps"). This may have been because of Japanese soldiers' reluctance to surrender. The total number of casualties shocked American military strategists. This made them apprehensive to [invade the other main islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Downfall "Operation Downfall") of Japan, because it would result in very high casualties.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-13)

### American occupation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: American occupation")\]

Japan became a [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism "Pacifism") country with the [1947 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Japan "Constitution of Japan"), so America decided to step into the role of protecting Japan against foreign threats. During the [American military occupation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan "Occupation of Japan") (1945–1952), which followed the [Imperial Japanese surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan "Surrender of Japan") on 2 September 1945, in Tokyo Bay, the United States controlled Okinawa Island and the rest of the Ryukyu Islands. The Amami Islands were returned to Japanese control in 1953. The remaining Ryukyu Islands were returned to Japan on 17 June 1971. America kept numerous U.S. military bases on the islands. There are 32 [United States military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces") bases on Okinawa Island[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-mitchell-14) by the [U.S.-Japan alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan-America_Security_Alliance "Japan-America Security Alliance") since 1951. U.S. bases on Okinawa played critical roles in the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War"), [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), [Laotian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_Civil_War "Laotian Civil War"), [Cambodian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_campaign "Cambodian campaign"), [War in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_(2001%E2%80%93present) "War in Afghanistan (2001–present)"), and [Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) Okinawa served as a prime staging post for the aforementioned wars.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) Its ports and airports were used to transport supplies. The base at Camp Chinen, Nanjo City was used by the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA "CIA") for covert operations. In 1965, [Admiral Ulysses S. Grant Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Ulysses_S._Grant_Sharp "Admiral Ulysses S. Grant Sharp") stated that "Without Okinawa, we couldn't continue fighting the Vietnam war."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15)

Intense use of the island by the U.S. military caused damage to the environment and residents.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) There were oil and fuel spills.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) Exposure to toxic substances caused illness of service members such as a nerve agent leak in 1969.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) Aircraft crashes, hit-and runs and murders killed residents.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) The perpetrators were often unpunished, since they could not be prosecuted in Okinawa Courts.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) The 1970s and 1980s also had severe pollution of waterways and wells with [PFAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFAS "PFAS"): toxic chemicals in foam used by fire fighting training at U.S. facilities such as [Kadena Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadena_Air_Base "Kadena Air Base").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-pfas1-16)

### 1970s narcotics trade

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: 1970s narcotics trade")\]

In the early 1970s, according to a U.S. government report, Okinawa was a key conduit for smuggling drugs such as heroin from Thailand via Okinawa to the United States.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) It was called "The Okinawa System" in the global drug trade.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) A testimony by a head of the Department of Defense said that drug abuse was "quite extensive."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) It began in the second half of 1968; [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijuana "Marijuana") was smuggled from Thailand to Okinawa and grown near an unidentified [U.S. Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marine_Corps "U.S. Marine Corps") training area in northern Okinawa.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) A 100kg (220lb) package of marijuana fell from an aircraft, and another 90kg (200lb) was discovered at a military post office.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) From the mid-1970s onwards, [LSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSD "LSD") and [heroin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroin "Heroin") became more prevalent.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) Deserters worked as "passport civilians" and smuggled the drugs into Okinawa.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) A lack of customs inspections made smuggling easy.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15) There were well organized Ryukyuan smuggling rings who brought heroin, LSD, and marijuana to Okinawa, and produced LSD on the island.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-narc-15)

### 1995 rape incident

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: 1995 rape incident")\]

Main article: [1995 Okinawa rape incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Okinawa_rape_incident "1995 Okinawa rape incident")

On 4 September 1995, two Marines by the names of Rodrico Harp and Kendrick Ledet, were convinced by Seaman Marcus Dion Gill to search for a local to abduct and rape. This resulted in them targeting a 12-year-old girl walking home alone in the town of [Kin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kin,_Okinawa "Kin, Okinawa"). After forcing the victim into a rental van, the trio drove to Blue Beach Training Ground where Gill beat her until she lost consciousness and then raped her. Once finished, he joked about the event with Ledet and Harp,[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-17) who were alleged to have had reservations about going through with the rape after getting into the vehicle. After dumping her out of the van and driving off, the victim called the police immediately, who had little trouble tracking them down. As a result, the three would become the center of an internationally observed trial that ignited months of protest from anti-base Okinawans. At the conclusion of the trial, the three received sentences to serve between 6.5 and 7 years of hard labor. Although the typical sentence length for rape by Japanese standards at the time, the young victim and her family had begged the panel of judges to pass life sentences.

### 21st century

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: 21st century")\]

In 2013, following escalating tensions following [competing claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands_dispute "Senkaku Islands dispute") to the uninhabited [Senkaku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands "Senkaku Islands"), the [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") began questioning Japan's sovereignty to the island of Okinawa, citing its past as the independent tributary state of Ryukyu.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-McCurry-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Boehler-19) On 31 October 2019, the main courtyard structures of [Shurijo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shurijo "Shurijo") were destroyed in a fire.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-jp-times-20) It marked the fifth time that Shurijo was destroyed following previous incidents in 1453, 1660, 1709 and 1945.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-OKI20191102-21) The 2019 fire burned down sections of [Shuri Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuri_Castle "Shuri Castle"): "Six castle buildings occupying some 4,200 square metres (45,000sqft) in total were gutted."[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-23)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-jp-times-20) Rebuilding efforts were underway as of 10 February 2020.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-24)

## Demographics

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Demographics")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Kokusai-dori08s3s4440.jpg/250px-Kokusai-dori08s3s4440.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kokusai-dori08s3s4440.jpg) Kokusai Street in [Naha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha "Naha")

In September 2009, the Japanese government estimated the population to be 1,384,762,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-okinawa-population-3) which included American military personnel and their families. The [Okinawan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_language "Okinawan language"), called _Uchināguchi_, is spoken by adults only,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-25) but several local groups promote the use of the Okinawan language by younger people.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-26)

Whereas the northern half of Okinawa Island is sparsely populated, the south-central and southern parts of the island are markedly [urbanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization "Urbanization")—particularly the city of Naha and the urban corridor stretching north from there to [Okinawa City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_(city) "Okinawa (city)"). The population distribution is approximately 120,000 in northern Okinawa, 590,000 in central Okinawa and 540,000 in southern Okinawa. It has a high population density of 1,014.93/km2.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-population-density-27)

During the [Meiji period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_period "Meiji period"), Okinawan ethnic identity, tradition, culture and language were suppressed by the [Meiji government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_government "Meiji government"), which sought to assimilate the Okinawans as Japanese ( [Yamato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_people "Yamato people")).[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Minahan-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristy2004173%E2%80%93175-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabson20084-30)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDubinskyDavies201315%E2%80%9316-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-FOOTNOTECaprio201449%E2%80%9350,_63,_66%E2%80%9367-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-FOOTNOTEInoue20173-33) Many ethnic Japanese have since migrated to Okinawa. The modern inhabitants of Okinawa are mainly ethnic [Okinawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_people "Okinawan people"), Japanese, [half Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C4%81fu "Hāfu") and mixed.

Okinawans are known for their [longevity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longevity "Longevity"). This particular island is a so-called [Blue Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Zone "Blue Zone"), an area where the people live longer than most others elsewhere in the world.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-34) Five times as many Okinawans reach 100 years old compared to the rest of Japan. As of 2002[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit) there were 34.7 centenarians for every 100,000 inhabitants, which is the highest ratio worldwide.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Santrock-35):131–132 Possible explanations are diet, low-stress lifestyle, caring community, activity, and spirituality of the inhabitants of the island.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Santrock-35)\[ _[pageneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")_\] The [Okinawan diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_diet "Okinawa diet") consists of low-fat, low-salt foods, such as whole fruits and vegetables, legumes, tofu, and seaweed.

Japan is one of the most rapidly aging societies in the world, with declining fertility and increasing longevity contributing to an accelerating population contraction. National fertility has fallen to around 1.2, far below the replacement level of 2.15. Against this backdrop, Okinawa remains a demographic outlier. Its fertility rates, while below replacement, are significantly higher than those found in major metropolitan regions such as Tokyo, Osaka, and Kanagawa.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-36)

### JC virus research study

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: JC virus research study")\]

In 1997, a scientific research study on the transmission of the ubiquitous [JC virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_polyomavirus_2 "Human polyomavirus 2") (JCV) was conducted on the population of Okinawa due to the specific separation of Japanese and military populations on Okinawa. By the time of the study, researchers had identified multiple subtypes of JCV, each of which was generally associated with a specific geographic region or ancestral population. Most humans become infected with some subtype of JCV by age of 10 years. The study conjectured that transmission occurred through prolonged close contact within households, rather than between populations living in close proximity, as was the case for the Japanese population and the military personnel living on Okinawa. Specifically, the study looked for transmission from the American population to the Japanese population.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-JVirol2002-37)
The study found no evidence of Japanese children having the American JCV subtype. The researchers proposed a "horizontal transmission from parents to children during long-term cohabitation".[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-JVirol2002-37) As a result of the study, JCV subtype analysis is now used to determine human migration patterns,[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Sugimoto_1997-38) including the population history of the [Jōmon people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_people "Jōmon people") of Okinawa.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-ScientificReport2020-39)

## Geography

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Geography")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Topographic_map_of_Okinawa_Island.png/250px-Topographic_map_of_Okinawa_Island.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Topographic_map_of_Okinawa_Island.png) Topographic map of Okinawa Island

Okinawa is the fifth largest island of Japan. The island has an area of 1,206.99 square kilometers (466.02sqmi). The coastline is 476 kilometers (296mi) long.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-toukei2014-40) The straight-line distance is about 106.6 kilometers (66.2mi) from north to south.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-rekishi-okinawajima73chu-41) Okinawa is in the northeastern end of [Okinawa Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture"). Since 1972 over 1,000 hectares (2,500 acres) of land has been added via [reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation "Land reclamation") (0.83% of the island's total area).

It is roughly 640 kilometres (400mi) south of the main island of Kyushu. Okinawa is connected to nearby islands near a land bridge: [Katsuren Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katsuren_Peninsula "Katsuren Peninsula") is connected via the [Mid-Sea Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Sea_Road "Mid-Sea Road") to Henza Island, Miyagi Island, Ikei Island, and Hamahiga Island. Similarly, from the [Motobu Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motobu_Peninsula "Motobu Peninsula") on the northwestern side, all of [Sesoko-jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesoko-jima?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sesoko-jima (page does not exist)") plus [Yagaji Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yagaji_Island?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yagaji Island (page does not exist)") and [Kōri-jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dri-jima?action=edit&redlink=1 "Kōri-jima (page does not exist)") are connected by bridges. Okinawa Island has several beaches such as Manza Beach, Emerald Beach, Okuma Beach, Zanpa Beach, Moon Beach and Sunset Beach (Chatan-cho). [Mount Omoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Omoto "Mount Omoto"), at 525.5m (1,724ft), is the highest mountain in Okinawa, with [Mount Yonaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Yonaha "Mount Yonaha") being the second highest.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-nipponika-42)

### Geology

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Geology")\]

The [Motobu Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motobu_Peninsula "Motobu Peninsula") in the north has limestone layers and [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst "Karst") development.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-rekishi-intro24jo-43) In the center and south is mainly a Ryukyu limestone layer and mudstone.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-rekishi-intro24jo-43) The topography is flat, there are few hills over 100m (328ft) with very few rivers. The subtropical rains accelerate erosion requiring many drainages and [uvala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uvala_(landform) "Uvala (landform)"). The southern end of the island consists of uplifted [coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef "Coral reef"), whereas the northern half has proportionally more [igneous rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock "Igneous rock"). The easily eroded [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone "Limestone") of the south has many caves, the most famous of which is Gyokusendō in [Nanjō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanj%C5%8D "Nanjō"). The northernmost [Cape Hedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Hedo "Cape Hedo") is only 22km (14mi) away from [Yoronjima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoronjima "Yoronjima"). [Cape Arasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Arasaki?action=edit&redlink=1 "Cape Arasaki (page does not exist)") is the southernmost location of Okinawa island. It is sometimes confused with Cape Kiyanmisaki.

### Flora and fauna

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Flora and fauna")\]

The northern half of Okinawa has one of the largest tracts of [subtropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_rainforest "Subtropical rainforest") in Asia called the [Yanbaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanbaru "Yanbaru"). There are many [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism "Endemism") species of flora and fauna.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Ufugi-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Marines-45) There are a small number of endemic [Yanbaru kuina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_rail "Okinawa rail") (also known as the Okinawa rail), a small flightless bird that is close to extinction. The critically endangered [Okinawa woodpecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_woodpecker "Okinawa woodpecker") is also endemic to the island. The [Indian mongoose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Asian_mongoose "Small Asian mongoose") was introduced to the island to prevent the native [habu pit viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protobothrops_flavoviridis "Protobothrops flavoviridis") from attacking the birds. It did not succeed in eliminating the habu but instead preyed on birds, increasing the threat to the Okinawa rail.

The [coconut crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_crab "Coconut crab") is the largest terrestrial hermit crab of Okinawa and the Ryukyu Islands.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-coconutcrab-46) It is an endangered species due to over-hunting, which has made them scarce on Okinawa island.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-coconutcrab-46) In 2021, coconut crabs were found to live in a small cave system on the islet Nagashima off the Henoko district.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-crab-47)

A small population of endangered [dugongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugongs "Dugongs") lives around Okinawa. The estimates are between 3 and 50 survivors.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-48)[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-Okinawa-49)

## Cuisine

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Cuisine")\]

Further information: [Okinawan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawan_cuisine "Okinawan cuisine")

There are many local pubs ( _[izakaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izakaya "Izakaya")_) and cafes that serve Okinawan cuisine and dishes, such as _[gōyā chanpurū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanpur%C5%AB "Chanpurū")_ (bitter melon stir fry), _fu chanpurū_ (wheat gluten [chanpurū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanpur%C5%AB "Chanpurū")), and _[tonkatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonkatsu "Tonkatsu")_ (tenderized, breaded, fried pork cutlet). Okinawan [soba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soba "Soba") is the signature dish and consists of wheat noodles served hot in a soup, usually with pork (rib or pork belly). This contrasts with the mainland soba, which is buckwheat noodles. [Rafute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafute "Rafute"), which is braised pork belly, is another popular Okinawan dish. American presence on the island has also led to some creative dishes such as [taco rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taco_rice "Taco rice"), which is now a common meal served in [bentos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bento "Bento"), and the common use of [Spam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spam_(food) "Spam (food)").

## Economy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Economy")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/OkinawaOnna3.jpg/250px-OkinawaOnna3.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:OkinawaOnna3.jpg) Farmland in Okinawa

Among the [prefectures of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_of_Japan "Prefectures of Japan"), Okinawa has the youngest and fastest-growing population but has the lowest employment rate and average income. The island economy is primarily driven by tourism and the U.S. military presence, with efforts in recent years to diversify into other sectors.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-52)

Other significant contributors to the economy include public utilities and public works, as well as, to a lesser extent, telecommunications ( [Okinawa Cellular Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Cellular_Telephone?action=edit&redlink=1 "Okinawa Cellular Telephone (page does not exist)")\[ [ja](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B2%96%E7%B8%84%E3%82%BB%E3%83%AB%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E9%9B%BB%E8%A9%B1 "ja:沖縄セルラー電話")\]), and alcoholic beverages ( [Orion Breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_Breweries "Orion Breweries")).[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-53)

The Motobu Peninsula has a large-scale quarry and cement factory, taking advantage of the limestone in the area.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-54) There is also agriculture with tropical fruit such as _[Malpighia emarginata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malpighia_emarginata "Malpighia emarginata")_.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-55)

### Tourism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Tourism")\]

Tourist attractions include [Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Churaumi_Aquarium "Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium") (at one time the world's largest aquarium), Century Beach, Pineapple Park, the Orion Beer Factory and [Hiji Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiji_Falls "Hiji Falls"). In recent years, Okinawa has become an increasingly popular destination for tourists from China and Southeast Asia.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-yoshida-56) In 2018, Okinawa attracted 9,842,400 tourists, a positive growth of 4.7% from 9,396,200 in the previous year.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-57)[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-58)

### Military bases

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Military bases")\]

The U.S. military bases account for 4 to 5% of the island economy. There is also a smaller contingent of Japanese military bases on the island. Several former U.S. military facilities on Okinawa have been re-developed as commercial areas, most notably the American Village in [Chatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatan,_Okinawa "Chatan, Okinawa"), which opened in 1998, and the Aeon Mall Okinawa Rycom in [Kitanakagusuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitanakagusuku,_Okinawa "Kitanakagusuku, Okinawa"), which opened in 2015.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-yoshida-56)

## U.S. military in Okinawa

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: U.S. military in Okinawa")\]

Main article: [United States Forces Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Japan "United States Forces Japan")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7e/18thopgroup-f15-eagles.jpg/250px-18thopgroup-f15-eagles.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:18thopgroup-f15-eagles.jpg) [F-15C Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15C_Eagles "F-15C Eagles") and an [F-15D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15D "F-15D") of the [18th Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Operations_Group "18th Operations Group"), Kadena Air Base[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/08/US_military_bases_in_Okinawa.svg/250px-US_military_bases_in_Okinawa.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:US_military_bases_in_Okinawa.svg) U.S. military bases in Okinawa (2010)

The United States maintains American military bases in Japan as part of the U.S.-Japan alliance since 1951. As of December 2024, there were just over 60,000 U.S. military and civilian personnel in Japan — 53,603 active-duty U.S., 397 reserve troops, and 7,049 civilian personnel employed by the [U.S. Defense Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense").[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-DOD-stats-59) In addition, there are approximately 35,000 U.S. dependents in Japan.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-JapanForward-March2025-60)[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-USAfacts-2023-61) Most U.S. military is in Okinawa Prefecture.

There are 120 active U.S. military bases in Japan,[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-EurasianTimes-Mar2024-62) with about 70% being in or around Okinawa.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-NYT-2025-63) They cover 25% of the island. The major bases are [Futenma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Futenma "Marine Corps Air Station Futenma"), [Kadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadena_Air_Base "Kadena Air Base"), [Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Hansen "Camp Hansen"), [Torii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torii_Station "Torii Station"), [Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Schwab "Camp Schwab"), [Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Foster "Camp Foster"), and [Kinser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Kinser "Camp Kinser").[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-jaffewp2012-64) There are 28 U.S. military facilities on Okinawa. They are mainly concentrated in the central area. At one point, Okinawa hosted approximately 1,200 nuclear warheads.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-65) There were several [nuclear weapons incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_nuclear_accidents "List of military nuclear accidents") on Okinawa and in the sea near the islands.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-66)[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-67)[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-68)

### Pollution

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Pollution")\]

In 2020, tests around Kadena Air Base showed severe contamination of the wells and waterways of Dakujaku River and Hija river with toxic chemicals [PFAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per-_and_polyfluoroalkyl_substances "Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-pfas1-16) This affects the drinking water of 450,000 residents.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-pfas1-16) PFAS was used in foam at fire fighting training sites on U.S. facilities during the 1970s and 1980s.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-pfas1-16)

### Moving the bases

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Moving the bases")\]

The [1971 Okinawa Reversion Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Okinawa_Reversion_Agreement "1971 Okinawa Reversion Agreement") officially ended the U.S. military occupation on Okinawa.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-69) The bases primarily exist to serve Japanese and American strategic interests,[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-beeb-70) despite recent efforts to move the bases out of core areas following incidents involving military personnel and resultant protests (including the [1995 Okinawa rape incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Okinawa_rape_incident "1995 Okinawa rape incident")).[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-71)[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-72)

In 2012, an agreement was struck between the United States and Japan to reduce the number of U.S. military personnel on the island, moving 9,000 personnel to other locations and moving bases out of heavily populated Greater Naha, but 10,000 Marines will remain on the island, along with other U.S. military units.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-jaffewp2012-64)[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-73) Attempts to completely close bases on the southern third of the island, where 90% of the population lives (all but about 120,000 people[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-74)) have been impeded by both the American desire that alternative locations be found where bases subject to closure could move to (e.g. [Henoko Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henoko_Peninsula?action=edit&redlink=1 "Henoko Peninsula (page does not exist)"), mid-island), as well as by local Okinawan opposition to any suggested locations on the island (who demand no U.S. troops at all anywhere on the island).[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-beeb-70) Tokyo says the U.S. bases are important for national security. Locals complain that despite being home to less than 1% of Japan's population and area, Okinawa hosts the majority of the U.S. military presence in Japan.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-75) In late December 2013, Governor [Hirokazu Nakaima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirokazu_Nakaima "Hirokazu Nakaima") gave permission for land reclamation to begin for a new U.S. military base at Henoko, reneging on previous promises and furthering the effort to consolidate the American troop presence on the island, though away from urban Naha.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-76)

In December 2016 the U.S. returned 10,000 acres (40km2; 16sqmi) of the Northern Training Area on Okinawa to Japan. This reduced the footprint of the U.S. forces by 20% on the island. It was the biggest land return since 1972.[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island#cite_note-nta-77)

### Airport

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: Airport")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/20100526%E9%82%A3%E8%A6%87%E7%A9%BA%E6%B8%AF.JPG/250px-20100526%E9%82%A3%E8%A6%87%E7%A9%BA%E6%B8%AF.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:20100526%E9%82%A3%E8%A6%87%E7%A9%BA%E6%B8%AF.JPG) [Naha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha_Airport "Naha Airport") (2010)

### Roads

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=35 "Edit section: Roads")\]

The [Okinawa Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Expressway "Okinawa Expressway") is a [toll road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road "Toll road") that runs from [Naha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha,_Okinawa "Naha, Okinawa") to [Nago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nago,_Okinawa "Nago, Okinawa"), and has a speed limit of 80km/h (50mph), the highest on the island.

### Ferries

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okinawa_Island&action=edit&section=36 "Edit section: Ferries")\]

There are many [ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry "Ferry") to many of the nearby islands, such as [Ie Shima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ie_Shima "Ie Shima"). [Tomarin Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomari,_Okinawa "Tomari, Okinawa") in Naha, has ferries to nearby islands such as [Aguni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguni_Island "Aguni Island"), [Tokashiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokashiki_Island "Tokashiki Island") and [Zamami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamami_Island "Zamami Island").
