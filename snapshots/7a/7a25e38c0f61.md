Jamnagar refinery

The **Jamnagar refinery**, also known as **RIL Jamnagar** or **RPL Jamnagar**, is a private sector crude [oil refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_refinery "Oil refinery") owned by [Reliance Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Industries "Reliance Industries") in Motikhavdi, [Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar_district "Jamnagar district"), Gujarat, India. The refinery was commissioned in July 1999 with an initial installed capacity of 668,000 barrels per day (106,200m3/d). Its current installed capacity after expansion is 1,240,000 barrels per day (197,000m3/d).[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) It is currently the [largest oil refinery in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oil_refineries "List of oil refineries").[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jamnagar_refinery&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

In December 2001, Reliance Petroleum Limited (RPL) announced the commissioning of its refinery into a [special economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zone "Special economic zone") in [Jamnagar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar_district "Jamnagar district") of [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat"), India. The completion of the refinery has enabled Jamnagar to emerge as a 'Refinery land', housing the world's largest refining complex with an aggregate refining capacity of 1.24 million barrels (197,000m3) of oil per day, more than any other single location in the world.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)

The globally competitive RPL refinery was commissioned in 36months. RPL contracted several companies having expertise in engineering construction and refining like [Bechtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechtel "Bechtel"), [UOP LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UOP_LLC "UOP LLC") and [Foster Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_Wheeler "Foster Wheeler") ( [multinational corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation "Multinational corporation")) amongst others. There were plans for the pipeline to process High Pour Point crude oil extracted at Barmer, [Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan "Rajasthan"), although this would require an electrically heated traced pipeline to be set up from [Barmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmer_district "Barmer district") to Jamnagar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)

The entire complex, as of 2013, consists of manufacturing and allied facilities, utilities, off-sites, port facilities and a township (415 acres) with housing for its 2,500 employees, on over 7,500 [acres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre "Acre") (3,000 [ha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hectare "Hectare"); 11.7 [sqmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile "Square mile")) of land. If all of the pipes used in the refinery were laid out, one after another, they would connect the whole of India from north to south.[&#8202;](#ref-back-groundreport1-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-groundreport1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)

To complete the structure, [Dhirajlal Hirachand Ambani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhirubhai_Ambani "Dhirubhai Ambani") , the father of Mukesh Ambani, had a [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango "Mango") plantation of 102,000 trees planted, making it larger than that of the Indian [Mughal Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar "Akbar").[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) It is said to be the largest mango plantation in Asia, as well as a refuge for [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_birds "Migratory birds").[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) Over 200 different varieties of mangoes are harvested on the plantation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lo-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-lo) Thanks to the plantation, India became one of the largest mango exporters in Asia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lo-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-lo)

### Export expansion

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jamnagar_refinery&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Export expansion")\]

In 2019, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), the domestic demand for polymer declined sharply in India. As a result, in order to keep production and sales running, [Reliance Industries Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Industries_Limited "Reliance Industries Limited") management decided to refocus the production of polymer on export markets. With the help of the [Maersk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maersk "Maersk") shipping and logistics group, exports from the Jamnagar refinery were quadrupled to over 10,000 x 40' containers, which transported and exported through [Port Pipavav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Pipavav "Port Pipavav").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

In the 2020s, following [sanctions imposed on Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "International sanctions during the Russian invasion of Ukraine") by mainly Western countries, but not by India, the Jamnagar refinery was one of the main refiners exporting oil products made from Russian oil to sanctioning nations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) The refinery has been the destination of [Russian shadow fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_shadow_fleet "Russian shadow fleet") vessels.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

## Labour and environmental issues

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jamnagar_refinery&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Labour and environmental issues")\]

### Labour issues

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jamnagar_refinery&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Labour issues")\]

The Jamnagar refinery has been the site of several incidents involving labour issues, worker fatalities, and unrest.

In September 2005, tensions between local villagers and workers at the refinery escalated into violent clashes following a dispute over access to water, with the unrest spilling over to the nearby Essar oil refinery. The unrest was rooted in growing local resentment over the arrival of approximately 10,000 migrant labourers from Indian states such as [Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha "Odisha"), [Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar "Bihar"), and [Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh "Uttar Pradesh"), who were working under precarious contracts. In response, village leaders forced their relocation to worker camps constructed by Reliance and [Essar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essar_Group "Essar Group"), before the situation culminated in violent confrontations involving an estimated 5,000 workers and villagers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

In September 2015, a construction worker employed on an expansion project at the refinery complex was found dead, hanging in a room within a labour colony near the site. While some sources reported that Reliance Industries stated the cause of death to be a heart attack,[&#8202;](#ref-back-ok-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-ok)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jc-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-jc) others cited cardiac arrest as the official explanation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ld-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-ld) The colony, which accommodated over 50,000 migrant workers, became the focus of unrest following the incident.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ok-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-ok) Tensions escalated when a team of Reliance officials visited the colony to investigate, prompting a violent reaction from some labourers; believing the death to have resulted from a workplace accident, two worker groups torched vehicles and engaged in [stone-pelting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_pelting_in_India "Stone pelting in India") at company officials and policemen.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jc-1)[\[16\]](#refnote-jc)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18) Authorities later stated that the death was likely due to natural causes or suicide. Police forces were deployed, using [tear gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tear_gas "Tear gas") and live fire, to control the situation, resulting in one death and three injuries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ld-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-ld)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ok-2)[\[15\]](#refnote-ok)

ln November 2016, a [flash fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_fire "Flash fire") in the refinery complex led to the death of two workers with six others sustaining burn injuries. Refinery operations continued as normal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)

### Greenhouse gas emissions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jamnagar_refinery&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Greenhouse gas emissions")\]

The Jamnagar refinery is the world's largest [greenhouse gas emitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions "Greenhouse gas emissions") among refineries. In 2022, its emissions were estimated at 19.76 million tonnes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)
