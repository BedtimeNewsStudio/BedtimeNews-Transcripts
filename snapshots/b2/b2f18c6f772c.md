**Leo Eric Varadkar** ([/vəˈrædkər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [_və-RAD-kər_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); born 18 January 1979) is an Irish former [Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael "Fine Gael") politician who served as [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach "Taoiseach") from 2017 to 2020 and from 2022 to 2024, as [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste "Tánaiste") from 2020 to 2022, and as [leader of Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_Fine_Gael "Leader of Fine Gael") from 2017 to 2024. A [TD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_D%C3%A1la "Teachta Dála") for the [Dublin West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_West "Dublin West") constituency from 2007 to 2024, he held a range of other ministerial positions from 2011 to 2024. Described as [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics "Centre-right politics") economically, he advocated [free markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market "Free market"), lower taxes, and [welfare reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_reform "Welfare reform"). On social issues, he supported successful constitutional referendums to [legalise same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-fourth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland") and to [liberalise Ireland's abortion laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-sixth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland").

Born in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin"), Varadkar is the third child and first son in his [multiracial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_people "Multiracial people") family. His father Ashok is a doctor who was born and raised in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"), India. His mother Miriam grew up on a farm in [Dungarvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungarvan "Dungarvan"), County Waterford.

Varadkar studied medicine at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin "Trinity College Dublin") and worked as a [non-consultant hospital doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-consultant_hospital_doctor "Non-consultant hospital doctor") and [general practitioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner "General practitioner"). A member of Fine Gael since his teenage years, he ran unsuccessfully in the [1999 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Irish_local_elections "1999 Irish local elections") but was co-opted onto [Fingal County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingal_County_Council "Fingal County Council") in 2003.

He was elected to the council in the [2004 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Irish_local_elections "2004 Irish local elections"), attaining the highest number of first-preference votes of any candidate in the country. First elected to [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann "Dáil Éireann") in the [2007 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Irish_general_election "2007 Irish general election"), he was appointed to the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ireland "Government of Ireland") of Taoiseach [Enda Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enda_Kenny "Enda Kenny") following the [2011 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Irish_general_election "2011 Irish general election"), which saw Fine Gael return to government after 14 years in [opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_(politics) "Opposition (politics)"). He served as [Minister for Transport, Tourism and Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Transport_(Ireland) "Minister for Transport (Ireland)") from 2011 to 2014, [Minister for Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Health_(Ireland) "Minister for Health (Ireland)") from 2014 to 2016, and [Minister for Social Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Social_Protection "Minister for Social Protection") from 2016 to 2017. During the campaign for the [2015 same-sex marriage referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-fourth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland"), he [came out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming_out "Coming out") as [gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_men "Gay men"), the first serving Irish minister to do so.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)

Following Kenny's resignation, Varadkar defeated [Simon Coveney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Coveney "Simon Coveney") in the [2017 Fine Gael leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Fine_Gael_leadership_election "2017 Fine Gael leadership election") and was appointed Taoiseach on 14 June 2017. Aged 38, he was at that time the youngest Taoiseach in the history of the state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Britannica-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-Britannica) He became the first Taoiseach from an ethnic minority group,[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) as well as Ireland's first, and the world's fifth, [openly gay head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_first_openly_LGBT_holders_of_political_offices "List of the first openly LGBT holders of political offices").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) He led Fine Gael into the [2020 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_general_election "2020 Irish general election"), in which the party won 35 seats, a loss of 15 seats since the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election "2016 Irish general election"). After lengthy negotiations, Fine Gael formed a three-party coalition government with [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il "Fianna Fáil") and the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_(Ireland) "Green Party (Ireland)"), with the Fianna Fáil and Fine Gael party leaders [rotating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_government "Rotation government") the offices of Taoiseach and Tánaiste.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Lehane-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-Lehane)

Varadkar served as Tánaiste and [Minister for Enterprise, Trade and Employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Enterprise,_Tourism_and_Employment "Minister for Enterprise, Tourism and Employment") from June 2020 to December 2022, when he exchanged positions with Fianna Fáil leader [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin "Micheál Martin") to begin his second term as Taoiseach. Citing personal and political reasons, he resigned as Fine Gael leader on 20 March 2024 and resigned as Taoiseach on 8 April; he was succeeded by [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris "Simon Harris").[&#8202;](#ref-back-8April-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-8April)[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) After deciding not to contest the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_general_election "2024 Irish general election"), he retired from public life in November 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc.com-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-bbc.com)[&#8202;](#ref-back-RTE_20240817-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-RTE_20240817) In April 2025, he took up a position as global advisor at Penta Group, a public-relations firm headquartered in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.")[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) In September 2025, he published his autobiography, _Speaking My Mind_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-:1)

## Early life

Born on 18 January 1979, in the [Rotunda Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotunda_Hospital "Rotunda Hospital"), Dublin, Varadkar is the third child and only son of Ashok and Miriam ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Howell) Varadkar. His father was born in Bombay (now [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai")), India, and moved to the United Kingdom in the 1960s, to work as a doctor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) His mother, born in [Dungarvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungarvan "Dungarvan"), County Waterford, met her future husband while working as a nurse in [Slough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slough "Slough").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian_20170602-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-Guardian_20170602) Early in 1971, they married in the UK.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)[&#8202;](#ref-back-IrishIndo_2018-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-IrishIndo_2018) Sophia, the elder of his two sisters,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Extra_SophiaV-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-Extra_SophiaV) was born while the family lived in [Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester "Leicester"). They moved to India,[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) before settling in Dublin in 1973,[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) where his other sister, Sonia, was born.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)

Varadkar was educated at the St Francis Xavier [national school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_school_(Ireland) "National school (Ireland)") in [Blanchardstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanchardstown "Blanchardstown") and then [The King's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King's_Hospital "The King's Hospital"), a [Church of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Ireland "Church of Ireland") secondary school in [Palmerstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerstown "Palmerstown"). At the age of 16, he joined [Young Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Fine_Gael "Young Fine Gael"). He was admitted to [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin "Trinity College Dublin") (TCD), where he briefly studied law before switching to its [School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Medicine_(Trinity_College_Dublin) "School of Medicine (Trinity College Dublin)"). At TCD, he was active in the university's Young Fine Gael branch and served as vice-president of the [Youth of the European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_of_the_European_People's_Party "Youth of the European People's Party"), the youth wing of the [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party "European People's Party"), of which [Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael "Fine Gael") is a member.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) Varadkar was selected for the [Washington-Ireland Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington-Ireland_Program "Washington-Ireland Program"), a half-year personal and professional development program in Washington, D.C., for students from Ireland.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23) While there, he interned for the [US House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_House_of_Representatives "US House of Representatives").[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

Varadkar graduated in 2003, after completing his internship at [KEM Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEM_Hospital "KEM Hospital") in Mumbai.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25) He then spent several years working as a [non-consultant hospital doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-consultant_hospital_doctor "Non-consultant hospital doctor") in [St. James's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._James's_Hospital "St. James's Hospital") and [Connolly Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connolly_Hospital "Connolly Hospital"), before specialising as a [general practitioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner "General practitioner") in 2010.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Britannica-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-Britannica)

## Early political career

### Fingal County Council (2003–2007)

Varadkar was twenty years old and a second-year medical student when he unsuccessfully contested the [1999 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Irish_local_elections "1999 Irish local elections") in the [Mulhuddart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulhuddart "Mulhuddart") local electoral area. Varadkar was co-opted to [Fingal County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingal_County_Council "Fingal County Council") in 2003, for the [Castleknock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castleknock "Castleknock") local electoral area, as a replacement for [Sheila Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Terry_(politician) "Sheila Terry (politician)"). At the [2004 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Irish_local_elections "2004 Irish local elections"), he received the highest first-preference vote in the country with 4,894 votes and was elected on the first count.[&#8202;](#ref-back-elecs_irl-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-elecs_irl)

### Dáil Éireann (2007–2011)

Varadkar was elected to [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann "Dáil Éireann") at the [2007 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Irish_general_election "2007 Irish general election") as a Fine Gael [TD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_D%C3%A1la "Teachta Dála") for the [Dublin West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_West "Dublin West") constituency.[&#8202;](#ref-back-oireachtas_db-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-oireachtas_db) After the 2007 general election, then [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Ireland) "Leader of the Opposition (Ireland)"), [Enda Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enda_Kenny "Enda Kenny"), appointed him to the [front bench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael_Front_Bench "Fine Gael Front Bench") as spokesperson for [Enterprise, Trade and Employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Enterprise,_Tourism_and_Employment "Minister for Enterprise, Tourism and Employment") until a 2010 reshuffle, when he became [spokesperson on Communications, Energy and Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Climate,_Energy_and_the_Environment "Minister for Climate, Energy and the Environment").[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) It was also during 2010 that Varadkar was reported to be a supporter of an attempt to oust Enda Kenny as leader of Fine Gael and replace him with [Richard Bruton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bruton "Richard Bruton"). The heave was not successful, but in the aftermath, Varadkar was able to repair his relationship with Kenny.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Che_Varadkar-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-Che_Varadkar) At the [2011 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Irish_general_election "2011 Irish general election"), Varadkar was re-elected to the Dáil, with 8,359 first-preference votes (19.7%).[&#8202;](#ref-back-elecs_irl-1)[\[26\]](#refnote-elecs_irl)

## Government minister (2011–2017)

### Minister for Transport, Tourism, and Sport (2011–2014)

When Fine Gael formed a coalition government with the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(Ireland) "Labour Party (Ireland)"), Varadkar was appointed [Minister for Transport, Tourism and Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Transport_(Ireland) "Minister for Transport (Ireland)") on 9 March 2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RTÉ-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-RTÉ) This appointment was considered a surprise, as Varadkar was not known as a sports lover. He said that while he knew "a lot of facts... I don't play the sports."[&#8202;](#ref-back-'True_blue'_Varadkar_red_card_flag-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-'True_blue'_Varadkar_red_card_flag)

In May 2011, Varadkar suggested Ireland was "very unlikely" to resume borrowing in 2012 and might need a [second bailout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-2008_Irish_economic_downturn#Economic_Adjustment_Programme_for_Ireland "Post-2008 Irish economic downturn"), causing jitters on international markets about Ireland's credibility.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33) Many of his cabinet colleagues frowned on Varadkar's forthrightness, as did the [European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Central_Bank "European Central Bank").[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34)[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35) [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach "Taoiseach") [Enda Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enda_Kenny "Enda Kenny") repeated the line of the [Government of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ireland "Government of Ireland"), that the State would not require a further bailout from the European Union or the International Monetary Fund, and said he had warned all ministers against publicly disparaging the economy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37) Varadkar said that reaction to the story was hyped up but that he was not misquoted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38) The _[Evening Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Herald "Evening Herald")_ repeatedly described Varadkar as [gaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaffe "Gaffe")-prone.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Herald_Gaffe_1-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-Herald_Gaffe_1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Herald_Gaffe_2-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-Herald_Gaffe_2)

### Minister for Health (2014–2016)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Minister_Varadkar_and_Joan_Burton_open_AMA_unit_at_Connolly_Hospital%2C_July2014.jpg/500px-Minister_Varadkar_and_Joan_Burton_open_AMA_unit_at_Connolly_Hospital%2C_July2014.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Minister_Varadkar_and_Joan_Burton_open_AMA_unit_at_Connolly_Hospital,_July2014.jpg) Health Minister Varadkar with Tánaiste [Joan Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Burton "Joan Burton") at the opening of a unit at [Connolly Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connolly_Hospital "Connolly Hospital"), [Blanchardstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanchardstown "Blanchardstown"), July 2014

In the cabinet reshuffle of July 2014, Varadkar replaced [James Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Reilly_(Irish_politician) "James Reilly (Irish politician)") as [Minister for Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Health_(Ireland) "Minister for Health (Ireland)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-RT-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-RT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42)

Varadkar was returned to the Dáil at the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election "2016 Irish general election"). He retained the health portfolio in an acting capacity until May of that year, due to [the delay in government formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_government_formation "2016 Irish government formation"). In one of his final acts as Minister for Health, Varadkar cut €12million from the €35million allocated to that year's budget for mental health care, telling the Dáil that the cuts were "necessary as the funding could be better used elsewhere."[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43)

### Minister for Social Protection (2016–2017)

On 6 May 2016, after government formation talks had concluded, Taoiseach Enda Kenny appointed Varadkar as [Minister for Social Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Social_Protection "Minister for Social Protection").[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44) During his time in the ministry, he launched a controversial[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-:0) campaign against welfare fraud called "Welfare Cheats Cheat Us All.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46) It was labelled a "hate campaign" by a former social welfare inspector, who claimed the campaign was "all about \[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[Varadkar's\] aspirations to be leader".[\[45\]](#refnote-:0)

## First term as Taoiseach (2017–2020)

### 2017

See also: [Government of the 32nd Dáil §31st government of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_32nd_D%C3%A1il#31st_government_of_Ireland "Government of the 32nd Dáil")

Varadkar and the prime minister of Canada, [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau "Justin Trudeau"), delivering joint remarks in July 2017

On 2 June 2017, Varadkar was [elected leader of Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Fine_Gael_leadership_election "2017 Fine Gael leadership election"), defeating [Simon Coveney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Coveney "Simon Coveney").[&#8202;](#ref-back-RTE2017-06-02a-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-RTE2017-06-02a) Although Coveney had the support of more Fine Gael members than Varadkar, the electoral college system more strongly weighted the votes of the party's parliamentarians, with these strongly backing Varadkar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)

Like [Enda Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enda_Kenny "Enda Kenny")'s second term, Varadkar relied upon the support of Independents and the abstention of [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il "Fianna Fáil") TDs to support his bid for Taoiseach. On 14 June 2017, he was elected as Taoiseach in a 57–50 vote with 47 abstentions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC_elect-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-BBC_elect) He became Ireland's first openly gay Taoiseach, as well as the youngest; however, he is not the youngest head of an Irish government, as both [Éamon de Valera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89amon_de_Valera "Éamon de Valera") and [Michael Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Collins_(Irish_Leader) "Michael Collins (Irish Leader)") were younger on assuming their respective offices in revolutionary governments prior to the establishment of the state. He is also the first head of government who is of half-Indian descent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian_20170602-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-Guardian_20170602) It was also the first time that one [Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael "Fine Gael") Taoiseach was succeeded by another.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FGsucceedsFG01-0)[\[nb 1\]](#refnote-FGsucceedsFG01)

One of Varadkar's first acts as Taoiseach was to announce [a referendum on abortion for 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_Bill_2018_(Ireland) "Thirty-sixth Amendment of the Constitution Bill 2018 (Ireland)"). He said that the government would also lay out a road map for achieving a low-carbon economy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-51)

His government nearly collapsed as a result of the [Garda whistleblower scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garda_whistleblower_scandal "Garda whistleblower scandal") and [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste "Tánaiste") (Deputy Prime Minister) [Frances Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Fitzgerald_(politician) "Frances Fitzgerald (politician)")'s role in it. [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il "Fianna Fáil"), the main opposition party, who were in a confidence-and-supply agreement with Fine Gael, threatened a motion of no confidence in the government. After days of gridlock, the crisis was averted, after Fitzgerald resigned from the cabinet to prevent triggering an election that could jeopardise the Irish position in [Brexit negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit_negotiations "Brexit negotiations").[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-52) Shortly after this, Varadkar appointed former leadership rival and [Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Trade "Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade") Simon Coveney as Tánaiste, [Heather Humphreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_Humphreys "Heather Humphreys") as [Minister for Business, Enterprise and Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Enterprise,_Tourism_and_Employment "Minister for Enterprise, Tourism and Employment") and [Josepha Madigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josepha_Madigan "Josepha Madigan") as [Minister for Culture, Heritage and the Gaeltacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Culture,_Heritage_and_the_Gaeltacht "Minister for Culture, Heritage and the Gaeltacht"), in a small reshuffle of the cabinet.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-53)[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-54)

Shortly after the Fitzgerald crisis, an impasse was reached in the [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit") talks, as [Arlene Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlene_Foster "Arlene Foster"), leader of the [Democratic Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unionist_Party "Democratic Unionist Party"), objected to a deal agreed to by Varadkar, [British Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Prime_Minister "British Prime Minister") [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May "Theresa May"), and [President of the European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Commission "President of the European Commission") [Jean-Claude Juncker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Juncker "Jean-Claude Juncker").[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-55)[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-56) This prevented an agreement from being reached as the deadline approached. Varadkar stated he was "surprised and disappointed" the UK could not reach a deal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-57) Later in the week, a consensus deal was finalised. Varadkar stated he had received guarantees from the UK there would be no hard border between Ireland and Northern Ireland. He later said he and his cabinet had "achieved all we set out to achieve" during the talks before quoting former British prime minister [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill"), by saying "This is not the end but it is the end of the beginning".[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-58)[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-59) An _[Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Times "The Irish Times")_ poll taken during these days showed Varadkar with a 53% approval rating, the highest for any Taoiseach since 2011, and showed Fine Gael with an eleven-point lead over Fianna Fáil. Government satisfaction was also at 41%, the highest in almost 10 years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-60) _Irish Times_ columnist Pat Leahy claimed Varadkar had ended 2017 "on a high" and IrishCentral called it the Taoiseach's "finest hour".[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-61)[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-62)

### 2018

In January 2018, his opinion poll approval ratings reached 60%, a ten-year high for any Taoiseach.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-63)

In January 2018, he announced that the [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-sixth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland") to repeal Ireland's [8th Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Eighth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland") which prevented any liberalisation of restrictive abortion laws would take place in May. If passed, it would allow the government to introduce new legislation. It was proposed that women would be allowed unrestricted access to abortion up until 12 weeks, with exceptions if the mother's life is in danger up until six months. Varadkar said he would campaign for liberalising the laws, saying his mind was changed by difficult cases during his tenure as [Minister for Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Health_(Ireland) "Minister for Health (Ireland)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-64) The referendum passed with 66% of the votes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-65)

Varadkar was included in [_Time_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)") magazine's _100 Most Influential People of 2018._[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-66)

### 2019

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/President_Trump_Meets_with_the_Taoiseach_of_Ireland_%2848012258958%29.jpg/500px-President_Trump_Meets_with_the_Taoiseach_of_Ireland_%2848012258958%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Trump_Meets_with_the_Taoiseach_of_Ireland_(48012258958).jpg) Varadkar and US president [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") in [Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon,_County_Clare "Shannon, County Clare"), Ireland in June 2019

On 24 January 2019, Varadkar said in an interview with [Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews "Euronews") that he was standing firm on the [Irish backstop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_backstop "Irish backstop") and called Brexit an act of self-harm that was not fully thought through. He also said the technology promised by the Brexiteers to solve the Northern Ireland border issue "doesn't yet exist".[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-67)

Varadkar stated he will refuse to ratify the [EU–Mercosur free trade agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union%E2%80%93Mercosur_free_trade_agreement "European Union–Mercosur free trade agreement") unless Brazil commits to protecting the environment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-68)[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-69) The fear is that the deal could lead to more [deforestation of the Amazon rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_of_the_Amazon_rainforest "Deforestation of the Amazon rainforest") as it expands market access to Brazilian beef.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-70)

### 2020

"A Ministerial Broadcast by An Taoiseach Leo Varadkar, TD" redirects here. For further details on how COVID-19 impacted the country, see [COVID-19 pandemic in the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland "COVID-19 pandemic in the Republic of Ireland").

On 14 January 2020, Varadkar sought a dissolution of the [32nd Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_D%C3%A1il "32nd Dáil"), which was granted by President [Michael D. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Higgins "Michael D. Higgins"), and scheduled [a general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_general_election "2020 Irish general election") for 8 February.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-71) In that election, Varadkar was re-elected in the Dublin West constituency,[&#8202;](#ref-back-irishtimes-2020-02-10-ge20-dublin-west-report-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-irishtimes-2020-02-10-ge20-dublin-west-report)[&#8202;](#ref-back-irishtimes-2020-02-10-ge20-dublin-west-results-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-irishtimes-2020-02-10-ge20-dublin-west-results) but Fine Gael fell to 35 seats, 15 fewer than in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election "2016 Irish general election"), and falling to third place behind [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il "Fianna Fáil") (38 seats) and [Sinn Féin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinn_F%C3%A9in "Sinn Féin") (37 seats). Varadkar ruled out any possibility of a Fine Gael–Sinn Féin coalition during the election campaign, though a "grand coalition" of Fianna Fáil and Fine Gael was floated as a final possibility.[&#8202;](#ref-back-carroll9feb-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-carroll9feb) However, on 12 February, Varadkar conceded that Fine Gael had lost the election and that he was very likely to become the next [Opposition Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Ireland) "Leader of the Opposition (Ireland)"). Varadkar added that Fine Gael was "willing to step back" to allow Sinn Féin, as the winner of the popular vote, to have the first opportunity to form a government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-75) On 20 February, Varadkar offered his resignation to President Higgins at [Áras an Uachtaráin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ras_an_Uachtar%C3%A1in "Áras an Uachtaráin"), pursuant to the constitution, remaining, however, as Taoiseach until [the formation of a new government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_government_formation "2020 Irish government formation").[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-76)

During this period, the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland "COVID-19 pandemic in the Republic of Ireland") arrived in Ireland. While in Washington, D.C., ahead of [Saint Patrick's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick's_Day "Saint Patrick's Day"), Varadkar announced measures intended to stop COVID-19 spreading, including the closure of all schools, universities and childcare facilities from the following day, as well as the closure of all cultural institutions and the cancellation of "all indoor mass gatherings of more than 100 people and outdoor mass gatherings of more than 500 people".[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-77) After returning home early, Varadkar addressed the nation on Saint Patrick's night during _A Ministerial Broadcast by An Taoiseach Leo Varadkar, TD_, introducing television viewers to the concept of " [cocooning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocooning_(behaviour) "Cocooning (behaviour)")", i.e. "At a certain point... we will advise the elderly and people who have a long-term illness to stay at home for several weeks".[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-78)[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-79)[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-80) The speech was the most watched television event in Irish history, surpassing the previous record held by _[The Late Late Toy Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Late_Toy_Show "The Late Late Toy Show")_ by an additional total of about 25% and was widely distributed globally.[&#8202;](#ref-back-81-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-81)[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-82) It was also plagiarised by Irish businessman [Peter Bellew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bellew "Peter Bellew"), the chief operating officer at British low-cost airline group [EasyJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EasyJet "EasyJet").[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-83)

In response to a March 2020 [Health Service Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Service_Executive "Health Service Executive") appeal to healthcare professionals, Varadkar rejoined the medical register and offered to work as a doctor one day each week.[&#8202;](#ref-back-guardian-5apr2020-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-guardian-5apr2020)

### Formation of three-party coalition government

A draft programme for government was agreed between Fianna Fáil, Fine Gael, and the Green Party on 15 June 2020. It was determined that the position of Taoiseach would rotate between Micheál Martin and Leo Varadkar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rte15June-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-rte15June) The programme needed the approval by each party's membership. Fianna Fáil and the Green Party require a simple majority and a [two-thirds vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-thirds_vote "Two-thirds vote"), respectively, in a postal ballot of all members, while Fine Gael uses an electoral college system, with its parliamentary party making up 50% of the electorate, constituency delegates 25%, councillors 15% and the party's executive council filling the final 10%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-86)

On 26 June, Fine Gael voted 80%, Fianna Fáil voted 74% and the Green Party voted 76% in favour of the programme. [Clare Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Bailey "Clare Bailey"), the leader of the [Green Party in Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_in_Northern_Ireland "Green Party in Northern Ireland") – a branch of the Irish Green Party – publicly rejected the idea of the Greens being part of the coalition deal with Fianna Fáil and Fine Gael. She said the coalition deal proposed the "most fiscally conservative arrangements in a generation".[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-87) The coalition deal allowed for a government to be formed on 27 June, with Fianna Fáil leader Micheál Martin taking over as [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach "Taoiseach").[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-88)[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-89) He was appointed by [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ireland "President of Ireland") [Michael D. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Higgins "Michael D. Higgins") on 27 June, marking the end of Varadkar's first term.[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-90)

### GP contract leak

On 31 October 2020, _[Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_(magazine) "Village (magazine)")_ published an article alleging that Varadkar had leaked confidential documents, including a draft contract between the [Health Service Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Service_Executive "Health Service Executive") and [general practitioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner "General practitioner") that was agreed but still subject to acceptance by GPs at the time, and officially unavailable to members of the Oireachtas. The article included screenshots of [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") messages, including one with a photo of the cover of the leaked document, and alleged the handwriting visible was Varadkar's.[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-91) While _Village_ claimed the leaks might be unlawful, Varadkar denied this was the case and described the article as "inaccurate and grossly defamatory". He said that the provision of the agreement by an informal communication channel was not the best practice.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-92)

In response to the claims by _Village_, the Green Party called for Varadkar to give a detailed account to the Dáil in response to the accusations. This request was seconded by Sinn Féin's health spokesperson, stating: "The facts for me here are clear – Leo, as the leader of Fine Gael and Taoiseach at the time, passed on a document to a friend about sensitive negotiations involving hundreds of millions of euro of taxpayers' money."[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-93) Varadkar's Fine Gael colleague [Paschal Donohoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paschal_Donohoe "Paschal Donohoe") also expressed a desire for Varadkar to answer questions in the Dáil.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-94)[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-95)[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-96) Varadkar apologised in the Dáil for "errors of judgement" in sharing a copy of the contract, and rejected any suggestion that he had anything to gain personally from giving the IMO document to the NAGP president as "false and deeply offensive".[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-97)
The leak was the subject of a criminal investigation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-98) In April 2022, a file was submitted to the [director of public prosecutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_Public_Prosecutions_(Ireland) "Director of Public Prosecutions (Ireland)") (DPP) for review.[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-99)[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-100) On 6 July 2022, the DPP decided that Varadkar would not face prosecution.[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-101)[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-102)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Irish_Government_coalition_leaders%2C_Dec_2022_%28cropped%29.jpg/500px-Irish_Government_coalition_leaders%2C_Dec_2022_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Irish_Government_coalition_leaders,_Dec_2022_(cropped).jpg) Varadkar beside his coalition partners [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin "Micheál Martin") and [Eamon Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eamon_Ryan "Eamon Ryan") in December 2022

### 2023

On 13 April 2023, Varadkar met with U.S. president [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") at [Farmleigh House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmleigh_House "Farmleigh House") during his [four-day visit to the island of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidential_trips_made_by_Joe_Biden_(2023)#April "List of presidential trips made by Joe Biden (2023)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-104)

On 6 May 2023, Varadkar, along with President [Michael D. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Higgins "Michael D. Higgins"), attended the [Coronation of Charles III and Camilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Charles_III_and_Camilla "Coronation of Charles III and Camilla"), marking the first time a serving taoiseach attended the coronation of a British monarch.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-105)

On 23 November 2023, a [mass stabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_stabbing "Mass stabbing") outside a [primary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_school "Primary school") in [Parnell Square East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parnell_Square_(Dublin) "Parnell Square (Dublin)"), Dublin happened, with a five-year-old girl and a woman in her 30s were seriously injured, and three others hurt.[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-106) Following the knife attack, [a riot took place in Dublin city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dublin_riot "2023 Dublin riot") due to the assailant being born in Algeria.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-107) Leo Varadkar responded, saying he was "shocked" by the knife attack and praised the emergency services for responding "very quickly".[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-108) He said later that the rioters had brought "shame" to Dublin for their families and themselves,[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-109) and were not motivated by patriotism but by "hate" and their "love" of "violence", "chaos", and "causing pain to others", and pledged to use the "full resources of the law, the full machinery of the state to punish those involved" in what he called "grotesque events". Varadkar also pledged to pass new laws to enable police "to make better use of" CCTV evidence and "modernise" laws regarding hate and incitement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-f24-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-f24)

### 2024

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b9/Leo_Varadkar_in_the_United_States_on_Saint_Patrick%27s_Day_2024_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Leo_Varadkar_in_the_United_States_on_Saint_Patrick's_Day_2024_(cropped).jpg) Varadkar on Saint Patrick's Day at the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House"), in 2024

In March 2024, [two referendums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_constitutional_referendums "2024 Irish constitutional referendums") were held on proposed [amendments to the Constitution of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Amendments to the Constitution of Ireland"). The Thirty-ninth Amendment of the Constitution (The Family) Bill 2023 proposed to revise the definition of family to include durable relationships outside marriage. The Fortieth Amendment of the Constitution (Care) Bill 2023 proposed to remove references to a woman's "life within the home" and "duties in the home" and add a new article on care within the family. Voters overwhelmingly rejected both proposed amendments, delivering a rebuke to a government and in the eyes of many, personally to Varadkar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-111)

#### Resignation and succession

On 20 March 2024, Varadkar announced his intention to step down as Taoiseach and Fine Gael leader, saying that he was no longer "the best person for the job".[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-112) He said his "reasons for stepping down are both personal and political".[&#8202;](#ref-back-rte_resignation-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-rte_resignation)[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-114)[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-115) He said that his resignation as Fine Gael leader would be with immediate effect, and that he would continue in office as Taoiseach pending the appointment of his successor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-116-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-116) On 24 March, [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris "Simon Harris") was elected unopposed in the [2024 Fine Gael leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Fine_Gael_leadership_election "2024 Fine Gael leadership election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-117-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-117) On 8 April, Varadkar submitted his resignation to President Higgins.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8April-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-8April) The [Dáil nomination for Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_vote_for_Taoiseach "Dáil vote for Taoiseach") was held on 9 April, leading to the appointment of Harris as the new Taoiseach by President [Michael D. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Higgins "Michael D. Higgins").

On 16 July, Varadkar announced that he would not stand for re-election at the [next general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_general_election "2024 Irish general election"), and would retire from politics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RTE_20240817-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-RTE_20240817)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc.com-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-bbc.com)

## Political views and profile

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/President_Joe_Biden_and_Irish_Taoiseach_Leo_Varadkar_2023.jpg/500px-President_Joe_Biden_and_Irish_Taoiseach_Leo_Varadkar_2023.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Joe_Biden_and_Irish_Taoiseach_Leo_Varadkar_2023.jpg) Varadkar with U.S. president [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") on [St Patrick's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_Day "St Patrick's Day") in 2023

After becoming a Teachta Dála in 2007, Varadkar developed a reputation in the late 2000s and early 2010s as somewhat of an outspoken maverick. Just one week into his role as a TD, Varadkar branded sitting Taoiseach [Bertie Ahern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertie_Ahern "Bertie Ahern") "cunning and devious" in the Dáil.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Britannica-2)[\[2\]](#refnote-Britannica) In 2011, he upset a number of his own party colleagues when he negatively compared embattled Fianna Fáil leader and Taoiseach [Brian Cowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Cowen "Brian Cowen") to former Fine Gael leader and Taoiseach [Garret FitzGerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garret_FitzGerald "Garret FitzGerald").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_May_2010-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_May_2010)[&#8202;](#ref-back-IrishIndo_2018-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-IrishIndo_2018) By 2012, Varadkar's tendency to make "off the cuff" remarks led to the _[Evening Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Herald "Evening Herald")_ describing him as "gaffe-prone".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Herald_Gaffe_1-1)[\[39\]](#refnote-Herald_Gaffe_1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Herald_Gaffe_2-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-Herald_Gaffe_2) In 2017, _[Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Times "Irish Times")_ columnist Stephen Collins described Varadkar as "coming across to the public, especially younger voters, as if he is not a politician at all".[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-119) In 2022, the _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times")_ noted that "critics have pointed to Mr. Varadkar's stiffness of manner and tendency to speak his mind, to the point of insensitivity".[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT_December_2022-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-NYT_December_2022)

In 2010, the _[Irish Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Examiner "Irish Examiner")_ described Varadkar as "conservative"[&#8202;](#ref-back-Opposes_abortion_in_cases_of_rape-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-Opposes_abortion_in_cases_of_rape) while that same year the Irish magazine _[Hot Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Press "Hot Press")_ described his policy positions as "radical right", although in the same article, he described himself as "centre-right", which he defined as "to be somebody who is right of centre is somebody who has broadly [liberal-conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_conservatism "Liberal conservatism")/ [Christian-democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_democracy "Christian democracy") ideals, and the basic principles of that is that before you can distribute wealth you have to create it. So the first thing that you need to do is set up an environment in which wealth can be created, and then it's the role of the government to distribute it reasonably equitably."[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_May_2010-1)[\[117\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_May_2010) In 2017, _[the Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ stated that "Varadkar's centre-right politics are clearly conservative".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian_20170602-2)[\[15\]](#refnote-Guardian_20170602) In 2022, the political magazine _[the Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phoenix_(magazine) "The Phoenix (magazine)")_ suggested that in 2010 Varadkar was the ideological leader of a "hard-right" faction within Fine Gael who unsuccessfully sought to replace leader Enda Kenny with Richard Bruton, but over the course of the next decade Varadkar was brought further and further into the political centre.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Che_Varadkar-1)[\[29\]](#refnote-Che_Varadkar)

In 2011, Varadkar cited Ryanair CEO [Michael O'Leary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_O'Leary_(businessman) "Michael O'Leary (businessman)") as the Irish person he most admired due to his forthrightness, and [Otto von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck "Otto von Bismarck") as a historic figure he admired, crediting Bismarck as a conservative who was able to enact social reforms.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Varadkar_is_no_typical-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-Varadkar_is_no_typical)[&#8202;](#ref-back-IrishIndo_2018-2)[\[17\]](#refnote-IrishIndo_2018) In 2021 Varadkar gave a dedicated lecture on [Noël Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Browne "Noël Browne") to students of [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin "Trinity College Dublin"), in which he summarised Browne's career. Varadkar noted Browne's cantankerous reputation but generally praised Browne, with Varadkar stating that he always "admired his idealism, his passion, and his determination to stand up for the causes and the people he believed in".[&#8202;](#ref-back-123-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-123)

### Economy

Varadkar is a proponent of tax cuts and welfare reform,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Varadkar_is_no_typical-1)[\[121\]](#refnote-Varadkar_is_no_typical) and supports investment in Ireland by [multinational corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation "Multinational corporation") such as [Apple Inc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc "Apple Inc"), alongside keeping Ireland's corporate tax rate low.[&#8202;](#ref-back-124-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-124) During his time as Minister for Social Protection in 2016 and 2017, Varadkar launched the "Welfare Cheats Cheat Us All" campaign, aimed at those committing welfare fraud.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Britannica-3)[\[2\]](#refnote-Britannica)

### Foreign policy

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Tallinn_Digital_Summit._Welcome_dinner_hosted_by_HE_Donald_Tusk._Handshake_%2837330678376%29.jpg/960px-Tallinn_Digital_Summit._Welcome_dinner_hosted_by_HE_Donald_Tusk._Handshake_%2837330678376%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tallinn_Digital_Summit._Welcome_dinner_hosted_by_HE_Donald_Tusk._Handshake_(37330678376).jpg) Varadkar alongside [Jüri Ratas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCri_Ratas "Jüri Ratas"), the prime minister of Estonia, and [Donald Tusk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Tusk "Donald Tusk"), President of the European Council, in 2017

Varadkar, alongside his party Fine Gael, universally opposed [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit"), and much of his time during his first tenure as Taoiseach was spent in negotiations with the British government over how the shared border between the United Kingdom and Ireland would operate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-125)

Varadkar is a supporter of [CETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CETA "CETA"), a proposed free trade agreement between Canada and the European Union.[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-126) However, Varadkar has faced opposition to many aspects of the agreement, not just from opposition parties, but also members of his own government coalition, especially members of the Green party who object in particular to a proposed "investment court" system. Critics of the agreement fear the investment court could be used to strike down environmentalist laws.[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-127)

In October 2023, he condemned [October 7 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks "October 7 attacks"), saying, "The \[&#8202;](#ref-back-128-0)[Irish\] government condemns it unreservedly, there can be no excuse for targeting women, children, taking children and women, civilians as hostage". Varadkar also urged "restraint" from the Israeli government, saying that "the free world is standing in solidarity with Israel", but that could change if the Israeli response "results in unnecessary civilian deaths in Gaza".[\[127\]](#refnote-128)

Varadkar later criticised [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel")'s [blockade of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2023_Israeli_blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip "October 2023 Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip") and what he called the "collective punishment" of people in Gaza.[&#8202;](#ref-back-129-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-129) In November, he commented on the release of 8-year-old Irish-Israeli citizen [Emily Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Hand "Emily Hand"), who had been kidnapped by Hamas, saying, "An innocent child who was lost has now been found and returned... A little girl was snatched from her home and held captive for almost seven weeks. She spent her ninth birthday as a hostage." He was criticised for the use of the phrases "lost" and "found" by Israel's foreign minister [Eli Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Cohen_(politician,_born_1972) "Eli Cohen (politician, born 1972)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-130) Varadkar rejected [calls to boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2024#Calls_for_exclusion "Israel in the Eurovision Song Contest 2024") the [Eurovision Song Contest 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2024 "Eurovision Song Contest 2024") due to Israel's participation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-131) In January 2024, he expressed doubts about South Africa's [genocide case against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_v._Israel_(Genocide_Convention) "South Africa v. Israel (Genocide Convention)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-132-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-132) In March 2024, he resisted calls to boycott a planned [Saint Patrick's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick's_Day "Saint Patrick's Day") (17 March) meeting with the American president [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden"), who has been the target of fierce criticism in Ireland over his stance on the Gaza war;[&#8202;](#ref-back-133-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-133)[&#8202;](#ref-back-134-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-134) Varadkar noted "differences of opinions \[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[between the U.S. and Ireland\] in relation to Israel and Gaza".[\[134\]](#refnote-135)

### LGBT issues

In a 2010 interview, Varadkar stated that while he did not consider homosexuality morally wrong, he opposed same-sex marriage, although he supported [civil partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_partnerships "Civil partnerships").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_May_2010-2)[\[117\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_May_2010) However, following the public acknowledgement of his homosexuality in January 2015, he began advocating for same-sex marriage during the national debate in the prelude to the [2015 referendum on same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-fourth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland").[&#8202;](#ref-back-136-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-136)[&#8202;](#ref-back-137-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-137)

In 2022, Varadkar stated that he was in support of the [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") community, and credited a gender recognition legislation brought into law during the Fine Gael/Labour government of 2011 to 2016 as a "huge step forward" in terms of Irish transgender issues.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_June_2022-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_June_2022)

### Abortion

In 2010, Varadkar stated that he opposed abortion,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_May_2010-3)[\[117\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_May_2010) that he was not in favour of introducing new legislation on abortion,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_May_2010-4)[\[117\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_May_2010) and that he opposed abortion in cases of rape because he felt it would lead to "abortion on demand".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Opposes_abortion_in_cases_of_rape-1)[\[120\]](#refnote-Opposes_abortion_in_cases_of_rape) However, by 2014, Varadkar had changed his position and began arguing in the Dáil in favour of abortion up to 12 weeks, and more if the mother's life was in danger.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Times_Varadkar's_shifting_view_on_abortion-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-Times_Varadkar's_shifting_view_on_abortion) During the national debate that occurred before the referendum on abortion in 2018, Varadkar said that there had been a "fundamental shift" in his views on abortion over the years, and repudiated his 2010 opposition to it.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Times_Varadkar's_shifting_view_on_abortion-1)[\[138\]](#refnote-Times_Varadkar's_shifting_view_on_abortion)[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-140)

### Immigration

In 2010, Varadkar was an advocate of a scheme in which immigrants to Ireland would be paid to return to their country of origin.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_May_2010-5)[\[117\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_May_2010) Varadkar suggested that at the time of the [Nice Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice_Treaty "Nice Treaty") referendums in Ireland in the early 2000s, the public were told there would not be large-scale immigration to Ireland in the aftermath, but this was not the case, before further suggesting that Ireland had not been suitably prepared for the amount of immigration it experienced during the [Celtic Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Tiger "Celtic Tiger") period.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_May_2010-6)[\[117\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_May_2010)

In a 2022 interview, Varadkar was critical of British politician [Priti Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priti_Patel "Priti Patel") in her role as [Home Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Secretary "Home Secretary"), stating that her plan to "send asylum seekers to Rwanda is disgusting".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_June_2022-1)[\[137\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_June_2022) In the same interview, Varadkar stated "I have always been supportive of migration" and "supportive of accepting refugees from war-torn countries", although he said he made the distinction between "people who come here legally and contribute to our society, and those who come here illegally and seek to gain status through subterfuge or falsehood".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hot_Press_June_2022-2)[\[137\]](#refnote-Hot_Press_June_2022) In June 2022, Varadkar began hosting a Ukrainian refugee in his home.[&#8202;](#ref-back-141-0)[\[140\]](#refnote-141)

In January 2023, Varadkar announced that his government would be looking at ways to strengthen border control against illegal immigration.[&#8202;](#ref-back-142-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-142)

## Personal life

Varadkar is the first Irish government leader of partly Indian origin and has visited India on a number of occasions. He completed his medical internship at [KEM Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_Memorial_Hospital_and_Seth_Gordhandas_Sunderdas_Medical_College "King Edward Memorial Hospital and Seth Gordhandas Sunderdas Medical College") in his father's childhood city of [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai").[&#8202;](#ref-back-143-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-143)

During an interview on [RTÉ Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Radio "RTÉ Radio") on 18 January 2015 (his 36th birthday), Varadkar spoke publicly for the first time about being gay: "it's not something that defines me. I'm not a half-Indian politician, or a doctor politician or a gay politician for that matter. It's just part of who I am, it doesn't define me, it is part of my character I suppose".[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-144) Varadkar was a prominent advocate of the [same-sex marriage referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-fourth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland").[&#8202;](#ref-back-145-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-145)[&#8202;](#ref-back-146-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-146) His partner, Matthew Barrett, is a doctor at [Mater Misericordiae University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mater_Misericordiae_University_Hospital "Mater Misericordiae University Hospital").[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-147)[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-148)

In 2017, Varadkar completed a course in professional Irish, and devised an [Irish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language "Irish language") form for his surname, _de Varad_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-149) He has said, "My philosophy towards Irish is just to speak it! Speak Irish! It's not about getting it perfect – it's about having fun and making an effort to speak it."[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-150)

Varadkar was raised in a Catholic household. He is [non-religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion "Irreligion"), but finds religion "fascinating".[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-151)

In September 2025, Varadkar published an autobiography, _Speaking My Mind_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-:1)
