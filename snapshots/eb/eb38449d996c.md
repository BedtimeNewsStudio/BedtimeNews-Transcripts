Top Questions

* Who is Hun Manet?

  Hun Manet, born on October 20, 1977, is a Cambodian politician who became prime minister in 2023, succeeding his father, Hun Sen, who had ruled for almost four decades. He is the eldest of five children born to Hun Sen and Bun Rany.

  Manet's early education culminated in becoming the first Cambodian to graduate from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1999. He furthered his studies in economics, earning degrees from New York University and the University of Bristol.

  Before becoming prime minister, Hun Manet had a long career in the Cambodian military. He joined the military in 1995 and ascended through the ranks, eventually becoming a four-star general in 2023, the highest military rank in Cambodia. In addition to his military career, Hun Manet gained political experience as a member of the standing committee of the ruling Cambodian People’s Party (CPP) from 2018.

**Hun Manet** (born October 20, 1977, Mémót district, Kmpóng Cham province (now in Tbong Khmŭm province), Cambodia) is a [Cambodian](https://www.britannica.com/place/Cambodia) politician who became prime minister in 2023. He succeeded his father, [Hun Sen](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Hun-Sen), who ruled for almost four decades, from 1985 to 2023.

## Early life and education

Hun Manet is the eldest son of five children born to Hun Sen and Bun Rany. Hun Sen later said in public speeches that Hun Manet is possibly a child of Neak Ta Anchanh Koh Thmar, a local powerful spirit, because, according to Hun Sen, several people witnessed a flashing light over his house when Hun Manet was born. Hun Manet grew up in [Phnom Penh](https://www.britannica.com/place/Phnom-Penh) while Cambodia was going through a [civil war](https://www.britannica.com/place/Cambodia/Civil-war#ref52488).

 

Hun Manet was the first Cambodian to graduate from the [United States Military Academy](https://www.britannica.com/topic/United-States-Military-Academy) in West Point, New York (B.A., 1999). He also studied [economics](https://www.britannica.com/money/economics) at [New York University](https://www.britannica.com/topic/New-York-University) (M.A., 2002) and the University of Bristol in the [United Kingdom](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom) (Ph.D., 2008). In 2006 Hun Manet married Pich Chanmony, the daughter of a Cambodian politician. Together they have two daughters and a son.

## Career

Before succeeding his father as prime minister, Hun Manet had a long career in Cambodia’s military. Having joined the Cambodian military in 1995, Hun Manet ascended rapidly through the ranks once he returned to the country from the United Kingdom. After serving as deputy commander of the army and deputy chairman of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces (RCAF) Joint Staff for a month, he was promoted to [major general](https://www.britannica.com/topic/general) in January 2011. Hun Manet was then named deputy commander in chief of the RCAF in 2019 and led the counterterrorism team. He was also named deputy chief of the prime minister’s bodyguard unit. Outside the military, Hun Manet accumulated political experience when he became a member of the standing committee of the ruling Cambodian People’s Party (CPP) in 2018 and was chosen to head the party’s youth wing in 2020. In 2021 Hun Sen named Hun Manet his successor, saying, “Even if he cannot be like his father, at least his capacity should match that of his father by 80 or 90 percent.” The CPP’s central committee later endorsed Hun Manet as the next prime minister. In 2023 Hun Manet was promoted to four-star general, the highest military rank in Cambodia.

## Hun Manet as prime minister

In July 2023 Hun Sen announced that he would resign as prime minister the next month and that Hun Manet would succeed him. Hun Sen also stated that he would remain as the head of the ruling party. The announcement came several days after the CPP declared victory in the 2023 parliamentary elections, in which the party faced no challenge because the only opposition party, the Candlelight Party, had been disqualified from running. In addition, through the imprisonment of several opposition politicians and the shutting down of one of the country’s major independent media outlets, dissent was nearly eliminated in the run-up to the elections.

 

Having been formally appointed prime minister by Cambodia’s king, Hun Manet was sworn in on August 22, 2023. Speaking to the National Assembly, he stated that it was “necessary to continue leading the country peacefully and securely and to maintain development and make reforms in all sectors.” International media remarked on the handover of the premiership from a father to his son as a significant generational transition, as about 75 percent of the country had only ever known Hun Sen as their prime minister. Whereas several experts have speculated whether Hun Manet would implement reforms or continue his father’s rule, Hun Manet has not provided a clear direction and instead stated that he would focus on improving the economy and social justice in Cambodia. In response to criticism about the legitimacy of the elections, Hun Manet said at a 2023 [United Nations General Assembly](https://www.britannica.com/topic/United-Nations-General-Assembly) session that they were “free and fair” and “credible and just.”

**Norodom Sihamoni** (born May 14, 1953, Phnom Penh, Cambodia) is the [king](https://www.britannica.com/topic/king-monarch) of [Cambodia](https://www.britannica.com/place/Cambodia) who succeeded his father, King [Norodom Sihanouk](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Norodom-Sihanouk), in October 2004 after Sihanouk [abdicated](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/abdicated) the [throne](https://www.britannica.com/topic/throne).

Sihamoni was the elder of Sihanouk’s two sons with his last queen, Monineath. At the time of Sihamoni’s birth, Cambodia was becoming independent of [France](https://www.britannica.com/place/France) (which until then had maintained a protectorate over Cambodia). However, the new [country](https://www.britannica.com/topic/nation-state) was in the middle of a protracted period of warfare and unrest in the region (the [Indochina wars](https://www.britannica.com/event/Indochina-wars)), and Sihamoni spent much of his life outside the country.

From an early age Sihamoni showed an aptitude for the arts. He went to study in Czechoslovakia when he was 9, and at age 14 he starred in *The Little Prince*, a film made by his father. After Sihanouk was [deposed](https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/deposed) from power in a 1970 coup, Sihamoni remained in [Prague](https://www.britannica.com/place/Prague) (now in the Czech Republic), where he attended the National Conservatory and the Academy of Music Arts, concentrating on dance, music, and theatre. In 1975 he joined his father in [North Korea](https://www.britannica.com/place/North-Korea) and began film school there. He followed his parents and returned to Cambodia during the totalitarian [Khmer Rouge](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Khmer-Rouge) regime (1975–79) of [Pol Pot](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Pol-Pot), and after 1976 he resided with his parents under [house arrest](https://www.britannica.com/topic/house-arrest) in the palace at [Phnom Penh](https://www.britannica.com/place/Phnom-Penh). Several of Sihamoni’s half-brothers and half-sisters, living elsewhere in the country, died during that period.

After the fall of Pol Pot, the family was evacuated to [China](https://www.britannica.com/place/China), where Sihamoni served as his father’s secretary for two years. In 1981 he moved to [Paris](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris), where he became a professor of classical dance and formed his own dance troupe, called Ballet Deva. He choreographed some of the troupe’s performances and also made two films with a dance focus. In 1992 he was named permanent representative of Cambodia to the [United Nations](https://www.britannica.com/topic/United-Nations). In 1993, the year his father was recrowned king, Sihamoni became Cambodia’s permanent representative to [UNESCO](https://www.britannica.com/topic/UNESCO), a position he held until 2004.

Sihamoni’s selection as king over one of his higher-profile siblings or uncles probably represented Sihanouk’s desire that someone neutral and politically untainted succeed him. Although Sihamoni reportedly was initially reluctant to become king, he accepted the role of monarch after a unanimous vote of approval by Cambodia’s nine-member Throne Council. Concerns that Sihamoni might find it hard to live up to the reputation of his [charismatic](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/charismatic) and politically savvy father were dispelled when, early in his reign, he showed himself to be a dignified king, humble toward his office and eager to reach out to the [Cambodian](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Khmer) population. In addition to formal trips abroad, Sihamoni made a series of visits to rural Cambodian villages and towns, where he met and talked with local residents. He also made known his desire to improve education and health care in Cambodia and to help revive the cultural life of the country.
