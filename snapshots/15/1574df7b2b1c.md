The **demographics of Israel**, monitored by the [Israel Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics "Israel Central Bureau of Statistics"), encompass various attributes that define the nation's populace. Since [its establishment in 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence "Israeli Declaration of Independence"), Israel has witnessed significant changes in its demographics. Formed as a [homeland for the Jewish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people "Homeland for the Jewish people"), Israel has attracted [Jewish immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah "Aliyah") from Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Americas.

The [Israel Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics "Israel Central Bureau of Statistics") defines the population of Israel as including Jews living in all of the [West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank "West Bank") and Palestinians in [East Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jerusalem "East Jerusalem") but excluding Palestinians anywhere in the rest of the West Bank, the Gaza Strip, and foreign workers anywhere in Israel. As of December 2023, this calculation stands at approximately 9,842,000 of whom:

- 73.2% (about 7,208,000 people) are [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Jews "Israeli Jews"), including about 503,000 living outside the self-defined borders of the State of Israel in the [West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank "West Bank")
- 21.1% (around 2,080,000 people) are Israeli citizens classified as [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_citizens_of_Israel "Arab citizens of Israel"), some identifying as [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_citizens_of_Israel "Palestinian citizens of Israel"), and including [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_in_Israel "Druze in Israel"), [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Israel "Circassians in Israel"), all other Muslims, [Christian Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Israel "Christianity in Israel"), [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Israel_and_Palestine "Armenians in Israel and Palestine") (which Israel considers "Arab")[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-cbs-pop-groups-2)
- An additional 5.7% (roughly 554,000 people) are classified as "others". This diverse group comprises those with Jewish ancestry but not recognized as Jewish by religious law, non-Jewish family members of Jewish immigrants, Christians other than Arabs and Armenians, and residents without a distinct ethnic or religious categorization.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-cbs-pop-groups-2)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-cbs-2023-12-28-1)

Israel's annual population growth rate stood at 2.0% in 2015, more than three times faster than the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") average of around 0.6%.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-OECD_Statistics-3) With an average of three children per woman, Israel also has the highest [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate "Fertility rate") in the OECD by a considerable margin and much higher than the OECD average of 1.7.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-4)

## Definitions

The definition of what constitutes the population of Israel varies depending on which territories are counted and which population groups are counted in each territory.

The [Israel Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics "Israel Central Bureau of Statistics") (CBS) definition of the **Area of the State of Israel**:[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-area-of-state-of-israel-definition-5)

- includes [East Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jerusalem "East Jerusalem") since 1967, which Israel unilaterally annexed
- includes the [Golan Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golan_Heights "Golan Heights") since 1982, which Israel unilaterally annexed
- excludes the West Bank other than East Jerusalem

The CBS' definition of the **Population of Israel**, however:[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-population-of-israel-definition-6)

- includes non-Israeli Palestinians (as well as Israeli Arabs/Palestinians) in East Jerusalem who have permission to live there
- includes [Israeli settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_settlers "Israeli settlers") and others with Israeli residency permits living in the [Area C of West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord "West Bank Areas in the Oslo II Accord")
- excludes Palestinian/Arab/other residents of Area C and East Jerusalem who do not have Israeli citizenship or residence
- excludes persons who are not registered (from 2008 on) and/or entered illegally, and foreign workers.

### Population growth rate

- 2.0% (2016)

During the 1990s, the Jewish [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth_rate "Population growth rate") was about 3% per year, as a result of massive immigration to Israel, primarily from the republics of the [former Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Soviet_Union "Former Soviet Union"). There is also a very high population growth rate among certain Jewish groups, especially adherents of [Orthodox Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism "Orthodox Judaism"). The growth rate of the [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_citizens_of_Israel "Arab citizens of Israel") population in Israel is 2.2%, while the growth rate of the Jewish population in Israel is 1.8%. The growth rate of the Arab population has slowed from 3.8% in 1999 to 2.2% in 2013, and for the Jewish population, the growth rate declined from 2.7% to its lowest rate of 1.4% in 2005. Due to a rise in fertility of the Jewish population since 1995 and immigration, the growth rate has since risen to 1.8%.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-28)

### Fertility

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/TFR_vs_PPP_2015.png/960px-TFR_vs_PPP_2015.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:TFR_vs_PPP_2015.png) Graph of [Total Fertility Rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Fertility_Rate "Total Fertility Rate") vs. [GDP (PPP) per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(PPP)_per_capita "List of countries by GDP (PPP) per capita") of each country, including Israel.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-30)

The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate") (TFR) of a population is the average number of children that an average woman would have, in her lifetime.

- 3.01 children born/woman (2019)

Jewish [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate") increased by 10.2% during 1998–2009, and was recorded at 2.90 during 2009. During the same time period, Arab TFR decreased by 20.5%. Muslim TFR was measured at 3.73 for 2009, and 2.9 for 2022.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-taub-31)

In 2000, the Jewish and Arab TFRs in Jerusalem were 3.79 and 4.43 respectively. By 2009, the Jewish TFR in Jerusalem was measured higher than the Arab TFR (2010: 4.26 vs 3.85, 2009: 4.16 vs 3.87). As of 2021, the Jewish and Arab TFRs in Jerusalem were 4.4 and 3.1 respectively.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-32) TFR for Arab residents in the West Bank was measured at 2.91 in 2013,[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-33) while that for the Jewish residents was reported at 5.10 children per woman.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-34)

The ethnic group with highest recorded TFR is the Bedouin of Negev. Their TFR was reported at 10.06 in 1998, and 5.73 in 2009. TFR is also very high among Haredi Jews. For [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazim "Ashkenazim") Haredim, the TFR rose from 6.91 in 1980 to 8.51 in 1996. The figure for 2008 is estimated to be even higher. TFR for [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardim "Sephardim")/ [Mizrahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrachim "Mizrachim") Haredim rose from 4.57 in 1980 to 6.57 in 1996.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-35) In 2020 the overall Jewish TFR in Israel (3.00) was for the first time measured higher than Arab Muslim TFR (2.99).

As of 2022, the fertility rates in Israeli cities dominated by specific demographic groups were: Haredi 6.1, Bedouin 4.4, Jewish non-Haredi 2.4, Arab 2.2, Druze 1.8.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-taub-31)

The Jewish median age in Jerusalem district and the West Bank are 24.9 and 19.7, respectively, and both account for 16% of the Jewish population, but 24% of 0- to 4-year-olds. The lowest median age in Israel, and one of the lowest in the world, is found in two of the West Bank's biggest Jewish cities: [Modi'in Illit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modi'in_Illit "Modi'in Illit") (11), [Beitar Illit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beitar_Illit "Beitar Illit") (11)[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-46) followed by Bedouin towns in the Negev (15.2).[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-47)

### Jews

Main articles: [Israeli Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Jews "Israeli Jews") and [Gerim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerim "Gerim")

According to [Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics "Israel Central Bureau of Statistics"), in 2008, of Israel's 7.3million people, 75.6 percent were Jews of any background.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-CBS_month_pop-54) Among them, 70.3 percent were [Sabras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabra_(person) "Sabra (person)") (born in Israel), mostly second- or third-generation Israelis, and the rest are [olim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah "Aliyah") (Jewish immigrants to Israel)—20.5 percent from Europe and the Americas, and 9.2 percent from Asia and Africa, including the [Arab countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world").[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-CBS_2008_jews_origin-55)

According to [Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics "Israel Central Bureau of Statistics"), in April 2023, of Israel's 9.7 million people, 73.5 percent, or 7.145 million, were Jews of any background.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-56)

There are no government statistics categorizing Israeli Jews as "Ashkenazi", "Mizrahi", etc, but studies and estimates have been conducted.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-57)[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-58) In a 2019 study, in a sample meant to be representative of the Israeli Jewish population, about 44.9% percent of Israel's Jewish population were categorized as [Mizrahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews "Mizrahi Jews") (defined as having grandparents born in Asia or North Africa), 31.8% were categorized as [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews "Ashkenazi Jews") (defined as having grandparents born in Europe, the Americas, Oceania and South Africa), 12.4% as " [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_post-Soviet_aliyah "1990s post-Soviet aliyah")" (defined as having progenitors who came from the ex-USSR in 1989 or later), about 3% as [Beta Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Israel "Beta Israel") (Ethiopia) and 7.9% as a mix of these, or other Jewish groups.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-59)

Recent _paternal_ ancestral background of Israeli Jews

### Arabs

Main article: [Arab citizens of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_citizens_of_Israel "Arab citizens of Israel")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Arabs_Israel_2018.png/500px-Arabs_Israel_2018.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Arabs_Israel_2018.png) Arabs in Israel, by natural region, 2018

Arab citizens of Israel are those Arab residents of Mandatory Palestine that remained within Israel's borders following the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War "1948 Arab–Israeli War") and the [establishment of the State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Establishment_of_the_State_of_Israel "Establishment of the State of Israel"). It is including those born within the state borders subsequent to this time, as well as those who had left during the establishment of the state (or their descendants), who have since re-entered by means accepted as lawful residence by the Israeli state (primarily family reunifications).

In 2019, the official number of Arab residents in Israel was 1,890,000 people, representing 21% of Israel's population.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-population_stat2019-61) This figure includes 209,000 Arabs (14% of the Israeli Arab population) in East Jerusalem, also counted in the Palestinian statistics, although 98 percent of East Jerusalem Palestinians have either Israeli residency or Israeli citizenship.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-62)

**Arab Muslims**

Most Arab citizens of Israel are Muslim, particularly of the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam") branch of Islam. A small minority are [Ahmadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya_in_Israel "Ahmadiyya in Israel") sect and there are also some [Alawites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawites "Alawites") (affiliated with Shia Islam) in the northernmost village of [Ghajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghajar "Ghajar") with Israeli citizenship. As of 2019, Arab citizens of Israel composed 21 percent of the country's total population.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-population_stat2019-61) About 82 percent of the Arab population in Israel are Sunni Muslims, a very small minority are Shia Muslims, another 9 percent are [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_in_Israel "Druze in Israel"), and around 9 percent are Christian (mostly [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox "Eastern Orthodox") and Catholic denominations).

**Bedouin**

Main article: [Israeli Bedouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Bedouin "Israeli Bedouin")

The Arab Muslim citizens of Israel include also the Bedouins, who are divided into two main groups: the Bedouin in the north of Israel, who live in villages and towns for the most part, and the Bedouin in the [Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev "Negev"), who include half-nomadic and inhabitants of towns and [Unrecognized villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrecognized_Bedouin_villages_in_Israel "Unrecognized Bedouin villages in Israel"). According to the [Israeli Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_(Israel) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Israel)"), as of 1999, 110,000 [Bedouins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedouin "Bedouin") live in the Negev, 50,000 in the Galilee and 10,000 in the central region of Israel.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-Bedouin_Demographics-63) The vast majority of Arab Bedouins of Israel practice Sunni Islam.

**Ahmadiyya**

The [Ahmadiyya community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya_in_Israel "Ahmadiyya in Israel") was first established in the region in the 1920s, in what was then [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine"). There is a large community in [Kababir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kababir "Kababir"), a neighborhood on [Mount Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel "Mount Carmel") in [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa "Haifa").[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-64)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-65) It is unknown how many Israeli Ahmadis there are, although it is estimated there are about 2,200 Ahmadis in Kababir alone.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-israelandyou-66)

**Arab Christians**

As of December 2013, about 161,000 Israeli citizens practiced Christianity, together comprising about 2% of the total population.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-MFA2014-67) The largest group consists of [Melkites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite "Melkite") (about 60% of Israel's Christians), followed by the Greek Orthodox (about 30%), with the remaining ca. 10% spread between the Roman Catholic (Latin), Maronite, Anglican, Lutheran, Armenian, Syriac, Ethiopian, Coptic and other denominations.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-MFA2014-67)

**Druze**

Main article: [Druze in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_in_Israel "Druze in Israel")

The Arab citizens of Israel include also the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze"), who numbered at an estimated 143,000 in April 2019.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-68) All of the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze") living in what was then [British Mandate Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine") became Israeli citizens after the declaration of the State of Israel. Druze serve prominently in the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces"), and are represented in mainstream Israeli politics and business as well, unlike Muslim or Christian Arabs who are not required to and generally choose not to serve in the Israeli army. Though a few individuals identify themselves as "Palestinian Druze",[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-69) the vast majority of Druze do not consider themselves to be 'Palestinian', and consider their Israeli identity stronger than their [Arab identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_identity "Arab identity"). A 2017 [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") poll reported that the majority of the Israeli Druze identified as ethnically [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab "Arab").[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-70)

### Syriac Christians

**Arameans**

Main articles: [Arameans in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arameans_in_Israel "Arameans in Israel") and [Maronites in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites_in_Israel "Maronites in Israel")

In 2014, Israel decided to recognize the Aramaic community within its borders as a national minority, allowing some of the [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Israel "Christianity in Israel") in Israel to be registered as "Aramean" instead of "Arab".[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-71) As of October 2014, some 600 Israelis requested to be registered as Arameans, with several thousand eligible for the status – mostly members of the Maronite community with some Assyrians as well.

The [Maronite Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites "Maronites") community in Israel of around 7,000 resides mostly in the Galilee, with a presence in Haifa, Nazareth and Jerusalem. It is largely composed of families that lived in [Upper Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Galilee "Upper Galilee") in villages such as [Jish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jish "Jish") long before the establishment of Israel in 1948. In the year 2000, the community was joined by a group of [Lebanese SLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_Army "South Lebanon Army") militia members and their families, who fled Lebanon after [2000 withdrawal of IDF from South Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation_of_Southern_Lebanon_(1982%E2%80%932000)#2000_Israeli_withdrawal "Israeli occupation of Southern Lebanon (1982–2000)").

**Assyrians**

Main article: [Assyrians in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Israel "Assyrians in Israel")

There are around 1,000 [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people "Assyrian people") living in Israel, mostly in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem") and [Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth "Nazareth"). Assyrians are an [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic "Aramaic") speaking, [Eastern Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity "Eastern Christianity") Christian minority who are descended from the ancient [Mesopotamians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamians "Mesopotamians"). The old [Syriac Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox "Syriac Orthodox") monastery of Saint Mark lies in Jerusalem. Other than followers of the Syriac Orthodox Church, there are also followers of the [Assyrian Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East "Assyrian Church of the East") and the [Chaldean Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaldean_Catholic_Church "Chaldean Catholic Church") living in Israel.

### Other citizens

**Copts**

Some 1,000 Israeli citizens belong to the [Coptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copts "Copts") community, originating in Egypt.

**Samaritans**

Main article: [Samaritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans "Samaritans")

The [Samaritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans "Samaritans") are an ethnoreligious group of the Levant. Ancestrally, they claim descent from a group of Israelite inhabitants who have connections to ancient Samaria from the beginning of the Babylonian Exile up to the beginning of the Common Era. 2007 population estimates show that 712 Samaritans live half in [Holon, Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holon,_Israel "Holon, Israel") and half at [Mount Gerizim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gerizim "Mount Gerizim") in the West Bank. The Holon community holds Israeli citizenship, while the Gerizim community resides at an Israeli-controlled enclave, holding dual Israeli-Palestinian citizenship.

**Armenians**

Main article: [Armenians in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Israel "Armenians in Israel")

About 4,000 [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians "Armenians") reside in Israel mostly in Jerusalem (including in the [Armenian Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Quarter "Armenian Quarter")), but also in Tel Aviv, Haifa and Jaffa. Armenians have a Patriarchate in Jerusalem and churches in Jerusalem, Haifa and Jaffa. Although Armenians of [Old Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Jerusalem "Old Jerusalem") have Israeli identity cards, they are officially holders of Jordanian passports.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-72)

**Circassians**

Main article: [Circassians in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Israel "Circassians in Israel")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Circassians_in_Israel.Jpg/500px-Circassians_in_Israel.Jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Circassians_in_Israel.Jpg) Circassians in [Kfar Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Kama "Kfar Kama")

In Israel, there are also a few thousand [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians "Circassians"), living mostly in [Kfar Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Kama "Kfar Kama") (2,000) and [Reyhaniye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehaniya "Rehaniya") (1,000).[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-73) These two villages were a part of a greater group of Circassian villages around the [Golan Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golan_Heights "Golan Heights"). The Circassians in Israel enjoy, like [Druzes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze "Druze"), a _status aparte_. Male Circassians (at their leader's request) are mandated for military service, while females are not.

**People from post-Soviet states**

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Russophone_shop_in_Haifa.jpg/500px-Russophone_shop_in_Haifa.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Russophone_shop_in_Haifa.jpg) [Russophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russophone "Russophone") shop in [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa "Haifa")

Ethnic [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians "Russians"), [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians "Ukrainians"), and [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians "Belarusians"), immigrants from the [former Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states "Post-Soviet states"), who were eligible to emigrate due to having, or being married to somebody who has, at least one Jewish grandparent and thus qualified for Israeli citizenship under the revised [Law of Return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Return "Law of Return"). A number of these immigrants also belong to various ethnic groups from the Former Soviet Union such as [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians "Armenians"), [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians "Georgians"), [Azeris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeris "Azeris"), [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks "Uzbeks"), [Moldovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovans "Moldovans"), [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars "Tatars"), among others. Some of them, having a Jewish father or grandfather, identify as Jews, but being non-Jewish by Orthodox [Halakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halakha "Halakha") (religious law), they are not recognized formally as Jews by the state. Most of them are in the mainstream of Israel culture and are called "expanded Jewish population". In addition, a certain number of former Soviet citizens, primarily women of Russian and Ukrainian ethnicity, emigrated to Israel, after marrying Muslim or Christian Arab citizens of Israel, who went to study in the former Soviet Union in the 1970s and 1980s. 1,557,698 people from the current Russia and Ukraine live in Israel.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-74)

**Finns**

Although most people of Finnish origin in Israel are [Finnish Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Jews "Finnish Jews") who immigrated to Israel, and their descendants, a small number of Finnish Christians moved to Israel in the 1940s before independence and gained citizenship following independence. For the most part, many of the original Finnish settlers intermarried with the other communities in the country, and therefore remain very small in number. A [Moshav shitufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshav_shitufi "Moshav shitufi") near Jerusalem named [Yad HaShmona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yad_HaShmona "Yad HaShmona"), meaning the "Memorial for the Eight", was established in 1971 by a group of Finnish Christian-Israelis, although today, most members are Israeli, and are predominantly Hebrew speakers, and the moshav has become a center of [Messianic Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messianic_Jews "Messianic Jews").[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-75)[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-76)

**Baháʼís**

The population of followers of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith "Baháʼí Faith") in Israel is almost entirely made up of volunteers serving at the [Baháʼí World Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_World_Centre "Baháʼí World Centre"). [Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1'u'll%C3%A1h "Bahá'u'lláh") (1817–1892), the Faith's founder, was banished to [Akka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel "Acre, Israel") and died nearby where his shrine is located. During his lifetime he instructed his followers not to teach or convert those living in the area, and the Baháʼís descending from those original immigrants were later asked to leave and teach elsewhere. For nearly a century there has been a policy by Baháʼí leaders to not accept converts living in Israel. The 650 or so foreign national Baháʼís living in Israel are almost all on temporary duty serving at the shrines and administrative offices.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-77)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-78)[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-79)

**Vietnamese**

The number of [Vietnamese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Vietnamese "Overseas Vietnamese") in Israel and their descendants is estimated at 150 to 200.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-80) Most of them came to Israel in between 1976 and 1979, after prime minister [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin "Menachem Begin") authorized their admission to Israel and granted them political asylum. The Vietnamese people living in Israel are Israeli citizens who also serve in the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces"). Today, the majority of the community lives in the [Gush Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Dan "Gush Dan") area in the center of Tel Aviv, but also a few dozen Vietnamese-Israelis or Israelis of Vietnamese origin live in [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa "Haifa"), [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem"), and [Ofakim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofakim "Ofakim").

**African Hebrew Israelites of Jerusalem**

Main article: [African Hebrew Israelites of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Hebrew_Israelites_of_Jerusalem "African Hebrew Israelites of Jerusalem")

The African Hebrew Israelite Nation of Jerusalem is a religious sect[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-jpost.com-81) of Black [Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans"), founded in 1960 by Ben Carter[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-religion.info-82)[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-83) a metal worker in Chicago. The members of this sect believe they are descended from the tribes of Judah driven from the Holy Land by the Romans during the First Jewish War (70 AD), and who reportedly emigrated to West Africa before being taken as slaves to the United States.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-religion.info-82)[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-84) With a population of over 5,000, most members live in their own community in [Dimona, Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimona,_Israel "Dimona, Israel"), with additional families in [Arad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arad,_Israel "Arad, Israel"), [Mitzpe Ramon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitzpe_Ramon "Mitzpe Ramon"), and the [Tiberias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias "Tiberias") area. The group believes that the ancient Israelites are the ancestors of Black Americans and that the actual Jews are "impostors".[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-85) Some scholarship does consider them to be of subsaharan African origin, rather than Levantine.[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-86) Their ancestors were Black [Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") who, after being expelled from [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia "Liberia"), illegally immigrated to Israel in the late 1960s using tourist visas, requesting that Israel provide them legal citizenship status. Israel granted their requests.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-87) The African Hebrew Israelites, like the [Haredim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredim "Haredim") and most [Israeli Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Arabs "Israeli Arabs"), are not required to serve in the military; however, some do.

**Naturalized foreign workers**

Some naturalized [foreign workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_worker "Foreign worker") and their children born in Israel, predominantly from the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria"), [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal "Senegal"), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania "Romania"), China, [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), and South America (mainly Colombia).

### Non-citizens

**African migrants**

Further information: [Sudanese refugees in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_refugees_in_Israel "Sudanese refugees in Israel") and [African immigration to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_immigration_to_Israel "African immigration to Israel")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/Meeting_between_Sudanese_refugees_and_Israeli_students.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Meeting_between_Sudanese_refugees_and_Israeli_students.jpg) Meeting between Sudanese refugees and Israeli students, 2007.

The number and status of African migrants in Israel is disputed and controversial, but it is estimated that at least 70,000 refugees mainly from [Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea "Eritrea"), [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan"), [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan"), [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia"), and the [Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Coast "Ivory Coast") reside and work in Israel. A count in late 2011 published in Ynet pointed out the number only in Tel Aviv is 40,000, which represents 10 percent of the city's population. The vast majority live in the southern parts of the city. There is a significant population in the southern Israeli cities of Eilat, Arad, and Beersheba.

**Foreign workers**

Further information: [Filipinos in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_in_Israel "Filipinos in Israel"), [Chinese people in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_Israel "Chinese people in Israel"), and [Palestinian workers in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_workers_in_Israel "Palestinian workers in Israel")

There are around 300,000 foreign workers, residing in Israel under temporary work visas, including Palestinians. Most of those foreign workers engage in agriculture and construction. The main groups of those foreign workers include the Chinese, [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_people "Thai people"), [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos "Filipinos"),[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-88) [Nigerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerians "Nigerians"), [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians "Romanians"), and [Latin Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Americans "Latin Americans").

**Other refugees**

Approximately 100–200 refugees from [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia "Bosnia"), [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo "Kosovo"), [Iraqi Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Kurdistan "Iraqi Kurdistan"), and [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") were absorbed in Israel as refugees. Most of them were also given Israeli resident status, and currently reside in Israel.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-89) As of 2006, some 200 ethnic [Kurdish refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_refugees "Kurdish refugees") from Turkey resided in Israel as illegal immigrants, fleeing the [Kurdish–Turkish conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish%E2%80%93Turkish_conflict_(1978%E2%80%93present) "Kurdish–Turkish conflict (1978–present)").[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-90)

## Languages

Main article: [Languages of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Israel "Languages of Israel")

According to a 2011 survey of Israelis over 20 years of age conducted by the [Israel Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics "Israel Central Bureau of Statistics"), 49% report Hebrew as their native language, Arabic 18%, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language") 15%, [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish "Yiddish") 2%, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language") 2%, English 2%, [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language "Spanish language") 1.6%, and 10% other languages (including [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language "Romanian language"), and [Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic "Amharic"), which were not offered as answers by the survey). ## Education

Main article: [Education in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Israel "Education in Israel")

Education between ages 5 and 15 is compulsory. It is not free, but it is subsidized by the government, individual organizations (such as the Beit Yaakov System), or a combination. Parents are expected to participate in courses as well. The school system is organized into kindergartens, 6-year primary schools, and either 6-year secondary schools or 3-year junior secondary schools + 3-year senior secondary schools (depending on region), after which a comprehensive examination is offered for university admissions.

### Citizenship and Entry Law

Main article: [Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_and_Entry_into_Israel_Law "Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law")

The [Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_and_Entry_into_Israel_Law "Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law") (Temporary Order) 5763 was first passed on 31 July 2003, and has since been extended until 31 July 2008. The law places age restrictions for the automatic granting of Israeli citizenship and residency permits to spouses of Israeli citizens, such that spouses who are inhabitants of the West Bank and Gaza Strip are ineligible. On 8 May 2005, the Israeli ministerial committee for issues of legislation once again amended the Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law, to restrict citizenship and residence in Israel only to Palestinian men over the age of 35, and Palestinian women over the age of 25. Those in favor of the law say the law not only limits the possibility of the entrance of terrorists into Israel, but, as [Ze'ev Boim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ze'ev_Boim "Ze'ev Boim") asserts, allows Israel "to maintain the state's democratic nature, but also its Jewish nature" (i. e., its Jewish demographic majority).[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-scotsman1-103) Critics, including the United Nations Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination,[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-104) say the law disproportionately affects [Arab citizens of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_citizens_of_Israel "Arab citizens of Israel"), since Arabs in Israel are far more likely to have spouses from the West Bank and Gaza Strip than other Israeli citizens.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-sfgate1-105)

In the constitutional challenges to the Citizenship and Entry to Israel Law, the state, represented by the Attorney General, insisted that security was the only objective behind the law. The state also added that even if the law was intended to achieve demographic objectives, it is still in conformity with Israel's [Jewish and democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_and_democratic_state "Jewish and democratic state") definition, and thus constitutional. In a 2012 ruling by the Supreme Court on the issue, some of the judges on the panel discussed demography, and were inclined to accept that demography is a legitimate consideration in devising family reunification policies that violate the right to family life.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-106)

#### Immigration from the USSR

Further information: [1970s Soviet Union aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_Soviet_Union_aliyah "1970s Soviet Union aliyah") and [1990s post-Soviet aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_post-Soviet_aliyah "1990s post-Soviet aliyah")

During the 1970s about 163,000 people of Jewish descent immigrated to Israel [from the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_from_the_Soviet_Union_in_the_1970s "Aliyah from the Soviet Union in the 1970s").

Later [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon "Ariel Sharon"), in his capacity as Minister of Housing & Construction and member of the Ministerial Committee for Immigration & Absorption, launched an unprecedented large-scale construction effort to accommodate the new Russian population in Israel so as to facilitate their smooth integration and encourage further Jewish immigration as an ongoing means of increasing the Jewish population of Israel.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-115) Between 1989 and 2006, about 979,000 Jews emigrated from the former Soviet Union to Israel.

### Emigration

Main article: [Yerida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerida "Yerida")

For many years definitive data on Israeli emigration was unavailable.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-116) In _The Israeli Diaspora_ sociologist Stephen J. Gold maintains that calculation of Jewish emigration has been a contentious issue, explaining, "Since Zionism, the philosophy that underlies the existence of the Jewish state, calls for return home of the world's Jews, the opposite movement—Israelis leaving the Jewish state to reside elsewhere—clearly presents an ideological and demographic problem."[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-117)

In the past several decades, emigration ( [yerida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerida "Yerida")) has seen a considerable increase. From 1990 to 2005, 230,000 Israelis left the country; a large proportion of these departures included people who initially immigrated to Israel and then reversed their course (48% of all post-1990 departures and even 60% of 2003 and 2004 departures were former immigrants to Israel). 8% of Jewish immigrants in the post-1990 period left Israel, while 15% of non-Jewish immigrants did. In 2005 alone, 21,500 Israelis left the country and had not yet returned at the end of 2006; among them 73% were Jews, 5% Arabs, and 22% "Others" (mostly non-Jewish immigrants, with Jewish ancestry, from USSR). At the same time, 10,500 Israelis came back to Israel after over one year abroad; 84% were Jews, 9% Others, and 7% Arabs.[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-118)

According to the Israel Central Bureau of Statistics, as of 2005, 650,000 Israelis had left the country for over one year and not returned. Of them, 530,000 are still alive today. This number does not include the children born overseas. It should also be noted that Israeli law grants citizenship only to the first generation of children born to Israeli emigrants.

## Future projections

In June 2013, the [Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics "Israeli Central Bureau of Statistics") released a demographic report, [projecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_population_projections "Human population projections") that Israel's population would grow to 11.4million by 2035, with the Jewish population numbering 8.3million, or 73% of the population, and the Arab population at 2.6million, or 23%. This includes some 2.3million Muslims (20% of the population), 185,000 Druze, and 152,000 Christians. The report predicts that the Israeli population growth rate will decline to 1.4% annually, with growth in the Muslim population remaining higher than the Jewish population until 2035, at which point the Jewish population will begin growing the fastest.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-120)

In 2017, the Central Bureau of Statistics projected that Israel's population would rise to about 18 million by 2059, including 14.4million Jews and 3.6million Arabs. Of the Jewish population, about 5.25million would be [Haredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_Judaism "Haredi Judaism"). Overall, the forecast projected that 49% of the population would be either Haredi Jews (29%) or Arabs (20%).[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-121) It also projected a population of 20 million in 2065.[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-122) Jews and other non-Arabs are expected to compose 81% of the population in 2065, and Arabs 19%. About 32% of the population is expected to be Haredi.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-123)

Other forecasts project that Israel could have a population as high as 23 million, or even 36million, by 2050.[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Israel#cite_note-124)
