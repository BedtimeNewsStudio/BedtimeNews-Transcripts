April 2019 Israeli legislative election

Early legislative elections were held in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel") on 9 April 2019 to elect the 120 members of the 21st [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset"). Elections had been due in November 2019, but were brought forward following a dispute between members of the [current government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_government_of_Israel "34th government of Israel") over a bill on [Haredi conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_conscription_in_Israel "Haredi conscription in Israel"), as well as impending [corruption charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_of_Benjamin_Netanyahu "Trial of Benjamin Netanyahu") against incumbent [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Israel "Prime Minister of Israel") [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu").

Netanyahu's [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud "Likud") tied with [Blue and White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_and_White_(political_alliance) "Blue and White (political alliance)") alliance of [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz "Benny Gantz"), both winning 35 seats. The balance of power was held by smaller parties, with a majority being right-wing and religious parties that had previously sat in coalition with Likud, which would have allowed Netanyahu to form the next government.

Due to fractures over [Haredi conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_conscription_in_Israel "Haredi conscription in Israel") within the Netanyahu coalition, [efforts at government formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fifth_government_of_Israel#First_election_%E2%80%93_April_2019 "Thirty-fifth government of Israel") ultimately failed, leading to a [snap election in September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2019_Israeli_legislative_election "September 2019 Israeli legislative election") five months later.

## Background

Further information: [Thirty-fourth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_government_of_Israel "Thirty-fourth government of Israel") and [List of members of the twentieth Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_twentieth_Knesset "List of members of the twentieth Knesset")

Defense Minister [Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman "Avigdor Lieberman") had opposed a [draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription") law (supported by the [Haredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_Judaism "Haredi Judaism") parties) which would allow full-time Torah students exemptions from serving in the [IDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces").[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz "Meretz") and [Yesh Atid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yesh_Atid "Yesh Atid") submitted a proposal on 12 March 2018 seeking the dissolution of the [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset").[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) Early elections were averted at that point in time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)

Lieberman would eventually leave the government over the [cease-fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza%E2%80%93Israel_clashes_(November_2018) "Gaza–Israel clashes (November 2018)") with [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas") in [Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip "Gaza Strip").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) This leaves the governing coalition with 61 seats (out of 120 in total).[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6) [The Jewish Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_Home "The Jewish Home") announced on 16 November 2018 that it would leave the government, as [Naftali Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naftali_Bennett "Naftali Bennett") (the head of the party) was not given Lieberman's former Defense Ministry post.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) Reports were that Netanyahu would not be giving the post to Bennett and was to meet with other coalition leaders on 18 November to determine a date for early election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) However, after further discussion, Bennett decided to stay on as education minister, narrowly avoiding the collapse of the Netanyahu government again.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) However, continued dysfunction over various issues, including military service for the Haredim, caused parliament to dissolve and early elections to be called for 9 April 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-INN1-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-INN1) Had early elections not been called, the regularly scheduled elections would have taken place seven months later, on 5 November 2019.

## Electoral system

Further information: [Elections in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Israel "Elections in Israel")

The 120 seats in the [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset") are elected by [closed list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_list "Closed list") [proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_representation "Proportional representation") in a single nationwide constituency. The [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold "Electoral threshold") for the election is 3.25%. In most cases, this implies a minimum party size of four seats, but on some occasions, a party can end up with three.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

While election day was on 9 April 2019, polls opened in embassies around the world on 28 March.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

### Surplus-vote agreements

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/20190409_091600_Polling_station_in_Israel_%28cropped%29.jpg/500px-20190409_091600_Polling_station_in_Israel_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:20190409_091600_Polling_station_in_Israel_(cropped).jpg) Voting in the election day in a polling station in HaBiluyim Primary School in [Ramat Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Gan "Ramat Gan").

Further information: [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method "D'Hondt method")

Two party lists can sign a [surplus-vote agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparentment#Israel "Apparentment") that allows them to compete for leftover seats as though they are running together on the same list. The [Bader–Ofer method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagenbach-Bischoff_system "Hagenbach-Bischoff system") disproportionately favors larger lists, meaning that such an alliance is more likely to receive leftover seats than both of its comprising lists would be individually. If the alliance receives leftover seats, the Bader–Ofer calculation is then applied privately, to determine how the seats are divided among the two allied lists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13) The following agreements were signed by parties prior to the election:

- [Union of Right-Wing Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Right-Wing_Parties "Union of Right-Wing Parties") and [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud "Likud")[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)
- [New Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Right_(Israel) "New Right (Israel)") and [Yisrael Beiteinu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Beiteinu "Yisrael Beiteinu")[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)
- [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz "Meretz") and [Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Labor_Party "Israeli Labor Party")[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)
- [United Torah Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Torah_Judaism "United Torah Judaism") and [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas "Shas")[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)
- [Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash "Hadash")– [Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'al "Ta'al") and [Balad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balad_(political_party) "Balad (political party)")- [Ra'am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_List "United Arab List")[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

## Campaign

For a more comprehensive list, see [Party lists for the April 2019 Israeli legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_lists_for_the_April_2019_Israeli_legislative_election "Party lists for the April 2019 Israeli legislative election").

Some parties, like Likud, Labor, the Jewish Home, Zehut and Meretz, have systems in which the leadership and most candidates on their lists are elected in primary elections.

### Blue and White

[Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz "Benny Gantz")'s [Israel Resilience Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_and_White_(political_party) "Blue and White (political party)") and [Moshe Ya'alon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Ya'alon "Moshe Ya'alon")'s [Telem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telem_(2019_political_party) "Telem (2019 political party)") unveiled its party slate on 19 February 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-inn19feb-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-inn19feb) [Yair Lapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Lapid "Yair Lapid")'s Yesh Atid party unveiled its party slate on 18 February 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-48) On 21 February 2019, the three parties agreed to run on a united list named [Blue and White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_and_White_(political_alliance) "Blue and White (political alliance)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-49)

### Union of Right-Wing Parties

[The Jewish Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jewish_Home "The Jewish Home") held its leadership primaries on 27 April 2017; [Naftali Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naftali_Bennett "Naftali Bennett") won with 80.3% of the vote, Yonatan Branski received 12.2% and Yitzhak Zagha received 7.47%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-toi1-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-toi1) In the aftermath of the formation of the [New Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Right_(Israel) "New Right (Israel)") and Bennett's leaving, the Jewish Home cancelled its primaries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-51) [Rafi Peretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafi_Peretz "Rafi Peretz") was elected leader of the Jewish Home on 4 February.[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-52)

The [Tkuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tkuma_(political_party) "Tkuma (political party)") party held its leadership primaries on 14 January 2019; [Bezalel Smotrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezalel_Smotrich "Bezalel Smotrich") defeated [Uri Ariel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Ariel "Uri Ariel").[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-53)

On 14 February 2019, Jewish Home agreed to run on a joint list with the [Tkuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tkuma_(political_party) "Tkuma (political party)") party. Jewish Home leader Rafi Peretz headed the joint list, with Tkuma chair Bezalel Smotrich as the number two.[&#8202;](#ref-back-as14feb-1)[\[29\]](#refnote-as14feb) On 20 February 2019, they agreed to include [Otzma Yehudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzma_Yehudit "Otzma Yehudit") in their list, titled the [Union of Right-Wing Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Right-Wing_Parties "Union of Right-Wing Parties").[&#8202;](#ref-back-toi20feboy-1)[\[30\]](#refnote-toi20feboy)[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-54) The inclusion of Otzma Yehudit prompted strong criticism.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-55)[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-56)[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-57)

### Labor

See also: [2019 Israeli Labor Party primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Israeli_Labor_Party_primary "2019 Israeli Labor Party primary") and [2019 Israeli Labor Party leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "2019 Israeli Labor Party leadership election")

The Labor Party held its leadership primaries [on 10 July 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "2017 Israeli Labor Party leadership election"); [Avi Gabbay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avi_Gabbay "Avi Gabbay") defeated [Amir Peretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Peretz "Amir Peretz") in the run-off, with [Isaac Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Herzog "Isaac Herzog") being defeated during the first round of voting.[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-58) The party held primaries on 11 February 2019 to choose members for its slate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-59)

### Likud

The Likud leadership primary election was originally scheduled for 23 February 2016 following Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu")'s proposal,[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-60) and later cancelled by a party court on the basis that the Likud constitution did not require a vote when there was only one candidate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-61)[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-62) Likud held the primary for the rest of its list on 5 February 2019, which resulted in several of Netanyahu's rivals winning senior spots.[&#8202;](#ref-back-toi-likud-primaries-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-toi-likud-primaries)[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-64) Voting irregularities surfaced in the primary results. In some cases, specific candidates received more votes in some locales than the total number of ballots cast in those locales. The Likud party investigated the matter.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-65) In the final results, Knesset speaker [Yuli Edelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuli_Edelstein "Yuli Edelstein") came in first place, followed by [Yisrael Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Katz_(politician,_born_1955) "Yisrael Katz (politician, born 1955)"), [Gilad Erdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilad_Erdan "Gilad Erdan"), [Gideon Sa'ar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon_Sa'ar "Gideon Sa'ar") and [Miri Regev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miri_Regev "Miri Regev").[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-66)

On 26 February 2019, the "Likud [TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV "TV")" [studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio "Studio") released the [graves video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud_video_of_graves "Likud video of graves"), which raised an ire over use of military cemetery.

On 28 February 2019, the Attorney General, [Avichai Mandelblit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avichai_Mandelblit "Avichai Mandelblit"), announced his intent to indict Prime Minister Netanyahu on three charges which include bribery, fraud and breach of trust. These include trading legislation for favorable press coverage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-67)

### Meretz

See also: [2018 Meretz leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Meretz_leadership_election "2018 Meretz leadership election")

Meretz held its leadership primaries on 22 March 2018; [Tamar Zandberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamar_Zandberg "Tamar Zandberg") won with 71% of the vote, Avi Buskila received 29%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-68) Meretz held its primary on 14 February 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-as14febm-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-as14febm)

### Yisrael Beiteinu

[Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman "Avigdor Lieberman")'s [Yisrael Beiteinu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Beiteinu "Yisrael Beiteinu") released its party slate on 19 February 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-toi19feb-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-toi19feb)

### Zehut

The Zehut party held Israel's first open primaries on 29 January 2019, in which all Israeli voters (including those living abroad) were able to vote via a secure online website. About 12,000 people voted in these primaries, which determined the order of the candidates who won in the party's internal primaries in September 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-71)[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-72)

## Allegations of misconduct

## Aftermath

Main article: [2018–2022 Israeli political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932022_Israeli_political_crisis "2018–2022 Israeli political crisis")

Leader of Blue and White faction Benny Gantz conceded, paving the way for incumbent Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to start talks with other parties to form a governing coalition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-87) On 15 and 16 April, leaders of all the parties who won seats in the Knesset met with President [Reuven Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rivlin "Reuven Rivlin") to recommend a designated person to form a government. Netanyahu received recommendations from leaders representing 65 seats in the Knesset, whereas Gantz received recommendations from leaders representing only 45 seats in the Knesset. Leaders of the two Arab parties, representing 10 seats in the Knesset, declined to make any recommendation. Based on the recommendations he received, Rivlin designated Netanyahu to form the next governing coalition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-88) After a month of negotiations, Netanyahu's failure to form a government led to a 74 to 45 vote in the Knesset in favour of dispersing just after midnight on 29 May 2019. [The new election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2019_Israeli_legislative_election "September 2019 Israeli legislative election") was scheduled for 17 September 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-89)
