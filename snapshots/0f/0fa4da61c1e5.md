First Minister,

You met with the Senior Vice President of the Trump Organisation, Eric Trump (ET), 
on Thursday 13 March. Also present at the meeting were Sarah Malone (SM) 
- Executive Vice President, Trump International, Scotland – Colin McAllister, John 
Webster and Emily Mackintosh.

The meeting, which had been requested by the Sarah Malone, coincided with a visit 
to Scotland during which Mr Trump would visit Trump org investments in Scotland –
including golf properties at Turnberry and the soon to launch second course at 
Trump Aberdeen Links in Balmedie.

You began by welcoming ET to Scotland, recognising the significant ongoing 
investment by Trump Org in Scotland’s economy – particularly our tourism and 
leisure sectors – and welcoming the opportunity to discuss future investment plans. 
You stressed the important role that inward investment played in transforming the 
Scottish economy, and your commitment to an open dialogue with all major 
investors.

You noted with concern the recent incident at the Trump Turnberry property, 
condemning the damage caused by vandalism and offered reassurance that the 
relevant authorities would continue to take the incident seriously.

ET restated his family’s commitment to and passion for Scotland. He stressed that 
this commitment was long term. He narrated the journey he had been on since 
purchasing the Turnberry property and redeveloping it, and set out plans for the 
launch of the second course at Trump Aberdeen links later this year. SM welcomed 
the opportunity to an open dialogue with the international investment community and 
set out some of the challenges she had faced at a local level.

at Turnberry in 2009.

also wish to host other R&A major championships, although The Open will be the

**GOLF EVENTS AND TOURISM**

## Background of importance of golf and golf events to Scotland

• Scotland is recognised internationally as the ‘Home of Golf’.

• We have more than 550 courses across the country, from championship courses, 
many of which host tournaments, to hidden gems.

• Scotland’s golf tournaments showcase Scotland as the perfect stage for major 
events and show our first-class courses to a global audience.

• Scotland has an impressive recent record in the delivery of golf events, including 
the Ryder Cup in 2014 and Solheim Cup in 2019 (both at Gleneagles) and hosting 
The Open approximately every two years.

• 2024 was an exceptional year for golf events in Scotland, as we successfully 
delivered all five of the high-profile tournaments that could be held here, 
comprising:

o The three major championships: The Open, The AIG Women’s Open; and the 
Senior Open;

o Our two annual national Opens: Genesis Scottish Open and the Women’s 
Scottish Open.

• All five of the events were supported by Scottish Government funding.

## Benefits of golf events to Scotland

• The Open brings direct economic benefit of at least £60 million each time it is held 
in Scotland, and usually significantly more.

• Independent research from Sheffield Hallam University’s Sport Industry Research 
Centre has found that The 152nd Open at Royal Troon generated an estimated 
£87.3 million in total economic impact – new money entering the economy – for 
Scotland. Total economic impact for Ayrshire was an estimated £43.8 million.

• The 2024 Genesis Scottish Open brought £19.2 million in economic impact for 
Scotland, including £7.8 million for East Lothian.

• Our golf events also bring destination marketing benefits, while customer feedback 
confirms that golf events inspire people to visit as golf tourists.

• Furthermore, Scotland’s golf events include the delivery of activity across key 
policy agendas such as health and wellbeing, sustainability, accessibility and 
inclusion.

## Visitors from the USA account for a significant proportion of Scotland’s visitors

• In 2023, there were 794,000 visitors to Scotland from USA with an associated 
spend of £1.2 billion. Visitors from USA accounted for 20% of all overseas visitors 
and over a third (34%) of all overseas visitor spend.

• Edinburgh was the most popular destination for visitors from USA with 487,000  
visitors (61% of all overseas visits) spending £463 million (37% of all overseas 
visitor spend). The Highlands were the next most popular with 18% and 9% of visits 
and spend respectively, followed by Glasgow (14% and 7%).

• 18% of all nights spent in the UK by visitors from the USA in 2023 were spent in 
Scotland, the second highest of all UK regions behind London (45%).

## International Connectivity

---

• Scotland is now served by United, Delta, JetBlue and American with direct routes 
to Boston, Washington, Newark, New York, Chicago, Atlanta, Philadelphia and 
Orlando.

• These routes are proving very successful for both the USA and Scottish markets, 
with the Atlanta route in particular proving to be attractive for golf tourism.

• We continue to work closely with Scotland’s airports to further grow direct 
connectivity with the USA with Dallas, Charlotte and Houston routes of particular 
interest.

• We are very supportive of Edinburgh Airport’s ambition to secure a USA preclearance facility which would further stimulate demand, making travel easier for 
leisure and business passengers, with visitors from the USA benefitting on their 
journey home.
