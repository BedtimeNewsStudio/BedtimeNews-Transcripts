# 2026 ELECTIONS AND INTERNET SHUTDOWNS WATCH

_Around the world, governments impose internet shutdowns during elections in order to undermine democratic participation and cling to power. This is an assault on democracy and human rights that cannot become normalized._

[WATCHLIST](#watchlist)

[TOOLKIT](#toolkit)

[UPDATES](#updates)

Over the past decade, the global [#KeepItOn coalition](https://www.accessnow.org/keepiton/) — a network of over 366 civil society organizations — has mobilized people and communities to monitor, document, and resist internet shutdowns. In recent years, it has become very clear that  **governments are increasingly shutting down the internet and restricting access to digital platforms before, during, and after elections. In 2025, we documented 12 election-related shutdowns, on a par with 2024.**

The [#KeepItOn coalition](https://www.accessnow.org/campaign/keepiton/)’s Election Watch campaign raises awareness of the dangers of election-related shutdowns by engaging with **government officials, the private sector, election observers, foreign and diplomatic missions, journalists, activists and human rights defenders.** The campaign defends democracy by **advocating for an open, inclusive, and secure internet access for all, before, during, and after elections**.

Our advocacy has elevated the issue onto global agendas. In 2025, following elections in Tanzania, the African Commission on Human and Peoples’ Rights (ACHPR) [denounced](https://achpr.au.int/en/news/press-releases/2025-11-01/human-rights-situation-tanzania) the five-day internet shutdown imposed by Tanzania’s government, while the African Union Election Observation Mission (AUEOM) and the Southern African Development Community (SADC) both [acknowledged](https://www.peaceau.org/en/article/preliminary-statement-of-the-african-union-election-observation-mission-to-the-october-2025-general-elections-in-the-united-republic-of-tanzania-the-african-union-election-observation-mission-calls-for-urgent-constitutional-reforms-and-inclusive-politic) the [impact of the blackout on election monitoring efforts](https://www.accessnow.org/internet-shutdowns-and-elections-handbook/), with the AUEOM declaring Tanzania’s elections undemocratic.

Ahead of Uganda’s general elections in January 2026, three ACHPR Commissioners and [UN experts](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/01/uganda-un-experts-urge-stronger-human-rights-safeguards-ahead-2026-elections) [warned](https://achpr.au.int/en/news/press-releases/2026-01-06/press-statement-escalating-restrictions-freedom-expression-media-f) of the risk of election-related shutdowns, following the #KeepItOn coalition’s call for the ACHPR to engage with Ugandan authorities. When the Ugandan authorities nevertheless proceeded to cut connectivity during the elections, the Commissioners [condemned](https://achpr.au.int/pt/node/4513) the shutdown and wider crackdown on human rights.

The #KeepItOn coalition also previously contributed to the ACHPR’s [adoption](https://achpr.au.int/en/adopted-resolutions/580-internet-shutdowns-elections-africa-achprres580-lxxvii) of a resolution denouncing election-related shutdowns, and to the Freedom Online Coalition’s (FOC) [statement](https://freedomonlinecoalition.com/joint-statement-internet-shutdowns-and-elections/) on the topic. Our advocacy has also led the governments of the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://www.accessnow.org/campaign/2023-elections-and-internet-shutdowns-watch/) (DRC), [Sierra Leone, Nigeria](https://www.accessnow.org/campaign/2024-elections-and-internet-shutdowns-watch/), [Bangladesh](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/internet-and-mobile-network-be-fully-operational-election-day-ec-secy-3508161), [Kenya](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/kenya-government-internet-connectivity-elections/), and [Ghana](https://www.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/NewsArchive/2020-Elections-Gov-t-will-not-shut-down-social-media-NCA-789116) to publicly commit to keeping the internet on during elections. With resistance to election-related shutdowns growing, we urge others to join the fight to protect democratic participation.

## **WATCHLIST**

More than [40 countries](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/1/1/all-the-big-elections-to-look-out-for-in-2026), representing a combined population of 1.6 billion people, are hosting elections in 2026. With people, especially Gen Z, increasingly facing repression and [violence](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cx2e3j819eqo) as they speak out for justice and good governance, the **need to safeguard democratic participation and digital freedoms has never been more urgent**.

The #KeepItOn coalition has identified at least 10 countries with a [history of shutdowns,](https://www.accessnow.org/campaign/keepiton/) including Uganda, Ethiopia, Armenia, Russia, and South Sudan, which we will monitor in 2026.

## **Elections on the watch list in Africa**

## **UGANDA**

**January 15 — General elections**

**What happened:**

Ugandan authorities [shut down the internet](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/01/uganda-internet-shutdown/) for five days during elections.

**Why we watched:**

Since 2016, Ugandan authorities have consistently imposed partial or complete internet shutdowns during general elections. In January 2026, two days before the country’s general elections, the Ugandan Communications Commission (UCC) [ordered](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/ucc-orders-mobile-network-operators-to-suspend-public-internet-access-5325326) internet service providers and mobile network operators to shut down internet and digital communications access. The government also ordered Starlink, which is yet to obtain an operating licence in Uganda, to block its terminals and services, and threatened VPN users. Despite [calls from civil society](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-coalition-raise-the-alarm-on-potential-shutdown-during-upcoming-elections-in-uganda/) and international human rights experts, including [ACHPR Commissioners](https://achpr.au.int/en/news/press-releases/2026-01-06/press-statement-escalating-restrictions-freedom-expression-media-f) and [UN Special Rapporteurs](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/01/uganda-un-experts-urge-stronger-human-rights-safeguards-ahead-2026-elections), for the government of Uganda to #KeepItOn, authorities went ahead and [imposed](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-uganda-internet-access-2026-elections/) a complete, five-day-long, nationwide blackout throughout elections.

[Government of Uganda must ensure unrestricted internet access throughout the upcoming elections](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-uganda-internet-access-2026-elections/)

## **Republic of the Congo**

**March 15  — General elections**

**What happened:**

Authorities in the Republic of Congo [shut down](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/2033101198132191436?s=20) the internet across the country as people cast their votes.

**Why we watched**

On March 21, 2021, just hours before polls were set to open, the Republic of Congo implemented a [complete internet shutdown](https://www.accessnow.org/congo-keepiton-election/), impacting all broadband and mobile internet connections, as well as SMS messaging, which lasted until [March 23](https://twitter.com/DougMadory/status/1374729708319965184?s=20). This was an unfortunate repeat of past transgressions; in 2015, authorities [in Congo-Brazzaville](https://www.accessnow.org/government-may-have-ordered-internet-shutdown-in-congo-brazzaville/) cut off the internet ahead of protests against extending the term of President Sassou Nguesso, while in 2016, authorities [shut down the internet during the presidential election](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2016/3/20/congo-in-media-blackout-for-presidential-elections), purportedly to stop the “illegal” publication of results.

## **BENIN**

**April 12 — Presidential elections**

**What happened:**

The #KeepItOn coalition received no reports of internet blackouts during the presidential elections in Benin.

**Why we watched:**

In 2021, authorities in Benin ensured unfettered internet access throughout the country’s general elections, following #KeepItOn coalition and partner [advocacy](https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2021/06/keeping-the-internet-on-during-benins-presidential-elections/). The government had previously [imposed shutdowns during elections](https://www.accessnow.org/benin-heads-to-elections-without-social-media-and-opposition-parties/) in 2019, when, within a day, they first blocked [social media platforms](https://qz.com/africa/1606670/benin-shuts-internet-blocks-whatsapp-facebook-ahead-of-election/) during parliamentary elections, then blocked VPNs, then cut internet connectivity entirely. Although there have been no recorded shutdowns since, an [attempted coup](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2025/12/16/has-benins-foiled-coup-made-ecowas-a-west-african-heavyweight-once-more) in December 2025, paired with steadily [shrinking](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/01/benin-candidates-human-rights/) civic space, raise significant concerns. We therefore appealed to the government of Benin to keep upholding human rights and to #KeepItOn throughout the upcoming general election.

## **SOMALILAND**

**TBD — Local elections**

**Why we are watching:**

Authorities in Somaliland have a history of imposing internet shutdowns to quell protests and silence dissent. In 2022, the government [shut down](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-in-somaliland/) the internet in response to protests against presidential elections being postponed, while in 2017 the Somaliland Electoral Commission ordered telecommunications companies to [block social media](https://www.hrw.org/news/2017/11/10/shuttering-social-media-during-somalilands-elections) platforms, including [Facebook, WhatsApp, Instagram, and LinkedIn](https://qz.com/africa/1126826/somaliland-is-blocking-facebook-twitter-linkedin-instagram-snapchat-and-viber-during-elections-to-avoid-fake-news), during presidential elections, citing “fake news” and hate speech as justifications. As the self-declared state heads to the polls on May 31, we call on authorities to #KeepItOn.

## **ETHIOPIA**

**June 1 — General elections**

**What happened:**

The #KeepItOn coalition received no reports of internet blackouts during the general election in Ethiopia.

**Why we’re watching:**

Since 2016, we have recorded at least 30 internet shutdowns in Ethiopia; the highest number for any African country. Most of these coincided with [protests,](https://www.accessnow.org/back-in-the-dark-ethiopia-shuts-down-internet-once-again/) [communal violence](https://storyteller.iom.int/stories/ethiopia-families-displaced-communal-violence-rebuild-their-lives-one-step-time), or [armed conflict](https://twitter.com/accessnow/status/1323964706382643200?lang=en), but each one disrupted fundamental rights, [endangered civilian lives](https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2025/country-chapters/ethiopia), and enabled warring parties to conceal human rights abuses and atrocities.

While election-related shutdowns are rare, the [resurgence of conflicts in Tigray and Amhara](https://martinplaut.com/2026/03/10/ethiopia-fresh-clashes-renew-fears-for-a-return-to-conflict-expert-comment/) and the [recent crackdown on civic space](https://liberties.aljazeera.com/en/ethiopias-new-civil-society-crackdown-risks-undoing-reform/) raise concerns about possible disruptions during June’s election. The government has intensified pressure on journalists and civil society groups through [arbitrary arrests, intimidation, threats, and forced self-censorship](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/07/29/ethiopia-proposed-legal-changes-threaten-civil-society), while prominent nongovernmental organizations were [suspended](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2024/11/ethiopia-suspension-of-three-human-rights-organizations-highlights-growing-crackdown-on-civic-space/) in 2025.

## **ZAMBIA**

**August 13 — General elections**

**What happened:**

The #KeepItOn coalition received no reports of internet blackouts during the general elections in Zambia.

**Why we watched:**

Zambian authorities imposed internet shutdowns during general elections in both 2021 and [2016](http://www.techtrends.co.zm/internet-outage/). In August 2021, despite [calls to #KeepItOn](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/as-contentious-election-nears-rights-groups-urge-zambia-to-keepiton/), the government [blocked](https://www.accessnow.org/shutdown-in-zambia-on-election-day-how-it-affected-peoples-lives-and-wellbeing/) access to major social media and messaging platforms, including WhatsApp, Twitter, Instagram, and Facebook, throughout the presidential elections. Access was restored after civil society filed a lawsuit and the High Court ruled against the shutdowns. Ahead of the 2026 election, we urged the government to uphold its [commitment](https://zambianeye.com/government-admits-failure-to-establish-why-there-was-internet-shutdown-during-2021-general-elections/) to ensure open and secure access to the internet and digital platforms across the country, which they did; a decision we applaud.

[Government of Zambia must uphold human rights and ensure unrestricted internet access throughout the upcoming elections](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-zambia-internet-access-upcoming-elections/)

## **THE GAMBIA**

**December 5 — General elections**

**Why we’re watching:**

On the eve of The Gambia’s 2016 presidential elections, President Yahya Jammeh [plunged the nation into digital darkness](https://www.accessnow.org/gambia-shuts-internet-eve-elections/), just as [journalists had speculated](https://www.accessnow.org/gambia-shuts-internet-eve-elections/) he would. Since then, there have been reports of frequent [network disruptions](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2039123.html) due to [undersea cable cuts](https://www.accessnow.org/gambia-ITAG). Last year saw widespread [protests](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20250723-gambians-protest-corruption-following-recent-scandals) against corruption and scandal, while journalists and civil society [remain under attack](https://freedomhouse.org/country/gambia/freedom-world/2025); raising concerns that authorities may restrict access to information when the country goes to the polls this year. We call on authorities to find a lasting solution to the undersea cable cuts to ensure people have access to free, open, and secure internet and digital communications tools throughout the elections, and to ensure journalists can operate freely.

## **SOUTH SUDAN**

**December 22 — General elections**

**Why we’re watching:**

South Sudanese authorities have repeatedly restricted access to the internet and social media in response to protests. In January 2025, in response to [widespread](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/news/east-africa/dozens-killed-in-south-sudan-revenge-attacks-4895850) violent protests and reprisal attacks against Sudanese people and businesses in the capital, Juba, and other parts of the country, which followed the Sudanese army killing South Sudanese nationals in Sudan, the government ordered internet and mobile service providers to shut down social media for 90 days. Following [strong condemnation](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/keepiton-in-south-sudan-restore-social-media/#:~:text=In%20efforts%20to%20curb%20the,protests%20spurred%20by%20economic%20hardship.) from rights groups, the shutdown was lifted just four days later. In 2021, authorities also cut off internet access for three hours after activists [demanded](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/internet-disrupted-streets-quiet-south-sudan-after-call-protests-2021-08-30/) the resignation of President Salva Kiir. The Minister of Information, Communication, and Postal Services denied ordering the shutdown, [attributing](https://www.voaafrica.com/a/africa_south-sudan-focus_protests-south-sudan-fizzle-amid-security-presence-internet-outage/6210138.html) the outage to technical problems, but [rights groups suspected](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2021/09/south-sudan-end-new-wave-of-repression-against-peaceful-protests/#:~:text=Amnesty%20International%20suspects%20that%20the,expression%2C%20association%20and%20peaceful%20assembly.) it was a deliberate attempt to squash dissent.

After repeated postponements, the country is preparing for elections in December amid a tense political climate. The #KeepItOn coalition urges authorities to guarantee unfettered internet access to the internet before, during, and after the elections.

## **Elections on the watch list in Eastern Europe & Central Asia**

## **ARMENIA**

**June 7 — Presidential elections**

**What happened:**

The #KeepItOn coalition received no reports of internet blackouts during the presidential election in Armenia.

**Why we are watching:**

Authorities in Armenia have a history of disrupting connectivity in times of unrest, as part of wider efforts to control information flows and [intimidate and censor the media](https://khosq.am/reports/%d5%b0%d5%a1%d5%b5%d5%a1%d5%bd%d5%bf%d5%a1%d5%b6%d5%b8%d6%82%d5%b4-%d5%ad%d5%b8%d5%bd%d6%84%d5%ab-%d5%a1%d5%a6%d5%a1%d5%bf%d5%b8%d6%82%d5%a9%d5%b5%d5%a1%d5%b6-%d5%be%d5%ab%d5%b3%d5%a1%d5%af%d5%ab-37/). In 2023,  Armenian authorities [tried, but failed, to legitimize](https://russian.eurasianet.org/%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%B2%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8-%D1%85%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%8F%D1%82-%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%88%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C-%D1%81%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%B8-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BF%D0%BE-%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B5%D1%82-%D1%86%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B7%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B5-%D0%B2-%D1%83%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%8F%D1%85-%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B3%D0%BE-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F) their ability to censor online content, block media outlets, and even restrict internet access during martial law. Previously, in September 2022, authorities [blocked access](https://x.com/ditord/status/1569661921909174276?s=20) to TikTok when fighting [erupted](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-europe-62888891) along the Azerbaijan-Armenia border between Azerbaijani and Armenian troops.

## **RUSSIA**

**September 18-20 — Legislative elections**

**What happened:**

The #KeepItOn coalition received no reports of internet blackouts during the legislative election in Russia.

**Why we are watching:**

Since its full-scale illegal invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Kremlin has [expanded](https://www.accessnow.org/russias-record-war-on-connectivity/) censorship and internet shutdowns as a tactic to control narratives and silence dissent. Beginning in 2024, platforms such as [YouTub](https://t.me/moscowtimes_ru/18466) [e](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2024/01/24/widespread-telegram-whatsapp-outages-reported-across-russia-a83830), [Telegram, and WhatsApp](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2024/01/24/widespread-telegram-whatsapp-outages-reported-across-russia-a83830) have been disrupted or permanently blocked in several regions, and the [advertising of VPNs](https://meduza.io/en/news/2024/03/03/russia-may-block-wikipedia-due-to-article-on-vpns-that-help-reach-blocked-sites-says-lawmaker) used to circumvent restrictions has been banned. In 2022, authorities [blocked access to Twitter, Instagram, and Facebook](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-63218095), and designated Meta as [a terrorist and extremist organization](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-63218095); part of a broader crackdown on online expression. Russian authorities have also [intensified](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/kremlin-must-end-internet-shutdowns-national-events/) internet shutdowns during key national events, including the 80th anniversary of victory in World War II in 2025 and the funeral of [opposition leader Alexey Navalny](https://en.zona.media/article/2024/03/01/farewell) in 2024.

## **Elections on the watch list in THE MIDDLE EAST AND NORTH AFRICA**

## **MOROCCO**

**September 23 — General elections**

**What happened:**

The #KeepItOn coalition received no reports of internet blackouts during the general election in Morocco.

**Why we watched**

Although Moroccan authorities have not [imposed](https://www.accessnow.org/morocco-complete-blackout-protests-al-hoceima/#:~:text=But%20according%20to%20information%20provided,and%20arrested%2C%20and%20contacting%20journalists.) internet shutdowns since 2017, last year’s [resurgence of mass protests](https://www.gisreportsonline.com/r/gen-z-protests-ignite-across-morocco/) calling for education and healthcare reforms, and for stronger anti-corruption measures, which were [met with a violent crackdown](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/10/15/morocco-protests-met-with-repression-violence), renewed concerns about potential disruptions ahead of general elections scheduled for September.

## **WHAT YOU CAN DO ABOUT ELECTION-RELATED SHUTDOWNS**

Fighting internet shutdowns requires collaboration, solidarity, awareness-raising, and documentation of evidence. As part of Access Now and the #KeepItOn coalition’s 2025 Election Watch campaign, we hope to strengthen our advocacy against shutdowns to harness democratic values globally. We will empower people and communities through awareness-raising, capacity building, and training, as well as equipping them with tools and resources to fight back against shutdowns. We will continue engaging with governments, private companies, individuals, and stakeholders in countries with upcoming elections, until those authorities commit to upholding internet access as a prerequisite for free, fair, and transparent elections.

Given the internet and digital communications platforms’ role in advancing democracy, we urge all stakeholders — including civil society actors, journalists, human rights defenders, governments, companies, and individuals — to collaborate with the #KeepItOn coalition and join the global movement against shutdowns today.
