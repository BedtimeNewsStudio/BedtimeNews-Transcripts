# The History of Celebration: From Walt Disney's Vision to a Thriving Florida Community

How a dream about the future became one of Central Florida's most beloved neighborhoods

![The history of Celebration Florida](https://t.realgeeks.media/resize/460x/https://u.realgeeks.media/floridaneighborhoodrealty%252FCelebration-Walt-Disney.jpg)

*By David Myers, Chief Creative Officer at FloridaNeighborhoodRealty.com | Last updated: February 2026*

Celebration is a master-planned community in Osceola County, Florida, founded by the Walt Disney Company in 1996. Located approximately 5 miles from Walt Disney World and 20 miles southwest of downtown Orlando, it covers roughly 4,900 acres with a population of 11,178 (2020 Census). The community was designed by architects Robert A. M. Stern and Jacquelin T. Robertson in a New Urbanism style. The median home price is approximately $595,000-$600,000. Disney divested control in the mid-2000s, and all properties are now privately owned.

Here's a question that trips up even longtime Central Florida residents: did Walt Disney really build a town? The answer is both yes and no — and the story behind Celebration, Florida is one of the most fascinating chapters in Orlando-area history. It involves a visionary animator, a futuristic city under a dome, celebrity architects, and a 1995 lottery where thousands paid for the *chance* to buy a house.

Whether you're considering a move to the Orlando area or just curious about this community near [Walt Disney World](/blog/walt-disney-world/ "Walt Disney World Florida resident information"), Celebration's history tells you a lot about what makes Central Florida unique.

## It All Started with a Dream Called EPCOT

To understand Celebration, you have to go back to 1966 — the year Walt Disney unveiled his vision for "The Florida Project." At the heart of everything was a town: the Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow, or EPCOT. Not a theme park — a real city of 20,000 residents under a climate-controlled dome, with underground roads and cutting-edge technology.

Walt passed away in December 1966, and the EPCOT city concept was shelved. The name became a theme park in 1982, but the residential dream gathered dust for two decades. Meanwhile, thousands of acres Disney had purchased in [Osceola County](/osceola-county-homes-sale/ "Osceola County Florida real estate") and [Orange County](/orange-county-homes-sale/ "Orange County Florida real estate") sat undeveloped in the southern Reedy Creek Improvement District.

## From Concept to Community: The 1990s Launch

Fast forward to the late 1980s. Disney CEO Michael Eisner challenged the Disney Development Company to "make history" with a real residential community. The architectural competition kicked off in 1987, and by 1991 Disney publicly announced plans for Celebration.

The master plan came from architects Robert A. M. Stern and Jacquelin T. Robertson. Philip Johnson designed the Welcome Center, Michael Graves the post office, and EDAW (now AECOM) handled landscaping. Urban Design Associates created pattern books ensuring every home maintained small-town charm.

Disney didn't recreate Walt's dome city. Instead, Celebration embraced *New Urbanism*: walkable neighborhoods, front porches facing the street, garages on rear alleys, parks within walking distance, and a vibrant town center. Think 1920s small-town America with modern infrastructure.

## The Lottery That Started It All

In November 1995, Disney held a lottery — thousands paid deposits for the *chance* to buy a lot. The first family moved in June 18, 1996, and by November 12 (now Founders Day), downtown was complete. Total investment: an estimated $2.5 billion across 4,900 acres.

Development rolled out in phases, from Celebration Village and Lake Evalyn to the final Artisan Park. The Urban Land Institute named it "New Community of the Year" in 2001, and Forbes later called it one of America's prettiest neighborhoods.

## Does Disney Still Own Celebration?

This is the most common question, so let's clear it up. No — Disney no longer owns or manages Celebration. In 2004, Disney sold the Town Center to private investors, and all residential properties are privately owned. The community is governed by the Celebration Residential Owners Association (CROA) and a [CDD](/blog/whats-cdd/ "What's a CDD") established under Florida law in 1994.

That said, Disney's fingerprints are everywhere. Several Disney business units still occupy office buildings, and two utilities operated from Walt Disney World provide services to residents. The community is even physically connected to the Disney resort via World Drive, running from near the Magic Kingdom 8 miles south into Celebration.

## What Makes Celebration Special Today

### Architecture and Design

Walking through Celebration feels genuinely different from typical Florida subdivisions. Homes reflect traditional American styles — Colonial, Victorian, Mediterranean, Coastal, and French — with no two adjacent homes looking alike. Front porches are required, garages are hidden on rear alleys, and the streets are designed to slow traffic and encourage walking. Nearly 30 years later, it still delivers on the promise of feeling like a real town.

### Downtown and Town Center

Celebration Town Center is the heart of the community. Market Street features boutique shopping, the Columbia Restaurant (Cuban and Spanish cuisine), Kilwins for ice cream, and Maple Street Biscuit Company for brunch. The lakeside promenade along Lake Rianhard features an interactive fountain, and horse-drawn carriage rides add to the atmosphere.

### Events and Community Life

The annual calendar includes an Exotic Car Festival, Oktoberfest, Independence Day fireworks, art shows, and community-wide yard sales. During fall and winter, the town creates "snowfall" (soap flakes — but the kids love it) in the Town Center. The Celebration Foundation coordinates community-building activities and scholarship programs year-round.

### Schools and Healthcare

Families are served by the Osceola County School District — Celebration School for K-8 and Celebration High School for grades 9-12, with private options including the Montessori School of Celebration. Healthcare is anchored by AdventHealth Celebration, currently expanding with a new 80-bed patient tower bringing total capacity to 427 beds.

## Living in Celebration: The Real Deal

Let's get practical. With a 2020 Census population of 11,178 and continued growth, here's what to know if you're considering a move.

### The Pros

* Stunning architecture with genuine small-town charm and walkability
* Only 5 miles from Walt Disney World and 20 miles from [Downtown Orlando](/downtown-orlando-homes-sale/ "Downtown Orlando homes for sale")
* Miles of walking trails and parks connecting every neighborhood
* Year-round community events and festivals
* On-site hospital, schools, shopping, and dining

### The Cons

* Strict HOA and deed restrictions (160+ pages of covenants)
* CDD fees on top of HOA dues increase monthly costs
* Median home prices around $595,000-$600,000 — premium for the area
* Tourist foot traffic in Town Center during events and holidays

### Our Take

If you value walkability, architectural beauty, and Disney proximity, Celebration is hard to beat. If strict HOA rules aren't your style, neighboring [Kissimmee](/kissimmee-homes-sale/ "Kissimmee homes for sale"), [Horizon West](/horizon-west-homes-sale/ "Horizon West homes for sale"), and [Dr. Phillips](/dr-phillips-homes-sale/ "Dr. Phillips homes for sale") offer great alternatives with distinct personalities.

## Getting to Celebration

Celebration's main entrance is off US-192 via Celebration Avenue, just over a mile east of [I-4](/blog/I-4/ "Interstate 4 - The Road You Love to Hate"). Key distances:

* **Walt Disney World** — 5 miles, 10-15 minutes
* **Downtown Orlando** — 20 miles, 30-40 minutes
* **Orlando International Airport (MCO)** — 22 miles, 25-35 minutes
* **Downtown Kissimmee** — 8 miles, 15-20 minutes

## What the Locals Know

What started as Walt Disney's 1960s dream of a futuristic city became a beautifully designed community rooted in the charm of the past. The New Urbanism principles guiding Celebration have proven more enduring and livable than any prototype under a dome could have been.

The streets named after flowers (not Disney characters — that's a myth), the front porches built for waving at neighbors, and the downtown that feels like a movie set all add up to something both aspirational and real. Whether you call it home or just visit for a Saturday stroll, Celebration proves that big vision plus thoughtful design creates something people love. ✨

For more information about community events, resources, and life in Celebration, visit the official [Celebration community website](https://celebration.fl.us/).
