WESLACO, Texas — Hundreds of migrants were captured on camera walking in the middle of the night across the New Mexico-Chihuahua border.

The video was taken by Jim Benvie, a member of a volunteer group called United Constitutional Patriots. The group has been camping out in the Chihuahua desert for two months.

The video – [streamed on Facebook Live](https://www.facebook.com/jim.benvie.3/videos/991379547731187/) – shows droves of asylum seekers comprised of men, women and children, walking up a dirt road just after midnight Tuesday. They crossed through a border fence gap in Sunland Park, New Mexico, to turn themselves in to Border Patrol, but they came across Benvie’s encampment instead.

“I literally walked out and I looked, and all I saw was hundreds of people coming at us,” said Benvie.

Benvie and three other men had been scouting the area Monday night before encountering what the U.S. Border Patrol estimated was 300 migrants.

“We held them there until Border Patrol came,” said Benvie. “And once they came then we did what we do, and that’s to help Border Patrol get them to the processing van. So, everybody is safe.”

The KENS 5 Border Team [first met the UCP](https://www.kens5.com/article/news/special-reports/at-the-border/armed-civilians-living-off-the-grid-to-catch-migrants-illegally-crossing-the-border/273-31c11a4e-def2-4aa9-a1b3-0fb99ed4bdbd) two weeks ago, and was shown what Benvie identified as gaps in border security at the corner where the borders of New Mexico, Texas, and Chihuahua, Mexico meet.

![](https://media.kens5.com/assets/KENS/images/b71ee3a9-71f9-47b9-b2ee-93bc9604b6f4/b71ee3a9-71f9-47b9-b2ee-93bc9604b6f4_1140x641.png)

Benvie has documented their encounters with smugglers and asylum seekers for the last 60 days.

“We’re just Americans. We’re veterans, we’re ex-law enforcement, we’re people that care about our national security,” he said. “We’re people that care about our strained Border Patrol.”

Border Patrol said it’s aware of the presence of the UCP but does not condone the group’s actions. Actions Benvie described as peaceful – and the guns they carry – purely for self-defense.

![](https://media.kens5.com/assets/KENS/images/753fefa1-6218-40a2-afdf-0316ff8700d1/753fefa1-6218-40a2-afdf-0316ff8700d1_1140x641.png)

Credit: Jim Benvie

“There’s danger that goes along with this,” admitted Benvie. “We do have the cartel here. We are interfering with their business. We don’t think that we can save the border, we’re down here because we want to show America the truth. That’s why we’re making these videos.”

The videos have been watched and shared by thousands of people on Facebook.

It is through their online presence that the UCP tries to raise money to continue shining a light on that they believe is a crisis at the border.

# San Antonio police working active scene near IDEA Mays campus on far west side

SAPD said the active presence was near Moon Light and Southwest Loop 410.

![San Antonio police are investigating an active presence near the IDEA Mays campus on the city's far west side.](https://media.kens5.com/assets/KENS/images/eb9fb95e-0c2a-46a2-83c9-3d0138932f66/eb9fb95e-0c2a-46a2-83c9-3d0138932f66_750x422.jpg)

SAN ANTONIO — San Antonio police are investigating an active presence near the IDEA Mays campus on the city's far west side, according to a post on the [department's social media pages](https://www.facebook.com/SanAntonioPD/posts/pfbid03ujCrQtNZTcp6m2TuoJ7tPaRwddrzuAner7wy22rMUXeQyjzgpcAqssxzh9so8o9l).

SAPD said the presence was near Moon Light and Southwest Loop 410. The IDEA campus is not far from the street, according to Google Maps.

Details about what caused the activity were not immediately clear as of Monday afternoon.

SAPD said parents picking up children would need to contact the school for a modified dismissal schedule. It is not clear if the incident involved the school.

IDEA Public Schools sent the following statement:

_"IDEA Mays is aware of a situation that allegedly occurred near our campus that is not associated with IDEA Public Schools. A SECURE has been activated while the San Antonio Police Department conducts its investigation. All students and staff are safe. All further questions should be directed to the San Antonio Police Department."_

SAN ANTONIO — It has been three days since the FAA grounded air travel in the U.S. this past Friday, but airports across the country have traffic flowing once again.

It was business as usual here at the San Antonio Airport from our KENS5 crew earlier this afternoon. Taking a look at the flight status boards, about 90 to 95% of them were on time with only a few cancellations, and travelers seemed to be in pretty good spirits following Friday’s stoppage.

We all know the holidays are just around the corner with Thanksgiving next week. And,  some good news, AAA doesn't think Friday's unusually long ground stop will have a major impact on Thanksgiving travel unless it happens again. They say travelers are resilient and from what they’ve seen in the past, it takes a lot to keep travelers home. They expect the vast majority of holiday travelers to opt for car trips over flights. But for those that are flying over the holidays, the travelers we found at the airport had this advice for anyone taking to the skies over Thanksgiving or Christmas.

Tom Mueller, who was flying back to his hometown of St. Louis, Missouri told us, “I think fortunately for us we didn’t have a connecting flight it is a direct flight so if I would tell anyone I would say try to get a direct flight because it makes it less complicated.” Garrett Myler, from San Antonio, who was flying out of town to the nation's capital said, “Probably to get to the airport a little earlier than I typically do. Maybe give yourself a proper two hours. But flying out of San Antonio you should be pretty good.”

If you are planning to fly post-shutdown you’re advised to check your flight status often, pack essentials in your carry on, arrive early on the day of your flight, and know your credit card protections.

Whether you are traveling today or anytime over the next month or two through the holidays, you can always check your flight status leaving San Antonio. All you have to do is go to [FlySanAntonio.com](https://flysanantonio.com/home/flights/flight-status/) for that necessary information.

# Seguin police investigating murder-suicide after disturbance call in Schertz

On Monday, Schertz police announced they are investigating this as a murder-suicide.

![](https://media.kens5.com/assets/KENS/images/6c52ee2d-ba9f-4b35-ae40-9b87eed3de0a/20260402T102335/6c52ee2d-ba9f-4b35-ae40-9b87eed3de0a_750x422.jpg)

Credit: KENS 5

SCHERTZ, Texas — A woman and a man died early Sunday after officers responding to a disturbance found them with apparent gunshot wounds, according to Schertz police.

The department received a 911 call around 12:40 a.m. Nov. 15 reporting a disturbance in the 14400 block of Interstate 10 East.

Officers found a 35-year-old woman and a 40-year-old man injured at the scene. Both died despite efforts to provide lifesaving care.

On Monday, Schertz police announced they are investigating this as a murder-suicide. The woman has been identified as Elizabeth Trujillo while the man, considered to be the suspect by police, has been identified as Leonard Santos.

Both causes of death have been determined to be gunshot wounds to the head, according to the Bexar County Sheriff's Office.

Officials believe the incident was isolated and there is no further threat to public safety.
