The **Type 42** or **_Sheffield_ class** was developed to provide the Royal Navy with a cost-effective, medium-sized guided-missile destroyer focused on fleet air defence. Conceived in the late 1960s after the cancellation of the more complex and expensive [Type 82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_82_destroyer "Type 82 destroyer"), the Type 42 was intended to protect naval task groups against airborne threats using the Sea Dart missile system.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Marriott_1945_RN_DestroyerBook-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-Marriott_1945_RN_DestroyerBook)

While the smaller design allowed for more ships to be built within budget constraints, it imposed compromises in endurance, sea-keeping, and space for future weapons or sensor upgrades.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Friedman2012-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-Friedman2012) Over its 38 years of service, the class underwent three production batches that addressed some of the class's limitations by lengthening the hull, improving radar and missile systems, and adding [close-in weapon systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-in_weapon_system "Close-in weapon system").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Marriott_1945_RN_DestroyerBook-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-Marriott_1945_RN_DestroyerBook)

Two vessels, [HMS _Sheffield_(D80)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Sheffield_(D80) "HMS Sheffield (D80)") and [HMS _Coventry_(D118)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Coventry_(D118) "HMS Coventry (D118)"), were lost in the 1982 [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War "Falklands War"), prompting further modifications including enhanced damage control measures, updated electronic warfare suites and changes to material used in the ships' furnishings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Friedman2012-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-Friedman2012) The class served as the backbone of Royal Navy air defence and demonstrated a trade-off between affordability and capability reflecting broader Royal Navy strategic priorities during the Cold War and post-Cold War periods, concluding in 2013 when they were replaced by the more capable [Type 45 destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_45_destroyer "Type 45 destroyer").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Marriott_1945_RN_DestroyerBook-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-Marriott_1945_RN_DestroyerBook)

In addition to British service, two Type 42s were built for the [Argentine Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Navy "Argentine Navy"). These two ships saw limited service, few upgrades and were eventually retired.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

## History

Following the cancellation of the Type 82 air-defence destroyers and the proposed [CVA-01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CVA-01 "CVA-01") aircraft carrier by the [Labour Government in 1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_government,_1964%E2%80%931970 "Labour government, 1964–1970"), the Type 42 was conceived as a lighter, cheaper alternative with similar capabilities. The class was fitted with the GWS30 [Sea Dart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Dart "Sea Dart") [surface-to-air missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-to-air_missile "Surface-to-air missile"), first deployed on the sole Type 82 destroyer, [HMS _Bristol_(D23)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Bristol_(D23) "HMS Bristol (D23)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) The ships also had a [flight deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_deck "Flight deck") and hangar to operate an [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare "Anti-submarine warfare") helicopter, increasing their versatility compared to the Type 82, which had a flight deck but no hangar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)

Feasibility designs completed by the [Royal Corps of Naval Constructors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Corps_of_Naval_Constructors "Royal Corps of Naval Constructors") in the late-1960s were for a naval staff requirement for an air-defence destroyer with a limited budget of £11.25 million and an artificial limiting displacement of 3,500 tons.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Daniel1-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-Daniel1) Chief Constructor Tony Austin RCNC considered that the design would require 4,100 tons.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Daniel1-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-Daniel1) Extreme decisions were made to reduce the displacement, most significant was shortening the fore-body of the ship, at a saving of around 1% of the total cost.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Daniel1-2)[\[9\]](#refnote-Daniel1) [Jack Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Daniel "Reginald Daniel") RCNC as the new head of the Forward Director Group concurred, and argued for the lengthening of the ship. This was not implemented and MK Purvis RCNC completed the design.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) This proved a poor decision, as the first of class [HMS Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Sheffield_(D80) "HMS Sheffield (D80)") suffered hull plating damage from slamming whilst transiting in the Channel. As a short-term solution, the early hulls were reinforced, resulting in a displacement of over 4,000 tons.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Daniel1-3)[\[9\]](#refnote-Daniel1) When [Jack Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Daniel "Reginald Daniel") was promoted to [Director General Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_General_Ships "Director General Ships") he was able to increase the length of the Batch 3 ships ( [HMS Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Manchester_(D95) "HMS Manchester (D95)") onwards).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Daniel1-4)[\[9\]](#refnote-Daniel1)

Fourteen vessels were constructed in three distinct batches. In addition to the Royal Navy ships, two were built to a Batch 1 specifications for the [Argentine Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_of_the_Argentine_Republic "Navy of the Argentine Republic"). [ARA _Hércules_(B-52)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARA_H%C3%A9rcules_(B-52) "ARA Hércules (B-52)") was built in the [Vickers Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Shipbuilding "Vickers Shipbuilding") yard in [Barrow-in-Furness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrow-in-Furness "Barrow-in-Furness"), [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"), and delivered August 1976. [ARA _Santísima Trinidad_(1974)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARA_Sant%C3%ADsima_Trinidad_(D-2) "ARA Santísima Trinidad (D-2)") was built at the AFNE [Río Santiago Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Santiago_Shipyard "Río Santiago Shipyard") in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires") and commissioned in July 1981.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

The class was budgeted at £19 million per hull but exceeded that limit and the original design proposal was £21 million. To cut costs, the first two batches had 47 feet (14m) removed from the bow forward of the bridge, reducing the [beam-to-length ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_(nautical)#Typical_values "Beam (nautical)"). These early Batch 1 ships performed poorly during sea trials in heavy weather, prompting a review.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) Batch 2 ships, beginning with [HMS _Exeter_(D89)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Exeter_(D89) "HMS Exeter (D89)"), incorporated improved sensors and minor layout changes. The ninth hull, [HMS _Manchester_(D95)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Manchester_(D95) "HMS Manchester (D95)"), was lengthened during construction, resulting in better seakeeping, and follow-on ships were built to this standard. Strengthening girders were added to the weather decks of Batch 1 and 2 ships, while Batch 3 vessels were built with an external [strake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strake "Strake") to counter longitudinal cracking.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

[HMS _Sheffield_(D80)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Sheffield_(D80) "HMS Sheffield (D80)") and [HMS _Coventry_(D118)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Coventry_(D118) "HMS Coventry (D118)") were both sunk in the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War "Falklands War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) This was the first conflict since [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") in which surface warships of the same design served on opposing sides. Although there was no direct contact between the ships, both _Santísima Trinidad_ and _Hércules_ locked on to [Sea Harrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Harrier "Sea Harrier"), XZ451 piloted by Flt Lt Mortimer, with their Type 909 fire-control radars on the 1 May 1982, Mortimer retreated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-hista-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-hista)[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

The final ship of the class, [HMS _Edinburgh_(D97)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Edinburgh_(D97) "HMS Edinburgh (D97)"), was decommissioned on 6 June 2013.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) _Hércules_ remained in service in a transport and amphibious role and was declared non-operational in 2020 and formally decommissioned in 2024, while _Santísima Trinidad_ sank at her berth at the [Port Belgrano Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Belgrano_Naval_Base "Port Belgrano Naval Base") in January 2013, she was in poor condition before sinking, having been cannibalised to keep _Hércules_ operational.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

## Design

The first batch was fitted with the [Type 965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_965_radar "Type 965 radar") or Type 966 surveillance radar, both of which had a slow data rate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PrestonFalklands112-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-PrestonFalklands112) The Type 992Q radar, used for target designation for the gun and missile systems, lacked Moving Target Indication (MTI). Although British radar manufacturers offered to retrofit MTI, the modification was never carried out.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PrestonFalklands112-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-PrestonFalklands112) Without MTI, the Type 992Q experienced difficulty in tracking aircraft when they were in front of land, or during snow or rain showers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PrestonFalklands112-2)[\[19\]](#refnote-PrestonFalklands112) In addition, the class had insufficient space for an efficient operations room.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PrestonFalklands112-3)[\[19\]](#refnote-PrestonFalklands112)

The class was armed with a single [4.5 inch Mark 8 naval gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4.5_inch_Mark_8_naval_gun "4.5 inch Mark 8 naval gun"), and earlier vessels carried six Ships Torpedo Weapon System (STWS) [torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo "Torpedo") launchers. The Argentine _Santísima Trinidad_ was equipped with the MM38 Exocet for a broader anti-ship capability. The [boat decks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat_deck "Boat deck") of the original design were replaced by special decks to install the missiles around the funnel. While this modification applied to both ARA ships, the launchers were never mounted on _Santísima Trinidad_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)

There were three production batches. Batch 1 and Batch 2 ships displaced 4,820 tonnes, while Batch 3 (sometimes referred to as the _Manchester_ class) displaced 5,200 tonnes. The Batch 3 ships were heavily upgraded, incorporating lessons from the Falklands War, although the [Sea Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Wolf_(missile) "Sea Wolf (missile)") missile system was never fitted. Two [Phalanx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_CIWS "Phalanx CIWS") [close-in weapon systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-in_weapon_system "Close-in weapon system") were mounted amidships to all surviving batches.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)

The electronics suite comprised either a Type 1022 [D band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D_band_(NATO) "D band (NATO)") long-range radar with Outfit LFB track extractor, or a [Type 965P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_965_radar "Type 965 radar") long-range air surveillance radar; one Type 996 [E band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_band_(NATO) "E band (NATO)")/ [F band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F_band_(NATO) "F band (NATO)") [3D radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_radar "3D radar") for target indication with Outfit LFA track extractor, or a Type 992Q surface-search radar; two Type 909 I/J-band [fire-control radars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_radar "Fire-control radar"); and an Outfit LFD radar track combiner.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

All ships were powered by [Rolls-Royce TM3B Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Olympus "Rolls-Royce Olympus") and [Rolls-Royce RM1C Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Tyne "Rolls-Royce Tyne") marinised gas turbines, in a COGOG (combined gas or gas) arrangement, driving through synchronous self-shifting clutches into a double-reduction, dual-tandem, articulated, locked-train gear system, and then to two five-bladed [controllable pitch propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controllable_pitch_propellers "Controllable pitch propellers"). Electrical power was supplied by four [Paxman Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paxman_Ventura "Paxman Ventura") 16YJCAZ [diesel generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_generator "Diesel generator"), each producing 1MW of [three-phase electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-phase_electric_power "Three-phase electric power") at 440V, 60Hz.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

The lead ship, [HMS _Sheffield_(D80)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Sheffield_(D80) "HMS Sheffield (D80)"), was initially fitted with exhaust deflectors on her funnel tops to direct high-temperature exhaust gases sideways, reducing heat damage to overhead aerials. This created a prominent target for [infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing "Infrared homing") missiles, so only _Sheffield_ and the two Argentine ships, [ARA _Hércules_(B-52)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARA_H%C3%A9rcules_(B-52) "ARA Hércules (B-52)"), and [ARA _Santísima Trinidad_(1974)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARA_Sant%C3%ADsima_Trinidad_(D-2) "ARA Santísima Trinidad (D-2)"), retained this feature. Subsequent ships were built with 'cheese grater' uptakes, which mixed machinery space ventilation air with the exhaust gases to reduce [infrared signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_signature "Infrared signature").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Availability

In February 1998, the Minister of State for Defence, [Dr John Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Reid,_Baron_Reid_of_Cardowan "John Reid, Baron Reid of Cardowan"), stated: "Type 42 destroyers achieved approximately 84 to 86 per cent average availability for operational service in each of the last five years. This discounts time spent in planned maintenance."[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

## Operational history and service

The Type 42 class was designed to counter long-range strategic bombers from the former Soviet Long-Range Aviation and [Soviet Naval Aviation (AV-MF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Naval_Aviation "Soviet Naval Aviation"), and to provide area defence for a [carrier battle group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_battle_group "Carrier battle group").

1982 Falklands War

Five ships took part in the Falklands War: _Sheffield_, _Coventry_, _Glasgow_, _Exeter_ and _Cardiff_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Type42GS-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-Type42GS) providing long-range air defence and achieving seven confirmed "kills". Coventry was credited with three aircraft: a [Puma SA.330L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_SA_330_Puma "Aérospatiale SA 330 Puma") and two [A-4 Skyhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk "Douglas A-4 Skyhawk").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FalklandsKills-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-FalklandsKills) Exeter shot down four aircraft: two A-4 Skyhawks, a [Learjet 35A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet_35 "Learjet 35") and a [Canberra bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Electric_Canberra "English Electric Canberra").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FalklandsKills-1)[\[24\]](#refnote-FalklandsKills) _Cardiff_ was involved in a [friendly fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendly_fire "Friendly fire") incident resulting in the [loss of a British Gazelle helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_British_Army_Gazelle_friendly_fire_incident "1982 British Army Gazelle friendly fire incident").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FalklandsKills-2)[\[24\]](#refnote-FalklandsKills)

_Sheffield_ was hit by an [Exocet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocet "Exocet") [air-to-surface missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-surface_missile "Air-to-surface missile") launched by an Argentine [Super Étendard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_%C3%89tendard "Super Étendard") on 4 May 1982 and sank six days later; _Coventry_ was sunk by 3, 250kg [unguided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unguided_bombs "Unguided bombs") on the 25 May 1982, with two detonating.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25) and _Glasgow_ was disabled by a bomb that passed through her aft engine room without exploding, damaging fuel systems and disabling the [cruising engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Tyne#Ships "Rolls-Royce Tyne"). These losses led to a reassessment to subsequent vessels.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FalklandsKills-3)[\[24\]](#refnote-FalklandsKills)

1991 Gulf War

On February 25, 1991, during [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm "Operation Desert Storm"), _Gloucester_ shot down an Iraqi [Silkworm missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworm_(missile) "Silkworm (missile)") targeting [USS _Missouri_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Missouri_(BB-63) "USS Missouri (BB-63)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

Task group deployments

Type 42s performed fleet contingency duties, including West Indies counter-drug operations, Falkland Islands patrols, NATO Mediterranean and Atlantic task group deployments, and Persian Gulf patrols. The increased deployment of Type 23 frigates in place of Type 42s to high-intensity operational areas reflected persistent serviceability and reliability problems, alongside growing obsolescence of their combat and machinery systems.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NavalTech-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-NavalTech)

The surviving [Argentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina") Type 42, _Hércules_, was based at [Puerto Belgrano Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Belgrano_Naval_Base "Puerto Belgrano Naval Base"), Argentina, and converted into an amphibious command ship through the addition of a new aft superstructure and hangar. She was originally fitted with four single Exocet missile launchers, two on either side of the funnel facing forward but these were removed during refit. As of 2020, _Hércules_ was reported to be non-operational.[&#8202;](#ref-back-zona-militar.com-1)[\[49\]](#refnote-zona-militar.com) The other Argentine vessel, _Santísima Trinidad_, capsized and sank alongside her berth at Puerto Belgrano on 22 January 2013, reportedly as a result of poor maintenance and negligence leading to a burst seawater main and catastrophic flooding.[&#8202;](#ref-back-telegraph.co.uk-1)[\[51\]](#refnote-telegraph.co.uk) She was formally taken out of service in 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Videla_Solá-1)[\[50\]](#refnote-Videla_Solá)

Prior to her demise, _Santísima Trinidad_ was extensively [cannibalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannibalization_(parts) "Cannibalization (parts)") for spare parts for her more active sister ship. In December 2015, she was refloated and placed in drydock to evaluate the cost of restoration as a museum ship. Finally, due to the very high cost required, it was decided to scrap her in 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[53\]](#refnote-:0) The ship was finally actually broken down in 2025 (after many years of uncertainty as to whether she would be scrapped or not).[&#8202;](#ref-back-SantisimaTrinidadScrapped20251-1)[\[54\]](#refnote-SantisimaTrinidadScrapped20251)[&#8202;](#ref-back-SantisimaTrinidadScrapped20252-1)[\[55\]](#refnote-SantisimaTrinidadScrapped20252)

## Replacement

All the Royal Navy Type 42 ships are now decommissioned. Initially, the United Kingdom sought to procure replacements through collaboration with seven other [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") nations under the [NFR-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFR-90 "NFR-90") project, and then with France and Italy via the [Horizon CNGF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_CNGF "Horizon CNGF") programme. Both collaborative ventures failed, leading to the national Type 45 project.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NAO_report-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-NAO_report) Six [Type 45 destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_45_destroyer "Type 45 destroyer"), [_Daring_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Daring_(D32) "HMS Daring (D32)"), [_Dauntless_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Dauntless_(D33) "HMS Dauntless (D33)"), [_Diamond_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Diamond_(D34) "HMS Diamond (D34)"), [_Dragon_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Dragon_(D35) "HMS Dragon (D35)"), [_Defender_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Defender_(D36) "HMS Defender (D36)") and [_Duncan_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Duncan_(D37) "HMS Duncan (D37)"), are all in commission and are considerably larger, displacing 7,500 tonnes compared to the Type 42's 3,600 tonnes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NAO_report-1)[\[56\]](#refnote-NAO_report)

## Notes

1. [&#8202;](#refnote-30)[↑](#ref-back-30-0)The term used in _Navy Estimates_ and _Defence Estimates_ is "accepted into service". _Hansard_ has used the term _acceptance date_. Leo Marriott in his various books uses the term "completed", as does _Jane's Fighting Ships_. These terms all mean the same thing: the date the Navy accepts the vessel from the builder. This date is important because maintenance cycles, etc. are generally calculated from the acceptance date.
2. [&#8202;](#refnote-31)[↑](#ref-back-31-0)"Unit cost, i.e. excluding the cost of certain items (e.g. aircraft, First Outfits)." – Text from _Defences Estimates_

"They do not include other costs, such as those for Government Furnished Equipment (GFE)—as they are not held centrally for each ship and could be provided only at disproportionate cost." [Bob Ainsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ainsworth "Bob Ainsworth"), [Minister of State for the Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_for_the_Armed_Forces "Minister of State for the Armed Forces"), 16 July 2008.
3. [&#8202;](#refnote-33)[↑](#ref-back-33-0)These two sources are in agreement about the dates vessels were commissioned, with the following exceptions:

   - _Sheffield_: Marriott 28 February 1975. Hansard 16 February 1975.
   - _Glasgow_: Marriott 25 May 1979. Hansard 24 May 1979.
   - _Cardiff_: Marriott 19 October 1979. Hansard 24 September 1979.
   - _Nottingham_: Marriott 8 April 1983. Hansard 14 April 1983.
   - _Liverpool_: Marriott 9 July 1982. Hansard 1 July 1982.

4. [&#8202;](#refnote-39)[↑](#ref-back-39-0)Moore, John _Jane's Fighting Ships, 1982–83_, pub Jane's Publishing Co Ltd, 1982, [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)") [0-7106-0742-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/0-7106-0742-3 "Special:BookSources/0-7106-0742-3")-page 553 said £40.4 million.

Marriott, Leo _Modern Combat Ships 3, Type 42_, pub Ian Allan, 1985, [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)") [0-7110-1453-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/0-7110-1453-1 "Special:BookSources/0-7110-1453-1")-page 15 said £40.4 million.

Aldrich, Richard James _Intelligence, Defence, and Diplomacy: British Policy in the Post-War World. Taylor & Francis_, pub 1994, [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)") [0-7146-4140-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/0-7146-4140-5 "Special:BookSources/0-7146-4140-5") page 119 says: "One example of how delay in procurement programmes can raise costs is the construction of the Type-42 destroyer HMS _Cardiff_. Vickers Shipbuilders had originally intended to deliver the vessel in 1975 for a total cost of £15 million. Owing to difficulties in recruiting labourer to work on construction the ship was only completed in 1978 and cost double the original price (over £30 million)." On page 129 it gives the source of this cost data as: " _Fourth Report from the Committee on Public Accounts, 1976–77_ (H.C. 304), April 1977, pp xii–xiii and Q. 92."

The cost quoted in _Aldrich_ is from a source written before the completion of the vessel, and so is less complete than the cost quoted in _Jane's_ and _Marriott_, which were written after completion of the vessel, and are nearly the same as the Hansard figure.
