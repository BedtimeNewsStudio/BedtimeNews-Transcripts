The **Bath County Pumped Storage Station** is a [pumped storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped_storage_hydroelectricity "Pumped storage hydroelectricity") [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectricity "Hydroelectricity") power plant with a maximum generation capacity of 3,003 MW,[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) an average of 2,772 MW,[&#8202;](#ref-back-think-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-think) and a total storage capacity of 24,000 MWh.[&#8202;](#ref-back-think-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-think) The station is located in the northern corner of [Bath County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_County,_Virginia "Bath County, Virginia"), on the southeast side of the [Eastern Continental Divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Continental_Divide "Eastern Continental Divide"), which forms this section of the border between Virginia and [West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia "West Virginia"). The station consists of two reservoirs separated by about 1,260 feet (380m) in elevation. It was the largest pumped-storage power station in the world until 2021, when it was surpassed by the [Fengning Pumped Storage Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fengning_Pumped_Storage_Power_Station "Fengning Pumped Storage Power Station").[&#8202;](#ref-back-domenr-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-domenr)

Construction on the power station, with an original capacity of 2,100 megawatts (2,800,000hp), began in March 1977 and was completed in December 1985 at a cost of $1.6billion,[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)[&#8202;](#ref-back-modcomp-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-modcomp) [Voith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voith "Voith")- [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens "Siemens") upgraded the six turbines between 2004 and 2009, increasing power generation to 500.5 MW and pumping power to 480 megawatts (640,000hp) for each turbine.[&#8202;](#ref-back-powergen-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-powergen)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) Bath County Station is jointly owned by [Dominion Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Resources "Dominion Resources") (60%) and [FirstEnergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FirstEnergy "FirstEnergy") (40%), and managed by Dominion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-domenr-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-domenr) It stores energy for [PJM Interconnection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJM_Interconnection "PJM Interconnection"), a regional transmission organization in 13 states and the District of Columbia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-think-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-think)

## Design

The upper and lower reservoirs are created by earth and rock-filled [embankment dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embankment_dam "Embankment dam"). The upper reservoir dam is 460 feet (140m) high, 2,200 feet (670m) long and has a structural volume of 18,000,000 cubic yards (14,000,000m3). The upper reservoir on Little Back Creek has a surface area of 265 acres (107ha) and storage capacity of 35,599 acre-feet (43,911,000m3). The lower reservoir dam on [Back Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_Creek_(Jackson_River) "Back Creek (Jackson River)") is 135 feet (41m) high and 2,400 feet (730m) in length. It has a structural volume of 4,000,000 cubic yards (3,100,000m3) and creates a reservoir with a surface area of 555 acres (225ha) and storage capacity of 27,927 acre-feet (34,447,000m3).[&#8202;](#ref-back-powergen-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-powergen)[&#8202;](#ref-back-domenr-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-domenr) Connecting the upper reservoir to the power station are three water conduits between 3,100–3,600 feet (940–1,100m) long. The conduits each lead to a 990 feet (300m) shaft which bifurcates into two [penstocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penstock "Penstock") (for a total of six) before reaching the turbines. Each penstock is 18 feet (5.5m) in diameter and between 900–1,260 feet (270–380m) in length. Maximum [static pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_pressure "Static pressure") from the top of the water delivery system to the turbines is 1,320 feet (400m).[&#8202;](#ref-back-powergen-2)[\[8\]](#refnote-powergen)[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

## Method of operation

Water is released from the upper reservoir during periods of high demand and is used to generate electricity. What makes this different from other hydroelectric dams is that during times of low demand, power is taken from [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal-fired_power_station "Coal-fired power station"), [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plant "Nuclear power plant"), and other power plants and is used to pump water from the lower to the upper reservoir. Although this plant uses more power than it generates, it allows these other plants to operate at close to peak efficiency for an overall cost savings. Back Creek and Little Back Creek, the water sources used to create the reservoirs, have a relatively small flow rate. However, since water is pumped between the reservoirs equally, the only water taken from these creeks now that the reservoirs are full is to replace the water lost to evaporation. During operation, the water level fluctuates by over 105 feet (30m) in the upper reservoir and 60 feet (20m) feet in the lower reservoir.

When generating power, the water flow can be as much as 13.5million US gallons (51,000m3) per minute (850m3/s). When storing power, the flow can be as much as 12.7million US gallons (48,000m3) per minute (800m3/s).

## Environment

A fishing habitat was created downstream of the facility.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Singletary-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-Singletary)
In times of drought[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13) water quality can be maintained[&#8202;](#ref-back-epawater-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-epawater) by using nearby recreational reservoirs to supply extra water to the creeks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-frbath-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-frbath) The creeks and recreational reservoirs have water quality sufficient for fish.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fish1-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-fish1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-fish2-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-fish2)
