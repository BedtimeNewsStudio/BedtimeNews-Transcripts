_Acela_

The _**Acela**_ ([/əˈsɛlə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [_ə-SEL-ə_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); originally the _**Acela Express**_ until September 2019) is [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak "Amtrak")'s flagship [passenger train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_train "Passenger train") service along the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor "Northeast Corridor") (NEC) in the [Northeastern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_megalopolis "Northeast megalopolis") between [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), and [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston") via 13 intermediate stops, including [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"), [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), and [Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island "Providence, Rhode Island"). _Acela_ trains are the fastest in the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas"), with a top speed of 160 miles per hour (260km/h), qualifying as [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail#North_America "High-speed rail"), but only for about 40 miles (64km) of the 457-mile (735km) route.[&#8202;](#ref-back-amtrakETT-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-amtrakETT)

In fiscal year 2025, _Acela_ carried more than 3.1 million passengers, second only to the slower and less expensive _[Northeast Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional "Northeast Regional")_, which had over 12 million passengers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-8) Ridership was down from the pre-COVID-19 pandemic high of 3,557,455 passengers in 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-9) Its 2025 revenue of $570 million was around 20.6% of Amtrak's total.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-10)

_Acela_ operates along routes that are used by slower regional passenger traffic, and only reaches the [maximum allowed speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_speed_limits_in_the_United_States "Rail speed limits in the United States") of the tracks along some sections, with the fastest peak speed along segments between [Mansfield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield,_Massachusetts "Mansfield, Massachusetts"), and [Richmond, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Rhode_Island "Richmond, Rhode Island"), as well as between [South Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Brunswick,_New_Jersey "South Brunswick, New Jersey") and [Trenton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenton,_New_Jersey "Trenton, New Jersey").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-11) _Acela_ trains use [active tilting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilting_train "Tilting train") technology, which helps control lateral [centrifugal force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifugal_force "Centrifugal force"), allowing the train to travel at higher speeds on the sharply curved NEC without disturbing passengers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-12) The high-speed operation occurs mostly along the 226-mile (364km) route from [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Penn_Station "New York Penn Station") in New York City to [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Union_Station "Washington Union Station") in Washington, D.C., with a fastest scheduled time of 2 hours and 45 minutes and an average speed of 82 miles per hour (132km/h), including time spent at intermediate stops.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyp-was-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-nyp-was)[&#8202;](#ref-back-note1-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-note1) Over this route, _Acela_ and the _Northeast Regional_ service captured an 83% share of air/train commuters between New York and Washington in 2021, up from 37% in 2000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-15)

The _Acela_'s speed is limited by traffic and infrastructure on the route's northern half. On the 231-mile (372km) section from Boston's [South Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Station "South Station") to New York's Penn Station, the fastest scheduled time is 3 hours and 30 minutes, or an average speed of 66 miles per hour (106km/h).[&#8202;](#ref-back-bos-was-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-bos-was)[&#8202;](#ref-back-note2-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-note2) Along this section, _Acela_ has captured a 54% share of the combined train and air market.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-17)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-18) The entire 457-mile (735km) route from Boston to Washington takes between 6 hours, 38 minutes and 6 hours, 50 minutes,[&#8202;](#ref-back-bos-was-2)[\[2\]](#refnote-bos-was) at an average speed of around 70 miles per hour (110km/h).[&#8202;](#ref-back-note3-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-note3)

In 2025, the original [Acela Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acela_Express_(trainset) "Acela Express (trainset)") equipment began to be replaced by new [Avelia Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avelia_Liberty "Avelia Liberty") trainsets, which Amtrak markets as the _**NextGen Acela**_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-20) The new trains have greater passenger capacity and an enhanced active tilt system that allows higher speeds on the many curved sections of the route.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-21)[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-22) The first five trainsets entered passenger service on August 28, 2025, and as of August 2026, 16 of the total order of 28 trainsets have entered service.[&#8202;](#ref-back-railfan-aug28-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-railfan-aug28)[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-24)

## History

### Background

Following the success of Japan's newly inaugurated [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen "Shinkansen") network in the 1960s, the [High Speed Ground Transportation Act of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Speed_Ground_Transportation_Act_of_1965 "High Speed Ground Transportation Act of 1965") authorized the U.S. government to explore the creation of high-speed rail, which resulted in the introduction of the higher-speed _[Metroliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metroliner_(train) "Metroliner (train)")_ trains between [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), and [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") in 1969, the predecessor to _Acela_. During the 1980s, the U.S. [Federal Railroad Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Railroad_Administration "Federal Railroad Administration") explored the possibilities of [high-speed rail in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_the_United_States "High-speed rail in the United States"). On December 18, 1991, five potential high speed rail corridors were authorized, including the Northeast Corridor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-25)

In the early 1990s, Amtrak tested several different high-speed trains from Europe on the Northeast Corridor. An [X 2000 train was leased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_2000#Exports "X 2000") from Sweden for test runs from October 1992 to January 1993, followed by revenue service between Washington, D.C., and New York City from February to May and August to September 1993. [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens "Siemens") showed the [ICE 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE_1 "ICE 1") train from Germany, organizing the [ICE Train North America Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE_1#ICE_train_North_America_tour "ICE 1") which started to operate on the Northeast Corridor on July 3, 1993.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-26)

### Building and development

With the testing of the trains from Europe complete, Amtrak was able to define a set of specifications for high-speed equipment and in October 1994, Amtrak requested bids from train manufacturers for a trainset that could reach 150 miles per hour (240km/h).[&#8202;](#ref-back-bedevils-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-bedevils) A consortium of [Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Transportation "Bombardier Transportation") (75%) and GEC Alsthom (now [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom "Alstom")) (25%) was selected in March 1996.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bedevils-1)[\[26\]](#refnote-bedevils)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Acela_Express_testing_at_TTCI%2C_June_2000.jpg/500px-Acela_Express_testing_at_TTCI%2C_June_2000.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Acela_Express_testing_at_TTCI,_June_2000.jpg) _Acela Express_ trainset undergoing testing at [TTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Technology_Center "Transportation Technology Center") in 2000

In 1999, Amtrak unveiled its plan for the _Acela Express,_ a high-speed train on the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor "Northeast Corridor") between Washington, D.C., and Boston.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-28)[&#8202;](#ref-back-overview-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-overview) Several changes were made to the corridor to make it suitable for higher-speed electric trains. The [Northend Electrification Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak's_60_Hz_traction_power_system "Amtrak's 60 Hz traction power system") extended existing electrification from [New Haven, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut "New Haven, Connecticut"), to Boston to complete the [overhead power supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_electrification_system "Railway electrification system") along the 454-mile (731km) route, and several grade crossings were improved or removed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-overview-1)[\[28\]](#refnote-overview)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-31) Prior to 2000, all trains bound for Boston had to switch to diesel power at New Haven.

A pilot trainset was completed by early 2000 and sent to [Transportation Technology Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Technology_Center "Transportation Technology Center") (TTC) for testing in June 2000. An inaugural VIP run of the _Acela_ occurred on November 16, 2000,[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-32) followed by the first revenue run on December 11, 2000, a few months after the intended date.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-33)

### Cost

Amtrak's original contract with the Bombardier-Alstom consortium was for the delivery of 20 trainsets (six coaches each, with power cars at front and rear) for $800 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-34) By 2004, Amtrak had settled contract disputes with the consortium, paying a total of $1.2 billion for the 20 trainsets plus 15 extra high-speed locomotives and the construction of maintenance facilities in Boston, New York, and Washington.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-35)

### Impact of the _Acela_

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Joe_Biden_and_Arlen_Specter.jpg/500px-Joe_Biden_and_Arlen_Specter.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Joe_Biden_and_Arlen_Specter.jpg) Vice President [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") and Senator [Arlen Specter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlen_Specter "Arlen Specter") riding the _Acela Express_ to Philadelphia in February 2009

By 2005, Amtrak's share of the common-carrier market between New York and Boston had reached 40%, from 18% pre- _Acela_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-black-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-black) With the increasing popularity of the faster, modern _Acela Express_, _Metroliner_ service was phased out in late 2006.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-37)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-38) To meet the demand, more _Acela_ services were added in September 2005.[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-39) By August 2008 crowding had become noticeable.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-40)

By 2011, the _Acela_ fleet had reached half of its designed service life. Amtrak proposed several replacement options, including one as part of its _A Vision for High-Speed Rail in the Northeast Corridor_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2012update-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-2012update) In 2011, Amtrak announced that forty new _Acela_ coaches would be ordered in 2012 to increase capacity on existing trainsets. The existing trains would have received two more coaches, lengthening the trainsets from a 1-6-1 configuration to 1-8-1 (power car—passenger cars—power car). The longer trainsets would have required the modifications of the _Acela_ maintenance facilities in Boston, New York and Washington. The first of the stretched trainsets was to have entered service in [fiscal year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year "Fiscal year") 2014.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-42) This plan was cancelled in 2012 in favor of replacing, rather than refurbishing, the _Acela_ fleet.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-43)

In January 2014, Amtrak issued a request for proposals on 28 or more new model _Acela_ trainsets, in a combined order with the [California High-Speed Rail Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_High-Speed_Rail_Authority "California High-Speed Rail Authority").[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-44) After discussions with manufacturers, Amtrak and the California High Speed Rail Authority concluded their needs were too disparate for common rolling stock and decided not to pursue the joint option.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-45)

### Fleet replacement

After the joint high-speed trainset procurement with California collapsed in 2014, Amtrak proceeded on its own. On August 26, 2016, then–Vice President [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") announced a $2.45billion federal loan to finance new equipment and Northeast Corridor upgrades, and Amtrak selected [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom "Alstom")'s [Avelia Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avelia_Liberty "Avelia Liberty") design, marketed as the NextGen Acela, ordering 28 trainsets to replace the 20 first-generation sets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rwage-award-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-rwage-award)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nextgen2016-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-nextgen2016) The larger fleet was intended to allow hourly New York–Boston service all day and half-hourly New York–Washington service at peak hours, with assembly taking place at Alstom's plants in [Hornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornell,_New_York "Hornell, New York") and [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York "Rochester, New York").[&#8202;](#ref-back-nextgen2016-1)[\[46\]](#refnote-nextgen2016)

Body construction began at Hornell in October 2017, when Alstom also unveiled the trainset's livery, and a prototype subsequently entered testing at the [Transportation Technology Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Technology_Center "Transportation Technology Center") in Pueblo, Colorado, and on the Northeast Corridor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-livery-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-alstom-livery)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rgi-emerges-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-rgi-emerges) The trainsets were initially expected to begin revenue service in 2021, with the entire fleet delivered the following year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rwage-award-1)[\[45\]](#refnote-rwage-award)

The program ultimately ran more than three years behind schedule. Amtrak and Alstom attributed much of the slippage to unforeseen difficulty completing the computer-simulation modeling required by the [Federal Railroad Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Railroad_Administration "Federal Railroad Administration") (FRA) and to compatibility problems between the European-derived design and the corridor's curve-heavy, century-old infrastructure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rwage-delay2023-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-rwage-delay2023) An October 2023 audit by the [Amtrak Office of Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Office_of_Inspector_General?action=edit&redlink=1 "Amtrak Office of Inspector General") found the program over budget and reported that each of the 12 trainsets built so far had defects—among them hydraulic leaks in the tilt system, corrosion caused by water pooling between cars, and five windows that had shattered spontaneously—and that Alstom had begun building trains before validating the crash-simulation model needed for FRA certification.[&#8202;](#ref-back-oig2023-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-oig2023)[&#8202;](#ref-back-inquirer-oig-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-inquirer-oig)[&#8202;](#ref-back-thedrive-oig-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-thedrive-oig) With the new fleet delayed, the first-generation sets were kept running well beyond their planned retirement, and Amtrak began removing several from service to harvest parts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-railfan-oig-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-railfan-oig)

On January 13, 2024, after the simulation model finally passed, the FRA cleared the _Avelia Liberty_ for on-track testing between Washington and Boston.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fra-ontrack2024-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-fra-ontrack2024) Qualification testing was completed and a spring 2025 launch was targeted; after that date passed, Amtrak revised its estimate to "coming soon".[&#8202;](#ref-back-amtraknewera-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-amtraknewera) On August 7, 2025, Amtrak announced that the trains would debut on August 28, and the first five trainsets entered revenue service that day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-57)[&#8202;](#ref-back-railfan-aug28-1)[\[22\]](#refnote-railfan-aug28)

### Legacy and influence

Since its introduction, the Acela has become a symbol of modern passenger rail in the United States and has influenced subsequent high-speed rail planning efforts across the country. The Train's operational success demonstrated consistent public demand for faster, business-oriented intercity travel, reinforcing federal and state interest in high-speed rail corridors such as [California High-Speed Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_High-Speed_Rail "California High-Speed Rail") and [Brightline West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightline_West "Brightline West"). Amtrak's experience with Acela's maintenance and ridership patterns has also informed the design of future services and infrastructure standards along the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor "Northeast Corridor") and beyond.[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-58)

### Branding

Before the introduction of the Acela, several classes of trains operated on the Northeast Corridor: the express _Metroliners_, the Philadelphia-New York _[Clockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clocker_(train) "Clocker (train)")_, _[Empire Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Service_(train) "Empire Service (train)")_ trains between New York City and [Niagara Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Falls,_New_York "Niagara Falls, New York") via the [Empire Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Corridor "Empire Corridor"), _[Keystone Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Service "Keystone Service")_ trains between New York City and to [Harrisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg,_Pennsylvania "Harrisburg, Pennsylvania") via the [Keystone Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Corridor "Keystone Corridor"), and the [umbrella term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_term "Umbrella term") _[NortheastDirect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NortheastDirect "NortheastDirect")_, applied to other trains on the corridor (in addition to unique names assigned to many departures).

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Acela_Regional_in_Boston_South_Station.jpg/500px-Acela_Regional_in_Boston_South_Station.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Acela_Regional_in_Boston_South_Station.jpg) An _Acela Regional_ train at [South Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Station "South Station"), Boston, in 2002

Amtrak announced the _Acela_ name in March 1999 as a part of the original announcement of the service itself.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-59)[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-60)
The branding team based the name " _Acela_" on the concepts of " [acceleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acceleration "Acceleration")" and "excellence".[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-61)[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-62) At the same time, Amtrak launched what it called the Capstone Program, a short-lived plan to [rebrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebranding "Rebranding") the [_NortheastDirect_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional "Northeast Regional"), _Keystone Service_ and _[Empire Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Service "Empire Service")_ trains as _Acela Regional_ and the [_Clocker_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clocker_(train) "Clocker (train)") trains as _Acela Commuter_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Acela-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-Acela)[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-64)[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-65)

The _Acela Regional_ name was first applied to _NortheastDirect_ trains 130–133 in 2000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-66) Those trains became the first electrified trains to run on the full Northeast Corridor between Washington, D.C. and Boston.[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-67) As more trains were electrified, they too were rebranded.

Following widespread rider confusion in distinguishing the three services, Amtrak removed the branding from the lower-speed _Acela Regional_ and _Acela Commuter_ trains in 2003.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-68)[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-69)

In 2019, Amtrak shortened the name of the service from _Acela Express_ to simply _Acela_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-70)

## Rolling stock

### First-generation trainsets

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Acela_Express_Rahway_%28cropped%29.jpg/500px-Acela_Express_Rahway_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Acela_Express_Rahway_(cropped).jpg) First-generation _Acela Express_ trainset in New Jersey, May2021

Main article: [Acela Express (trainset)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acela_Express_(trainset) "Acela Express (trainset)")

The first-generation _Acela_ trainset is a unique set of vehicles designed specifically to satisfy governmental rolling stock requirements established primarily by the Federal Railroad Administration (FRA). This includes the ability to withstand a collision with a freight train at speed without collapsing. Most manufacturers that bid on the _Acela_ were unable to meet the structural requirements, due to increased costs and complications for the manufacture of the trains, and the need for manufacturers to make significant engineering changes to their standard designs. In the end, only three qualified bidders remained: [ABB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABB "ABB") (Swedish-Swiss manufacturer of the [X 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_2000 "X 2000") train), Siemens (manufacturer of the German [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity-Express "Intercity-Express")), and a consortium of Bombardier (manufacturer of the LRC trains) and Alstom (manufacturer of the French [TGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGV "TGV")).[&#8202;](#ref-back-black-1)[\[35\]](#refnote-black)

The design, using identical 6,200 horsepower (4,600kW) [power cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_car "Power car") at each end which operate on voltages of 12 kV, 12.5 kV, and 25 kV [AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current "Alternating current"), and either 25 or 60 [Hz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") frequency, derives several components from the TGV,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Perren,_Brian_p156-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-Perren,_Brian_p156) such as the third-generation TGV's traction system (including the four asynchronous AC motors per power car, rectifiers, inverters, and regenerative braking), the trucks/bogies structure (a long-wheelbase dual transom H frame welded steel with outboard mounted tapered roller bearings), the brake discs (although there are only three per axle, versus four on the TGV), and crash energy management techniques to control structural deformation in the event of an accident.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TGVweb-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-TGVweb)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Perren,_Brian_p156-1)[\[70\]](#refnote-Perren,_Brian_p156)

The tilting carriages are based upon Bombardier's earlier [LRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LRC_(train) "LRC (train)") trains used on [Via Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Rail "Via Rail") rather than the TGV's non-tilting articulated trailers. _Acela_ power cars and passenger cars are much heavier than those of the TGV in order to meet the FRA's crash standards.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-73) French and Canadian crews testing the _Acela_ referred to it as "the pig" due to its weight.[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-74)[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-75) The extra weight leads to the _Acela_'s power-to-weight ratio being about 22.4hp per tonne, compared to 30.8hp for a [SNCF TGV Reseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNCF_TGV_R%C3%A9seau "SNCF TGV Réseau") trainset.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Perren,_Brian_p156-2)[\[70\]](#refnote-Perren,_Brian_p156) The Tier II crash standards, adopted in 1999, have also resulted in the passenger cars being designed without steps and trapdoors, which means that the trainsets can only serve lines with [high-level platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_platform_height "Railway platform height") such as the Northeast Corridor. _Acela_ trains are semi-permanently coupled (but not articulated as in the TGV) and are referred to as trainsets. Bombardier later used the _Acela_ carriage design and a [diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine "Diesel engine")/ [gas turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine "Gas turbine") variant of the power car for its experimental [JetTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetTrain "JetTrain").[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-76)

### Second-generation trainsets

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Amtrak_Avelia_Liberty_on_its_inaugural_revenue_run_%28cropped%29.jpg/500px-Amtrak_Avelia_Liberty_on_its_inaugural_revenue_run_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Amtrak_Avelia_Liberty_on_its_inaugural_revenue_run_(cropped).jpg) Second-generation _Avelia Liberty_ trainset passes through [Edison station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_station "Edison station") in New Jersey on August 28, 2025, the first day of service

Main article: [Avelia Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avelia_Liberty "Avelia Liberty")

The second-generation _Acela_ equipment is the [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom "Alstom") [Avelia Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avelia_Liberty "Avelia Liberty"), marketed by Amtrak as the NextGen Acela and part of the company's Avelia family of high-speed trains, developed specifically for the North American market. Its passenger cars derive from the [AGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotrice_%C3%A0_grande_vitesse "Automotrice à grande vitesse") used in Italy, the concentrated [power cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_car "Power car") share their design with the [Avelia Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avelia_Horizon "Avelia Horizon") developed for the French [TGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGV "TGV") network, and the tilt system is adapted from the [Pendolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendolino "Pendolino").[&#8202;](#ref-back-pr-avelia-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-pr-avelia)[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-debut-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-alstom-debut) As with the first generation, the design was adapted to satisfy FRA crashworthiness requirements, in this case through Alstom's crash-energy-management system rather than the heavier conventional structures used on the original _Acela_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-livery-1)[\[47\]](#refnote-alstom-livery)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rgi-emerges-1)[\[48\]](#refnote-rgi-emerges)

Each trainset is formed of two short-wheelbase power cars bracketing nine articulated passenger cars, with capacity to insert three additional cars if demand grows.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rgi-emerges-2)[\[48\]](#refnote-rgi-emerges)[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-livery-2)[\[47\]](#refnote-alstom-livery) Unlike the original _Acela_, whose cars ride on their own [bogies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogie "Bogie") and are only semi-permanently coupled, the _Avelia Liberty_ is articulated: the wheel-and-axle frames sit beneath the gangways between adjacent cars, forming a single connected trainset. Placing the bogies between the cars and away from the seating areas reduces noise and vibration.[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-debut-1)[\[77\]](#refnote-alstom-debut)[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-solutions-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-alstom-solutions) Each set carries 386 passengers—378 seats plus eight wheelchair spaces—roughly 25% more than the 304-seat first-generation set.[&#8202;](#ref-back-First_Impressions-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-First_Impressions)

The _Avelia Liberty_ uses Alstom's Tiltronix anticipative tilt system, which leans the cars by up to 6.3°—compared with 4.2° on the first generation—to permit roughly 30% higher speeds through curves.[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-debut-2)[\[77\]](#refnote-alstom-debut)[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-presskit-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-alstom-presskit) The two power cars provide a combined output of about 7,000kW (9,400hp), and the trainsets are designed for 220mph (350km/h) with tilt disabled and 186mph (299km/h) in tilting service, though they are limited to 160mph (260km/h) by current Northeast Corridor track and signaling.[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-presskit-1)[\[80\]](#refnote-alstom-presskit)[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-livery-3)[\[47\]](#refnote-alstom-livery) Alstom describes the design as lighter and more energy-efficient than the equipment it replaces, recovering energy through regenerative braking.[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-solutions-1)[\[78\]](#refnote-alstom-solutions)

#### Operating speeds

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Acela_Express_and_Metro-North_railcar.jpg/500px-Acela_Express_and_Metro-North_railcar.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Acela_Express_and_Metro-North_railcar.jpg) An _Acela Express_ train passes a [Metro-North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad "Metro-North Railroad") [New Haven Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven_Line "New Haven Line") train in southwestern [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut "Connecticut")

Although the first-generation Acela Express trainsets were designed with a top speed of 165mph (266km/h) and the second-generation Avelia Liberty trainsets are designed to reach 220mph (354km/h), the existing infrastructure of the Northeast Corridor significantly limits speeds.[&#8202;](#ref-back-alstom-presskit-2)[\[80\]](#refnote-alstom-presskit)

The maximum speed limit on the Northeast Corridor is 160mph (260km/h) on 40 miles (64km) of the 457-mile (735km) route, in four sections of track in Rhode Island, Massachusetts,[&#8202;](#ref-back-amtrakETT-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-amtrakETT) and New Jersey. The _Acela_ achieves an average speed (including stops) of 82mph (132km/h) between Washington and New York,[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyp-was-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-nyp-was)[&#8202;](#ref-back-note1-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-note1) and an average speed of 66mph (106km/h) from New York to Boston.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bos-was-3)[\[2\]](#refnote-bos-was)[&#8202;](#ref-back-note2-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-note2) The average speed over the entire route is 70.3mph (113km/h).[&#8202;](#ref-back-bos-was-4)[\[2\]](#refnote-bos-was)[&#8202;](#ref-back-note3-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-note3)

Speeds are limited by the route the corridor takes through urban areas, and there are several speed restrictions below 60–80mph (97–129km/h) over bridges or through tunnels that are over a century old. Altogether, Amtrak has identified 224 bridges along _Acela_'s route that are beyond their design life.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-82)

South of the Delaware River, the _Acela_'s top speed is 135mph (217km/h).[&#8202;](#ref-back-amtrakETT-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-amtrakETT) One limiting factor is the [overhead catenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_line "Overhead line") support system which was constructed before 1935 and lacks the constant-tension features of the new catenary east of New Haven.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2012update-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-2012update) The [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad "Pennsylvania Railroad") ran _Metroliner_ test trains in the late 1960s as fast as 164mph (264km/h) and briefly intended to run the _Metroliner_ service at speeds reaching 150mph (241km/h). Certification testing for commercial operation at 160mph (257km/h) involving test runs at up to 165mph (266km/h) began between Trenton and New Brunswick in September 2012.[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-83) Passenger operation at 150mph (241km/h) began in this region in late May 2022.

The fastest schedule between New York and Washington, DC, was 2hours, 43minutes in 2012. $450 million was allotted by President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama")'s administration to replace catenary and upgrade signals[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-84) between [Trenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenton,_New_Jersey "Trenton, New Jersey") and [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick,_New_Jersey "New Brunswick, New Jersey"), which will allow speeds of 160mph (257km/h) over a 23mi (37km) stretch. The improvements were scheduled to be completed in 2016, but have been delayed; the project was partially completed in late May 2022, with the remainder projected in 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-85) This section of track holds the record for the highest speed by a train in the US, which is 170.8mph (274.9km/h), achieved in a test run by the U.S./Canada-built [UAC TurboTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAC_TurboTrain "UAC TurboTrain") on December 20, 1967.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2012update-2)[\[40\]](#refnote-2012update)[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-86)

North of New York City, [Amtrak upgraded the track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak's_60_Hz_traction_power_system "Amtrak's 60 Hz traction power system") along the Connecticut shoreline east of New Haven to allow maximum speeds in excess of 110mph (177km/h), in preparation for the _Acela_ launch.[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-87) Although this area contains the fastest current operating speeds (150mph (241km/h)), it also has the slowest section of the NEC: between [New Rochelle, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York "New Rochelle, New York"), and New Haven, Connecticut. This section is owned by [Metro-North Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad "Metro-North Railroad") and the [Connecticut Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Department_of_Transportation "Connecticut Department of Transportation") and is heavily used by commuter trains which limit the speed of the _Acela_. Amtrak's trains achieve 90mph (145km/h) only on a limited 4mi (6.4km) stretch in New York State and rarely exceed 60mph (97km/h) at any time eastbound through Connecticut until reaching New Haven.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-88) In 1992, ConnDOT began plans to upgrade the catenary system and replace outdated bridges on the New Haven Line to enable the _Acela_ to run slightly faster. As of May2017[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Acela&action=edit) the catenary replacement and bridge work were under way and expected to be completed by mid-2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-89)\[ _[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\]

On July 9, 2007, Amtrak introduced a limited-stop round trip, with trains stopping only at Philadelphia between New York and Washington. This shortened the trip between the two cities to 2hours 35minutes, making the trip roughly an hour faster than some of the _Northeast Regional_ train services. These trains were an experiment to find ways to expedite travel time on the _Acela_; Amtrak has since dropped them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-90)

### High speed infrastructure

The dense population of the northeastern United States makes the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor "Northeast Corridor") the most heavily traveled portion of the American passenger rail system. [Two-thirds of rail passengers in the United States live in or near New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_New_York_City "Transportation in New York City"), also home to the nation's busiest passenger rail station, [Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_(New_York_City) "Pennsylvania Station (New York City)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-91) In order to compete with [airliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airliner "Airliner"), Amtrak needed to increase the speed of trains in the region. The former [Shore Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore_Line_Railway_(Connecticut) "Shore Line Railway (Connecticut)") from New Haven to Boston is burdened by sharp turns and [grade crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_crossing "Level crossing"), the crossings being of special concern.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-92)

Tilting enables passengers to ride more comfortably on curved sections of track faster than would otherwise be possible, by leaning into the bend. _Acela_ trainsets use active tilting above 60mph (97km/h) on most of the system, but some segments of track in the Northeast Corridor are too close together for the cars to safely tilt while maintaining FRA minimum space between trains on parallel tracks. Metro-North Railroad restricts tilting on the segment of track north of New York which it owns. The system was originally designed for a 6.8° tilt, but the cars were redesigned 4in (102mm) wider to accommodate wider seats and aisles that reduced allowable tilt to 4.2° to fit within the clearance constraints of the existing tracks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TGVweb-1)[\[71\]](#refnote-TGVweb) Traveling at higher than 135mph (217km/h) also requires constant-tension catenary, which is only implemented on the more modern catenary system north of New York City. South of New York City, the trains are restricted to 135mph (217km/h). By comparison, the _Northeast Regional_ and the now-defunct _Metroliner_ service reached 125mph (201km/h).

_Acela_ service was originally expected to begin in late 1999 but was delayed. The catenary system could not support the intended speeds between Washington DC and New York City, but the newer system between New York City and Boston allows the higher speeds. Attention was drawn to the decreased 4.2° tilt, but this was not the root of the speed problem, as the tracks from New York to Boston are similar to those between New York and Washington, and the tilt mechanism is not the factor enabling higher speeds.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bedevils-2)[\[26\]](#refnote-bedevils)[&#8202;](#ref-back-TGVweb-2)[\[71\]](#refnote-TGVweb) Following repairs, the first _Acela_ service began on December 11, 2000, a year behind schedule.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-93)

_Acela_ travels between Boston and New York in about three and a half hours (an improvement of half an hour); New York to Washington runs take a minimum of two hours and forty-five minutes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bos-was-5)[\[2\]](#refnote-bos-was) These schedules, as well as the relative convenience of direct downtown-to-downtown rail service as opposed to air travel, especially after the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"), have made the _Acela Express_ more competitive with the [air shuttles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_shuttle "Air shuttle"). Due to this competition, [Southwest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Airlines "Southwest Airlines") canceled service between Washington and New York.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-94)

#### Platform track speeds

Acela passing through a platform track at Kingston Station in Rhode Island at 150 mph.

Due to the high speed at which _Acela_ trains bypass platforms of local stations, concerns have mounted in some communities over inadequate warnings and safeguards for passengers waiting for other trains, including that the two-foot wide yellow [platform markings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_platform#Markings "Railway platform") may not keep people at a safe distance. At [Kingston station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_station_(Rhode_Island) "Kingston station (Rhode Island)") in Rhode Island and [Mansfield station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_station_(MBTA) "Mansfield station (MBTA)") in Massachusetts, _Acela_ trains pass by at 150mph (241km/h).[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-95)[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-96) Suggestions include platform [safety barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_barrier "Safety barrier"), or use of different announcements for approaching _Acela_ trains versus slower ones.[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-97)

### Outages

In August 2002, shortly after their introduction, _Acela_ trainsets were briefly removed from service when the brackets that connected truck ( [bogie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogie "Bogie")) dampers (shocks) to the powerunit carbodies ("yaw dampers") were found to be cracking.[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-98)[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-99) The _Acela_ returned to service when a program of frequent inspections was instituted. The damper brackets have since been redesigned and old brackets replaced by the newer design.

On April 15, 2005, the _Acela_ was removed from service when cracks were found in the [disc brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_brake "Disc brake") of many passenger coaches.[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-100) The Bombardier-Alstom consortium replaced the discs under warranty. Limited service resumed in July 2005, as a portion of the fleet operated with new brake discs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-101) _[Metroliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metroliner_(train) "Metroliner (train)")_ trains, which the _Acela Express_ was intended to replace, filled in during the outage. Amtrak announced on September 21, 2005, that all 20 trainsets had been returned to full operation.

In October 2012, _Acela_ service was cancelled immediately before, during, and after [Hurricane Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy "Hurricane Sandy"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-102) which damaged the [North River Tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_River_Tunnels "North River Tunnels") causing lasting delays and reliability problems.

In March 2020, all _Acela_ trips were suspended as part of a round of service reduction in response to the [COVID-19 pandemic in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States "COVID-19 pandemic in the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-covid-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-covid) Amtrak resumed _Acela_ service on June 1, 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-104)

### Composition

The _Acela Express_ trainset consists of two [power cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_car "Power car"), a [Café car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_car "Café car"), a [First Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_travel "First class travel") car, and four [Business Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_class "Business class") cars, [semi-permanently coupled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangway_connection "Gangway connection") together. It has fewer seats than its regional service counterparts. The First Class car has 44 seats, being three seats across (one on one side, two on the other side), four-seat tables and assigned seating. There are 260 Business Class seats on each trainset; these cars have four seats across (two on each side), four-seat tables, and assigned seating.[&#8202;](#ref-back-specs-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-specs) Baggage may be stowed in overhead compartments or underneath seats. Trains are wheelchair- [accessible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessibility "Accessibility"). Each car has one or two toilets, with one being ADA compliant.

### Operations and staffing

_Acela_ offers two classes of seating, Business Class and First Class. Unlike most other Amtrak trains, Business Class is the _de facto_ standard class on _Acela_ trains; there is no coach service.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-105)

_Acela_ maintenance is generally taken care of at the Ivy City facility in Washington, DC; [Sunnyside Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyside_Yard "Sunnyside Yard") in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens "Queens"), New York; or Southampton Street Yard in Boston.

The _Acela_ trainsets underwent minor refurbishments between mid-2009 and 2010 at Penn Coach Yard, next to 30th Street Station in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"), Pennsylvania. These refurbishments included new blue leather seats throughout the trainset.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Amtrak_Introduces_Blue,_if_Not_Corinthian,_Leather-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-Amtrak_Introduces_Blue,_if_Not_Corinthian,_Leather)

In May 2018, Amtrak announced a 14-month program to refresh the interiors of the _Acela_ trainsets, including new seat cushions and covers, new aisle carpeting, and a deep clean. This refurbishment program has been completed as of June 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-107)

#### Wi-Fi service

Wireless Internet station service began in 2004.[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-108) In 2010, with services provided by The GBS Group, all _Acela_ trains began offering "AmtrakConnect" supporting [IEEE 802.11a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_802.11 "IEEE 802.11")/b/g/n, 2.4GHz and 5GHz and standard [VPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_private_network "Virtual private network") connections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-109) In 2016, Amtrak upgraded to a faster wifi service.[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-110)

#### Staffing

Generally, Amtrak train crews consist of an engineer, a conductor, and at least one assistant conductor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-111) _Acela_ trains also have an On-Board Service crew consisting of two First Class attendants and a Café Car attendant. In addition to the food service provided in the Café Car, on most trains an attendant will also provide at-seat cart service, serving refreshments throughout the train. First Class passengers are served meals at their seats on all services.[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-112)

## Notable incidents

- During the [Northeast blackout of 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_blackout_of_2003 "Northeast blackout of 2003"), a northbound _Acela Express_ train was stuck on the [Hell Gate Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_Gate_Bridge "Hell Gate Bridge") for over nine hours, until a rescue engine from [Sunnyside Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyside_Yard "Sunnyside Yard") was able to tow the train back to [New York's Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_(New_York_City) "Pennsylvania Station (New York City)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-113-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-113)
- The first _Acela_ [grade crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_crossing "Level crossing") accident occurred on September 27, 2005, when a car rolled under closed crossing gate arms in [Waterford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterford,_Connecticut "Waterford, Connecticut"), and was struck by a train traveling at 70 miles per hour (110km/h), killing three automobile passengers. None of the 130 _Acela_ passengers were injured. The gates were found to have been functioning properly,[&#8202;](#ref-back-High-Tech_Gates_Fail_to_Avert_Car-Train_Crash-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-High-Tech_Gates_Fail_to_Avert_Car-Train_Crash)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Investigators_Seek_Answers_In_Fatal_Crash_That_Killed_Two;_Cause_of_Waterford_car-train_accident_may_never_be_known-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-Investigators_Seek_Answers_In_Fatal_Crash_That_Killed_Two;_Cause_of_Waterford_car-train_accident_may_never_be_known) but the incident drew much criticism regarding the eleven remaining grade crossings along Amtrak's busy Northeast Corridor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Family_sues_over_fatal_car_crash_on_railroad_tracks-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-Family_sues_over_fatal_car_crash_on_railroad_tracks)
- On March 24, 2017, an _Acela Express_ train derailed at low speed in [New York's Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_(New_York_City) "Pennsylvania Station (New York City)"), during morning rush hour. All 248 passengers were safely evacuated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Amtrak_Acela_derails_at_NY_Penn_Station,_some_service_disruptions-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-Amtrak_Acela_derails_at_NY_Penn_Station,_some_service_disruptions) The derailment was caused by a defective section of track, of which Amtrak was aware, but had not yet fixed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Amtrak_Knew_of_Flaw_That_Caused_Penn_Station_Derailment-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-Amtrak_Knew_of_Flaw_That_Caused_Penn_Station_Derailment)
- On February 6, 2018, _Acela Express_ train No. 2150 split apart between the first and second cars in the trainset, at 124mph (200km/h), near [Havre de Grace, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havre_de_Grace,_Maryland "Havre de Grace, Maryland"). There were no injuries of the crew nor the 52 passengers on board, who were transferred to _[Northeast Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional "Northeast Regional")_ train No. 180.[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-119)[&#8202;](#ref-back-120-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-120)
