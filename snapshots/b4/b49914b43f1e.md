From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"Churchill was a racist" redirects here. For the racial views held by Winston Churchill, see [Racial views of Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_views_of_Winston_Churchill "Racial views of Winston Churchill").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Edward_Colston_-_empty_pedestal.jpg/250px-Edward_Colston_-_empty_pedestal.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Edward_Colston_-_empty_pedestal.jpg) The empty plinth of the [statue of Edward Colston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Edward_Colston "Statue of Edward Colston") in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol")

A number of statues and memorials were the subject of protests and petitions during the [George Floyd protests in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests_in_the_United_Kingdom "George Floyd protests in the United Kingdom") in 2020.

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Actions_against_memorials_in_the_United_Kingdom_during_the_George_Floyd_protests&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

Further information: [George Floyd protests in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests_in_the_United_Kingdom "George Floyd protests in the United Kingdom")

For several years, a campaign entitled [Rhodes Must Fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Must_Fall "Rhodes Must Fall") had worked towards the removal of statues to [Cecil Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Rhodes "Cecil Rhodes"). A list of 60 statues, monuments and plaques considered by activists to "celebrate slavery and racism" was published online as an interactive map titled _Topple the Racists_ by the Stop Trump Coalition. In addition to Rhodes, historical figures listed included [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus"), Sir [Francis Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Drake "Francis Drake"), [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell "Oliver Cromwell"), King [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England "Charles II of England"), [Admiral Lord Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Nelson,_1st_Viscount_Nelson "Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson"), the prime ministers [Earl Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Grey,_2nd_Earl_Grey "Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey") and [William Ewart Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone "William Ewart Gladstone").[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)

## England

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Actions_against_memorials_in_the_United_Kingdom_during_the_George_Floyd_protests&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: England")\]

The [statue of Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Winston_Churchill,_Parliament_Square "Statue of Winston Churchill, Parliament Square") in [Parliament Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_Square "Parliament Square"), London, had graffiti sprayed on it over two successive days, including the phrase "Churchill was a racist",[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) alluding to [his much disputed views on race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_views_of_Winston_Churchill "Racial views of Winston Churchill").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-:0) The [memorial to Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_to_Queen_Victoria,_Leeds "Memorial to Queen Victoria, Leeds") in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds "Leeds") was also vandalised.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) On 5 June, a group of protesters sprayed the abbreviation " [ACAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.C.A.B. "A.C.A.B.")", meaning _All Cops Are Bastards_, on the [memorial to Earl Haig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Haig_Memorial "Earl Haig Memorial") in [Whitehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehall "Whitehall"), London; when soldiers from the [Household Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_Cavalry "Household Cavalry") in plain clothes scrubbed the graffiti off, protesters shouted abuse at them for doing so.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)

The [statue of Edward Colston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Edward_Colston "Statue of Edward Colston") in [The Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Centre,_Bristol "The Centre, Bristol"), [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol"), was toppled and thrown into [Bristol Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Harbour "Bristol Harbour") on 7 June.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) However, in the [subsequent criminal proceedings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_v_Rhian_Graham,_Milo_Ponsford,_Jake_Skuse_and_Sage_Willoughby "R v Rhian Graham, Milo Ponsford, Jake Skuse and Sage Willoughby") four people were charged with [criminal damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_damage "Criminal damage") but [acquitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquitted "Acquitted") by a jury after a trial.

On the same day, a protester climbed onto the [Cenotaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cenotaph "The Cenotaph") in London and unsuccessfully attempted to set fire to the [Union Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Flag "Union Flag").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

A sculpted head of a black man was removed from the 18th-century [inn sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inn_sign "Inn sign") of the [Green Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Man,_Ashbourne "Green Man, Ashbourne") in [Ashbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashbourne,_Derbyshire "Ashbourne, Derbyshire"), [Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire "Derbyshire"). The act was performed by residents of the town, who said that they had done so in order to protect it from vandalism. The sculpture was later returned to the local council, its legal owner.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

In [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford "Oxford"), a crowd of protesters gathered outside [Oriel College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford "Oriel College, Oxford"), demanding that its statue of [Cecil Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Rhodes "Cecil Rhodes") [be removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Must_Fall "Rhodes Must Fall").[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Statue_of_Robert_Milligan%2C_West_India_Quay_on_9_June_2020_-_statue_covered_and_with_Black_Lives_Matter_sign_03.jpg/250px-Statue_of_Robert_Milligan%2C_West_India_Quay_on_9_June_2020_-_statue_covered_and_with_Black_Lives_Matter_sign_03.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Statue_of_Robert_Milligan,_West_India_Quay_on_9_June_2020_-_statue_covered_and_with_Black_Lives_Matter_sign_03.jpg) The [statue of Robert Milligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Robert_Milligan "Statue of Robert Milligan") on 9 June 2020, the day of its removal

On 9 June the [Mayor of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_London "Mayor of London"), [Sadiq Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_Khan "Sadiq Khan"), announced the formation of the [Commission for Diversity in the Public Realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_for_Diversity_in_the_Public_Realm "Commission for Diversity in the Public Realm"), which would conduct a review of the capital's public landmarks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) The [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") politician [Lord Adonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Adonis "Andrew Adonis") asked the Government to begin a public consultation on the [statue of Robert Clive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Robert_Clive,_London "Statue of Robert Clive, London") outside the [Foreign and Commonwealth Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Office "Foreign and Commonwealth Office").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Evening_Standard_2020-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-Evening_Standard_2020) That evening the [statue of Robert Milligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Robert_Milligan "Statue of Robert Milligan"), a merchant and slave trader, outside the [Museum of London Docklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_London_Docklands "Museum of London Docklands") was removed by the local authority and the [Canal & River Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_&_River_Trust "Canal & River Trust").[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

On 11 June the [Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy's_and_St_Thomas'_NHS_Foundation_Trust "Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust"), the Guy's and St Thomas' Charity and [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London "King's College London") issued a joint statement announcing that the [statue of Robert Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Robert_Clayton "Statue of Robert Clayton") at [St Thomas' Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Thomas'_Hospital "St Thomas' Hospital") and [that of Thomas Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Thomas_Guy "Statue of Thomas Guy") at [the hospital named after him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy's_Hospital "Guy's Hospital") would be removed from public view.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Dunham_Massey_2016_058.jpg/250px-Dunham_Massey_2016_058.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dunham_Massey_2016_058.jpg) The ‘kneeling slave’ or 'blackamoor' holding a sundial on his head outside [Dunham Massey Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunham_Massey_Hall "Dunham Massey Hall"), which was moved to storage.

In June 2020, the [National Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust "National Trust") removed the Grade II-listed, eighteenth-century statue [Dunham Massey Hall sundial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunham_Massey_Hall_sundial "Dunham Massey Hall sundial") from the forecourt of [Dunham Massey Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunham_Massey_Hall "Dunham Massey Hall") in [Greater Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester "Greater Manchester").[&#8202;](#ref-back-telegraph-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-telegraph)[&#8202;](#ref-back-altrincham2020206-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-altrincham2020206)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) It depicted a kneeling [blackamoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackamoor_(decorative_arts) "Blackamoor (decorative arts)") carrying the sundial above his head, a depiction seen as degrading, being categorised as a 'kneeling slave'. The statue was set up as one monument to honour the [1st Earl of Warrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Booth,_1st_Earl_of_Warrington "Henry Booth, 1st Earl of Warrington") by his son, the [second Earl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Booth,_2nd_Earl_of_Warrington "George Booth, 2nd Earl of Warrington") in c. 1735 and cast after a model by [John Nost I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nost "John Nost") for [William III of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_III_of_England "William III of England")’s Privy Garden at [Hampton Court Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Court_Palace "Hampton Court Palace").[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)

A [statue of Robert Baden-Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Robert_Baden-Powell,_Poole "Statue of Robert Baden-Powell, Poole") in [Poole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poole "Poole"), Dorset, was slated for temporary removal after criticism over events during his army career and his comments of support concerning [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") and _[Mein Kampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mein_Kampf "Mein Kampf")_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23) but initial attempts to remove it faced technical difficulties and local people later prevented council workers from removing it.[&#8202;](#ref-back-morris-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-morris)

The Shadow Foreign Secretary [Lisa Nandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Nandy "Lisa Nandy") raised concerns about the [Empire murals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Murals "Empire Murals") (1914–1921) by [Sigismund Goetze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund_Goetze "Sigismund Goetze") in the Foreign and Commonwealth Office in a letter to the Foreign Secretary, [Dominic Raab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Raab "Dominic Raab").[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Cannizaro_Park%2C_Wimbledon%2C_The_statue_of_Emperor_Haile_Selassie.jpg/250px-Cannizaro_Park%2C_Wimbledon%2C_The_statue_of_Emperor_Haile_Selassie.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cannizaro_Park,_Wimbledon,_The_statue_of_Emperor_Haile_Selassie.jpg) The [bust of Haile Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bust_of_Haile_Selassie "Bust of Haile Selassie") in Wimbledon, photographed in 2019

Following the toppling of the statue of [Ras Makonnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Makonnen "Ras Makonnen"), a functionary of the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire "Ethiopian Empire"), in [Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar "Harar"), [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia "Ethiopia"), [a bust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bust_of_Haile_Selassie "Bust of Haile Selassie") of his son [Haile Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie "Haile Selassie") was toppled in [Cannizaro Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannizaro_Park "Cannizaro Park"), [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon,_London "Wimbledon, London"), South West London. Demonstrations had spread across Ethiopia following the murder of [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_people "Oromo people") singer and activist [Hachalu Hundessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachalu_Hundessa "Hachalu Hundessa") on 29 June 2020. The bust in London was destroyed by Oromo activists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC_2_July-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-BBC_2_July)

The statue of [Robert Clive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Clive "Robert Clive") in [Shrewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury "Shrewsbury") was the subject of two petitions in favour of its removal and one against; the first two combined received about three times the number of signatures than the third. [Shropshire Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire_Council "Shropshire Council") voted 28–17 against taking any action to remove the statue.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

## Scotland

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Actions_against_memorials_in_the_United_Kingdom_during_the_George_Floyd_protests&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Scotland")\]

There were protests against the [Melville Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melville_Monument,_Edinburgh "Melville Monument, Edinburgh") in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh") due to the disputed belief that [Henry Dundas, 1st Viscount Melville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dundas,_1st_Viscount_Melville "Henry Dundas, 1st Viscount Melville"), whom it commemorates, led to a delay in the abolition of slavery, and due to his long association with the slave trade. Graffiti was sprayed onto the monument and calls were made for it to be taken down.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28)[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)

In a number of streets in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow "Glasgow"), activists placed new name placards under the names of streets named after individuals with connections to the slave trade. Among these is [Buchanan Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchanan_Street "Buchanan Street"), named after [Andrew Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Buchanan_of_Drumpellier "Andrew Buchanan of Drumpellier"), who owned plantations in Virginia, which protesters renamed George Floyd Street.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

The [equestrian statue of Robert the Bruce, Bannockburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_statue_of_Robert_the_Bruce,_Bannockburn "Equestrian statue of Robert the Bruce, Bannockburn"), was defaced with graffiti on 12 June.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)

## Wales

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Actions_against_memorials_in_the_United_Kingdom_during_the_George_Floyd_protests&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Wales")\]

[Rhondda Cynon Taf County Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_Cynon_Taf_County_Borough_Council "Rhondda Cynon Taf County Borough Council") announced "an urgent review of all local authority-owned sites and buildings in the county borough to determine what statues, busts, plaques and memorials are present at these locations. If it is the case that any of these may be deemed inappropriate then we will be requesting officers to ensure that they are removed from those particular locations".[&#8202;](#ref-back-WalesOnlineReviewJun20-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-WalesOnlineReviewJun20)

A statue of [Thomas Picton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Picton "Thomas Picton") is part of a display of statues named "Heroes of Wales" in [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff "Cardiff")'s [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Hall,_Cardiff "City Hall, Cardiff"), unveiled in 1916. In June 2020 the [Lord Mayor of Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mayor_of_Cardiff "Lord Mayor of Cardiff"), Dan De'Ath, and the leader of [Cardiff Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_Council "Cardiff Council"), Huw Thomas, supported calls to remove the statue due to Picton's treatment of slaves.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WalesOnlinePictJun20-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-WalesOnlinePictJun20) A campaign to remove [Picton's monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmarthen#Picton's_monument "Carmarthen") in Carmarthen also arose.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WalesJun20-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-WalesJun20)

A plaque in Brecon dedicated to Thomas Phillips, the captain of the slave ship _[Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal_(slave_ship) "Hannibal (slave ship)")_, was removed by an unknown person. Brecon town council said in a statement that it would "in consultation with the local community and interested parties will take time to consider what, if anything, should take its place". The plaque had been "under review" by the council before its disappearance but no decision had been made on its future.[&#8202;](#ref-back-GuardJun20-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-GuardJun20)
