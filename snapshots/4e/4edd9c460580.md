Gretchen Whitmer kidnapping plot

On October 8, 2020, the U.S. [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") (FBI) announced the arrests of 13 men suspected of orchestrating a [domestic terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism_in_the_United_States "Domestic terrorism in the United States") plot to [kidnap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_in_the_United_States "Kidnapping in the United States") [Gretchen Whitmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretchen_Whitmer "Gretchen Whitmer"), the [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Michigan "List of governors of Michigan") of [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan"), and otherwise [using violence to overthrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_d'%C3%A9tat "Coup d'état") the [state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Michigan "Government of Michigan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-DFP-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-DFP)[&#8202;](#ref-back-WSOCTV-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-WSOCTV)[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nytimes-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-nytimes) Some have labeled the attempt as an example of [stochastic terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_terrorism "Stochastic terrorism"), where violent rhetoric by prominent figures inspired the plot.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sci-am-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-sci-am)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Linguistic-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-Linguistic) Half of the suspects were tied to a [paramilitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramilitary "Paramilitary") militia group that called themselves the Wolverine Watchmen.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Garrison_2021-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-Garrison_2021) Six of the suspects were charged in [federal court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_crime_in_the_United_States "Federal crime in the United States"), while the other seven were charged with state crimes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nytimes-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-nytimes) A week later, a fourteenth suspect was arrested and charged in state court.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14th-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-14th)

Two men [pleaded guilty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plea_in_the_United_States "Plea in the United States") and offered to testify on behalf of the prosecution as part of a favorable [plea deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plea_deal "Plea deal"), while two men were [acquitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquittal_in_the_United_States "Acquittal in the United States") at trial.[&#8202;](#ref-back-plea-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-plea)[&#8202;](#ref-back-notguilty-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-notguilty)[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) Two other men, who had received a [hung jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hung_jury "Hung jury") at the first trial, were later [convicted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convicted "Convicted") of kidnapping conspiracy and conspiracy to possess weapons of mass destruction. One of those men was also convicted of another explosives charge.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2nd_trial-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-2nd_trial)

Three other men were later convicted of felony counts of gang membership, providing material support to terrorism, and illegal weapons possession in the first state trial stemming from the case.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) However, the convictions of these men were later overturned on appeal in 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) Two more defendants pleaded guilty at a later trial to providing or attempting to provide material support, whereas three others were acquitted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) The defense has argued that this is a case of [entrapment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrapment "Entrapment"), claiming to have identified twelve [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") informants. They contend that the entire plot was fabricated by federal agents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

### Prior concerns about militia and far-right groups

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Prior concerns about militia and far-right groups")\]

See also: [American militia movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_militia_movement "American militia movement")

Preceding the [2020 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_elections "2020 United States elections"), law enforcement officials, members of [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress"), and groups tracking extremism in the country warned about the increasing threat potential from [militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_militia_organizations_in_the_United_States "List of militia organizations in the United States") and [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_(United_States) "Radical right (United States)") groups.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-CNN) The [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League "Anti-Defamation League") identified [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan") as a state where the modern militia movement found its roots and where a number of militia groups remain active.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) However, Jon Lewis, a research fellow at the Program on Extremism at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University "George Washington University"), said the Wolverine Watchmen group, which the suspects were members of, "flew under the radar", adding, "This is simply not a big group that we'd ever heard of."[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

The Wolverine Watchmen group was reportedly an offshoot of the [Michigan Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Militia "Michigan Militia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23) The day after the suspects were arrested, the Michigan Militia issued a statement disavowing the group.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24) Other Michigan-based militia groups also distanced themselves from the plotters' actions and intentions. The Michigan Home Guard, a militia that one of the suspects had once been a member of before being kicked out in 2020, released a statement condemning the Wolverine Watchmen's actions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-othermilitias-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-othermilitias)

The plot to kidnap Governor [Gretchen Whitmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretchen_Whitmer "Gretchen Whitmer") developed from June through September.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-Guardian) Within the week preceding the Wolverine Watchmen arrests, there were at least three other state and federal arrest operations linked to the [boogaloo movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogaloo_movement "Boogaloo movement"), to which the Wolverine Watchmen group's ideology closely aligned.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

### COVID-19 pandemic and mitigation measures in Michigan

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: COVID-19 pandemic and mitigation measures in Michigan")\]

Main article: [COVID-19 pandemic in Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Michigan "COVID-19 pandemic in Michigan")

See also: [COVID-19 anti-lockdown protests in the United States §Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_anti-lockdown_protests_in_the_United_States#Michigan "COVID-19 anti-lockdown protests in the United States")

Whitmer, the main target of the plot, had seen her political profile elevated over the preceding months due to her early response to the outbreak of COVID-19 in Michigan, in which she enacted strict statewide mitigation measures such as a [lockdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns "COVID-19 lockdowns") of the state. A Democrat, Gov. Whitmer garnered national attention for what some advocacy groups and experts called overly restrictive stay-at-home and social distancing orders. She also became a target of certain local militia groups, while multiple advocacy groups organized protests against lockdowns throughout April and May.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DFP-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-DFP)[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-CNN)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NPR-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-NPR)[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30) President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") offered his support for the protests, calling Whitmer anti-business and by the moniker, "that woman from Michigan" and tweeting on April 17: "LIBERATE MICHIGAN!"[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian-1)[\[26\]](#refnote-Guardian)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-33) Two weeks later, on April 30, [armed protestors entered the Michigan State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_30_storming_of_the_Michigan_State_Capitol "April 30 storming of the Michigan State Capitol").[&#8202;](#ref-back-The_New_York_Times_2021-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-The_New_York_Times_2021)[&#8202;](#ref-back-AP_News_2020-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-AP_News_2020)[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-36)

Ultimately, on October 2, 2020, the [Michigan Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Supreme_Court "Michigan Supreme Court") made two rulings in response to the executive branch’s approach to shutdown, as the power to exact significant shutdown specifics, e.g. in emergency scenarios, were in contest by interested members representing districts within Michigan’s legislative bodies. The first was a 4–3 ruling that declared a 1945 law—which Whitmer had attempted using—unconstitutional. The second was a unanimous ruling that declared a 1976 act did not give Whitmer the power to enact the measures without legislative approval.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NPR-1)[\[28\]](#refnote-NPR)[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-37)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-38) Administrative officials in the [Michigan Department of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services "Michigan Department of Health and Human Services") – and later, the [Michigan Occupational Safety and Health Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Occupational_Safety_and_Health_Administration "Michigan Occupational Safety and Health Administration") – disagreed with the legislature and circumvented the Supreme Court’s ruling and Legislature by ordering similar measures as Whitmer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NPR-2)[\[28\]](#refnote-NPR)[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-39)[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-40)[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-41)

## Participants in the plot

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Participants in the plot")\]

The suspected participants were tied to a paramilitary militia group that called themselves the Wolverine Watchmen. The group was co-founded by Pete Musico and Joseph Morrison; the latter is considered the group's "commander".[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailybeast-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-dailybeast)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-WMSN-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-WMSN) An [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News "NBC News") investigation into the suspects' social media profiles found links between their ideologies and those of the broader [boogaloo movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogaloo_movement "Boogaloo movement").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NBC2-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-NBC2) The Wolverine Watchmen group recruited members on [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") from November 2019 until June 2020, when Facebook purged all boogaloo-related material.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-46)

The suspects named in the federal indictment, charged with conspiracy to commit kidnapping, were Adam Fox, Ty Garbin, Barry Croft, Kaleb Franks, Daniel Harris, and Brandon Caserta. Five of the men were Michigan residents, while the sixth, Croft, was from [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware "Delaware").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-Mangan)[&#8202;](#ref-back-KSAZ-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-KSAZ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-MichiganLive-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-MichiganLive) Adam Fox and Barry Croft were accused of being the ringleaders of the plot.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-50) The suspects charged with state crimes, including providing [material support for terrorist acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_support_for_terrorist_acts "Material support for terrorist acts"), firearm crimes, and gang membership, were Wolverine Watchmen founders Musico and Morrison, along with Shawn Fix, Eric Molitor, Michael Null, William Null, and Paul Bellar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wjrt-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-wjrt) At least four of the 13 suspects had attended prior rallies at the [Michigan State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Capitol "Michigan State Capitol").[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-52) On October 15, a fourteenth suspect, Brian Higgins of Wisconsin, was charged at the state level with material support of an act of terrorism.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14th-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-14th) Garbin pleaded guilty on January 27, 2021, and agreed to testify against his five alleged co-conspirators.[&#8202;](#ref-back-plea-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-plea)

### Adam Fox

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Adam Fox")\]

Adam Fox was considered the [mastermind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_boss "Crime boss") of the plot.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-53) He was born Adam Dean Waggoner but changed his last name to his mother's maiden name in 2014.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bios-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-bios) He was living in the basement of his former employer, a [vacuum cleaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_cleaner "Vacuum cleaner") repair shop in [Grand Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Rapids,_Michigan "Grand Rapids, Michigan"). Fox received permission for that living arrangement from the shop's owner who felt empathy towards Fox, as Fox was [homeless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeless "Homeless") and had pet dogs. The basement was used to hold multiple meetings discussing the plot.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-55)[&#8202;](#ref-back-WOOD-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-WOOD)[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-57)

Fox posted a [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") video in June, mentioning Whitmer's handling of the pandemic as one of his motives for the plot.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WSOCTV-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-WSOCTV) According to his employer, Fox espoused anti-police and anti-government views, along with support for the [boogaloo movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boogaloo_movement "Boogaloo movement"), and had recently become worried about the U.S. becoming a [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism") country and [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") politicians taking away his guns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WOOD-1)[\[55\]](#refnote-WOOD) Fox had previously been a member of another militia group called the Michigan Home Guard, but he was kicked out due to "rage issues" and threatening other members on social media.[&#8202;](#ref-back-othermilitias-1)[\[25\]](#refnote-othermilitias)

While under surveillance, Fox was recorded by the FBI as saying "Snatch and grab, man. Grab the fuckin' governor. Just grab the bitch. Because at that point, we do that... it's over" and "I want to have the governor hog-tied, laid out on a table, while we all pose around like we just made the world's biggest goddamn drug bust, bro."[&#8202;](#ref-back-esq-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-esq) Fox was convicted in federal court for kidnapping conspiracy and conspiracy to use a weapon of mass destruction. He faced a maximum sentence of life in prison.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Snell-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-Snell) On December 27, 2022, he was sentenced to 16 years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-60)

### Joseph M. Morrison

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Joseph M. Morrison")\]

Joseph M. Morrison is considered the leader of the Wolverine Watchmen.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Seck-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-Seck)[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-62) His home in [Munith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munith,_Michigan "Munith, Michigan"), which he shared with Musico, was used as a training site.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-63) Photographs of the home show a [Confederate battle flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America#Battle_flag "Flags of the Confederate States of America") and a variation of the U.S. flag with alternating, vertical red and white stripes and a circle of stars surrounding the words " [Liberty or Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Give_me_liberty_or_give_me_death! "Give me liberty or give me death!")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailybeast-1)[\[41\]](#refnote-dailybeast)[&#8202;](#ref-back-freep-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-freep) Morrison's neighbors told _[The Daily Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Beast "The Daily Beast")_ that the home's residents were "disrespectful" and said large groups regularly gathered there on weekends, whereupon gunfire would be heard.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailybeast-2)[\[41\]](#refnote-dailybeast) His online alias was "Boogaloo Bunyan".[&#8202;](#ref-back-freep-1)[\[63\]](#refnote-freep)

Brian Higgins was a resident of [Wisconsin Dells, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Dells,_Wisconsin "Wisconsin Dells, Wisconsin"), when he was arrested on October 15.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WISN-TV-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-WISN-TV) He was alleged to have provided his night-vision goggles and [dashcam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashcam "Dashcam") to help conduct surveillance on Whitmer's home.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-107) Higgins was one of five suspects turned over for trial on December 7, 2022. Defense attorneys have argued that at least twelve [informants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informant "Informant") and/or undercover agents for the FBI were embedded among the suspected planners.[&#8202;](#ref-back-at_least_12-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-at_least_12) The FBI has acknowledged using three informants and two undercover agents in their investigation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-undercover_fbi-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-undercover_fbi)
The investigation was overseen by [Steven D'Antuono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_M._D'Antuono "Steven M. D'Antuono"), Special Agent in Charge of the FBI's Detroit Field Office.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Deadline_Detroit-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-Deadline_Detroit)

## Timeline of events

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Timeline of events")\]

### Initial investigations

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Initial investigations")\]

The [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") (FBI) reported that they became aware of group chats on social media in early 2020 threatening to conduct the violent overthrow of state governments and law enforcement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wjrt-1)[\[50\]](#refnote-wjrt)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1) A [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") veteran, Dan Chappel, had joined the group but became concerned about their use of a hunting app that could be used to track the addresses of police officers. He informed a friend and a local officer from an unidentified police department in Michigan, who then relayed the concerns to the FBI. During the initial investigation of social media chats, the FBI said that they encountered Barry Croft and Adam Fox. The FBI then interviewed Chappel, who agreed to become a confidential source after they raised concerns that there were plans to kill officers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Detroit.AlsoWanted-1)[\[76\]](#refnote-Detroit.AlsoWanted)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-2)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-2)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching)

According to many of the plotters, as detailed in a report by _[BuzzFeed News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuzzFeed_News "BuzzFeed News")_, their first meetings were arranged by someone who turned out to be a longtime government informant from Wisconsin, who paid for hotel rooms and food as incentives.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-3)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching) On March 30, Pete Musico, a co-founder of the Wolverine Watchmen, made a comment about placing Whitmer under [citizen's arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen's_arrest "Citizen's arrest") and numerous other statements on tape that prosecutors later said had indicated "a violent intent".[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP.Citizen-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-FreeP.Citizen)

Intentions by the group to obtain Whitmer's address reportedly went as far back as April 19, according to court records.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP.Citizen-1)[\[118\]](#refnote-FreeP.Citizen) That same month, the Wolverine Watchmen built a fake house with [PVC tubing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_pipework "Plastic pipework"), referred to as a "kill house", and Chappel showed them how to push inside the building and clear the rooms; according to Chappel, Musico would circle the fake house and pretend to wait for Whitmer so he could "catch that bitch as she came out the [emergency exit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_exit "Emergency exit")."[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-4)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching) On April 30, all members of the group attended an armed protest at the [Michigan State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Capitol "Michigan State Capitol"), which prosecutors said was the beginning of opportunities to conduct surveillance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP.Citizen-2)[\[118\]](#refnote-FreeP.Citizen) Chappel reportedly heard "whispers" about storming the State Capitol during this time, but this did not happen when [Michigan State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Police "Michigan State Police") officers stood down and granted the protesters access to the building.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-5)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching)

On May 8, the FBI obtained a federal [search warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_warrant "Search warrant") to review Croft's [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") account. Agents found messages "plotting potential acts of violence", including a May 3 post referencing a male individual who "may be first" and was wanted "in custody"; the FBI claims this individual was South Carolina Governor [Henry McMaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_McMaster "Henry McMaster").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Detroit.AlsoWanted-2)[\[76\]](#refnote-Detroit.AlsoWanted) The plot was reportedly hatched in an official capacity on June 6, among a group of about a dozen individuals meeting in [Dublin, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin,_Ohio "Dublin, Ohio"), a suburb located northwest of [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-3)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Timeline-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-Timeline)[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-121) A confidential source at the meeting reported that the group primarily sought to establish a new, self-reliant society that adhered to the [U.S. Bill of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights "United States Bill of Rights"). Participants reportedly discussed peaceful and violent actions of achieving this goal, with talks shifting to how state governments were allegedly violating the [U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States "Constitution of the United States"), how "tyrants" should be killed, and that those present should return home to recruit neighbors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DFP-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-DFP)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nytimes-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-nytimes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-4)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

The FBI subsequently began infiltrating the group online and in person with additional informants in June, according to a criminal complaint.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN-2)[\[19\]](#refnote-CNN)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP2-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-FreeP2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-123-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-123) A total of twelve informants were ultimately involved in the investigation, according to a federal court filing obtained by _BuzzFeed News_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-at_least_12-1)[\[110\]](#refnote-at_least_12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-124-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-124) Starting at that point, the FBI began compiling photographs, video footage, telephone calls, and encrypted messages made by the suspects and storing them as evidence on a [USB flash drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB_flash_drive "USB flash drive"). The content, said to consist of "hundreds of hours of undercover audio recordings and more than 13,000 pages of encrypted text messages", was released by the [U.S. District Court for the Western District of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Michigan "United States District Court for the Western District of Michigan") on October 16.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DetroitNews.Bomb-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-DetroitNews.Bomb)[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-126)

### Recruitment

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Recruitment")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot_-_Meeting_place.jpg/250px-Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot_-_Meeting_place.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot_-_Meeting_place.jpg) A building in [Grand Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Rapids,_Michigan "Grand Rapids, Michigan") that was a meeting place for the plotters

After the Ohio meeting, Fox contacted the militia – already being investigated by the FBI – seeking assistance for the plot. On June 14, the militia held a [field training exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_training_exercise "Field training exercise"). That day, Fox contacted one confidential source in a recorded phone call, detailing the Ohio meeting, and he requested "200 men" to attack the State Capitol in order to take Whitmer and others as hostages and to hold a "trial" for the governor, accusing her of [treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason "Treason"). During the call, Fox reportedly said to the source that the operation had to occur prior to the [2020 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-5)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

During a [Second Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Second Amendment to the United States Constitution") event at the State Capitol on June 18, Fox was recorded by a confidential source attempting to recruit militia group leaders to unite in an effort to attack the State Capitol.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-6)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP.Citizen-3)[\[118\]](#refnote-FreeP.Citizen) Two days later on June 20, Fox invited individuals, including one confidential source, to where he was employed in Grand Rapids. According to the source, Fox collected cellphones to prevent recordings and led participants through a trapdoor from the main floor, though the source wore a concealed recording device unknown to those gathered.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-7)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1) Audio from the meeting provided to the FBI contained discussions of an assault on the Capitol, how to counterattack first responders, and the use of [Molotov cocktails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov_cocktail "Molotov cocktail") to destroy law enforcement vehicles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DFP-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-DFP)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-8)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Timeline-1)[\[119\]](#refnote-Timeline) It was concluded that the group would meet on the first weekend of July, where they would discuss plans and perform training exercises.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-9)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

In a June 25 Facebook video captured by the FBI, Fox held a livestream criticizing the justice system, the Government of Michigan and the state's order closing gyms, calling Whitmer a "tyrant bitch" and stating to viewers, "I don't know, boys, we gotta do something. You guys link with me on our other location system, give me some ideas of what we can do".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-10)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

### Training and planning

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Training and planning")\]

#### Initial assault plan

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Initial assault plan")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/71msc_1.jpg/250px-71msc_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:71msc_1.jpg) The [Michigan State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Capitol "Michigan State Capitol"), where the group originally planned an assault

Training first occurred on June 28 in [Munith, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munith,_Michigan "Munith, Michigan") at a militia member's property, with Fox, his girlfriend, Ty Garbin, Kaleb Franks, Caserta, and a confidential source participating in the exercises. Participants were told to leave if they felt uncomfortable with attacking the government and participating in kidnapping.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-11)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1) According to Musico's defense attorney, Fox started making comments about storming the State Capitol during this training session, but there was no agreement from the other members, and Musico expressed concern about the plan's tactical viability, saying, "It's a fishbowl."[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP.Citizen-4)[\[118\]](#refnote-FreeP.Citizen) Court documents released on November 13 alleged that in addition to storming the State Capitol, there were plans of executing hostages and televising them over the course of a week, or locking people inside the building, while the [Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Legislature "Michigan Legislature") was in session, and setting it on fire with the intention of leaving no survivors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-127)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Independent.Execution-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-Independent.Execution)

According to Musico's attorney, from that day to October 1, there was no activity from the Wolverine Watchmen due to a fight between Fox and Musico and Musico's eventual departure from the group.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP.Citizen-5)[\[118\]](#refnote-FreeP.Citizen) However, the FBI's confidential source for the investigation said that participants met for weapons exercises in [Cambria, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambria,_Wisconsin "Cambria, Wisconsin"), between July 10 and 12, with Croft, Garbin, and a militia member attempting to make an [improvised explosive device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvised_explosive_device "Improvised explosive device") (IED) on July 11, though the device was defective. Franks also brought a rifle equipped with a [suppressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppressor_(firearms) "Suppressor (firearms)"), firing it at the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-12)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

At a second meeting in Ohio held on July 18, audio collected by a confidential source revealed discussions of attacking a Michigan State Police station, though Garbin rejected plans of an assault on the Capitol building, instead suggesting to attack Whitmer's family vacation home on Birch Lake in [Elk Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk_Rapids,_Michigan "Elk Rapids, Michigan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-13)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NBC-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-NBC) In a July 14 call recorded by a source, Fox said he researched the governor's office, concluding that Whitmer would not be present there and was heard saying, "In all honesty right now ... I just wanna make the world glow, dude. I'm not even fuckin' kidding. I just wanna make it all glow dude. I don't fuckin' care anymore, I'm just so sick of it. That's what it's gonna take for us to take it back, we're just gonna have to everything's gonna have to be annihilated, man. We're gonna topple it all, dude. It's what great frickin' conquerors, man, we're just gonna conquer every fuckin' thing man." In a July 26 call with a confidential source, Fox said "Maybe we should just make a bunch of cupcakes and send them out", with the source interpreting this as Fox seeking to initiate a "widespread bombing campaign", according to the FBI.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-14)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

#### Whitmer vacation home plans

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Whitmer vacation home plans")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/Governors_Summer_Cottage_Mackinac_Island_Lawrence_Andrew_Young_Cottage.jpg/250px-Governors_Summer_Cottage_Mackinac_Island_Lawrence_Andrew_Young_Cottage.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Governors_Summer_Cottage_Mackinac_Island_Lawrence_Andrew_Young_Cottage.jpg) The [Michigan Governor's Summer Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Governor's_Summer_Residence "Michigan Governor's Summer Residence") on [Mackinac Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackinac_Island "Mackinac Island"), a site considered for the kidnapping

Fox invited participants to his Grand Rapids location on July 27, where a confidential source provided recordings of discussions that shifted to kidnapping Whitmer when she was arriving at, or leaving, either her personal vacation home or the [Michigan Governor's Summer Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Governor's_Summer_Residence "Michigan Governor's Summer Residence") on [Mackinac Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackinac_Island "Mackinac Island").[&#8202;](#ref-back-DFP-4)[\[1\]](#refnote-DFP)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP2-1)[\[121\]](#refnote-FreeP2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[42\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-15)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-130)[&#8202;](#ref-back-LSJ-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-LSJ) According to the FBI, in sourced audio, Fox could be heard saying, "Snatch and grab, man. Grab the fuckin' Governor. Just grab the bitch. Because at that point, we do that, dude -- it's over." Fox was also heard in the recording suggesting that the group hire a realtor to find Whitmer's exact residence, collecting information on the surrounding area and recruiting individuals skilled with [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology "Information technology") and [demolitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition "Demolition").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-16)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

Participants met in Munith a second time for training on August 9, and afterwards, they communicated via group calls and chats monitored by a confidential source. In these communications, Fox suggested researching the governor's residence in Lansing and destroying Whitmer's boat. Harris said in a chat, "Have one person go to her house. Knock on the door and when she answers it just cap her ... at this point. Fuck it. ... I mean ... fuck, catch her walking into the building and act like a passers-by and fixing \[ _[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_\] dome her then yourself." During further chats on August 18, participants concentrated on finding the Whitmer family's vacation home and planned on how to escape the area by boat.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-17)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

#### Surveillance begins

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Surveillance begins")\]

On August 23, participants met at Harris' home in [Lake Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Orion,_Michigan "Lake Orion, Michigan"), where a secret recording was captured of the group discussing surveillance of the vacation home and Franks shared that he spent nearly $4,000 on [night vision goggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_vision_goggles "Night vision goggles") and a helmet. A source captured audio of an August 29 surveillance operation, where Fox drove by the Whitmer family's vacation home, took photographs, and discussed response times of police in the area. On the recorded audio, Fox was heard saying, "We ain't gonna let 'em burn our fuckin' state down. I don't give a fuck if there's only 20 or 30 of us, dude, we'll go out there and use deadly force." The next day on August 30, the FBI said screen captures of the group chat showed Garbin suggesting the demolition of a bridge over the [Elk River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk_River_(Michigan) "Elk River (Michigan)"), near the vacation home, to slow and distract police.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-18)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-6)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching)

Due to recommendation of the Michigan State Police and the [Michigan Department of Technology, Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Department_of_Technology,_Management_and_Budget "Michigan Department of Technology, Management and Budget"), the construction of a $1.1 million barrier and electric fence at the [Michigan Governor's Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Governor's_Mansion "Michigan Governor's Mansion") in Lansing was announced on September 4 after weeks of construction, with spokeswoman Tiffany Brown saying "perimeter security and other safety upgrades" were being performed. Regarding the security upgrades, _[The Detroit News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Detroit_News "The Detroit News")_ wrote "Whitmer, a Democrat who was first elected in 2018, has been the subject of menacing messages at Capitol demonstrations against her past COVID-19 stay-at-home orders", with the newspaper also citing other threats against Whitmer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-:2)

While driving in three vehicles–with an undercover FBI agent and confidential source present–from a group camp in [Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther,_Michigan "Luther, Michigan") towards the vacation home on September 12 and early September 13, Croft suggested that the group attempt to kidnap Whitmer that night, though the idea was dismissed. According to the FBI, Croft and Fox, who were in the first vehicle, planned on detonating a bomb on a bridge of [U.S. Route 31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_31_in_Michigan "U.S. Route 31 in Michigan") in Elk Rapids, photographing the underside of the bridge, and deciding where to place explosives. The group in the first vehicle then drove to a boat launch across the lake from the vacation home, waiting for a second vehicle to conduct surveillance on the location. The third vehicle, which was also occupied by an undercover agent, was tasked with monitoring for any followers or suspicious activity in the area. On the way back to Garbin's property, the group discussed abandoning the kidnapping plan and instead destroying the vacation home entirely.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-19)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)

#### Plans finalize

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Plans finalize")\]

At a discussion recorded at Garbin's property on September 13, Fox said the plan would strictly be a kidnapping, with the end result reportedly being either the abandonment of Whitmer on a boat in [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan "Lake Michigan") or her being subjected to a trial as a "tyrant" in Wisconsin. When a plan to destroy the bridge leading to the lake in order to slow down responding security and police officers was raised, an undercover agent told Fox that the explosives necessary for destroying the bridge would cost about $4,000. The group decided that a final training exercise would be conducted later in October, though Fox pushed for an earlier date the following day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-20)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-7)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching)[&#8202;](#ref-back-133-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-133) Fox ordered the explosives from the undercover agent that same day. Also on that day, Croft and Harris detonated an IED containing shrapnel near human [silhouette targets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silhouette_target "Silhouette target") to test its effectiveness.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN.WMD-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-CNN.WMD) On October 2, Fox told a confidential source that he purchased an 800,000-volt [taser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taser "Taser") to be used for the kidnapping.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DFP-5)[\[1\]](#refnote-DFP)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-21)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1) That same month, he reportedly contacted Musico, who advocated for a nonviolent approach.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FreeP.Citizen-6)[\[118\]](#refnote-FreeP.Citizen) Fox, Garbin, Harris, and Franks planned to meet with an undercover FBI agent to purchase explosives on October 7, though Caserta did not attend due to work obligations, while Croft returned to Delaware prior to the meeting.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-22)[\[84\]](#refnote-:1) The prosecution was hampered in that an abduction date was never determined and the plot's details varied among the witnesses.[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-135)

### Arrests

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Arrests")\]

All of the members involved were arrested on the night of October 7, and charges were filed against them the next day. Search warrants and arrests were executed all across Michigan. The arrests were reportedly part of a planned effort between federal and state authorities, launched after evidence was discovered indicating the suspects wanted to kidnap Whitmer before the 2020 election. At least seven FBI field offices, along with the operational divisions at the [J. Edgar Hoover Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Edgar_Hoover_Building "J. Edgar Hoover Building") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), were reportedly involved in coordinating the arrests.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN2-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-CNN2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-137-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-137)[&#8202;](#ref-back-138-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-138)[&#8202;](#ref-back-139-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-139) A week later, a fourteenth suspect was arrested and charged in state court.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14th-2)[\[9\]](#refnote-14th) The investigation also involved people from [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore") to [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_metropolitan_area "Kansas City metropolitan area").[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-8)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching) Several weapons and other items were seized, including at least 1,916 rounds of pistol, rifle, and shotgun ammunition; hundreds of firearms; extended magazines; various [suppressors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppressor "Suppressor"); [speedloaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedloader "Speedloader"); and bomb-making equipment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-140)

## Legal proceedings

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Legal proceedings")\]

### Federal

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: Federal")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/United_States_Department_of_Justice_-_FOX.pdf/page1-250px-United_States_Department_of_Justice_-_FOX.pdf.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:United_States_Department_of_Justice_-_FOX.pdf) [Criminal complaint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complaint "Complaint") of the [United States District Court for the Western District of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Michigan "United States District Court for the Western District of Michigan") regarding the incident

[![Wikisource logo](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Wikisource-logo.svg/40px-Wikisource-logo.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wikisource-logo.svg)

English [Wikisource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikisource "Wikisource") has original text related to this article:

**[United States v. Fox (complaint)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/en:United%20States%20v.%20Fox%20(complaint) "s:en:United States v. Fox (complaint)")**

Six people (Adam Fox, Ty Garbin, Barry Croft, Kaleb Franks, Daniel Harris, and Brandon Caserta) were charged in the [United States District Court for the Western District of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Michigan "United States District Court for the Western District of Michigan") for conspiring to commit kidnapping. The federal charges carry a potential sentence of life imprisonment if convictions are obtained.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian-2)[\[26\]](#refnote-Guardian) A federal judge said the federal government had [probable cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probable_cause "Probable cause") in the cases against five of the defendants and declared they could move forward to trial, while also denying bond to four of them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-141-0)[\[140\]](#refnote-141)[&#8202;](#ref-back-142-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-142)

During a court hearing on October 13, an FBI agent testified that the conspirators had considered leaving Whitmer in a boat in the middle of [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan "Lake Michigan") and disabling its motor. He also testified that the group had discussed, during early stages of the planning, kidnapping [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia "Virginia") Governor [Ralph Northam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Northam "Ralph Northam"), who had also imposed strict lockdown orders in response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Virginia "COVID-19 pandemic in Virginia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-143-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-143)[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-144) This prompted the FBI to notify members of Northam's security staff about the potential threat.[&#8202;](#ref-back-145-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-145)

On October 26, federal prosecutors announced the FBI had found "explosive device components" and [privately made firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_made_firearm "Privately made firearm") and are considering adding federal terrorism charges after they are analyzed by experts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DetroitNews.Bomb-1)[\[124\]](#refnote-DetroitNews.Bomb)[&#8202;](#ref-back-146-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-146) On October 28, an unsealed search warrant revealed that some of the defendants had discussed South Carolina Governor [Henry McMaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_McMaster "Henry McMaster") as another possible target during the early stages of planning in March. The warrant also revealed that, in late June, Croft had posted on Facebook a hit-list of politicians that he said he wanted to hang. The list included the names of McMaster, President Trump, former Presidents [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") and [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton"), former [U.S. State Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State "United States Secretary of State") [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton"), New York Representative [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez "Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez"), other [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") and [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") elected officials, liberals, Muslims, and "all anti-Americans".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Detroit.AlsoWanted-3)[\[76\]](#refnote-Detroit.AlsoWanted)[&#8202;](#ref-back-PostAndCourier.Target-1)[\[101\]](#refnote-PostAndCourier.Target)

On October 30, Franks' attorney requested a judge to reconsider her decision to keep him imprisoned, saying he is diabetic and fears contracting [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") in jail.[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-147) On December 16, all six federal defendants were [indicted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indictment#United_States "Indictment") by a [grand jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_juries_in_the_United_States "Grand juries in the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-148) On January 1, 2021, Croft, who remains jailed in a [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") [federal prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Detention_Center,_Philadelphia "Federal Detention Center, Philadelphia"), asked to be released while he awaited his court hearings, due to the constant delays in a prisoner transfer and the subsequently slow progress of the case against him. Federal prosecutors argued against releasing him, describing Croft as a "violent extremist" and saying such a decision would be "unreasonable".[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-149) On January 15, 2021, a judge ordered all six defendants to stand trial on March 23.[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-150)[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-151) The trial date was later postponed to October 12.[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-152) Croft, Fox, Harris, Franks, and Caserta pleaded not guilty to the charges.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Garrison_2021-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-Garrison_2021) On January 27, 2021, Garbin pleaded guilty to conspiring to commit kidnapping. His sentencing was scheduled for July 8. As part of a [plea bargain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plea_bargain "Plea bargain"), he agreed to testify against his co-defendants in exchange for no additional charges being filed against him by prosecutors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-153-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-153) On August 26, 2021, Garbin was sentenced to six years in prison and fined $2,500.[&#8202;](#ref-back-154-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-154)

On April 28, 2021, federal prosecutors charged Fox, Croft, and Harris with conspiracy to use weapons of mass destruction, in relation to the explosives that Fox ordered from the undercover agent on September 13, 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN.WMD-1)[\[133\]](#refnote-CNN.WMD) In December 2021, the five remaining defendants (Adam Fox, Barry Croft, Kaleb Franks, Daniel Harris, and Brandon Caserta) – who were then still in custody – filed a motion to have the charges against them dismissed on the grounds of [entrapment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrapment#United_States "Entrapment").[&#8202;](#ref-back-155-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-155) Robert Jonker, the Chief federal district court judge assigned to the case rejected the defense's motion to dismiss on January 26, 2022. Though Jonker is allowing the defense to argue entrapment to the jury, he stated, "at this pretrial stage, the Court concludes the defense has not demonstrated as a matter of law that Defendants' wills were overcome by the actions of the government."[&#8202;](#ref-back-mlive202201-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-mlive202201) After Franks pleaded guilty in February, the trial of the remaining four defendants was scheduled to commence March 8, 2022.[&#8202;](#ref-back-mlive202201-1)[\[155\]](#refnote-mlive202201)[&#8202;](#ref-back-157-0)[\[156\]](#refnote-157) The trial was delayed by a participant's positive [COVID-19 test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_test "COVID-19 test"), and the court was scheduled to re-convene on March 17, 2022.[&#8202;](#ref-back-158-0)[\[157\]](#refnote-158)

On March 8, 2022, trial began for Fox, Croft, Harris and Caserta in the United States District Court with [Judge Jonker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_James_Jonker "Robert James Jonker") presiding.[&#8202;](#ref-back-159-0)[\[158\]](#refnote-159) On April 8, 2022, after five days of deliberation, Caserta was acquitted of the only charge he faced and Harris was found not guilty on all charges, while Fox and Croft received a [hung jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hung_jury "Hung jury").[&#8202;](#ref-back-160-0)[\[159\]](#refnote-160) The judge declared a mistrial and ordered Fox and Croft remain in jail until re-trial.[&#8202;](#ref-back-notguilty-1)[\[11\]](#refnote-notguilty)[&#8202;](#ref-back-notguilty2-0)[\[160\]](#refnote-notguilty2) On August 10, 2022, the retrial of Fox and Croft began.[&#8202;](#ref-back-second_trial-0)[\[161\]](#refnote-second_trial) On August 23, 2022, Fox and Croft were found guilty of kidnapping conspiracy and conspiracy to possess weapons of mass destruction at their retrial.[&#8202;](#ref-back-163-0)[\[162\]](#refnote-163) Croft was also convicted of another explosives charge.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2nd_trial-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-2nd_trial) On October 6, 2022, Franks was sentenced to four years in prison.[&#8202;](#ref-back-164-0)[\[163\]](#refnote-164) On December 27, 2022, Fox was sentenced to serve sixteen years in federal prison followed by five years of supervision.[&#8202;](#ref-back-165-0)[\[164\]](#refnote-165) On December 28, 2022, Croft was sentenced to serve more than nineteen years and seven months in federal prison.[&#8202;](#ref-back-166-0)[\[165\]](#refnote-166)[&#8202;](#ref-back-167-0)[\[166\]](#refnote-167)

### State

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: State")\]

[Michigan Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Attorney_General "Michigan Attorney General") [Dana Nessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Nessel "Dana Nessel") charged seven other men (Pete Musico, Joseph Morrison, Shawn Fix, Eric Molitor, Michael Null, William Null, and Paul Bellar) with state crimes, including providing material support for terrorist acts, firearm crimes, and gang membership.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wjrt-2)[\[50\]](#refnote-wjrt) Two of the defendants were each set a bond of $10 million, with preliminary examinations scheduled for October 21.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MichiganLive-1)[\[48\]](#refnote-MichiganLive) Musico's bond was later reduced to $100,000 after his attorney convinced the judge his role in the plot was overstated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-musicobond-1)[\[92\]](#refnote-musicobond) On October 20, Bellar was charged with state crimes and was extradited from his home state of South Carolina for a formal trial.[&#8202;](#ref-back-168-0)[\[167\]](#refnote-168) On November 10, Fix was released on a $250,000 bond.[&#8202;](#ref-back-169-0)[\[168\]](#refnote-169) Three days later, bond was reduced for Morrison, Musico, and Bellar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-170-0)[\[169\]](#refnote-170) Bellar was released on a $75,000 bond on November 13.[&#8202;](#ref-back-171-0)[\[170\]](#refnote-171)

On October 15, Brian Higgins from Wisconsin was charged by Nessel with material support of an act of terrorism, which carries a 20-year sentence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14th-3)[\[9\]](#refnote-14th)[&#8202;](#ref-back-WISN-TV-1)[\[105\]](#refnote-WISN-TV) On October 19, Higgins was released on [bail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bail_in_the_United_States "Bail in the United States"), with one of the imposed restrictions being that he not try to make contact with the other 13 defendants.[&#8202;](#ref-back-172-0)[\[171\]](#refnote-172) On November 18, his attorney announced his client's plans to challenge his extradition from Wisconsin to Michigan. The basis of the challenge was that the extradition paperwork was signed by Whitmer, the target of the plot, thus creating a conflict of interest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Independent.Execution-1)[\[127\]](#refnote-Independent.Execution) On December 15, a judge in [Columbia County, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_County,_Wisconsin "Columbia County, Wisconsin") ruled there was enough probable cause for the transfer to occur. His extradition was delayed after his attorney filed an appeal on the ruling.[&#8202;](#ref-back-173-0)[\[172\]](#refnote-173)

On December 4, twin brothers Michael and William Null were released on bond.[&#8202;](#ref-back-174-0)[\[173\]](#refnote-174) On December 18, a [Jackson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_County,_Michigan "Jackson County, Michigan") judge denied a bond reduction request for Morrison, saying he could still pose a threat to the public.[&#8202;](#ref-back-175-0)[\[174\]](#refnote-175) On January 14, 2021, Molitor was released on bond.[&#8202;](#ref-back-176-0)[\[175\]](#refnote-176) On March 29, 2021, Morrison, Musico, and Bellar were bound over for trial after a three-day-long preliminary hearing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-177-0)[\[176\]](#refnote-177) At the same hearing, threat of terrorism charges were dismissed against Morrison and Musico; Bellar did not face that charge.[&#8202;](#ref-back-178-0)[\[177\]](#refnote-178) On April 14, 2021, a judge lifted Michael and William Null's curfew and house arrest restrictions, but they were still required to wear GPS tethers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-179-0)[\[178\]](#refnote-179)

On June 7, 2021, Musico called for his case to be dismissed, arguing entrapment by federal authorities and the confidential informant and claiming he would not have associated himself with Fox without police interference. He also claimed the informant led the training sessions for the Wolverine Watchmen; an FBI agent said in March that he instructed the informant to take on a leadership role during those sessions to prevent suspects from getting injured and thus requiring a law enforcement response while the Wolverine Watchmen were armed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-180-0)[\[179\]](#refnote-180)[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuzzFeed.Watching-9)[\[116\]](#refnote-BuzzFeed.Watching) Attorneys for Morrison and Bellar later similarly called for the case's dismissal due to entrapment. However, on March 1, 2022, a Jackson County judge denied the motion to dismiss.[&#8202;](#ref-back-181-0)[\[180\]](#refnote-181)

On October 26, 2022, Bellar, Morrison, and Musico were convicted of materially aiding a terrorist and being a member of a gang as part of a plot to kidnap Gov. Whitmer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Berg-4)[\[65\]](#refnote-Berg) Bellar was sentenced to 20 years in prison with a minimum of seven to serve; Morrison to 20 years with a minimum of 10 to serve; and Musico to 20 years with a minimum of 12 to serve.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wolverine_Watchmen_Sentenced-3)[\[66\]](#refnote-Wolverine_Watchmen_Sentenced) The last five defendants (the Null brothers, Shawn Fix, Higgins and Molitor) were turned over for trial on December 7, 2022.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PBS-4)[\[94\]](#refnote-PBS) Ahead of trial, Higgins pled guilty to attempting to provide material support for terrorist acts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-182-0)[\[181\]](#refnote-182) On June 7, 2023, Fix also pled guilty.[&#8202;](#ref-back-183-0)[\[182\]](#refnote-183) Molitor and the Nulls maintained their innocence; their trial began on August 23, 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-184-0)[\[183\]](#refnote-184) All three men were acquitted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-acquits-3)[\[97\]](#refnote-acquits)

On June 9, 2026, Morrison's conviction was overturned by the Michigan Court of Appeals. The ruling was made on technical grounds, with the court holding that kidnapping was not "an underlying violent felony" as defined by the state's terrorism laws and therefore could not support a conviction for terrorist offenses. Nessel vowed to appeal the verdict.[&#8202;](#ref-back-185-0)[\[184\]](#refnote-185) On July 20, 2026, Musico and Bellar's convictions were also overturned.[&#8202;](#ref-back-186-0)[\[185\]](#refnote-186)

## Reactions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=33 "Edit section: Reactions")\]

### Whitmer's response

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=34 "Edit section: Whitmer's response")\]

Whitmer spoke out during a livestream held after the thwarted plot was revealed by the FBI. She thanked the law enforcement agencies involved in the investigation, called the plotters "sick and depraved men", and cast blame on President Trump for refusing to explicitly condemn far-right groups and for his handling of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States "COVID-19 pandemic in the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN-3)[\[19\]](#refnote-CNN)[&#8202;](#ref-back-187-0)[\[186\]](#refnote-187) Whitmer later urged in a tweet that the suspects be called [domestic terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism_in_the_United_States "Domestic terrorism in the United States") and not members of any [militia organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_organizations_in_the_United_States "Militia organizations in the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-188-0)[\[187\]](#refnote-188) In an October 11 interview with _[Face the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Nation "Face the Nation")_, she said security threats against her still existed and that extremists like the Wolverine Watchmen are "finding comfort and support in the rhetoric coming out of Republican leadership from the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") to [our state House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_House_of_Representatives "Michigan House of Representatives")."[&#8202;](#ref-back-189-0)[\[188\]](#refnote-189) Whitmer wrote about the plot in an opinion piece in _[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic")_ on October 27, where she continued to blame Trump's rhetoric for causing divisiveness in the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-190-0)[\[189\]](#refnote-190)

### Other political responses

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=35 "Edit section: Other political responses")\]

Michigan Attorney General [Dana Nessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Nessel "Dana Nessel") called the plot "one of the largest cases in recent history" and labeled the case as "rather unprecedented" in nature.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TDN-0)[\[190\]](#refnote-TDN) State Senator [Mike Shirkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Shirkey "Mike Shirkey"), Michigan House Speaker [Lee Chatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Chatfield "Lee Chatfield"), and Ohio Governor [Mike DeWine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_DeWine "Mike DeWine") condemned the plot.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN-4)[\[19\]](#refnote-CNN)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NPR-3)[\[28\]](#refnote-NPR)[&#8202;](#ref-back-192-0)[\[191\]](#refnote-192) Chatfield later criticized Whitmer for not warning state lawmakers in advance about the plotters' original intent to storm the Michigan State Capitol building.[&#8202;](#ref-back-193-0)[\[192\]](#refnote-193) Delaware Governor [John Carney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carney_(Delaware_politician) "John Carney (Delaware politician)"), who pardoned one of the suspects in 2019, called the federal charges "disturbing" and said, "This is also another warning sign about the growing threat of violence and radicalization in our politics."[&#8202;](#ref-back-KSAZ-2)[\[47\]](#refnote-KSAZ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-DelawareOnline-2)[\[77\]](#refnote-DelawareOnline) Virginia Governor [Ralph Northam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Northam "Ralph Northam"), who had also been discussed by the plotters as a potential target, declined to comment about the details of the plot but said he and his family felt safe with the protection of the [Virginia State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Police "Virginia State Police").[&#8202;](#ref-back-194-0)[\[193\]](#refnote-194)

#### President Trump's responses

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=36 "Edit section: President Trump's responses")\]

In an October 8 interview, President Trump criticized Whitmer for her rebuke of him in response to the kidnapping plot, saying he condemned all forms of "extreme violence" and calling for her to reopen her state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-195-0)[\[194\]](#refnote-195)[&#8202;](#ref-back-196-0)[\[195\]](#refnote-196)

During that interview, Trump falsely claimed that Michigan's schools and churches were closed under Whitmer's orders.[&#8202;](#ref-back-197-0)[\[196\]](#refnote-197) In response to Trump's remarks, Whitmer said it "tells you everything you need to know" about the difference between him and Democratic presidential candidate [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden").[&#8202;](#ref-back-198-0)[\[197\]](#refnote-198) Biden also reacted to Trump's remarks on October 16, saying the "failure to condemn these folks is stunning."[&#8202;](#ref-back-199-0)[\[198\]](#refnote-199)

Trump continued to attack Whitmer for her COVID-19 mitigation measures at a rally in [Muskegon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskegon,_Michigan "Muskegon, Michigan") on October 17, which was matched with chants of ["Lock her up!"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election#Notable_expressions,_phrases,_and_statements "2016 United States presidential election"), to which he replied, "Lock 'em all up." Trump's daughter-in-law [Lara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_Trump "Lara Trump"), a campaign surrogate, later insisted he was merely "having fun" at the rally.[&#8202;](#ref-back-200-0)[\[199\]](#refnote-200) In a subsequent interview with NBC's _[Meet the Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_the_Press "Meet the Press")_, Whitmer called Trump's rhetoric "incredibly disturbing" and said it is "inspiring and incentivizing and inciting this kind of domestic terrorism". She also countered Trump's claims at the rally that she should relax the statewide COVID-19 restrictions, saying Michigan has not had a stay-at-home order since spring.[&#8202;](#ref-back-201-0)[\[200\]](#refnote-201) At an October 27 rally in [Lansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansing,_Michigan "Lansing, Michigan"), Trump downplayed the domestic terror aspect of the plot and continued the verbal attacks on Whitmer, which were also met with chants of "Lock her up!".[&#8202;](#ref-back-202-0)[\[201\]](#refnote-202)[&#8202;](#ref-back-203-0)[\[202\]](#refnote-203)

### Law enforcement responses

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=37 "Edit section: Law enforcement responses")\]

Since the kidnapping plot at one point involved an intended attack on police, state law enforcement agencies were put on high alert in response.[&#8202;](#ref-back-OnAlert-0)[\[203\]](#refnote-OnAlert)

#### Statements by Barry County Sheriff

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=38 "Edit section: Statements by Barry County Sheriff")\]

Dar Leaf, the elected sheriff of [Barry County, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_County,_Michigan "Barry County, Michigan"), appeared to defend two of the suspects in an October 8, 2020 interview, saying that "a lot of people are angry with the governor, and they want her arrested. So are they trying to arrest or was it a kidnap attempt? Because you can still in Michigan if it's a felony, make a felony arrest." Citing the state's [citizen's arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen's_arrest "Citizen's arrest") statute, he continued, "It doesn't say if you are an elected office that you're exempt from that arrest. I have to look at it from that angle and I'm hoping that's more what it is, in fact, these guys are innocent till proven guilty so I'm not even sure if they had any part of it."[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fox17Arrest-0)[\[204\]](#refnote-Fox17Arrest)

The interview went viral on the internet, along with the revelation that Leaf had shared the stage with one of the suspects at a May 18 [anti-lockdown protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_anti-lockdown_protests#Michigan "2020 United States anti-lockdown protests").[&#8202;](#ref-back-WWMT-1)[\[98\]](#refnote-WWMT) Attorney General Nessel condemned the remarks as "dangerous",[&#8202;](#ref-back-BostonGlobe-0)[\[205\]](#refnote-BostonGlobe) while a number of other Michigan sheriffs expressed their outrage as well.[&#8202;](#ref-back-OnAlert-1)[\[203\]](#refnote-OnAlert) Law experts disagreed with the notion that the Wolverine Watchmen were simply planning to conduct a legal citizen's arrest on Whitmer, saying it was far-fetched.[&#8202;](#ref-back-207-0)[\[206\]](#refnote-207) Leaf later clarified his comments, claiming he simply wanted a fair trial for the accused and did not agree with their actions, which he called "horrible".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fox17Response-0)[\[207\]](#refnote-Fox17Response) His earlier comments and his ties with one of the suspects during the anti-lockdown protest have spurred calls for him to resign.[&#8202;](#ref-back-209-0)[\[208\]](#refnote-209) On October 13, Leaf acknowledged the criticism but said he would not resign.[&#8202;](#ref-back-210-0)[\[209\]](#refnote-210)

### Michigan State Capitol security concerns

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=39 "Edit section: Michigan State Capitol security concerns")\]

Democrats in the [Michigan Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Legislature "Michigan Legislature") renewed calls for a ban on guns in the State Capitol building in response to news of the plot, following an unsuccessful September proposal drafted in response to armed anti-lockdown protesters storming the building in April.[&#8202;](#ref-back-211-0)[\[210\]](#refnote-211)

On December 14, the day that Michigan's electors to the [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College "United States Electoral College") were set to meet at the capital, authorities closed the Michigan State Capitol to the public and shuttered state legislators' offices, after "credible threats of violence" were received. The building was left open only to those needed for the Electoral College.[&#8202;](#ref-back-212-0)[\[211\]](#refnote-212) Earlier that day, State Representative [Gary Eisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Eisen "Gary Eisen") claimed there would be a " [Hail Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail_Mary_pass#Metaphorical_usage_in_other_fields "Hail Mary pass")" effort regarding a plan to prevent electors from casting a vote. When asked if he can assure the public that it will be a safe day in [Lansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansing "Lansing"), and that nobody would get hurt, Eisen replied, "No. I don't know. Because what we're doing today is uncharted."[&#8202;](#ref-back-213-0)[\[212\]](#refnote-213) Shortly after the interview, Chatfield and Speaker-Elect [Jason Wentworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Wentworth "Jason Wentworth") released a statement disavowing any threat of violence or intimidation and announcing Eisen would be removed from his committee assignments for the rest of the term.[&#8202;](#ref-back-214-0)[\[213\]](#refnote-214)

On January 11, 2021, Michigan banned the open carry of firearms on the State Capitol, citing the kidnapping plot and the [storming of the United States Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol "Storming of the United States Capitol"). The ban was supported by both Democratic and Republican state lawmakers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-215-0)[\[214\]](#refnote-215) On January 15, 2021, the [Michigan National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_National_Guard "Michigan National Guard") was requested, and heavy fencing was put up around the State Capitol, in preparation for an armed protest two days later, on January 17.[&#8202;](#ref-back-216-0)[\[215\]](#refnote-216)

### Other responses

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Gretchen_Whitmer_kidnapping_plot&action=edit&section=40 "Edit section: Other responses")\]

A spokesperson for [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), which had been used by the suspects in plotting Whitmer's kidnapping, said the company would be cooperating with the FBI's investigation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN2-1)[\[135\]](#refnote-CNN2) A scheduled October 13 campaign stop by [Eric Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Trump "Eric Trump") at a [Lyon Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Township,_Oakland_County,_Michigan "Lyon Township, Oakland County, Michigan") gun store was moved to a banquet center in [Novi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi,_Michigan "Novi, Michigan") after it was discovered one of the suspects had worked there for three weeks before being fired.[&#8202;](#ref-back-217-0)[\[216\]](#refnote-217) Some users on social media falsely asserted that the Wolverine Watchmen were [anarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_the_United_States "Anarchism in the United States") affiliated with the [antifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifa_(United_States) "Antifa (United States)") and [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter "Black Lives Matter") movements. Some posts promoting these theories were flagged by Facebook as part of its anti-misinformation campaign, while [PolitiFact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PolitiFact "PolitiFact") rated the posts as "Mostly False", noting that only one of the suspects, Daniel Harris, is known to have attended a BLM protest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PolitiFact-2)[\[85\]](#refnote-PolitiFact)
