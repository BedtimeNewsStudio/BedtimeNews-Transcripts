## Abstract

### Background

Dyslipidemia remains a major modifiable contributor to China’s cardiovascular disease (CVD) burden, yet large-scale evidence on risk-stratified management gaps is lacking.

### Methods

In this nationwide study across 1,785 hospitals in 28 Chinese provinces, 604,250 outpatients with dyslipidemia were enrolled. We analyzed lipid levels, quantified control rate among treated population and rates of requiring lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) among untreated population across regions, socioeconomic status, and demographic groups.

### Results

Total cholesterol (TC) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels were higher among females, middle-aged individuals, and individuals with obesity. LDL-C levels were also higher among urban residents, while TC showed no significant urban-rural difference. Triglyceride (TG) levels were higher in males, middle-aged individuals, individuals with obesity, and rural residents. Among treated population, LDL-C/non-HDL-C control rates reached 95% in low-risk, 70–90% in moderate-risk, 40–50% in high-risk, and 5–15% in very-high-risk groups. Among untreated population, rates of requiring LLT reached about 20% in the low-risk group and over 70% in moderate- and high-risk groups. After adjusting for covariates, males, older individuals, smokers, patients with hypertension or type 2 diabetes mellitus, as well as those in rural and low-gross-domestic-product areas were associated with lower lipid control rates and higher treatment needs.

### Conclusions

Our findings highlight the urgent need for risk-stratified lipid management in primary care, improved access to LLT, and policies addressing regional and socioeconomic disparities to enhance lipid control and reduce CVD burden in China. Lessons from China can inform global strategies to improve lipid management and reduce the CVD burden.

## Methods

### Study design and population

The current study is a cross-sectional nationwide survey of dyslipidemia in China, initially funded by the National Key Research and Development Program of China. Participating sites were identified through a multistage site-selection process within the project network. We initially invited hospitals from our established collaborative network, all of which were tertiary hospitals with strong research engagement, resulting in virtually no refusals. These tertiary centers subsequently invited primary healthcare institutions within their affiliated medical alliances to participate. Through this approach, the study network comprised 200 lipid demonstration centers (tertiary hospitals with specialized capacity for dyslipidemia management) and 1,585 lipid screening agencies (mainly county hospitals, community healthcare institutions, and other participating screening sites). These institutions covered 28 provinces across all seven geographic regions of China, encompassing both urban and rural settings (Additional file 1: Table [1](#Tab1)). From September 2022 to November 2024, eligible outpatients with diagnosed dyslipidemia were consecutively enrolled at participating sites during the study period.

Dyslipidemia was defined according to the historical peak lipids level: LDL-C ≥ 3.40 mmol/L (130 mg/dL), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) < 1.00 mmol/L (40 mg/dL), triglycerides (TG) ≥ 1.70 mmol/L (150 mg/dL), self-reported cardiovascular disease, or self-reported use of LLT, in accordance with the 2016 Chinese Adult Dyslipidemia Prevention Guideline \[ [22](#ref-CR22 "Joint committee issued Chinese guideline for the management of dyslipidemia in adults. Major lipids measurements (total cholesterol \[TC\], LDL-C, TG, and HDL-C) were required for ASCVD risk stratification and outcome definition; therefore, participants with missing data on these variables were excluded from the main analysis. For multivariable regression analyses, a complete-case approach was used, and individuals with missing values in model covariates were excluded from the corresponding model. Of the 703,357 patients initially included, 99,107 were excluded due to incomplete lipid data, and 604,250 individuals were included in the final analysis. Among these, 219,244 were treated and 385,006 were untreated. After excluding 3 treated and 2 untreated patients with missing variables, the final regression analyses included 219,241 treated and 385,004 untreated patients respectively (Additional file 1: Fig. [1](#Fig1)).

### Lipid measurements

Lipid measurements were obtained from laboratory reports generated during patients’ routine clinical care, rather than from a central laboratory. All involved hospital laboratories were required to meet the national quality control standards (WS/T 403–2012) during the study period, ensuring the accuracy and comparability of test results. To ensure data quality, lipid data were collected and verified by trained research staff based on photographs of original laboratory reports uploaded by patients. Participants with missing major lipid indicators required for ASCVD risk stratification and lipid target assessment were excluded, and only biologically implausible extreme lipid values were removed during data cleaning. For sites using the Friedewald formula for LDL-C calculation, patients with extreme TG values were excluded during enrollment to ensure the reliability of calculated results. Because measurements were performed across multiple hospitals, some inter-laboratory heterogeneity in analyzers, assay manufacturers, and measurement methods may remain. Detailed data collection, key variables, and quality control procedures are also illustrated (Additional file 1: Text 1).

### ASCVD risk stratification and outcome definitions

ASCVD risk stratification was performed using a rule-based classification according to the Chinese lipid management guidelines, based on the composite clinical information collected in this study rather than a calculated risk score model (Additional file 1: Fig. [2](#Fig2)) \[ [22](#ref-CR22 "Joint committee issued Chinese guideline for the management of dyslipidemia in adults. [2016 Chinese guideline for the management of dyslipidemia in adults]. Zhonghua xin xue guan bing za zhi. 2016;44:833–53.                    https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.0253-3758.2016.10.005                                    .")\]. This information included self-reported clinical ASCVD history, major comorbidities, and lipid measures where required by the guideline framework. Historical peak lipid values were used for the lipid component of risk classification, whereas the most recent lipid measurements were used to report current lipid levels and define lipid-control outcomes. Because detailed information on recurrent ASCVD events was unavailable, very-high-risk and extremely-high-risk individuals could not be further separated; therefore, secondary prevention patients were classified as very-high-risk in the present analysis.

### Covariates

Covariate selection for the multivariable models was prespecified a priori based on clinical relevance and previous literature. Because these analyses were intended to identify factors associated with lipid control and unmet treatment need in this cross-sectional study, rather than to estimate a specific causal effect, variables were grouped into conceptual domains rather than being strictly classified as confounders or mediators. These domains included sociodemographic characteristics (sex, age group, residence, geographic region, and regional gross domestic product \[GDP\] level), lifestyle factors \[smoking (never, former, current), drinking (never, former, current), and physical activity (no exercise, < 1 time/week, 1–2 times/week, 3–4 times/week, and 5–7 times/week; with each session lasting ≥ 30 min)\], body mass index (BMI) group, comorbidities (hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus \[T2DM\]), and markers of baseline disease severity. Historical peak lipid measures were included as indicators of prior dyslipidemia severity, whereas the outcomes were defined using the most recent lipid measurements. All models were fitted using generalised linear models with a binomial distribution and logit link.

### Statistical analysis

Levels of major lipids in each subgroup were tabulated by treatment status. Rates of control for treated population and rates of requiring lipid management for untreated population in each risk stratification were plotted and tabulated by age, sex, BMI, urban/rural residence, geographic region, and GDP. Stratified analyses by subgroups were conducted to identify high-risk groups with low control rates, highlighting inequities in lipid management.

Continuous variables were presented as means ± SD, categorical variables as counts and percentages. Normality was assessed for continuous variables. Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for two-group comparisons; one-way ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis test for comparisons among > 2 groups. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test (χ²). We further examined factors associated with achievement of treatment targets among individuals and with the need for lipid management among untreated individuals. Guided by clinical experience and previous literature, covariates included demographic characteristics (sex, age group, residence, geographic region, and regional GDP level), lifestyle factors \[smoking (never, former, current), drinking (never, former, current), and physical activity (no exercise, < 1 time/week, 1–2 times/week, 3–4 times/week, and 5–7 times/week; with each session lasting ≥ 30 min)\], BMI group, lipid profiles (TC, TG, LDL-C, and HDL-C), and comorbidities (hypertension and T2DM). All models were fitted using generalised linear models with a binomial distribution and logit link. Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.1). Two-sided p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

This nationwide study included 604,250 Chinese outpatients with diagnosed dyslipidemia, comprising 331,222 males (54.8%) (Table [1](#Tab1)). Overall, 219,244 (36.3%) received LLT (Table [2](#Tab2)), while 385,006 (63.7%) remained untreated (Additional file 1: Table [2](#Tab2)).

### Risk stratified lipid control and management gaps

Risk stratified control rates for primary (LDL-C) and secondary (non-HDL-C) targets across epidemiological strata in the treated population are shown (Figs. [1](#Fig1) and [2](#Fig2)). Notably, higher ASCVD risk stratification was associated with lower control rates: while 95% of individuals in low-risk group achieved primary or secondary targets, rates dropped below 70–90% in moderate-risk, 40–50% in high-risk, and 5–15% in the very-high-risk group. Males demonstrated higher control rates than females across all risk stratification (Fig. [1](#Fig1)). Younger age groups outperformed others in low-, moderate-, and high-risk groups, whereas middle-aged individuals showed the poorest control in the low-risk group, and individuals > 45 years underperformed in moderate- and high-risk groups. Individuals with higher BMI, especially individuals with obesity, had poorer control across all risk stratification. Geographically, East/South/Southwest China showed lower control rates across low-to-high-risk groups, while North/Central/Northwest China had higher rates (Fig. [2](#Fig2)). Very-high-risk groups showed particularly poor control in North/Northwest/Northeast/East China. Provinces with the first/fourth tiers GDP underperformed in low-to-high-risk groups, while the third/fourth tiers GDP provinces had the poorest control in the very-high-risk group. Rural residents outperformed urban counterparts in low-to-high-risk groups, but underperformed in the very-high-risk group.

Among the untreated population, approximately 20% of individuals in the low-risk group required lipid management, compared to over 70% in the moderate- to high-risk groups and more than 97% in the very-high-risk group (Figs. [3](#Fig3) and [4](#Fig4)). Across all ASCVD risk stratification, females had higher treatment needs than males (Fig. [3](#Fig3)). The age distribution of treatment needs showed a complex pattern: in the low-risk group, middle-aged individuals had the highest unmet need, whereas in the moderate- and high-risk groups, younger individuals were more likely to require therapy. Geographically, the Northeast and South regions had the highest treatment needs (Fig. [4](#Fig4)). Urban residents had higher unmet needs than rural counterparts across all ASCVD risk stratification. Provinces in the lowest GDP quartile consistently showed the highest treatment needs across all risk stratification.

### Targeting the vulnerable: stratified analyses among subpopulations

Additionally, stratified analyses incorporating demographic, geographic, and socioeconomic variables were conducted. Among the treated population, females consistently showed lower LDL-C control rates than males, with the gender gap more pronounced in North China, urban areas, and the third tiers GDP provinces, but diminished in Central/Southwest/Northwest/South China, rural areas, and the fourth GDP provinces (Additional file 1: Table 3). Similarly, older individuals had lower LDL-C control rates than younger counterparts (Additional file 1: Table 4). This age-related disparity was more pronounced among urban residents and those in the first/fourth tiers GDP provinces, but attenuated in rural and the third tier GDP settings. No consistent geographic patterns were observed. Individuals with higher BMI consistently showed lower LDL-C control rates than those with lower BMI, though this disparity narrowed in rural and third tier GDP settings (Additional file 1: Table 5). Stratified analyses of second target control among treated population across all subgroups were also illustrated (Additional file 1: Tables 6, 7 and 8).

### Factors associated with lipid control among treated population and lipid management need among untreated population

After adjustment, females were more likely than males to achieve lipid targets (LDL-C: odds ratio \[OR\] 1.55, 95% confidence interval \[CI\] 1.51–1.60; non-HDL-C: OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.49–1.58) (Fig. [5](#Fig5)). Older age was associated with a lower likelihood of target achievement. Current smokers were less likely to achieve control compared with never-smokers (LDL-C: OR 0.63, 95% CI 0.61–0.66; non-HDL-C: OR 0.61, 95% CI 0.58–0.63). Rural residents also showed reduced odds of achieving lipid targets compared with urban residents (LDL-C: OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.81–0.86; non-HDL-C: OR 0.80, 95% CI 0.78–0.83). Participants living in lower-GDP regions, and those with hypertension or T2DM, were less likely to achieve control. Furthermore, among treated population, higher intensity of LLT was associated with poorer target achievement. After risk stratification, females were more likely than males to achieve lipid targets in the moderate-risk, high-risk, and very-high-risk groups, while no significant difference was observed in the low-risk group (Additional file 1: Table 9).

## Discussion

This nationwide study provides a contemporary picture of dyslipidemia management in China in a large outpatient sample of patients with diagnosed dyslipidemia. Rather than focusing mainly on overall prevalence or crude treatment/control rates, our study examined where management gaps are concentrated when patients are stratified by ASCVD risk as well as by demographic, geographic, and socioeconomic characteristics. Three findings merit particular attention. First, middle-aged individuals, those with higher BMI, and residents of urban or higher-GDP areas showed disproportionate burdens of adverse lipid profiles and suboptimal control, suggesting that socioeconomic development have not yet translated automatically into commensurate improvements in lipid management. Second, among treated population, control rate was substantially poorer in high- and very-high-risk groups. Third, among untreated population, unmet need for LLT remained considerable, especially in those at elevated ASCVD risk. Together, these findings highlight the need for more targeted, risk-based management strategies in routine care.

Levels of major lipids revealed disproportionate burdens of dyslipidemia across each epidemiological subgroup. Regional differences revealed complex patterns. Notably, high-GDP provinces did not exhibit better control and even underperformed in some risk groups. This counterintuitive pattern underscores the complexity of managing chronic conditions in populations undergoing profound dietary and lifestyle changes, where increased detection and survival may also contribute to the observed prevalence of poor control. Urban-rural disparities were complex: rural residents achieved better LDL-C control in low-to-moderate risk groups but fell behind in very-high risk groups and non-HDL-C control, indicating that tailored strategies are needed to address the distinct barriers faced by different risk strata across the urban-rural continuum.

This apparent disadvantage in crude analysis may initially seem inconsistent with previous studies reporting that women have greater awareness of dyslipidemia \[ [12](#ref-CR12 "Pan L, Yang Z, Wu Y, Yin R-X, Liao Y, Wang J, et al. In risk-stratified analyses, females in the treated population were more likely than males to achieve lipid targets in the moderate-, high-, and very-high-risk groups, whereas no significant sex difference was observed in the low-risk group. Among untreated participants, females were less likely than males to meet criteria for lipid management only in the moderate-risk group, with no significant differences in the other risk categories. Overall, these findings indicate that sex differences in lipid management may vary by baseline cardiovascular risk.

Older individuals, smokers, and patients with hypertension or T2DM exhibited lower rates of lipid control or higher treatment needs, which aligns with the elevated ASCVD risk observed in these epidemiological subgroups \[ [3](#ref-CR3 "Chong B, Jayabaskaran J, Jauhari SM, Chan SP, Goh R, Kueh MTW, et al. Our findings may have important policy implications. While China has made substantial progress in expanding healthcare coverage and statin accessibility over the past decade, the concurrent acceleration of dietary and lifestyle shifts during rapid urbanization has introduced new complexities in chronic disease management. Treatment and control rates, particularly among high-risk populations, have not fully kept pace with the rising burden. Building on the existing foundation of primary care infrastructure, our findings underscore the need for a shift from universal access to quality-targeted, risk-stratified interventions. A multi-faceted approach is essential to address these challenges. First, stronger national policy support is needed to promote behavioral interventions, such as increased physical activity, reduced sedentary behavior, and weight management, to jointly target dyslipidaemia, obesity, T2DM, and hypertension. Second, strengthening the primary care as the foundation of lipid management by integrating lipid control targets into national programs, such as the Equalization of National Basic Public Health Services. Third, enhancing multisectoral implementation by focusing on three key areas: supporting providers to deliver standardized care and patient education, improving patients’ health literacy and adherence, and leveraging community platforms to reinforce sustainable lifestyle changes. Lastly, targeted surveillance and intervention programs may be beneficial for populations with persistently low control rates and high treatment need, including urban residents, women, middle-aged adults, and individuals with high BMI, who may face overlapping biological, behavioral, and system-level barriers to lipid control.

The relevance of our findings extends beyond China. For developing countries undergoing rapid socioeconomic transition, our results offer two key lessons. First, economic growth does not automatically improve lipid control, as evidenced by poorer control in high-GDP provinces and urban areas. This aligns with global evidence that the epicenter of non-optimal cholesterol has shifted to East and Southeast Asia \[ [39](#ref-CR39 "NCD Risk Factor Collaboration (NCD-RisC). Repositioning of the global epicentre of non-optimal cholesterol. Nature. 2020;582:73–7.                    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2338-1                                    .")\]. Second, the substantial treatment gap among high-risk untreated individuals mirrors challenges in other low- and middle-income countries, where statin availability in public facilities remains limited \[ [40](#ref-CR40 "Li C, Spencer G, Husain MJ, Nugent R, Auzenne D, Kostova D, et al. Barriers to accessibility of medicines for hyperlipidemia in low- and middle-income countries. PLOS Glob Public Health. 2024;4:e0002905.                    https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0002905                                    .")\]. China’s experience with expanding insurance coverage for novel LLTs may inform similar policy efforts elsewhere. For high-income countries, our findings highlight shared challenges \[ [41](#ref-CR41 "Sheth S, Banach M, Toth PP. Closing the gap between guidelines and clinical practice for managing dyslipidemia: where are we now? Expert Rev Cardiovasc Ther. 2024;22:441–57.                    https://doi.org/10.1080/14779072.2024.2398444                                    ."), [42](#ref-CR42 "Guadamuz JS, Shooshtari A, Qato DM. Global, regional and national trends in statin utilisation in high-income and low/middle-income countries, 2015–2020. BMJ Open. 2022;12:e061350.                    https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2022-061350                                    .")\]. The “higher-risk, poorer-control” pattern we observed has also been documented in Western populations, suggesting that suboptimal risk-based management and difficulties in treatment intensification may not be unique to China. Additionally, China’s rapidly aging population, where middle-aged adults face both high lipid burden and poor control, offers lessons on prioritizing interventions across life stages relevant to other aging societies.

The strengths of this study include its nationwide scope, large sample size, and detailed stratification by demographic, geographic, and socioeconomic variables. This study also has several limitations. First, participants were recruited from outpatients with diagnosed dyslipidemia rather than from a community-based general population sample. Therefore, our findings are most applicable to patients who had already entered routine care and may not represent undiagnosed individuals or those with limited healthcare access. This sampling frame may overestimate treatment access and lipid control, while underrepresenting the detection gap and untreated burden in the broader community. Accordingly, our results should be interpreted as reflecting management gaps among Chinese outpatients with diagnosed dyslipidemia, rather than the entire Chinese population with dyslipidemia. Second, as hospital-level participation rates were not uniformly recorded across all regions, the extent to which our study sites represent the full spectrum of Chinese healthcare settings may be limited. Additionally, the reliance on a collaborative network with high engagement may introduce selection bias toward more specialized or well-resourced institutions. Third, ASCVD risk stratification was implemented using guideline-based rule classification with the clinical information available in this study. Because detailed information on recurrent ASCVD events and some related clinical variables was limited, we could not further distinguish very-high-risk from extremely-high-risk individuals, and secondary prevention patients were analyzed within the very-high-risk category. Fourth, as a study of dyslipidaemia patients rather than a general population sample, our findings cannot provide updated national prevalence estimates. Fifth, 99,107 participants (14.1%) were excluded because of incomplete lipid data. Based on the data management process, these missing data were mainly related to delayed upload of lipid results before database lock and other administrative or non-systematic data availability issues. However, some degree of selection bias remains possible, and the exclusion of these participants may have affected the estimation of regional and socioeconomic disparities. Sixth, self-reported data on LLT and cardiovascular history may be subject to recall bias, although standardized procedures were employed to improve accuracy. Seventh, lipid measurements were obtained from routine testing across multiple participating hospitals rather than from a central laboratory. Although standardized data checks were applied and biologically implausible extreme values were excluded during data cleaning, inter-laboratory heterogeneity in analyzers and assay methods may still have introduced some measurement variability. Eighth, ASCVD risk stratification was based on guideline-recommended variables available in the dataset. Because detailed ASCVD event history and some related clinical variables were limited, we were unable to further distinguish very-high-risk from extremely-high-risk individuals, which may have affected the precision of risk classification in the highest-risk subgroup. Ninth, ASCVD risk stratification incorporated historical peak lipid values recorded through the study questionnaire, with source-document verification available where possible. Because historical peak values may be subject to recall error, incomplete verification, and inter-laboratory variation, this approach may have introduced some misclassification of risk categories. In particular, the use of peak lipid values may overestimate ASCVD risk and unmet treatment need among untreated individuals, whereas using only recent on-treatment lipid values could underestimate baseline risk among treated individuals. Therefore, risk-stratified results should be interpreted with this trade-off in mind. Tenth, urban and rural residence was defined based on self-reported long-term place of residence. This may misclassify migrant populations who reside in urban areas but retain rural health insurance and face barriers to urban healthcare, potentially affecting the observed urban-rural differences in lipid management.

## Conclusions

In conclusion, this nationwide study provides a contemporary picture of dyslipidemia management among Chinese outpatients with diagnosed dyslipidemia. We identified three priority areas for action: substantial subgroup disparities in lipid burden, poorer target achievement among high-risk treated patients, and considerable unmet treatment needs among untreated high-risk patients. These findings support more equitable, risk-stratified management strategies within routine care and strengthened primary care pathways. Importantly, the challenges observed in China, including rising dyslipidemia prevalence, treatment gaps, and limited translation of risk stratification into clinical practice, are also common in many developing and aging societies. Lessons from China may therefore inform global efforts to improve lipid management and reduce the CVD burden.
