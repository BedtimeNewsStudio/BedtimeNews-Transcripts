# Latin America and the Caribbean

1 Overview (1) This is the guiding philosophy of Japan’s foreign policy toward LAC, advocated by Prime Minister Abe in 2014. The three “Juntos” (Together) refer to “Develop Together,” “Lead Together,” and “Inspire Together.” (1) Japan’s Positioning of Latin America and the Caribbean As the free and open international order based on the rule of law faces serious challenges, many countries in the Latin America and the Caribbean (LAC) region place importance on values and principles such as free-dom, democracy, the rule of law, and human rights. In recent years, strengthening cooperation with LAC coun-tries that share these values and principles has become increasingly important for Japan. The LAC region, which has a population of approximately 660 million and a gross regional product of approximately 7.1 tril-lion U.S. dollars and continues to post positive economic growth, is an important market. The region is also rich in critical mineral resources, energy, and food resources, and is growing its international prominence from the perspective of strengthening supply chain resilience and economic security as well. On the other hand, in coun-tries and regions where income inequality is severe, chal-lenges persist, including the activities of criminal orga-nizations such as drug cartels, a severe deterioration in public safety, irregular migration to North America, and the social integration of returning migrants, all of which also serve as impediments to further growth.
In the LAC region, there are the Nikkei (Japanese immigrants and their descendants) communities num-bering approximately 3.1 million, or about 60% of the world’s Nikkei population, who have fostered a tradi-tional sense of affinity toward Japan in LAC countries through contributions to the local areas for more than a century. On the other hand, generations have transi-tioned over the 100 years since the Japanese migration began, and the challenge is how to maintain and deepen ties between Japan and the communities going forward, including among the younger generation. From this per-spective, in addition to programs inviting members of the Nikkei community to Japan and dispatching Japanese students (exchanges with Nikkei of the same generation), MOFA has been implementing measures to strengthen cooperation with the Nikkei communities, including supporting the holding of events by the Nikkei commu-nity in various countries to promote network-building.
(2) Japanese Diplomacy toward Latin Amer-ica and the Caribbean Countries Japan’s diplomacy toward the LAC region has devel-oped under the guiding principle of the three “Juntos!!” (meaning “together”)(1) since 2014. In 2024, Prime Minister Kishida gave a policy speech on the LAC region in Brazil entitled “Paving a Pathway to ‘Human Dignity’ with Latin America and the Caribbean,” updat-ing Japan’s guideline for the first time in a decade. In the speech, he outlined the following three key directions: (a) Ensuring the free and open international order based on the rule of law, (b) Overcoming common challenges that humanity faces such as the environment and climate change, and (c) Pursuing prosperity that can be shared by all people in the world and does not come at others’ expense. Taking into account changes in the interna-tional community and the issues it faces, he indicated Japan’s resolve to work together with LAC countries to open a new path through dialogue. In addition, in the same year, Foreign Minister Kamikawa announced the Diplomacy Initiative toward LAC in Panama. The initiative pursues new forms of cooperation with LAC countries by integrating themes of growing importance, Latin America and the Caribbean Latin America and the Caribbean Section Section 4 DIPLOMATIC BLUEBOOK 2026 122 Chapter 2 Japan’s Foreign Policy by Region such as the oceans and gender equality, as well as Japan’s unique bond with the LAC region through the Nikkei community, into Japan’s traditional diplomatic efforts. Under this guideline for LAC diplomacy and the initia-tive to implement it, Japan’s diplomacy toward the LAC region deepened further in 2025.
In 2025, designated as the “Japan-Brazil Friendship and Exchange Year (the 130th anniversary of the estab-lishment of diplomatic relations between Japan and Brazil),” Japan hosted Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva as a State Guest in March, during which a summit meeting was held between Prime Minister Ishiba and President Lula, among other engagements. The year also coincided with the “Japan-SICA (Central American Integration System) Friendship Year,” during which President César Bernardo Arévalo de León of Guatemala, President José Raúl Mulino Quintero of Panama, and Prime Minister John Briceño of Belize visited Japan. In addition, ministers from Costa Rica, Honduras, and the Dominican Republic also visited Japan, further deepening relations between Japan and Central America. On the occasion of the National Day ceremonies of the respective countries at Expo 2025 Osaka, Kansai, held from April through October, 11 heads of state or government and 16 ministerial-level (2) CELAC: Comunidad de Estados Latinoamericanos y Caribeños (Community of Latin American and Caribbean States) (3) MERCOSUR: Mercado Común del Sur (Common Market of the South) dignitaries, including 13 foreign ministers, from the LAC region visited Japan, and meetings were held at various levels.
On the economic front, supply chain connections are being strengthened, including the continuing trend of increases in the number of Japanese compa-nies operating in the LAC region, which reached 3,095 in 2024. Japan is working on promoting free trade together with LAC countries including through the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), in which Mexico, Peru, and Chile also participate. In November of that year, the process began for Costa Rica’s accession to the CPTPP , and in November 2025, it was decided that the process for Uruguay’s accession to the Agreement would be initiated.
In the field of development cooperation, for some areas of the LAC region that have achieved economic growth, South-South cooperation is being accelerated by countries that have already “graduated” or will “graduate” from the status of recipient countries on the Development Assistance Committee (DAC) List of ODA Recipients by the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD). Japan is promoting triangular cooperation with these countries. 2 Regional Organizations In addition to the Community of Latin American and Caribbean States (CELAC),(2) the Organization of American States (OAS), and the Forum for East Asia-Latin America Cooperation (FEALAC), the following regional organizations in the LAC region conduct pol-icy coordination on various issues.
(1) Common Market of the South (MERCOS-UR)(3) MERCOSUR comprises Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay, and Bolivia (Venezuela is currently suspended), and with a few exceptions, tariffs have been eliminated for all goods traded within the region. Regarding the Free Trade Agreement (FTA) negotiations, talks are ongoing with the Republic of Korea (ROK), Canada, and the United Arab Emirates (UAE), among others. In January 2026, however, MERCOSUR signed an FTA with the European Union (EU).
In regard to relations with Japan, at a summit meet-ing between Prime Minister Ishiba and President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of Brazil in March, the two leaders confirmed that the “Strategic Partnership Framework between Japan and MERCOSUR” would be launched at an early date, and that consultations toward deepening trade relations would be advanced under the Framework. In December, the launch of the Framework was announced at the MERCOSUR Summit, and in January 2026, the first meeting under the Framework was held.

In 1908, Japanese emigration to Brazil began, and there-after, Japanese immigrants and their descendants (Nikkei) formed the world’s largest Nikkei community in Brazil. Today, approximately 2.7 million Japanese-Brazilians live in Brazil and 210,000 Brazilians reside in Japan. Th is special people-to-people exchange has served to solidify the bond between the two countries. Furthermore, since 2014, the two countries have been deepening their bilateral relations as Strategic and Global Partners, sharing values and principles such as freedom, democracy, and the rule of law. Th is partnership extends not only to politics, economy, and people-to-people exchange, but across a wide range of areas including public security, disaster risk reduction, criminal justice, the environment, and education.
The year 2025 marked the 130th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic relations between Japan and Brazil, which share deep historical ties. Designating the year as the “Japan-Brazil Friendship Exchange Year,” the two countries concurred on promoting cooperation in various fields, including cultural, tourism, and sports exchanges, as well as support for Japanese language education as a foundation for such exchanges. In this milestone year, Japan welcomed President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of Brazil as a State Guest in March. In addition, Her Imperial Highness Princess Kako of Akishino visited Brazil in June and interacted with locals across eight cities (São Paulo, Maringá, Rolândia, Londrina, Campo Grande, Brasília, Rio de Janeiro, and Foz do Iguaçu) over 11 days.
In addition, in commemoration of the “Japan-Brazil Friendship Exchange Year,” more than 200 events were held in Japan and over 550 events were held in Brazil. In Japan, the Asakusa Samba Carnival Parade Contest, marking its 40th edition in 2025, was held as part of these commemorative events and drew approximately 470,000 participants. In Brazil, singer AIKAWA Nanase, appointed as “Goodwill Ambassador for the Japan-Brazil Friendship Exchange Year,” participated in the 21st Londrina Festival and delivered a live performance that electrifi ed the audience. Her song is used for “Matsuri Dance” (an original dance performed with J-POP hits and Japanese traditional drums) and is widely popular in Brazil.
Th e friendship cultivated through the “Japan-Brazil Friendship Exchange Year” is further solidifying the relation-ship of trust built over 130 years between the governments and peoples of the two countries, bridging geographical distance. It is expected that bilateral relations will continue to develop even further, with this relationship of trust as their foundation.
