**Donald Martin Fehr** (born July 18, 1948)[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) is an American former sports executive and union leader. He was the fifth executive director of the [NHL Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_Players_Association "NHL Players Association") from 2010 to 2023. He became nationally prominent while serving as the executive director of the [MLB Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Players_Association "MLB Players Association") from 1983 to 2009.

## Life and career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Donald_Fehr&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Life and career")\]

Fehr's parents are Irene Sylvia ( _née_ Gulko) and Louis Alvin Fehr, of German-Jewish descent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) He was raised in [Prairie Village, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Village,_Kansas "Prairie Village, Kansas"). He graduated from [Indiana University Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington "Indiana University Bloomington") in 1970 with a degree in political science and was a member of [Sigma Alpha Mu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Alpha_Mu "Sigma Alpha Mu") fraternity. Fehr received his [J.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor "Juris Doctor")[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) degree from the [University of Missouri-Kansas City School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri-Kansas_City_School_of_Law "University of Missouri-Kansas City School of Law").[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)

### MLBPA

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Donald_Fehr&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: MLBPA")\]

As a young lawyer, Fehr assisted the MLBPA in the [Andy Messersmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Messersmith "Andy Messersmith") and [Dave McNally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_McNally "Dave McNally") arbitration case (later known as the [Seitz decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seitz_decision "Seitz decision")). In 1977, [Marvin Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Miller "Marvin Miller") hired Fehr as the Players Association general counsel.

In December 1985, Fehr was voted executive director of the MLBPA after having served as acting director since December 9, 1983. Fehr successfully challenged the owners' [collusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_collusion "Baseball collusion"), leading to the owners paying $280 million in damages to the players.

Fehr led the players union through the [1994–95 Major League Baseball strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_Major_League_Baseball_strike "1994–95 Major League Baseball strike") and subsequent [World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_World_Series "1994 World Series") cancellation. He was instrumental in implementing the rejection of future admissions into the MLBPA of replacement players who planned to fill in during the strike of 1995. Fehr attended the [1995 New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_New_York_Yankees_season "1995 New York Yankees season")' home opener against the [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Texas_Rangers_season "1995 Texas Rangers season"), which saw only 50,425 fans show up making it was the smallest opening day crowd at [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium_(1923) "Yankee Stadium (1923)") since [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_New_York_Yankees_season "1990 New York Yankees season").[&#8202;](#ref-back-ActOfWar-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-ActOfWar) Fehr's presence angered many fans who blamed him for ruining their team's postseason chances and what would have been [Don Mattingly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Mattingly "Don Mattingly")'s postseason debut.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fehr-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-Fehr) Fans booed Fehr and yelled "You ruined the game!"[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fehr-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-Fehr) in response to him having attended the final game played at Yankee Stadium before the strike, and booed as he left the stadium;[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) one fan also held up a sign saying " _$HAME ON YOU!_",[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) to which Fehr responded by [flipping off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_finger "The finger") the fan.

On June 22, 2009, Fehr announced his intention to step down as the MLBPA executive director position, recommending [Michael Weiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Weiner_(executive) "Michael Weiner (executive)") as his successor. This was subject to the approval of the union's executive board and possible ratification by all players.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) He officially relinquished his job to Weiner in December 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

On September 8, 2021, twelve years after leaving Major League Baseball, Fehr delivered the Hall of Fame induction speech for former MLBPA Executive Director [Marvin Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Miller "Marvin Miller"), who was elected in 2020, eight years after his death.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

### NHLPA

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Donald_Fehr&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: NHLPA")\]

Shortly after leaving his position as executive director of the MLBPA, Fehr took up a position as an advisor to the NHL Players' Association. On December 18, 2010, Fehr was voted in by the NHLPA as their executive director.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

With the NHL locking out the players at midnight on September 15, 2012, Fehr became the only executive director of a players' union to be directly involved in work stoppages in two sports. Six of the eight contract negotiations he has been involved in have resulted in work stoppages, including five consecutive negotiations between the MLBPA and Major League Baseball.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)

In an October 2021 report detailing the sexual assault of [Kyle Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Beach "Kyle Beach") by the [Chicago Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks "Chicago Blackhawks")’ video coach Brad Aldrich and ensuing coverup by the organization revealed Beach reported this assault to Fehr, who ultimately did nothing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18) In an October 28 statement, Fehr admitted the NHLPA failed Beach.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)

Fehr left his NHLPA post in February 2023, replaced by former [United States Secretary of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Labor "United States Secretary of Labor") [Marty Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Walsh "Marty Walsh").[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)
