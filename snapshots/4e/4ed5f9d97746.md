| Killing bid conviction 'unsafe' |
| **A would-be killer jailed for trying to murder a friend with non-toxic mercury has won a first stage towards having his conviction quashed.**

Edward Latham, 25, from Nottingham, but of no fixed address, was jailed for life after he pleaded guilty to the attempted murder of Sarah Smith.

Three Appeal Court judges have ruled that Latham had "arguable grounds" that his conviction was "unsafe".

They gave him permission to take his case to a full appeal hearing.

**'Flatmates'**

The prosecution in the case claimed Latham hoped he could obtain £750,000 from the family of Mrs Smith, from Derbyshire, by killing her.

The judges heard Latham was receiving psychiatric treatment at the Chesterfield Royal Hospital in December 2000 when he met Mrs Smith and the two struck up a friendship.

They later moved in together as flatmates.

Latham - who had been in and out of psychiatric hospitals for a number of years - said he was feeling unwell on 12 December 2000 and asked for a mercury thermometer.

**'Non-toxic thermometer'**

It was said by the prosecution that he then broke the thermometer, took the mercury out and placed it into a glass of cola which he then gave to Mrs Smith.

It was the Crown case that he believed the dose would be fatal - unaware that mercury from a thermometer was non-toxic.

He was arrested after telling his parents that he had tried to kill Miss Smith.
