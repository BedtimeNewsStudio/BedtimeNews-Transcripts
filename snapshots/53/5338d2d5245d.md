The **Warsaw radio mast** ( [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language "Polish language"): _Maszt radiowy w Konstantynowie_, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation "Literal translation")'[Konstantynów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantyn%C3%B3w,_P%C5%82ock_County "Konstantynów, Płock County") radio mast') was a [radio mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_masts_and_towers "Radio masts and towers") located near [Gąbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%85bin "Gąbin"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland"), and was the [world's tallest structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_and_structures_in_the_world "List of tallest buildings and structures in the world") at 646.30 m (2,120 ft) from 1974 until its collapse on 8 August 1991.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kohlstedt-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-Kohlstedt) The mast was designed with extreme height to broadcast Polish state media around the world, including to parts of [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada") and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), and even reaching [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kępa-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-Kępa)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Polish_Film_Chronicles_nr_39_1991_on_Warsaw_Radio_Mast_w-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Polish_Film_Chronicles_nr_39_1991_on_Warsaw_Radio_Mast_w) The official name of the facility was _Radiofoniczny Ośrodek Nadawczy w Konstantynowie_ (Radiophonic Transmission Centre Konstantynów), _Radiowe Centrum Nadawcze w Konstantynowie_ (Radio Transmission Centre Konstantynów; RCN Konstantynów) or _Warszawska Radiostacja Centralna (WRC) w Gąbinie_ (Warsaw Central Radio Station Gąbin). It broadcast [Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio "Polskie Radio")'s [Program I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio_Program_I "Polskie Radio Program I") (unofficially referred to as "Jedynka").[&#8202;](#ref-back-radiopolska.pl-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-radiopolska.pl)

Designed by Jan Polak, its construction started with [earthworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthworks_(engineering) "Earthworks (engineering)") for the foundations on 5 July 1969,[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) while construction of the tower itself began on 18 October 1972 with a ceremony, and was completed on 18 May 1974. Its transmitter, whose installation started in October 1973, entered regular service on 22July 1974.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Andruszkiewicz-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-Andruszkiewicz) The opening of the mast was met with extensive celebration and was covered by the [Polish Film Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Film_Chronicle "Polish Film Chronicle").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Polish_Film_Chronicles_nr_39_1991_on_Warsaw_Radio_Mast_w-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-Polish_Film_Chronicles_nr_39_1991_on_Warsaw_Radio_Mast_w)[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) The tower was used by Warsaw Radio-Television (Centrum Radiowo-Telewizyjne) for radio broadcasting on a [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency "Radio frequency") of AM-LW ( [longwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longwave "Longwave")) 227 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") before 1February 1988 and 225 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") (1332metres) afterwards.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) Its base was 115.2 metres (378ft) above sea level. Because it had a [potential difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_difference "Potential difference") of 120kV during operation,[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) it stood on a 2-metre-high (6.6ft) [insulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_(electricity) "Insulator (electricity)"). It operated as a [mast radiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mast_radiator "Mast radiator") ( [half-wave radiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-wave_radiator "Half-wave radiator")), so its height was half of its 1332-metre broadcasting [wavelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength "Wavelength"). The signals from its 2 [MW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatt "Megawatt") transmitters could be received across essentially the entire globe. The structure's weight was debated, with some Polish sources claiming it weighed 420 tonnes (930,000lb).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kohlstedt-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-Kohlstedt)

Upon its construction, it surpassed the height of the then-tallest structure in the world, the [KVLY-TV mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVLY-TV_mast "KVLY-TV mast") in [Blanchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanchard,_North_Dakota "Blanchard, North Dakota"), [North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota "North Dakota"), United States. Following its collapse, the KVLY-TV mast once again became the tallest structure in the world until 2009. After 1991, the [tallest structure in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_structures_in_Poland "List of tallest structures in Poland") has been the [FM- and TV-mast Kosztowy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM-_and_TV-mast_Olsztyn-Pieczewo "FM- and TV-mast Olsztyn-Pieczewo"), 358.7-metre-tall (1,177ft) tall.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kohlstedt-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-Kohlstedt)[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) As of 2026, it was the third-tallest artificial structure ever built, after the [Burj Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burj_Khalifa "Burj Khalifa") tower in the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") in 2009, and [Merdeka 118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka_118 "Merdeka 118") tower in [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") in 2022.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kohlstedt-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-Kohlstedt)

## Construction

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Warsaw_radio_mast&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Construction")\]

The mast's location, which was finalised to be in Gąbin, was chosen because of its high [soil conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_resistivity?action=edit&redlink=1 "Soil resistivity (page does not exist)") and the need for a position in the centre of [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) A large amount of research was used to decide where to build the mast, which included factors such as soil resistance, ownership of property, and the quality of infrastructure (especially roads).

The mast was initially constructed to replace the [Raszyn radio transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raszyn_radio_transmitter "Raszyn radio transmitter"), which had been constructed in 1931 and rebuilt in 1949. The Raszyn transmitter was designed to cover the [Eastern Borderlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kresy "Kresy"), and so Polish authorities did not consider reception in the [Recovered Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovered_Territories "Recovered Territories"). When it became evident that Raszyn could not provide coverage to the entire country, the station received an increase of power to 500kW, but this too failed. In the late 1960s it was decided to construct a new central transmitter at the geographical centre of Poland (post-WWII borders). Gąbin was chosen not only due to its central position, but also its high [soil conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_resistivity?action=edit&redlink=1 "Soil resistivity (page does not exist)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-radiopolska.pl-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-radiopolska.pl) During storms, when the mast could not operate, the signal would be temporarily switched to Raszyn, a process that was eased thanks to direct phone lines from RCN Konstantynów to both Raszyn and the energy sector.[&#8202;](#ref-back-radiopolska.pl-2)[\[12\]](#refnote-radiopolska.pl)

The Warsaw radio mast was a [guyed steel lattice mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_masts_and_towers#Steel_lattice "Radio masts and towers") of equilateral triangular cross section, with a face width of 4.8 metres (16ft). The vertical steel tubes forming the structures of the mast had a [diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diameter "Diameter") of 245millimetres (10in); the thickness of the walls of these tubes varied between 8–34mm (0.31–1.33in) depending on height. The mast consisted of 86sections, each of which had a length of 7.5 metres (25ft). The mast had 3arrays of [guy wires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_wire "Guy wire"), each array attached to the mast at 5levels: 121.78 metres (399.5ft), 256.78 metres (842.5ft), 369.28 metres (1,211.5ft), 481.78 metres (1,580.6ft), and 594.28 metres (1,949.7ft) above ground.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Andruszkiewicz-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-Andruszkiewicz) Each guy was fixed on a separate anchor block at the ground and was 50 millimetres (2.0in) in diameter. To prevent the guy wires from interfering with the radio transmissions, the guys were insulated at regular intervals. The weight of guys and insulators used to anchor the mast was 80 tonnes (180,000lb).[&#8202;](#ref-back-O-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-O)[&#8202;](#ref-back-skyscraperpage.com-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-skyscraperpage.com) An elevator and separate protected ladders were installed in the interior of the mast to facilitate access to the various mast components, including the [aircraft warning lamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_warning_lamps "Aircraft warning lamps"). The elevator was designed by [Alimak Hek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alimak_Hek?action=edit&redlink=1 "Alimak Hek (page does not exist)")\[ [sv](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alimak%20Hek "sv:Alimak Hek")\], a Swedish company, and took 45 minutes to reach the top. The lift was nicknamed "Alimak", as a nod to the company that designed it. Safety was a priority, so "Alimak" was provided with an emergency stop function, but no incidents occurred that might have required it. A maximum of 3 people could ride up the elevator, although sources claim 3 people would have felt crowded. The elevator was powered by an [internal combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine"). An area for maintenance supplies was present, in order to transport materials such as [lightbulbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_warning_lights "Aircraft warning lights") for the [aircraft warning lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_warning_lights "Aircraft warning lights"). Inspection crews travelled up it once per month to replace light bulbs, and to watch for cracks in the steel. If a crack was found, the transmitters were turned off and the Raszyn radio mast took over the broadcast. Any cracks were then fixed by welders from Mostostal Zabrze by patching them with sheet metal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioPolska-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-RadioPolska)

In the lower half of the mast, there was a vertical steel tube, attached to the mast's outer structure with large [insulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_(electricity) "Insulator (electricity)"). This tube was grounded at the bottom, and connected electrically to the mast structure by an adjustable metal bar at a height of 328.68 metres (1,078.3ft) when the tower transmitted on 227 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") [longwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longwave "Longwave") and at a height of 334.18 metres (1,096.4ft) when it [switched to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Frequency_Plan_of_1975 "Geneva Frequency Plan of 1975") 225 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") on 1February 1988.[&#8202;](#ref-back-skyscraperpage.com-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-skyscraperpage.com) This technique allowed adjusting the impedance of the mast for the transmitter and worked by applying a [direct current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current "Direct current") ground at a point of low [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency "Radio frequency") voltage, to conduct static charge to ground without diminishing the radio energy. Static electrical charge can build up to high values, even at times of no thunderstorm activity, when such tall structures are insulated from ground. Use of this technique provides better lightning protection than using just a spark gap at the mast base, as it is standard at most mast radiators insulated against ground.[&#8202;](#ref-back-O-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-O)

The mast was equipped in 16 levels with arrays of six air traffic warning lights with 200 W power. At the top there was a flashing beacon consisting of two lamps of 1,000 W.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioPolska-1)[\[16\]](#refnote-RadioPolska)[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Gabin_radio_mast2.png/250px-Gabin_radio_mast2.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gabin_radio_mast2.png) A map of the facility and location

A 600-metre (2,000ft) long special [overhead radio frequency transmission line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency_power_transmission "Radio frequency power transmission") of 120 [ohms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm "Ohm") was used to transfer the signal from the transmitter building to the mast. Featuring an [asymmetrical coaxial power supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxial_power_connector "Coaxial power connector"), it consisted of the inner "hot" core of 24 copper wires connected to a small ring at each post, and each was connected by three insulated cables to the outer ring. The outer ring served as the support of another set of 24 wires, known as the "outer braid." The transmission line was monitored whenever the mast was in operation; at night the line was illuminated with street lights, to prevent trespassers from electrocuting themselves.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl) The transmitter building had a volume of 17,000 cubic metres (600,000ft3) and was approximately 600 metres (2,000ft) from the mast. The transmitter consisted of two 1,000kW units built by [Brown, Boveri & Cie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown,_Boveri_&_Cie "Brown, Boveri & Cie"). An [atomic clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_clock "Atomic clock") was used to generate the transmission frequency in order to provide a very accurate, stable signal source which could be used as a frequency standard by anyone within signal range. The transmitters had the ability for each component to be turned on manually or all be started with one button. The transmitters were capable of receiving remote input, but the devices needed to use this were not implemented in Konstanynów. When high-voltage power was switched on, the transmitter was protected by two locked doors and crews were informed by way of illuminating a red lightbulb. This lightbulb was controlled by a [photodiode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photodiode "Photodiode") that would additionally flash the lightbulb if a short-circuit in the transmitter was detected.

The station, which had an area of 65ha (160 acres), also had a 76-metre (249ft) lattice tower of rectangular cross-section close to the transmitter building. This tower was used to provide a [radio link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_transmitter_link "Studio transmitter link") for programme feeds from the studio, which ran from the [Palace of Culture and Science, Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_and_Science,_Warsaw "Palace of Culture and Science, Warsaw") via a radio relay tower at [Wiejca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiejca "Wiejca").[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl) At Konstantynów, the signal was [focused](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_(optics) "Focus (optics)") on a [parabolic dish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolic_antenna "Parabolic antenna") at 30dB and was captured in a [horn antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_antenna "Horn antenna") at the focal point of the dish. Upon reaching the transmitters, a dehydrator ensured that no condensation occurred. The transmission building used a [NEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEC "NEC") radio set and the TN60 multiple telephony system. This allowed for 60 telephone lines connected to the [Polska Poczta, Telegraf i Telefon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polska_Poczta,_Telegraf_i_Telefon "Polska Poczta, Telegraf i Telefon") fixed-line telephone network to be run off the station but the full possibilities of this system were never used.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)

To supply power to the station, a 110kV [substation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_substation "Electrical substation") was built. The substation was [over-engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overengineering "Overengineering") due to the strategic importance of the station as Poland's central transmitter: although the power consumption of the transmitting station was large at an estimated 6,000kW, the substation was capable of supplying much more. Six small towers were erected around the periphery of the station's grounds in order to support [aircraft warning lamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_warning_lights "Aircraft warning lights") where the guy ropes were located.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Miś-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-Miś)

## Problems

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Warsaw_radio_mast&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Problems")\]

Approximately ten years after completion of the mast, inspections revealed structural damage caused by wind-induced oscillations at the mast, the backstay [insulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_(electricity) "Insulator (electricity)") and the guys. Repair work was considered to be expensive and difficult, so replacement of the mast by a stronger construction of the same height was considered; however, this was not carried out due to Poland's worsening economic situation. Repainting was started in 1988, but could not be completed because not enough paint was available.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) A lack of equipment and training of maintenance teams led to a severe degradation of the mast's condition by the late 1980s. An inspection in May 1989 found that 13 strands of the upper guys had been fractured and, by the time of the collapse of the mast in 1991, seven guy wire [insulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_(electricity) "Insulator (electricity)") had been damaged. Another cause of the structure's deterioration was insufficient knowledge of the [strains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strain_(mechanics) "Strain (mechanics)") exerted on structures of such a great height. Additionally, on 23 March 1992, Adam Brzeziński, managing director of [Mostostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostostal "Mostostal") Zabrze at the time, stated to [Dziennik Zachodni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dziennik_Zachodni "Dziennik Zachodni") that Mostostal should have prepared a technical report before starting construction on the risks faced during repair of a strained mast.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-2)[\[22\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl)

## Staff

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Warsaw_radio_mast&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Staff")\]

The manager of the radio station from 1974 to 1980 was Zygmunt Duczmalewski (died 2016). His successor, Witold Czowgan (1947–2017), served as the manager from 1983 to 1986.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-6)[\[26\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl)

The mast had a total of 30 engineers and technicians. It also had 15 administrative staff. Because a large portion of the staff was recruited from around Poland, a new [block of flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_panel_system-building "Large panel system-building") was built 30km (19mi) away in [Sochaczew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochaczew "Sochaczew") to accommodate the staff. More specialised staff were accommodated in the guest rooms of the transmission building. The facility also had a complement of guards, hired directly by the [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_(Poland) "Ministry of Interior (Poland)"), mainly from the local population of Konstantynów.[&#8202;](#ref-back-O-5)[\[14\]](#refnote-O)[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

## Collapse

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Warsaw_radio_mast&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Collapse")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D1%80%D1%83%D1%85%D0%BD%D1%83%D0%B2%D1%88%D0%B5%D0%B9_%D0%92%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%88%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B9_%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%87%D1%82%D1%8B.jpg/250px-%D0%9E%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D1%80%D1%83%D1%85%D0%BD%D1%83%D0%B2%D1%88%D0%B5%D0%B9_%D0%92%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%88%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B9_%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%87%D1%82%D1%8B.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:%D0%9E%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D1%80%D1%83%D1%85%D0%BD%D1%83%D0%B2%D1%88%D0%B5%D0%B9_%D0%92%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%88%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B9_%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%87%D1%82%D1%8B.jpg) The mast in January, 1992, five months after its collapse.

At 16:00 UTC (17.00 local time) on 8 August 1991 a [catastrophic failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catastrophic_failure "Catastrophic failure") led to the collapse of the mast. During replacement of frayed guy wires, one of the main cables had to be replaced by two temporary ones. After the main cable had been disconnected and before the temporary ones could be connected, a gust of wind twisted the temporarily unmoored tower, pulling loose the other guys. The unsupported mast first bent, and then snapped at roughly half its height. The [helix building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helix_building "Helix building") and the [transmitter building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmitter_building "Transmitter building") and transmitter were not damaged.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) The transmission line was also unaffected, but it was dismantled shortly after the collapse of the tower, leaving only the sustaining poles in place. A mobile crane belonging to Mostostal Zabrze was destroyed when the tower collapsed. As the foreman did not keep a construction log it was hard to determine the exact events that led to the catastrophe.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-7)[\[22\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl) The construction coordinator and the division chief of the company responsible for maintaining the mast were found liable for the collapse, and both were sentenced to 2.5 years in prison. The construction manager's sentence was eventually shortened to six months.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kohlstedt-8)[\[1\]](#refnote-Kohlstedt)[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

## Replacement

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Warsaw_radio_mast&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Replacement")\]

After the collapse, Polskie Radio used the old [Raszyn transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longwave_transmitter_Raszyn "Longwave transmitter Raszyn") with its 335-metre (1,099ft) mast near [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw"), which had been used since 1978 for daytime transmissions of a [second Polish broadcasting service programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio_Program_II "Polskie Radio Program II") in the longwave range on the [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency "Radio frequency") AM-LW 198 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz"), for transmissions on AM-LW 225 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") with a power of 500kW.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-8)[\[31\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl) It is not possible to transmit from Raszyn on AM-LW (long wave) 198 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz")/1515metres and 225 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz")/1333metres simultaneously due to risk of [interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interference_(communication) "Interference (communication)"), so the transmissions on the second longwave frequency AM-LW (long wave) 198 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") had to be discontinued until either a second longwave broadcasting transmitting facility was built in Poland or a special frequency switch, which would allow transmissions on both frequencies, was installed at the Raszyn transmitter. The latter, simpler solution would have decreased the effectiveness and reliability of both transmitters and was therefore rejected.[&#8202;](#ref-back-radiopolska.pl-3)[\[4\]](#refnote-radiopolska.pl)

Because of the importance of Polish longwave transmitters to [Polish people abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_diaspora "Polish diaspora"), as early as April1992 the Polish government planned to rebuild the mast at Konstantynów.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32) In September1995 the Polish government was set to rebuild the mast. Although refurbishment of the old foundations, which could be reused, had already started, the rebuilding of the mast was cancelled due to protests by local residents, who claimed that radiation from the mast was a health hazard.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33) While the accuracy of these claims has not been verified, a new site for the transmitter was sought. Several other locations were considered, but due to the continuing resistance of nearby inhabitants (backed by the [Solidarity trade union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_(Polish_trade_union) "Solidarity (Polish trade union)")), planned mast height and transmitter range were both greatly reduced, and an old military site just southeast of [Solec Kujawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longwave-transmitter_Solec_Kujawski "Longwave-transmitter Solec Kujawski") was chosen.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34) There were once again protests in this location, but tensions were eased when Polish Radio donated PLN 3.5 million towards the development of the local community.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35) There, a new longwave transmission facility was built in 1998–1999, with a transmitter of 1200kW output power for the frequency AM-LW (long wave) 225 [kHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz"). This facility, which was inaugurated on 4September 1999, uses one 330-metre (1,080ft) and one 289-metre (948ft) grounded top-fed masts as [aerials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_(radio) "Antenna (radio)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-radiopolska.pl-4)[\[4\]](#refnote-radiopolska.pl)

After the inauguration of the transmitter at Solec Kujawski, the transmitter at Raszyn was again used for transmitting on the [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency "Radio frequency") AM-LW (long wave) 198kHz for [Polskie Radio Parlament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio_Parlament?action=edit&redlink=1 "Polskie Radio Parlament (page does not exist)")\[ [pl](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie%20Radio%20Parlament "pl:Polskie Radio Parlament")\] until it ended LW transmissions in 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)

At Gąbin, there was also a [radio link relay tower](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maszt%20radiolinii%20w%20Konstantynowie "pl:Maszt radiolinii w Konstantynowie"), commissioned in 1974, last used in 1998 and demolished in 2021. It was used as a radio relay link to receive Polskie Radio Program I programming from the microwave link at the [RTCN Warszawa (PKiN) transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_and_Science#RTCN_Warszawa_(PKiN)_transmitter "Palace of Culture and Science") to Konstantynów. In front of the entrance to the mast, a cross was erected by local residents associated with the now defunct _Association for the Protection of Human Life at the Highest Mast of Europe._[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-9)[\[31\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl)

From 2001 to 2012, the facility was managed by the Real Estate department of Telekomunikacja Polska S.A. (TP SA). Due to the 2012 merger of TP and [Orange Polska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Polska "Orange Polska"), the facility is now managed by the Orange Polska SA Real Estate Sales Office. The facility is protected against unauthorised access, as it is worth approximately [PLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_z%C5%82oty "Polish złoty") 3,650,000 ($1 million [USD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")).[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)

## State since 1991

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Warsaw_radio_mast&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: State since 1991")\]

Except for the mast and the [radio-frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-frequency "Radio-frequency") [transmission line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_line "Transmission line") that led to it, nearly all components of the facility have remained in place, unused and slowly deteriorating. Some local people have visited the remains of the mast on anniversaries, such as the 20th anniversary in 2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl-10)[\[26\]](#refnote-www.rcnkonstantynow.pl)

In 2018, the Swiss [Brown, Boveri & Cie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown,_Boveri_&_Cie "Brown, Boveri & Cie") 2 piece radio transmitter (Brown Boveri SL 61 B3) was donated by Orange Polska, a Polish telecommunications provider, to the _Babice Transatlantic Radio Station Culture Park Association_. It was then transferred to [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw") for renovation. There are plans to restore the transmitter for active usage in a museum.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Miś-4)[\[20\]](#refnote-Miś)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Now_Gąbin_|_Equipment_from_the_Radio_Center_in_Konstantynów_will_get_a_new_life-1)[\[24\]](#refnote-Now_Gąbin_|_Equipment_from_the_Radio_Center_in_Konstantynów_will_get_a_new_life)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)

At the end of September 2021, the Konstantynów relay tower was decommissioned and demolished.

## In popular culture

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Warsaw_radio_mast&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: In popular culture")\]

The Warsaw radio mast was added to _[The Guinness Book of World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guinness_Book_of_World_Records "The Guinness Book of World Records")_ for the mast's height, which was later surpassed by the [Burj Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burj_Khalifa "Burj Khalifa").[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39)

The Warsaw Radio mast's successor and predecessor, the [Raszyn Mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raszyn_radio_transmitter "Raszyn radio transmitter"), was depicted on several stamps issued by the [Polish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_government "Polish government").[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40)[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41)[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42)

The _[Polish Film Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Film_Chronicle "Polish Film Chronicle")_, a Polish [newsreel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsreel "Newsreel") typically shown in cinemas before a movie, showed the Warsaw Radio Mast.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Polish_Film_Chronicles_nr_39_1991_on_Warsaw_Radio_Mast_w-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-Polish_Film_Chronicles_nr_39_1991_on_Warsaw_Radio_Mast_w)[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43)
