KGB

The **Committee for State Security** ( [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Комитет государственной безопасности, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian "Romanization of Russian"):_Komitet gosudarstvennoy bezopasnosti_, IPA:[\[kəmʲɪˈtʲedɡəsʊˈdarstvʲɪn(ː)əjbʲɪzɐˈpasnəsʲtʲɪ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian "Help:IPA/Russian")), abbreviated as **KGB** ( [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): КГБ, IPA:[\[ˌkɛɡɛˈbɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian "Help:IPA/Russian"); [listen to both](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e0/Ru-KGB.ogg/Ru-KGB.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ru-KGB.ogg "File:Ru-KGB.ogg")) was the main [security agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_agency "Security agency") of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") from 1954 to 1991. It was the direct successor of preceding Soviet [secret police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_police "Secret police") agencies including the [Cheka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheka "Cheka"), [OGPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_State_Political_Directorate "Joint State Political Directorate"), and [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD "NKVD"). Attached to the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_the_Soviet_Union "Council of Ministers of the Soviet Union"), it was the chief government agency of "union-republican jurisdiction", carrying out internal security, [foreign intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_agency "Intelligence agency"), [counter-intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-intelligence "Counter-intelligence") and [secret police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_police "Secret police") functions. Similar agencies operated in each of the [republics of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union "Republics of the Soviet Union") aside from the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic "Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic"), where the KGB was headquartered, with many associated ministries, state committees and state commissions.

The agency was a [military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_service "Military service") governed by army laws and regulations, in the same fashion as the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Army "Soviet Army") or the [MVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Internal_Affairs_(Soviet_Union) "Ministry of Internal Affairs (Soviet Union)") [Internal Troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Troops "Internal Troops"). While most of the KGB archives remain classified, two online documentary sources are available.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) Its main functions were [foreign intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_intelligence "Foreign intelligence"), counter-intelligence, operative-investigative activities, guarding the state border of the USSR, guarding the Soviet leadership, preserving the security of government communications as well as combating [dissent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_dissidents "Soviet dissidents") and separatism within Soviet society.

On 3 December 1991, the KGB was officially dissolved.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) It was succeeded in Russia by the [Foreign Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Intelligence_Service_(Russia) "Foreign Intelligence Service (Russia)") (SVR) and what would later become the [Federal Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Security_Service "Federal Security Service") (FSB). Following the [1991–1992 South Ossetia War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Ossetia_war_(1991%E2%80%931992) "South Ossetia war (1991–1992)"), the self-proclaimed [Republic of South Ossetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Ossetia "Republic of South Ossetia") established its own KGB, keeping the unreformed name.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) In addition, [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus "Belarus") established its successor to the [KGB of the Byelorussian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_State_Security_of_the_Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic "Committee for State Security of the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic") in 1991, the [Belarusian KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Security_Committee_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus "State Security Committee of the Republic of Belarus"), also keeping the unreformed name.

## History

Further information: [Chronology of Soviet secret police agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology_of_Soviet_secret_police_agencies "Chronology of Soviet secret police agencies")

See also: [Cheka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheka "Cheka"), [OGPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OGPU "OGPU"), [NKGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKGB "NKGB"), and [Ministry of State Security (Soviet Union)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_State_Security_(Soviet_Union) "Ministry of State Security (Soviet Union)")

[Restructuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restructuring "Restructuring") in the [MVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Internal_Affairs_(Soviet_Union) "Ministry of Internal Affairs (Soviet Union)") following the fall of [Lavrentiy Beria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavrentiy_Beria "Lavrentiy Beria") in June 1953 resulted in the formation of the KGB under [Ivan Serov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Serov "Ivan Serov") in March 1954.

[Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "General Secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union") [Leonid Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev "Leonid Brezhnev") overthrew [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_Soviet_Union "Premier of the Soviet Union") [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev "Nikita Khrushchev") in 1964. Brezhnev (in power: 1964–1982) was concerned about ambitious spy-chiefs – the [communist party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Communist Party of the Soviet Union") had managed Serov's successor, the ambitious KGB Chairman, [Aleksandr Shelepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Shelepin "Aleksandr Shelepin") (in office: 1958–1961), but Shelepin carried out Brezhnev's palace _coup d'état_ against Khrushchev in 1964 (despite Shelepin not then being in the KGB). Brezhnev sacked Shelepin's successor and protégé, [Vladimir Semichastny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Semichastny "Vladimir Semichastny") (in office: 1961–1967) as KGB Chairman and reassigned him to a [sinecure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinecure "Sinecure") in the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic "Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic"). Shelepin found himself demoted from the chairman of the Committee of Party and State Control in 1965 to [Trade Union Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Union_Central_Council_of_Trade_Unions "All-Union Central Council of Trade Unions") chairman (in office 1967–1975).

In the 1980s, the Soviet Union [glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost "Glasnost") provoked KGB Chairman [Vladimir Kryuchkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Kryuchkov "Vladimir Kryuchkov") (in office: 1988–1991) to lead the August [1991 Soviet _coup d'état_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt "1991 Soviet coup d'état attempt") in an attempt to depose President [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev"). The failed _coup d'état_ and the [collapse of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union") heralded the end of the KGB on 3 December 1991. The KGB's modern-day successors are the FSB ( [Federal Security Service of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Security_Service_(Russia) "Federal Security Service (Russia)")) and the SVR ( [Foreign Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Intelligence_Service_(Russia) "Foreign Intelligence Service (Russia)")).

## In the US

### Between the World Wars

The [GRU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRU_(Soviet_Union) "GRU (Soviet Union)") (Foreign military intelligence service of the Soviet Union) recruited the ideological agent [Julian Wadleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Wadleigh "Julian Wadleigh"), who became a State Department diplomat in 1936. The [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD "NKVD")'s first US operation was establishing the legal residency of [Boris Bazarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Bazarov "Boris Bazarov") and the illegal residency of [Iskhak Akhmerov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskhak_Akhmerov "Iskhak Akhmerov") in 1934.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999104-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999104) Throughout, the [Communist Party USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA "Communist Party USA") (CPUSA) and its General Secretary [Earl Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Browder "Earl Browder"), helped NKVD recruit Americans, working in government, business, and industry.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

Other important, low-level and high-level ideological agents were the diplomats [Laurence Duggan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Duggan "Laurence Duggan") and [Michael Whitney Straight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Whitney_Straight "Michael Whitney Straight") in the State Department, the statistician [Harry Dexter White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Dexter_White "Harry Dexter White") in the [Treasury Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Treasury "United States Department of the Treasury"), the economist [Lauchlin Currie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauchlin_Currie "Lauchlin Currie") (an FDR advisor), and the "Silvermaster Group", headed by statistician [Greg Silvermaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Gregory_Silvermaster "Nathan Gregory Silvermaster"), in the Farm Security Administration and the Board of Economic Warfare.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999104–105-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999104–105) Moreover, when [Whittaker Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittaker_Chambers "Whittaker Chambers"), formerly Alger Hiss's courier, approached the Roosevelt Government to identify the Soviet spies Duggan, White, and others, he was ignored. Hence, during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") (1939–45)—at the [Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Conference "Tehran Conference") (1943), [Yalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Conference "Yalta Conference") (1945), and [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Conference "Potsdam Conference") (1945) conferences—Big Three Ally [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin") of the USSR, was better informed about the war affairs of his US and UK allies than they were about his.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999111-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999111)

Soviet espionage was at its most successful in collecting scientific and technological intelligence about advances in [jet propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine "Jet engine"), [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar") and [encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encryption "Encryption"), which impressed Moscow, but stealing atomic secrets was the capstone of NKVD espionage against Anglo–American science and technology. To wit, British [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") team physicist [Klaus Fuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Fuchs "Klaus Fuchs") (GRU 1941) was the main agent of the [Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_and_Ethel_Rosenberg "Julius and Ethel Rosenberg") spy ring.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) In 1944, the New York City residency infiltrated top secret [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory "Los Alamos National Laboratory") in New Mexico by recruiting [Theodore Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Hall "Theodore Hall"), a 19-year-old Harvard physicist.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

### During the Cold War

The KGB failed to rebuild most of its US illegal resident networks. The aftermath of the Second [Red Scare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Scare#'Second_Red_Scare'_(1947%E2%80%931957) "Red Scare") (1947–57) and the crisis in the [CPUSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA "Communist Party USA") hampered recruitment. The last major illegal resident, [Rudolf Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Abel "Rudolf Abel") (Vilyam Genrikhovich Fisher/"Willie" Vilyam Fishers), was betrayed by his assistant, [Reino Häyhänen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reino_H%C3%A4yh%C3%A4nen "Reino Häyhänen"), in 1957.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

Recruitment then emphasised mercenary agents, an approach especially successful\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]\[ _[quantify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\] in scientific and technical espionage, since private industry practised lax internal security, unlike the US Government. One notable KGB success occurred in 1967, with the walk-in recruitment of [US Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy "US Navy") [Chief Warrant Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_warrant_officer "Chief warrant officer") [John Anthony Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Anthony_Walker "John Anthony Walker"). ## In the Soviet Bloc

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Cell_33_in_KGB_Cells_Museum%2C_Tartu.jpg/500px-Cell_33_in_KGB_Cells_Museum%2C_Tartu.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cell_33_in_KGB_Cells_Museum,_Tartu.jpg) Cell doors at the KGB Cells Museum in [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu "Tartu"), [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia "Estonia") in 2007

It was Cold War policy for the KGB of the Soviet Union and the secret services of the [satellite states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_state "Satellite state") to extensively monitor public and private opinion, internal subversion and possible revolutionary plots in the [Soviet Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Bloc "Soviet Bloc"). In supporting those Communist governments, the KGB was instrumental in crushing the [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956 "Hungarian Revolution of 1956") and the [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring "Prague Spring") of " [Socialism with a Human Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_with_a_human_face "Socialism with a human face")" in Czechoslovakia, 1968.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

During the Hungarian revolt the founding member KGB chairman [Ivan Serov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Serov "Ivan Serov") personally supervised the post-invasion "normalization" of the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) Consequently, the KGB monitored the satellite state populations for occurrences of "harmful attitudes" and "hostile acts"; yet, stopping the Prague Spring, deposing a nationalist Communist government, was its greatest achievement. \[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

The KGB prepared the Red Army's route by infiltrating [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia") with many illegal residents disguised as Western tourists. They were to gain the trust of and spy upon the most outspoken proponents of [Alexander Dubček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dub%C4%8Dek "Alexander Dubček")'s new government. They were to plant subversive evidence, justifying the USSR's invasion, that right-wing groups—aided by Western intelligence agencies—were going to depose the Communist government of Czechoslovakia. Finally, the KGB prepared [hardline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardline "Hardline"), pro-USSR members of the [Communist Party of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Czechoslovakia "Communist Party of Czechoslovakia") (KSČ), such as [Alois Indra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Indra "Alois Indra") and Vasiľ Škultéty, to assume power after the Red Army's invasion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

The KGB's Czech success in the 1960s was matched with the failed suppression of the [Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_(Polish_trade_union) "Solidarity (Polish trade union)") labour movement in 1980s Poland. The KGB had forecast political instability consequent to the election of Archbishop of Kraków [Karol Wojtyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II "Pope John Paul II") as the first Polish Pope, John Paul II, whom they had categorised as "subversive" because of his anti-Communist sermons against the one-party régime of the [Polish United Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_United_Workers'_Party "Polish United Workers' Party") (PZPR). Despite its accurate forecast of crisis, the PZPR hindered the KGB from destroying the nascent Solidarity-backed political movement, fearing explosive civil violence if they imposed the KGB-recommended martial law. Aided by their Polish counterpart, the [Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Service_(Poland) "Security Service (Poland)") (Służba Bezpieczeństwa—SB), the KGB successfully infiltrated spies to Solidarity and the Catholic Church,[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) and in Operation X co-ordinated the [declaration of martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Poland "Martial law in Poland") with Gen. [Wojciech Jaruzelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Jaruzelski "Wojciech Jaruzelski") and the Polish Communist Party;[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999531-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin1999531) however, the vacillating, conciliatory Polish approach blunted KGB effectiveness—and Solidarity then fatally weakened the Communist Polish government in 1989.

Nadezhin saw that China threatened the USSR by claiming a historic right to regions under the USSR's control. China also wanted to displace the USSR as the leader of the international socialist movement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Filip-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-Filip) The KGB wanted to infiltrate the Chinese security services with "a sufficient number of agents". Top agents also believed that the KGB needed to do more to ensure the protection of the USSR from Chinese spies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Filip-1)[\[21\]](#refnote-Filip)

## Notable operations

- With the [Trust Operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Operation "Trust Operation") (1921–1926), the OGPU successfully deceived some leaders of the right-wing, counter-revolutionary [White Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement "White movement") back to the USSR for execution.
- [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD "NKVD") infiltrated and destroyed Trotskyist groups; in 1940, the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") agent [Ramón Mercader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Mercader "Ramón Mercader") assassinated [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky "Leon Trotsky") in Mexico City.
- KGB favoured [active measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_measures "Active measures") (e.g. [disinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinformation "Disinformation")), in discrediting the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR "USSR")'s enemies.
- For war-time, KGB had ready [sabotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabotage "Sabotage") operations arms caches in target countries.

According to declassified documents, the KGB aggressively recruited former German (mostly [Abwehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abwehr "Abwehr")) intelligence officers after the war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT7Jul2006-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-NYT7Jul2006) The KGB used them to penetrate the [West German intelligence service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gehlen_Organization "Gehlen Organization").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT7Jul2006-1)[\[22\]](#refnote-NYT7Jul2006)

In the 1960s, acting upon the information of KGB defector [Anatoliy Golitsyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Golitsyn "Anatoliy Golitsyn"), the CIA counter-intelligence chief [James Jesus Angleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Jesus_Angleton "James Jesus Angleton") believed KGB had [moles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_(espionage) "Mole (espionage)") in two key places—the counter-intelligence section of CIA and the FBI's counter-intelligence department—through whom they would know of, and control, US counter-espionage to protect the moles and hamper the detection and capture of other Communist spies. Moreover, KGB counter-intelligence vetted foreign intelligence sources, so that the moles might "officially" approve an anti-CIA [double agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_agent "Double agent") as trustworthy. In retrospect, the captures of the moles [Aldrich Ames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldrich_Ames "Aldrich Ames") and [Robert Hanssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hanssen "Robert Hanssen") proved that Angleton, though ignored as over-aggressive, was correct, despite the fact that it cost him his job at CIA, which he left in 1975.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In the mid-1970s, the KGB tried to secretly buy three banks in Northern California to gain access to high-technology secrets. Their efforts were thwarted by the CIA. The banks were Peninsula National Bank in Burlingame, the First National Bank of Fresno, and the Tahoe National Bank in South Lake Tahoe. These banks had made numerous loans to advanced technology companies and had many of their officers and directors as clients. The KGB used the [Moscow Narodny Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Narodny_Bank "Moscow Narodny Bank") to finance the acquisition, and an intermediary, Singaporean businessman Amos Dawe, as the frontman.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

### Bangladesh

On 2 February 1973, the [Politburo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politburo "Politburo"), which was led by Yuri Andropov at the time, demanded that KGB members influence [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh") (which was then newly formed), where [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "Sheikh Mujibur Rahman") was scheduled to win parliamentary elections. During that time, the Soviet secret service tried hard to ensure support for his party and his allies and even predicted an easy victory for him. In June 1975, Mujib formed a new party called [BAKSAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAKSAL "BAKSAL") and created a one-party state. Three years later, the KGB in that region increased from 90 to 200, and by 1979, it printed more than 100 newspaper articles. In these articles, the KGB officials accused [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman "Ziaur Rahman"), popularly known as "Zia", and his regime of having ties with the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AndrewMitrokhin2005-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-AndrewMitrokhin2005)

In August 1979, the KGB accused some officers who were arrested in [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka "Dhaka") in an overthrow attempt, and by October, Andropov approved the fabrication of a letter in which he stated that [Muhammad Ghulam Tawab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ghulam_Tawab "Muhammad Ghulam Tawab"), an Air Vice-Marshal at the time, was the main plotter, which led the Bangladesh, [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") and [Sri Lankan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") press to believe that he was an American spy. Under Andropov's command, Service A, a KGB division, falsified the information in a letter to [Moudud Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moudud_Ahmed "Moudud Ahmed") in which it said that he was supported by the American government and by 1981 even sent a letter accusing the [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_administration "Reagan administration") of plotting to overthrow President Zia and his regime. The letter also mentioned that after Mujib was assassinated, the United States contacted [Khondaker Mostaq Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khondaker_Mostaq_Ahmad "Khondaker Mostaq Ahmad") to replace him as a short-term President. When the election happened at the end of 1979, the KGB made sure that the Bangladesh Nationalist Party would win. The party received 207 out of 300 seats, but the Zia regime did not last long, falling on 29 May 1981 when after numerous escapes, Zia was assassinated in [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong "Chittagong").[&#8202;](#ref-back-AndrewMitrokhin2005-1)[\[24\]](#refnote-AndrewMitrokhin2005)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\]

### Afghanistan

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/KGB_special_operative_Igor_Morozov_on_the_armored_vehicle.jpg/500px-KGB_special_operative_Igor_Morozov_on_the_armored_vehicle.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KGB_special_operative_Igor_Morozov_on_the_armored_vehicle.jpg) KGB special operative [Igor Morozov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Morozov_(politician) "Igor Morozov (politician)") (left) sits on top of the [BTR-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-60 "BTR-60") armoured vehicle during his assignment to the [Badakhshan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badakhshan_Province "Badakhshan Province"), c.1982.

The KGB started infiltrating Afghanistan as early as 27 April 1978. During that time, the [People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Party_of_Afghanistan "People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan") (PDPA)[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25) was planning the overthrow of [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Afghanistan "President of Afghanistan") [Mohammed Daoud Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Daoud_Khan "Mohammed Daoud Khan"). Under the leadership of Major General [Sayed Mohammad Gulabzoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayed_Mohammad_Gulabzoy "Sayed Mohammad Gulabzoy") and Muhammad Rafi– [code named](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_name "Code name") Mammad and Niruz respectively–the Soviet secret service learned of the imminent uprising. Two days after the uprising, [Nur Muhammad Taraki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Muhammad_Taraki "Nur Muhammad Taraki"), leader of the PDPA, issued a notice of concern to the Soviet ambassador [Alexander Puzanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Puzanov "Alexander Puzanov") and the resident of [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul "Kabul")-based KGB embassy Viliov Osadchy that they could have staged a coup three days earlier hence the warning. On that, both Puzanov and Osadchy dismissed Taraki's complaint and reported it to Moscow, which broke a 30-year contract with him soon after.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AndrewMitrokhin2005-2)[\[24\]](#refnote-AndrewMitrokhin2005)[&#8202;](#ref-back-kgbafghanistan-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-kgbafghanistan)

The centre then realised that it was better for them to deal with a more competent agent, which at the time was [Babrak Karmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babrak_Karmal "Babrak Karmal"), who later accused Taraki of taking bribes and even of having secretly contacted the United States embassy in Kabul. On that, the centre again refused to listen and instructed him to take a position in the Kabul residency by 1974. On 30 April 1978, Taraki, despite being cut off from any support, led the coup which later became known as [Saur Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saur_Revolution "Saur Revolution"), and became the country's leader, with [Hafizullah Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafizullah_Amin "Hafizullah Amin") as vice-chairman of the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_(Afghanistan) "Council of Ministers (Afghanistan)") and vice-chairman of the [Revolutionary Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Council_(Afghanistan) "Revolutionary Council (Afghanistan)"). On 5 December 1978, Taraki compared the [Saur Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saur_Revolution "Saur Revolution") to the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution "Russian Revolution"), which struck\[ _[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\] [Vladimir Kryuchkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Kryuchkov "Vladimir Kryuchkov"), the FCD chief of that time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AndrewMitrokhin2005-3)[\[24\]](#refnote-AndrewMitrokhin2005)[&#8202;](#ref-back-kgbafghanistan-1)[\[26\]](#refnote-kgbafghanistan)

On 27 March 1979, after losing the city of [Herat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herat "Herat") in an [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Herat_uprising "1979 Herat uprising"), Amin became the next [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Afghanistan "Prime Minister of Afghanistan"), and by 27 July became [Minister of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_(Afghanistan) "Ministry of Defense (Afghanistan)") as well. The centre though was concerned of his powers since the same month he issued them a complaint about lack of funds and demanded US$400,000,000. Furthermore, it was discovered that Amin had a master's degree from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), and that he preferred to communicate in English instead of Russian. Unfortunately for Moscow's intelligence services, Amin succeeded Taraki and by 16 September [Radio Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Afghanistan "Radio Afghanistan") announced that the PDPA received a fake request from Taraki concerning health issues among the party members. On that, the centre accused him of "terrorist" activities and expelled him from the party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AndrewMitrokhin2005-4)[\[24\]](#refnote-AndrewMitrokhin2005)[&#8202;](#ref-back-kgbafghanistan-2)[\[26\]](#refnote-kgbafghanistan)

The following day General Boris Ivanov, who was behind the mission in Kabul along with General Lev Gorelov and Deputy Defense Minister Ivan Pavlovsky, visited Amin to congratulate him on his election to power. On the same day the KGB decided to imprison Sayed Gulabzoy as well as [Mohammad Aslam Watanjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Aslam_Watanjar "Mohammad Aslam Watanjar") and [Assadullah Sarwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assadullah_Sarwari "Assadullah Sarwari") but while in captivity and under an investigation all three denied the allegation that the current Minister of Defence was an American secret agent. The denial of claims was passed on to [Yuri Andropov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Andropov "Yuri Andropov") and [Leonid Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev "Leonid Brezhnev"), who as the main chiefs of the KGB proposed operation Raduga to save the life of Gulabzoy and Watanjar and send them to [Tashkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent "Tashkent") from [Bagram Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagram_Airfield "Bagram Airfield") by giving them fake passports. With that and a sealed container in which an almost breathless Sarwari was laying, they came to Tashkent on 19 September.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AndrewMitrokhin2005-5)[\[24\]](#refnote-AndrewMitrokhin2005)[&#8202;](#ref-back-kgbafghanistan-3)[\[26\]](#refnote-kgbafghanistan)

During the continued investigation in Tashkent, the three were put under surveillance in one of the rooms for as long as four weeks where they were investigated for the reliability of their claims by the KGB. Soon after, they were satisfied with the results and sent them to [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria "People's Republic of Bulgaria") for a secret retreat. On 9 October, the Soviet secret service had a meeting in which Bogdanov, Gorelov, Pavlonsky and Puzanov were the main chiefs who were discussing what to do with Amin who was very harsh at the meeting. After the two-hour meeting they began to worry that Amin would establish an [Islamic republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_republic "Islamic republic") in Afghanistan and decided to seek a way to put Karmal back in. They brought him and three other ministers secretly to Moscow during which time they discussed how to put him back in power. The decision was to fly him back to Bagram by 13 December. Four days later, Amin's nephew, Asadullah, was taken to Moscow by the KGB for acute food poisoning treatment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AndrewMitrokhin2005-6)[\[24\]](#refnote-AndrewMitrokhin2005)[&#8202;](#ref-back-kgbafghanistan-4)[\[26\]](#refnote-kgbafghanistan)

On 19 November 1979, the KGB had a meeting on which they discussed Operation Cascade, which was launched earlier that year. The operation carried out bombings with the help of [GRU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRU_(Soviet_Union) "GRU (Soviet Union)") and [FCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Chief_Directorate "First Chief Directorate").[&#8202;](#ref-back-kgbafghanistan-5)[\[26\]](#refnote-kgbafghanistan) On 27 December, the centre received news that KGB Special Forces Alpha and Zenith Group, supported by the 154th OSN GRU, also known as _Muslim battalion_ and paratroopers from the [345th Independent Guards Airborne Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/345th_Independent_Guards_Airborne_Regiment "345th Independent Guards Airborne Regiment") [stormed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Storm-333 "Operation Storm-333") the [Tajbeg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajbeg_Palace "Tajbeg Palace") and killed Amin and his 100–150 personal guards.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27) His 11-year-old son died due to shrapnel wounds.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) The Soviets installed Karmal as Amin's successor. Several other government buildings were seized during the operation, including the [Interior Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_Affairs_(Afghanistan) "Ministry of Interior Affairs (Afghanistan)") building, the Internal Security ( [KHAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHAD "KHAD")) building, and the General Staff building ( [Darul Aman Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Aman_Palace "Darul Aman Palace")). Out of the 54 KGB operators that assaulted the palace, 5 were killed in action, including Colonel Grigori Boyarinov, and 32 were wounded. [Alpha Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Group "Alpha Group") [veterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veteran "Veteran") call this operation one of the most successful in the group's history. In June 1981, there were 370 members in the Afghan-controlled KGB intelligence service throughout the nation which were under the command of Ahmad Shah Paiya and had received all the training they need in the Soviet Union. By May 1982, the Ministry of Internal Affairs was set up in Afghanistan under the command of KHAD. In 1983, Boris Voskoboynikov became the next head of the KGB while Leonid Kostromin became his Deputy Minister.[&#8202;](#ref-back-kgbafghanistan-6)[\[26\]](#refnote-kgbafghanistan)

## August 1991 and dissolution

Main article: [1991 Soviet coup d'état attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt "1991 Soviet coup d'état attempt")

The KGB dissolved on December 3, 1991. Its immediate successor agencies were the Federal Security Agency of the RSFSR (AFB),[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29) the Inter-Republican Security Service (MSB),[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30) the Central Intelligence Service (TsSR), and the Committee for the Protection of the State Border (KOGG).\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] In 1993, the KGB was succeeded overall by the [Federal Counterintelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Counterintelligence_Service "Federal Counterintelligence Service") (FSK) of Russia (itself a direct successor to the AFB),[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31) which in-turn was succeeded by the [Federal Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Security_Service "Federal Security Service") of the Russian Federation (FSB).[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)

## Organisation

The Committee for State Security was a militarised organisation adhering to military discipline and regulations. Its operational personnel held army style ranks, except for the maritime branch of the Border troops, which held navy style ranks. The KGB consisted of two main components - organs and [troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB_Security_Troops "KGB Security Troops"). The organs included the services directly involved in the committee's main roles - intelligence, counter-intelligence, military counter-intelligence etc. The troops included military units within the KGB's structure, completely separate from the Soviet armed forces - the [Border Troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Border_Troops "Soviet Border Troops"), the Governmental Signals Troops (which in addition to providing communications between the central government and the lower administrative levels, also provided the communications between the [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_General_Staff "Soviet General Staff") and the military districts), the Special Service Troops (which provided [EW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_warfare "Electronic warfare"), [ELINT, SIGINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELINT "ELINT") and cryptography) as well as the Spetsnaz of the KGB (the [Kremlin Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin_Regiment "Kremlin Regiment"), [Alpha Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Group "Alpha Group"), [Vympel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vympel "Vympel"), etc.). ### Mode of operation

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/KGB_first_law.jpg/500px-KGB_first_law.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KGB_first_law.jpg) The decree establishing the KGB

A _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ magazine article in 1983, reported that the KGB was the world's most effective information-gathering organisation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36) It operated [legal and illegal espionage residencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_spy "Resident spy") in target countries where a _legal resident_ gathered intelligence while based at the Soviet embassy or consulate, and, if caught, was protected from prosecution by [diplomatic immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_immunity "Diplomatic immunity"). At worst, the compromised spy was either returned to the Soviet Union or was declared _[persona non grata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_non_grata "Persona non grata")_ and expelled by the government of the target country. The _illegal resident_ spied, unprotected by diplomatic immunity, and worked independently of Soviet diplomatic and trade missions, ( _cf._ the [non-official cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-official_cover "Non-official cover") CIA officer). In its early history, the KGB valued illegal spies more than legal spies, because illegal spies infiltrated their targets with greater ease. The KGB residency executed four types of espionage: (i) political, (ii) economic, (iii) military-strategic, and (iv) [disinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinformation "Disinformation"), effected with "active measures" (PR Line), [counter-intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-intelligence "Counter-intelligence") and security (KR Line), and scientific–technological intelligence (X Line); quotidian duties included [SIGINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_intelligence "Signal intelligence") (RP Line) and illegal support (N Line).[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin199938-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAndrewMitrokhin199938)

The KGB classified its spies as:

- _agents_ (a person who provides intelligence) and
- _controllers_ (a person who relays intelligence).

The false-identity (or _legend_) assumed by a USSR-born _illegal_ spy was elaborate, using the life of either:

- a "live double" (a participant to the fabrications) or
- a "dead double" (whose identity is tailored to the spy).

The agent then substantiated his or her false identity by living in a foreign country before emigrating to the target country. For example, the KGB would send a US-bound illegal resident via the Soviet embassy in [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Russia_in_Ottawa "Embassy of Russia in Ottawa"), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada").

[Tradecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradecraft "Tradecraft") included stealing and photographing documents, code-names, contacts, targets, and [dead letter boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_drop "Dead drop"), and working as a "friend of the cause" or as _[agents provocateurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_provocateur "Agent provocateur")_, who would infiltrate the target group to sow dissension, influence policy, and arrange [kidnappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping "Kidnapping") and [assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination "Assassination").[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)
