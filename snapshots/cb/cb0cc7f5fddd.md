# Samsung Biologics Union Strikes on 1st for 20% Profit-Sharing

Strike Threatens 640 Billion Won Losses and Global Client Trust Amid CDMO Supply Concerns

[By Choi Won-woo](https://www.chosun.com/english/people/choi-wonwoo/)

Published 2026.04.29. 15:10Updated 2026.04.29. 21:43

![삼성바이오로직스 상생노조, 창사 이래 첫 파업 예고](https://www.chosun.com/resizer/v2/JTRSX2KJPJHFBDQPZNIRK7YNRI.jpg?auth=ca526a1db2aecec351db8c3eb551f211557919b1fb60093b812704970a362f16&width=616)

Samsung Biologics labor union announces it will go on a full-scale strike from the 1st of next month. /Newsis

The Samsung Biologics labor union will commence a full-scale strike starting on the 1st of next month. This will mark the first strike since the company’s founding in 2011. Concerns have emerged that disruptions in biopharmaceutical production—a continuous process requiring 24-hour cell cultivation—could lead to losses amounting to hundreds of billions of Korean won and damage to the trust of global clients.

According to industry sources, the Samsung Biologics union began a partial strike involving around 60 participants on the 28th. The union has announced plans for a five-day full-scale strike starting on the 1st of next month. Approximately 2,000 out of the total 3,900 employees have expressed their intention to participate. The company estimates that if the strike proceeds as planned, disruptions in processes and a decline in client trust could result in damages amounting to 640 billion Korean won.

Earlier, the Incheon District Court partially accepted Samsung Biologics’ application for a provisional injunction to prohibit the union from engaging in labor disputes on the 23rd. While the court restricted strikes in three processes directly linked to preventing deterioration and spoilage—such as concentration, buffer exchange, bulk filling, and the manufacturing and supply of related buffers—it dismissed the application regarding six processes related to cultivation and purification. The court reasoned that while work to prevent the deterioration of already produced raw materials and products should be protected, it cannot prohibit labor disputes across the entire cultivation process. The union plans to proceed with the strike, excluding the restricted processes. The company has appealed, arguing that the remaining items should also be subject to the injunction.

Biopharmaceutical factories differ from general manufacturing plants. The entire process, from cell thawing to cultivation, purification, and filling, must be controlled 24/7, 365 days a year. If conditions such as temperature, oxygen, and nutrient supply are disrupted, cells may die or proteins could degrade. In such cases, the pharmaceuticals under production become subject to disposal. In the contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO) business, where companies produce drugs on behalf of global pharmaceutical firms, supply stability is considered a key competitive advantage. Once a delay in delivery or supply risk is exposed, there is a possibility that clients may shift their orders to competitors.

Differences of opinion regarding the strike have reportedly emerged within the union. Notably, criticism arose internally after it was revealed that the union chairman remained in Hawaii until the 30th, just before the strike was set to begin. The union chairman reportedly stated, “The trip was a pre-planned schedule due to my pregnant wife, and the company was notified of my absence.” There are also criticisms that the union’s encouragement of strike participation through collective annual leave is inappropriate. Allegations have surfaced that the union urged members in online chat rooms to post comments criticizing the company’s stance on the internal intranet and to verify whether employees who expressed their intention not to participate in the strike are union members or have attended rallies.

The union is demanding a one-time payment of 30 million Korean won per person, an average 14% wage increase, and a 20% profit-sharing distribution of operating profits. The company has proposed a 6.2% increase. Despite 13 rounds of negotiations, the two sides have failed to narrow their differences. The company plans to minimize production disruptions by deploying available personnel and will respond in accordance with the court’s ruling. A Samsung Biologics representative stated, “The company intended to respond to the Ministry of Employment and Labor Central Regional Employment and Labor Office’s proposal for labor-management dialogue before the strike, but it is regrettable that the chairman was absent,” adding, “We expect a dialogue to take place after the chairman’s return.”
