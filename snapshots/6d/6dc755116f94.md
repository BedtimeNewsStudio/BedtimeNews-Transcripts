# **Research on Higher Education and Science**

Over 400 universities, some three million students – research on higher education and science plays a major role in nurturing this vast body of knowledge and bright minds for the future.

![](https://static1.bmbfcluster.de/3/3/9/3/4_ecd59877ac5f698/33934teg_17613c7025e8b8b.jpg)

Opportunities

- Higher education institutions play a crucial role in the development of society and the economy and in finding solutions to global societal challenges. Research on higher education and science generates **evidence-based knowledge on the further development** of these institutions.
- Research on higher education and science promotes dialogue between different disciplines, industry, society and policy-makers, thus accelerating the translation of new knowledge into **practice**.
- Research on higher education and science is about developing and testing new **digital teaching and learning methods** to improve the efficiency of higher education.
- Research on higher education and science identifies structural inequalities in the education system, but also starting points for supporting diversity and **equal opportunities** to create a more just and inclusive higher education and science system for all.

What problems do students with disabilities have to overcome in the higher education system and what tools can help them? Why do up to 40 percent of students in the natural sciences drop out? How can we further strengthen the transfer of knowledge from research into application?

Research on higher education and science seeks to answer these and many other questions.

A strong science system lays the foundations for a knowledge-based society. Higher Research, Technology and Space institutions develop solutions to the societal challenges of our time. They train the skilled professionals of tomorrow. They need optimum framework conditions to achieve the best possible results in doing so.

The dedicated funding priority of the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR) supports research on the science and higher education system. To enhance this system, researchers look at prerequisites, structures and performance assessment in research and higher education as well as at general conditions. At the same time, they strive to develop solutions and recommendations for action for policy and decision-makers.

## **BMFTR funding for research on topics ranging from academic freedom to student dropout**

The funding provided by the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR) reflects the broad range of responsibilities of the science system. In Germany, the sub‑discipline of research on higher education has traditionally placed its focus on teaching. Over the past years, funding lines in this area addressed topics such as digital higher education, study success and student dropout, or enhancing teaching quality.

Research on science, the other sub‑discipline, centres on looking into the science system as such with all its stakeholders and structures. How are private higher education institutions developing? How can academic freedom be protected? These are only two questions that research funded by the BMFTR strives to answer with a view to using newly gained knowledge to enhance German higher education institutions.

In addition, the Federal Government and the _Länder_ fund the [German Centre for Higher Education Research and Science Studies](https://www.dzhw.eu/ "External link Deutsches Zentrum für Hochschul- und Wissenschaftsforschung (DZHW) (Opens new window)") (DZHW) in order to strengthen empirical research in this field. The aim is to accelerate the translation of new knowledge into practice. The DZHW sees its role as that of a partner and service provider for stakeholders in the science community and in higher Research, Technology and Space policy.

## **BMFTR-funded research on students covers topics ranging from participation and income to health and canteen use**

In addition to scholarly research, training students is a core task of higher education institutions. But who are the people who make their way into higher education? What are the preconditions for student success?

How can we create better structures for students with impairments? To answer these questions, we provide funding for scientific studies and service units of the [Deutsches Studierendenwerk](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/themen/internationale-studierende "External link Internationale Studierende (studierendenwerke.de) (Opens new window)") that facilitate student access to higher education institutions. Find out more about research on students [here](https://www.wihoforschung.de/wihoforschung/de/home/home_node.html "External link Wissenschafts- und Hochschulforschung (wihoforschung.de) (Opens new window)").

## **Facts and figures – funding for data infrastructures and research information**

What funding measures have proven successful? Which have not?

Robust, comparable data is needed to shed light on the strengths and weaknesses of the German research system. In order to strengthen this database, we provide funding for data infrastructure projects, including via the Competence Network for Bibliometrics. These projects are aimed, for example, at collecting data about authors, journals, institutions and funding providers in order to facilitate new ways of analysing the higher education system. In addition, we support living labs that bring researchers and practitioners together to promote the widespread use of research information systems across higher education institutions and research organisations, thereby strengthening transparency and participation within the research system.

## **Knowledge generation and transfer into practice**

Next to teaching and research, knowledge transfer has started to play an increasingly important role in the science and higher education system over recent years. The aim is to translate research findings into society and thus improve their usability. When it comes to research on higher education and science, theory and practice are particularly closely intertwined and benefit from a mutual exchange of expertise. The BMFTR has launched a dedicated funding line to support research on knowledge transfer and look into different structures and instruments for enhancing this transfer into practice.
