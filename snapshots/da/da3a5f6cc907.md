**MV _Rhosus_** was a [general cargo ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_cargo_ship "General cargo ship") that was [abandoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_abandonment "Ship abandonment") in [Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut "Beirut"), Lebanon, after the ship was declared [unseaworthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaworthiness_(law) "Seaworthiness (law)") and the charterers lost interest in the cargo. The 2,750 tonnes of [ammonium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_nitrate "Ammonium nitrate") which the ship was carrying was confiscated and brought to shore in 2014, and later caused the catastrophic [2020 Beirut explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Beirut_explosion "2020 Beirut explosion"). The vessel's owner at the time of abandonment was [Cyprus-based Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Cypriots "Russian Cypriots") businessman Igor Grechushkin. The ship sank in the [Port of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Beirut "Port of Beirut") in 2018.

## Description

_Rhosus_ was a single-deck general cargo ship with a length of 86.6 metres (284ft), beam of 12 metres (39ft), and draught of 4.9m (16ft). The ship's [gross tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_tonnage "Gross tonnage") was 1,900; [net tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_tonnage "Net tonnage") 964; and [deadweight tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadweight_tonnage "Deadweight tonnage") 3,226tonnes, and it had two cargo holds with a grain capacity of 4,136 cubic metres (146,100cuft) and bale capacity of 3,837 cubic metres (135,500cuft).[&#8202;](#ref-back-seaweb-4)[\[2\]](#refnote-seaweb)[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-6) The ship was normally run by around nine or ten crew members.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.newsmaker-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-rhosus.newsmaker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-siberiantimes-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-siberiantimes) _Rhosus_'s single main diesel engine, a 4-stroke 6LU32G unit manufactured by Hanshin Diesel Works, was rated at 736kW (987hp), drove a single fixed pitch propeller, and gave the vessel a service speed of 10.5 knots (19.4km/h; 12.1mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-seaweb-5)[\[2\]](#refnote-seaweb)

## History

The ship was built by Tokuoka Zosen K.K. in [Naruto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto,_Tokushima "Naruto, Tokushima"), Japan, as the [grab suction dredger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dredging "Dredging") _Daifuku Maru No. 8_ for the Japanese shipping company Daifuku Kaiun KK and delivered in October 1986.[&#8202;](#ref-back-LR2000-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-LR2000) In 2002, the ship was sold to another Japanese shipping company, Nishi Nippon Kaiyo, but was reportedly sold already in March of the same year to a South Korean owner and renamed _Seokjung No. 505_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-equasis-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-equasis)[&#8202;](#ref-back-seaweb-6)[\[2\]](#refnote-seaweb)

In March 2005, the ship was sold to Hong Kong Zheng Long Shipping Co Ltd, renamed _Zheng Long_ and registered briefly under the [Belizean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize "Belize") flag. In June of the same year, the ownership changed to another Hong Kong-based shipping company, Rui Hua (HK) Shipping Co Ltd, and the vessel was reflagged to [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama"). In June 2007, the ship was sold to a Panamanian-registered company, Sea Star International Shipping Group Inc, and renamed _New Legend Glory_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-equasis-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-equasis)[&#8202;](#ref-back-seaweb-7)[\[2\]](#refnote-seaweb)

The ship's most recent reported registered owner, Panamanian-registered Briarwood Corp, acquired the vessel in August 2008 and renamed it _Rhosus_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[b\]](#refnote-10) After lengthening the hull from 53 metres (174ft) to 86.6 metres (284ft) and converting the ship to a general cargo vessel,[&#8202;](#ref-back-LR2000-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-LR2000) _Rhosus_ was reflagged first to Georgia in 2009 and later to Moldova in 2012.[&#8202;](#ref-back-equasis-4)[\[1\]](#refnote-equasis)[&#8202;](#ref-back-seaweb-8)[\[2\]](#refnote-seaweb) By 2012, the vessel was effectively owned by  Cypriot businessman Charalambos Manoli, who sold it that year to [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk "Khabarovsk")-born Russian businessman Igor Grechushkin, who was reported to be living in [Limassol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limassol "Limassol"), Cyprus, in 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-washingtonpost-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-washingtonpost)[&#8202;](#ref-back-balkaninsight-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-balkaninsight)[&#8202;](#ref-back-siberiantimes-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-siberiantimes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-idnomd-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-idnomd) This was Grechushkin's first foray into running his own ship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC:Beirut-blast-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-BBC:Beirut-blast)

On 28 July 2013, _Rhosus_ was detained at the [Port of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Seville "Port of Seville") by Spanish [port state control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_state_control "Port state control") authorities due to a number of deficiencies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-13)

### Abandonment

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/MV_Rhosus_Beirut.jpg/500px-MV_Rhosus_Beirut.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MV_Rhosus_Beirut.jpg) _Rhosus_ (right) moored in the Port of Beirut in 2017

In September 2013, the freighter was chartered to carry a cargo of high-density [ammonium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_nitrate "Ammonium nitrate") from Georgian fertilizer maker, Rustavi Azot LLC, in [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country) "Georgia (country)") to be delivered to the manufacturer of explosive products Fábrica de Explosivos Moçambique (FEM) in [Matola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matola "Matola"), [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique").[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[c\]](#refnote-14) On 27 September 2013, _Rhosus_ set sail from [Batumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batumi "Batumi") bound to [Beira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beira,_Mozambique "Beira, Mozambique") carrying 2,750 [tonnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne "Tonne") of ammonium nitrate in bags,[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.shiparrested-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-rhosus.shiparrested)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.bbc-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-rhosus.bbc)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.euronews-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-rhosus.euronews) a cargo which the captain regarded as "dangerous", but not so dangerous that it could not be transported.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-18) On 21 November 2013, the ship ported in Beirut.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.bbc-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-rhosus.bbc)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.euronews-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-rhosus.euronews)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-19) Some sources stated that it was forced to port due to mechanical issues and possibly engine problems,[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.tv2-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-rhosus.tv2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.fleetmon-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-rhosus.fleetmon) while other sources said that the owner did not have sufficient funds to pay tolls for the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal") and attempted but failed to take on a shipment of heavy machinery from Beirut to [Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba "Aqaba"), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[d\]](#refnote-23)[&#8202;](#ref-back-washingtonpost-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-washingtonpost)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NOSdeWaarschuwingenInBeirutWarenEr-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-NOSdeWaarschuwingenInBeirutWarenEr)[&#8202;](#ref-back-spiegel-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-spiegel) The heavy machinery was stacked over the hatch leading to the cargo space containing the ammonium nitrate, causing the hatch covers to buckle and damaging the ship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC:CaptBoris-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-BBC:CaptBoris)[&#8202;](#ref-back-reuters.disaster-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-reuters.disaster)

After inspection by [port state control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_state_control "Port state control"), _Rhosus_ was found unseaworthy, and it was forbidden to set sail.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.tv2-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-rhosus.tv2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.fleetmon-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-rhosus.fleetmon) Eight Ukrainians and one Russian were aboard,[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[e\]](#refnote-27) and with the help of a Ukrainian consul, five Ukrainians were repatriated, leaving the Russian captain and three Ukrainian crew members—the chief engineer, the third engineer, and the [boatswain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boatswain "Boatswain")—to take care of the ship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[f\]](#refnote-28)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.newsmaker-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-rhosus.newsmaker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.fleetmon-2)[\[18\]](#refnote-rhosus.fleetmon)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sur-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-sur)

The owner of _Rhosus_, Igor Grechushkin, claimed to have become bankrupt[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[g\]](#refnote-31) and, after the charterers lost interest in the cargo, he abandoned the ship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.newsmaker-3)[\[5\]](#refnote-rhosus.newsmaker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-32) _Rhosus_ then quickly ran out of provisions, while the crew were unable to disembark due to immigration restrictions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.shiparrested-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-rhosus.shiparrested) The captain sold some fuel in the ship in order to pay for lawyers to free them from their confinement on the ship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC:CaptBoris-1)[\[21\]](#refnote-BBC:CaptBoris) Creditors also obtained three [warrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_(law) "Warrant (law)") to [arrest the ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_arrest "Ship arrest").[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[h\]](#refnote-34)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.newsmaker-4)[\[5\]](#refnote-rhosus.newsmaker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.shiparrested-2)[\[12\]](#refnote-rhosus.shiparrested)

According to _[Lloyd's List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd's_List "Lloyd's List")_, the Beirut port authority seized the ship on 4 February 2014, due to US$100,000 in unpaid bills.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC:Beirut-blast-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-BBC:Beirut-blast) The ship had accrued port fees and been fined for refusing cargo.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC:CaptBoris-2)[\[21\]](#refnote-BBC:CaptBoris)[&#8202;](#ref-back-reuters.disaster-1)[\[22\]](#refnote-reuters.disaster) Lawyers argued for the crew's repatriation on compassionate grounds, due to the danger posed by the cargo still aboard the ship, and an Urgent Matters judge in Beirut allowed them to return home after having been stuck aboard the ship for about a year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.newsmaker-5)[\[5\]](#refnote-rhosus.newsmaker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.shiparrested-3)[\[12\]](#refnote-rhosus.shiparrested)[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[i\]](#refnote-37) The dangerous cargo was then brought ashore in 2014 and placed in a building, Hangar 12, at the port, pursuant to a court order, until it [exploded, with catastrophic consequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Beirut_explosion "2020 Beirut explosion"), on 4 August 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.shiparrested-4)[\[12\]](#refnote-rhosus.shiparrested)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.fleetmon-3)[\[18\]](#refnote-rhosus.fleetmon)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.jazeera-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-rhosus.jazeera)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters_Toll_Expected-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-Reuters_Toll_Expected)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jazeera.live.2020.08.05-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-jazeera.live.2020.08.05)

### Fate

In a 2020 interview, the former master of _Rhosus_ stated that there was a small hole in the hull and, with no crew on board to periodically pump the sea water out, the vessel sank "two or three years ago" after the cargo had been unloaded.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-41) [Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews "Euronews") reported that the records of _Lloyd's List_ showed that _Rhosus_ was seized in February 2014, and that it sank without a crew in the breakwaters of the Port of Beirut in February 2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rhosus.euronews-2)[\[14\]](#refnote-rhosus.euronews) _The New York Times_ confirmed via [multispectral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multispectral_imaging "Multispectral imaging") satellite imagery that the ship sank between 16 and 18 February 2018 alongside a Beirut pier.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fate.nytimes-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-fate.nytimes)
Subsequent analysis\[ _[by whom?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")_\] shows the ship to still lie there.

Since 2018, _Rhosus_'s flag has been reported as "unknown" in official databases and the vessel's status was updated to "total loss" in August 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-equasis-5)[\[1\]](#refnote-equasis)[&#8202;](#ref-back-seaweb-9)[\[2\]](#refnote-seaweb)[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-43)

## Investigation

A thorough investigation by _[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel "Der Spiegel")_ and the [Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_Crime_and_Corruption_Reporting_Project "Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project") (OCCRP) into the [2020 Beirut explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Beirut_explosion "2020 Beirut explosion") concluded that:[&#8202;](#ref-back-spiegel-1)[\[20\]](#refnote-spiegel)[&#8202;](#ref-back-OCCRP-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-OCCRP)

- A Cypriot businessman, Charalambos Manoli, might have been the owner, while Igor Grechushkin was merely leasing it through a company in the Marshall Islands called Teto Shipping Ltd.
- Panamanian-registered Briarwood Corp, which owned the vessel in August 2008, might belong to Manoli, according to a 2012 document found by OCCRP journalists.
- A Georgian company called Maritime Lloyd, owned by Manoli, certified the seaworthiness of the ailing freighter in late July 2013. But days later, Port of Seville authorities detained the ship, citing 14 defects, such as corroded decks, lack of auxiliary power, and problems with radio communication.
- During _Rhosus_'s last voyage, Manoli owed €962,000 to [FBME Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBME_Bank "FBME Bank"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[j\]](#refnote-45) which is accused of helping the Shia militant group [Hezbollah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbollah "Hezbollah"). However, _Rhosus_ might have been offered up as collateral to the bank at one stage.
- Mozambican company, Fábrica de Explosivos Moçambique (FEM),[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[k\]](#refnote-46) which ordered the shipment, is 95 percent owned by the family of Portuguese businessman Antonio Moura Vieira, through a company called Moura Silva & Filhos, which was previously investigated for arms trafficking and supplying explosives used in the [2004 Madrid train bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Madrid_train_bombings "2004 Madrid train bombings").
- The intermediary company, Savaro Ltd,[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[l\]](#refnote-48) hired a Lebanese lawyer in February 2015 to inspect the quality and quantity of the ammonium nitrate in the port warehouse. The experts reported that 1,900 out of 2,700 bags were ripped and had their contents spilling out. Hence, they did not try to take back the ammonium nitrate in the end.
- One of the warehouse's gates was missing, in addition to a large hole in the south wall, which caused fear that the ammonium nitrate might be stolen to be used as explosives.
- The size of the explosion was equivalent to only 700 to 1,000 tons of ammonium nitrate.

In October 2020, Lebanon's state-run _National News Agency_ said that the country's state prosecution had asked [Interpol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpol "Interpol") to detain two Russian citizens, the captain and the owner of _Rhosus_, as its cargo of ammonium nitrate was blamed for the explosion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-49)

On September 16, 2025 it was reported that Igor Grechushkin was arrested in [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia "Sofia"), [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria "Bulgaria"), accused of being linked to the vessel Rhosus. His arrest at [Sofia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasil_Levski_Sofia_Airport "Vasil Levski Sofia Airport") was made under an [Interpol red notice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpol_notice "Interpol notice") issued by Lebanon, and he is being held for up to 40 days while Bulgarian authorities await [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon")’s formal [extradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extradition "Extradition") request.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-50)[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-51)[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-52)

On September 25, 2025, Grechushkin had reportedly been transferred to the Republic of Cyprus, where officials will decide whether he will face trial locally or be deported to Lebanon. When asked by the [Cyprus Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Mail "Cyprus Mail") how Grechushkin was able to live in and depart from Cyprus without being arrested, Cypriot police explained that Cyprus has no extradition agreement with Lebanon. “Cyprus does not extradite Cypriot citizens to Lebanon,” police said, adding that the Red Notice was issued after Lebanese authorities had turned to Interpol.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-53)

On 16 September it was reported that he was arrested in Bulgaria. Following the arrest Lebanon quickly filed a formal extradition request for him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-54)[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-55)
