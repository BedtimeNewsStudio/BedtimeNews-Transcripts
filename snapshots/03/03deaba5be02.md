SEOUL, Dec. 15 (Yonhap) -- South Korea and Saudi Arabia discussed the progress of bilateral business agreements signed since last year while exchanging ideas on new areas of collaboration, the industry ministry said Friday.

Industry Minister Bang Moon-kyu met his Saudi counterpart,[Bandar Alkhorayef](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20231214000400320 "Opens in new web browser window"), in Seoul as the two countries have signed US$44.6 billion worth of business deals since last year, according to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy.

Bang noted that the two countries are smoothly carrying out the deal, and vowed to work closely with the Middle Eastern nation down the road.

During the meeting, the industry minister expressed hope for enhancing collaboration with Saudi Arabia, specifically in sectors such as mineral processing, and suggested taking proactive measures to identify additional areas for collaboration.

In October, South Korean President Yoon Suk Yeol made a state visit to Saudi Arabia and held a summit with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. The two countries' companies and institutions then signed 51 deals worth a combined $15.6 billion.

The crown prince also visited South Korea in November 2022, when the two nations signed $29 billion worth of business agreements.
