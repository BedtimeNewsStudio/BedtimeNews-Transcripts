The accession of the **[United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom")** to the **[European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities "European Communities") (EC)** – the collective term for the [European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Coal_and_Steel_Community "European Coal and Steel Community"), the [European Economic Community (EEC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community "European Economic Community") and the [European Atomic Energy Community (EAEC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Atomic_Energy_Community "European Atomic Energy Community") – took effect on 1 January 1973. This followed ratification of the [Accession treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Accession_1972 "Treaty of Accession 1972") which was signed in [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels "Brussels") on 22 January 1972 by the Conservative prime minister [Edward Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Heath "Edward Heath"), who had pursued the UK's application to the EEC since the late 1950s. The ECSC and EEC would later be integrated into the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") under the [Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty "Maastricht Treaty") and [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon "Treaty of Lisbon") treaties in the early 1990s and mid-2000s.

The UK had been the first country to establish a Delegation to the ECSC in 1952, and the first country to sign an [Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Association_Agreement "European Union Association Agreement") with the Community in 1954. The UK had first applied to join in 1961, but this was vetoed by French President [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle") in 1963. A second application, in 1967, was again vetoed by France.[&#8202;](#ref-back-UKinEU-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-UKinEU) After de Gaulle had relinquished the French presidency in 1969, the UK made a third and successful application for membership. Denmark and Ireland also joined as part of the same [expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_enlargement_of_the_European_Communities "1973 enlargement of the European Communities").[&#8202;](#ref-back-EUED-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-EUED)

The UK's negotiation team in 1970–72 included [Con O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Con_O'Neill_(diplomat) "Con O'Neill (diplomat)") and [David Hannay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hannay,_Baron_Hannay_of_Chiswick "David Hannay, Baron Hannay of Chiswick").[&#8202;](#ref-back-BEEC-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-BEEC)

The [Treaty of Accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Accession_1972 "Treaty of Accession 1972") was signed in [January 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_1972 "January 1972") by prime minister [Edward Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Heath "Edward Heath"), leader of the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) Parliament's [European Communities Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities_Act_1972_(UK) "European Communities Act 1972 (UK)") was enacted on 17 October, and the UK's instrument of ratification was deposited the next day (18 October),[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) letting the United Kingdom's membership of the EC come into effect on 1 [January 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_1973 "January 1973").[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

## Origin

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/66/Heathdod_%28cropped%29.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Heathdod_(cropped).JPG) [Edward Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Heath "Edward Heath") as [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom "Prime Minister of the United Kingdom") who was staunchly pro-European led the UK into the [European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities "European Communities") in 1973.

When proposals for a European [customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_union "Customs union") were advanced after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), there was widespread political opposition in the UK: the [Federation of British Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_British_Industries "Federation of British Industries") and the government's economic ministries opposed British participation as the establishment of a common external tariff would mean the end of the [Imperial Preference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Preference "Imperial Preference") system of trade within the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") and the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations"), and would expose British business to increased competition from the continent, in particular from Germany. Meanwhile, the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") believed that it would lead to cost-of-living increase for the British working class, forcing them to consume more expensive agricultural produce from continental Europe instead of cheaper food from the imperial dominions, and that what they saw as the domination of mainland western European politics by anti-socialist [Christian democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_democracy "Christian democracy") would threaten the newly constructed [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state "Welfare state") introduced by the [Attlee ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attlee_ministry "Attlee ministry"). As a result, the UK's initial attitude to moves toward European economic integration was rather detached: it was only an observer to the negotiations on the creation of the ECSC which culminated in the [1951 Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1951) "Treaty of Paris (1951)"), and similarly sent a mid-ranking civil servant from the [Board of Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Trade "Board of Trade") as an observer to the ministerial [Messina Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messina_Conference "Messina Conference") which led to the [Treaty of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rome "Treaty of Rome").[&#8202;](#ref-back-georgiou-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-georgiou)

Shortly after the creation of the ECSC in 1952, the UK became the first country to establish a Delegation in [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg "Luxembourg"), the seat of the High Authority (present-day [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission "European Commission")) at the time. On 24 December 1953 the High Authority invited the British Government to enter into negotiations for the establishment of an association. On 29 April 1954 the British Government invited the High Authority to London to begin discussions on the proposed association and on 21 December 1954 the Agreement of Association was signed in London entering into force on 23 September 1955. This was the first example of an [EU Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Association_Agreement "EU Association Agreement").[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)

The Agreement of Association established a Standing Council of Association which was intended to provide 'a means for the continuous exchange of information and for consultation in regard to matters of common interest concerning coal and steel' (Article 6). The first meeting took place on 17 November 1955 in Luxembourg. At the second meeting on 22 March 1956 in London, the High Authority and the British Government agreed to open tariff negotiations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) That year the British government also made a counter-proposal to the Treaty of Rome negotiations, advocating the creation of a larger but less integrated [free-trade area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-trade_area "Free-trade area") encompassing all members of the [Organisation for European Economic Co-operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_European_Economic_Co-operation "Organisation for European Economic Co-operation") (now the OECD): this would have established a European trade bloc but would not have introduced a common external tariff, which would have allowed the UK to maintain an Imperial Preference policy. However, this effort was not successful. Subsequently, political opinion in Britain shifted towards greater engagement with the European Communities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-georgiou-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-georgiou)

There were potential economic benefits for Britain joining the EEC in respect of earnings. Following the establishment of the EEC in 1958, Britain had fallen behind EEC member states in terms of earnings. While in 1958 Britons earned on average 50% more than those in Italy and as much as workers in the Netherlands, West Germany, and France, this situation changed by 1969, with average earnings in Italy having caught up with Britain while in the other Common Market countries average earnings were between 25% and 50% higher than in Britain.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

## Opposition to the Treaty of Accession 1972

See also: [1973 enlargement of the European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_enlargement_of_the_European_Communities "1973 enlargement of the European Communities")

The [Treaty of Accession 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Accession_1972 "Treaty of Accession 1972"), which brought the United Kingdom into the European Communities, had already been met with opposition in the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom "Parliament of the United Kingdom"), notably from [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_UK "Labour Party UK")'s [Peter Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Shore "Peter Shore"), who was [Shadow Leader of the House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Leader_of_the_House_of_Commons "Shadow Leader of the House of Commons") at the time. He said:

"This is a treaty which carries the most formidable and far-reaching obligations. It is a treaty—the first in our history—which would deprive the British Parliament and people of democratic rights which they have exercised for many centuries. I can think of no treaty, to cite only one characteristic of the Rome Treaty, in which the British Parliament agree that the power to tax the British people should be handed over to another group, or countries, or people outside this country, and that they should have the right in perpetuity to levy taxes upon us and decide how the revenues of those taxes should be spent."[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

It was also noted that if the United Kingdom refused to join the European Communities, [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark"), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland "Ireland") and [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") would not accede either.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

## Fanfare for Europe

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/European_Communities_UK1973.webp/500px-European_Communities_UK1973.webp.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:European_Communities_UK1973.webp) UK stamps commemorating accession

Upon accession to the EC, numerous events were held as part of a two-week "Fanfare for Europe" programme, including over 300 events featuring music, art, entertainment and sport from national and European performers. The Queen attended an opening gala at the [Royal Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Opera_House "Royal Opera House"). Despite an investment of £350,000 of public money, events were not well attended. A series of international football matches between the member states, including a home team captained by [Bobby Charlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Charlton "Bobby Charlton"), left [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium "Wembley Stadium") only half-full. Even so, only 12% of the electorate agreeing that membership had not brought the United Kingdom any benefit. The Heath government subsequently lost its majority in the [February 1974 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_1974_United_Kingdom_general_election "February 1974 United Kingdom general election") and renegotiation of membership being a prominent aspect of the victorious Labour Party's manifesto in the [October 1974 United Kingdom general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_1974_United_Kingdom_general_election "October 1974 United Kingdom general election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)

## In popular culture

In the British science fiction television series _[Doctor Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Who "Doctor Who")_ ninth season's " [The Curse of Peladon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curse_of_Peladon "The Curse of Peladon")" and its four-part serial plot line can be seen as a political allegory about whether Britain should join the EEC at the time, with the alien delegates of the Galactic Federation representing the EEC, the planet's King Peladon representing those hoping to join the Federation and the High Priest Hepesh, who wants to preserve the status quo; representing those not wanting to join.
