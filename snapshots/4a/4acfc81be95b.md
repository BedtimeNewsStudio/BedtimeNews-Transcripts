The **James M. Inhofe National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023** (**NDAA 2023**) is a [United States federal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_acts_of_the_116th_United_States_Congress "List of acts of the 116th United States Congress") which specifies the budget, expenditures and policies of the [U.S. Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") (DOD) for [fiscal year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year#United_States "Fiscal year") 2023. Analogous [NDAAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act "National Defense Authorization Act") have been passed annually for over 60 years.

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

The House bill [H.R. 7900](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/7900) was passed on July 14, 2022.[[1]](#cite_note-agreement-1) Hearings on the Senate amendment in the [House Committee on Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Committee_on_Rules "House Committee on Rules") were scheduled for December 4. On December 6, a political agreement was reached between the leaders of the [Senate Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Armed_Services "United States Senate Committee on Armed Services") and [House Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Armed_Services "United States House Committee on Armed Services").[[1]](#cite_note-agreement-1) President Biden signed and enacted [H.R. 7776](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/7776) on December 23, 2022.[[2]](#cite_note-2) In his [signing statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signing_statement "Signing statement"), Biden expressed reservations about restrictions on the transfer of Guantánamo Bay detainees, the potential infringement on the president's constitutional authority regarding national security and sensitive intelligence information, and limitations on the president's discretion in foreign policy and international negotiations.[[3]](#cite_note-3)

## Provisions

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Provisions")]

Military funding authorized by the bill includes:[[4]](#cite_note-4)

* Full funding of the [*Ford*-class carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier "Gerald R. Ford-class aircraft carrier")
* Full funding of the [*Columbia*-class ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia-class_submarine "Columbia-class submarine")

Versions of the legislation expanded the [Selective Service System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_Service_System "Selective Service System") (military draft) to females,[[5]](#cite_note-5) but it was cut in the final bill.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

The NDAA requires the secretary of defense to rescind the directive, implemented in August 2022, that members of the [U.S. armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._armed_forces "U.S. armed forces") be [vaccinated against COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine "COVID-19 vaccine"). During the period the requirement was in effect, 98% of [active duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_duty "Active duty") service members and 96% of all service members had been vaccinated.[[7]](#cite_note-Garamone-7) The bill does not require reinstatement for personnel previously ejected for non-compliance with the order.[[8]](#cite_note-8)[[9]](#cite_note-9)

Section 583 of this NDAA authorizes the president to posthumously promote [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant "Ulysses S. Grant") to the grade of [General of the Armies of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_of_the_Armies "General of the Armies"), equal to the rank and precedence held by both [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington") on July 4, 1976[[10]](#cite_note-10) and [General John J. Pershing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Pershing "John J. Pershing") in and after 1919 (41 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large "United States Statutes at Large") [283](https://legislink.org/us/stat-41-283), ch. 56).[[11]](#cite_note-11)

### Unidentified flying objects (UFOs)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Unidentified flying objects (UFOs)")]

The NDAA and its Intelligence Authorization Act (IAA) includes provisions for reporting of [unidentified flying objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidentified_flying_object "Unidentified flying object") (UFOs), otherwise known as unidentified aerial phenomena or UAPs, including [whistleblower protections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblower_protection_in_the_United_States "Whistleblower protection in the United States") and exemptions to [nondisclosure orders and agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreement "Non-disclosure agreement") (i.e. [50 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_50_of_the_United_States_Code "Title 50 of the United States Code") [§ 3373b](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3373b)).[[12]](#cite_note-12)[[13]](#cite_note-13) It also requires the military to review UFO sightings back to 1945.[[14]](#cite_note-14)

Language on the topic of UAP was also included in the previous [2022 NDAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2022 "National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022") (e.g. [50 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_50_of_the_United_States_Code "Title 50 of the United States Code") [§ 3373](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3373)), the [Intelligence Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_Appropriations_Act,_2022 "Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2022") (e.g. [50 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_50_of_the_United_States_Code "Title 50 of the United States Code") [§ 3373a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3373a)), as well as an accompanying [Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence "United States Senate Select Committee on Intelligence") report for the [Intelligence Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_Appropriations_Act,_2021 "Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021") (i.e. [S. Rept. 116-233](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-report/116th-congress/senate-report/233)).

### BURMA Act of 2022

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: BURMA Act of 2022")]

See also: [Myanmar civil war (2021–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_civil_war_(2021–present) "Myanmar civil war (2021–present)")

This included the [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma "Burma") Unified through Rigorous Military Accountability Act of 2022 (BURMA Act of 2022). The Act aims to restore democracy and address human rights violations in the country through sanctions on leaders of the Myanmar military ([Tatmadaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatmadaw "Tatmadaw")), members of the State Administrative Council (SAC) which was chaired by the commander-in-chief of the military, military-affiliated business entities, conglomerates and state-owned enterprises such as the Myanmar Oil Gas Enterprise (MOGE) – which are the revenue sources of the military and the operational sources to military materials. The sanctions additionally include freezing of assets held in the United States' banks, foreign exchange transactions, and travel bans on individuals for their entry to the United States. It also includes supporting pro-democracy groups, such as the [National Unity Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_Government_of_Myanmar "National Unity Government of Myanmar") (NUG), and providing different types of assistance to strengthen federalism and non-lethal assistance for [ethnic armed organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_armed_organizations_in_Myanmar "List of ethnic armed organizations in Myanmar") (EAOs). The provision of humanitarian assistance for refugees, internally displaced persons, and victims of violence is also mentioned to be continued.[[15]](#cite_note-15) The Act provides the president with additional discretionary authority to make policy changes with respect to Myanmar and enables the secretary of state to assist civilian and international entities with identifying and documenting [war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crime "War crime"), crimes against humanity, and genocide in Myanmar.[[16]](#cite_note-16)

In response, the Burmese junta issued a statement dubbing the legislation an interference in Myanmar's internal affairs, and encroachment on the country's sovereignty.[[17]](#cite_note-17) [The government of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_China "Government of China") also increased diplomatic efforts to engage ethnic armed organizations and the junta to protect their business and geopolitical interests.[[18]](#cite_note-18)

### Taiwan Enhanced Resilience Act

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Taiwan Enhanced Resilience Act")]

The included Taiwan Enhanced Resilience Act contains provisions for increased military aid and security cooperation.[[19]](#cite_note-19) The legislation and a recent visit by the [speaker of the US House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives "Speaker of the United States House of Representatives") are said[*[weasel words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words "Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words")*] to have triggered large military exercises near Taiwan.[[20]](#cite_note-20)

### Aqua Alert Act

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Aqua Alert Act")]

The Aqua Alert Act was attached as an amendment to the NDAA by Congressman [Dean Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Phillips "Dean Phillips") of Minnesota in July 2022.[[21]](#cite_note-21) Similar to an [Amber alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_alert "Amber alert") system, this amendment grants the Coast Guard the ability to create an electronic notification system with the purpose of notifying the public so that they can render aid in searches for distressed individuals on waterways.[[22]](#cite_note-HR_7776-22)

### CCP leadership wealth report

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2023&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: CCP leadership wealth report")]

Inserted by [Marco Rubio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Rubio "Marco Rubio"), section 6501 of the NDAA mandated the [director of national intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_National_Intelligence "Director of National Intelligence") produce a public "Report on wealth and corrupt activities of the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party" within one year.[[23]](#cite_note-23) The release of the report was subsequently postponed.[[24]](#cite_note-24) The report was released in March 2025.[[25]](#cite_note-25)
