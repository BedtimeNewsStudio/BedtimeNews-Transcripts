## [1.](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B3%B4%EC%8B%A0%ED%83%95\#toc) outline

**Boshintang** ( [補](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%E8%A3%9C "補") [湯](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%E8%BA%AB "身") [)](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%E6%B9%AF "湯") is a Korean soup cooked with [dog](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B0%9C%EA%B3%A0%EA%B8%B0 "dog meat") meat.

As [pet](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B0%98%EB%A0%A4%EB%8F%99%EB%AC%BC "companion animal") culture took root , controversies over [animal abuse](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%8F%99%EB%AC%BC%ED%95%99%EB%8C%80 "animal cruelty") gradually arose, and [social problems](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B0%9C%EA%B3%A0%EA%B8%B0/%EA%B8%88%EC%A7%80%20%EB%85%BC%EB%9E%80 "Dog meat/ban controversy") emerged such as the unsanitary and inhumane slaughter of dogs unsuitable for consumption, or the indiscriminate theft and slaughter of other people's pet dogs. Eventually, with the enactment [of the Special Act on the Cessation of Breeding, Slaughter, and Distribution of Dogs for Food](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B0%9C%EC%9D%98%20%EC%8B%9D%EC%9A%A9%20%EB%AA%A9%EC%A0%81%EC%9D%98%20%EC%82%AC%EC%9C%A1%C2%B7%EB%8F%84%EC%82%B4%20%EB%B0%8F%20%EC%9C%A0%ED%86%B5%20%EB%93%B1%20%EC%A2%85%EC%8B%9D%EC%97%90%20%EA%B4%80%ED%95%9C%20%ED%8A%B9%EB%B3%84%EB%B2%95 "Special Act on the End of Breeding, Slaughtering, and Distribution of Dogs for Food Purposes") in 2024 , it is expected that this practice will disappear into history starting in 2027.

## [2.](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B3%B4%EC%8B%A0%ED%83%95\#toc) designation

Its original name is **Gaejangguk** . 'Bosintang ' is a euphemistic name for Gaejangguk, and in addition to Bosintang, there are Boyangtang, Yeongyangtang, [and](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%E8%BA%AB "身") [Sacheoltang](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%E8%A3%9C "補") [.](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%E6%B9%AF "湯") [\[1\]](#fn-1) It is also written as such, and is also called Meongmeongtang. [\[2\]](#fn-2) In old establishments, it is sometimes referred to as 'Gutang' [.](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%E7%8B%97 "狗")

In North Korea, it has been called 'Dangogi-guk.' In North Korea's [standard](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%AC%B8%ED%99%94%EC%96%B4 "cultural language") language, dog meat is referred to as "Dangogi," and because [the interdental](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%82%AC%EC%9D%B4%EC%8B%9C%EC%98%B7 "sassy clothes") 's' is pronounced only verbally and not reflected in the written form, it is written as 'Gogi-guk' rather than 'Gogi-guk.' It is officially served to foreign guests, and there are many restaurants throughout the region selling various Dangogi dishes. This is likely related to the fact that [Kim Jong-il](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B9%80%EC%A0%95%EC%9D%BC "Kim-Jung-il") liked dog meat during his lifetime. It is said that Kim Jong-il always ate dog meat dishes on [the Dog Days of Summer](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B3%B5%EB%82%A0 "dog day") .

Bosintang, formerly known as Gaejangguk, Dangogiguk, or Gutang, gradually came to be called Bosintang starting in the late 1950s or early 1960s, after Baek Yun-jin, a reporter for the Hankook Ilbo from Daegu, began specifically referring to the Gaejangguk and soup made with dog meat from his hometown in [the Gyeongsang-do](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B2%BD%EC%83%81%EB%8F%84 "Gyeongsang-do") region as what is now known as Bosintang. The Syngman Rhee government coined the term Bosintang at the request of business owners seeking permission to establish dog ranches in the provinces, signifying that it is "a high-protein nutritional food for public health that is very good for the body." [\[3\]](#fn-3) I helped with the dog ranch permit process. [\[4\]](#fn-4)

Previously, bosintang did not necessarily mean dog meat soup made from dog meat. Any stew eaten as a health food, such as samgyetang or duck stew, was given a name. So, when you go to a Boshintang restaurant, they not only sell dog meat, but they also often sell [samgyetang](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%82%BC%EA%B3%84%ED%83%95 "Samgyetang") , (oriental medicine) [boiled](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%98%A4%EB%A6%AC "duck") [duck](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B0%B1%EC%88%99 "baeksook") , and [chicken doritang](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%8B%AD%EB%8F%84%EB%A6%AC%ED%83%95 "Chicken doritang") . However, when you think of Bosintang, the first thing that comes to mind is dog meat Bosintang, so Samgyetang House does not necessarily call its restaurant Bosintang House.

## [3.](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B3%B4%EC%8B%A0%ED%83%95\#toc) taste

Boshintang is usually made with [perilla leaves](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B9%BB%EC%9E%8E "Sesame") , [pepper](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%ED%9B%84%EC%B6%94 "pepper") , [perilla seed](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%93%A4%EA%B9%A8 "perilla") powder, and [soybean paste](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%90%9C%EC%9E%A5 "soybean paste") , and then added with various spices to flavor it. This recipe is a way to capture the unique smell of dog meat, and similar seasonings are used in [gamjatang](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B0%90%EC%9E%90%ED%83%95 "Gamjatang") , black goat soup, and Jeolla-do style duck soup.

Therefore, the taste is quite mild. [\[5\]](#fn-5) The flavor of Bosintang, generously seasoned with spices that are neither spicy nor overpowering, ranks quite unique among Korean dishes. If compared to a familiar food, it is similar to Bbyeodagui Haejangguk, but much richer and smoother. It is also similar to [Gamjatang](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B0%90%EC%9E%90%ED%83%95 "Gamjatang") , though the spice flavor is slightly stronger. In particular, the aromas of perilla leaves and pepper are intense. In some parts of Gyeongsang Province, a plant from the Lamiaceae family called [Banga](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B0%B0%EC%B4%88%ED%96%A5 "baechohyang") is added.

When you order Bosintang, they give you a dipping sauce for the meat, and there are even people who seek out Bosintang just because they like the taste of this sauce that blends well with the meat. [\[6\]](#fn-6) Depending on the restaurant, it is usually a dipping sauce made by mixing [perilla](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%93%A4%EA%B8%B0%EB%A6%84 "perilla oil") oil, salt, onions, and garlic. A major advantage of dog meat is that the meat is very tender because it is cooked thoroughly for a long time. Since Korean dog meat dishes use spices such as perilla seeds, perilla leaves, and garlic, as well as soybean paste which absorbs odors, they can be said to have almost no gamey smell compared to dog meat dishes from Southeast Asia and China. [\[7\]](#fn-7) If the bosintang has a strong gamey smell, it means the restaurant is not properly cooking it using spices.

As with most other foods, Bosintang varies considerably by region. In Gyeongsang-do and parts of Gangwon-do, dog meat is slaughtered, buried in earthenware jars of soybean paste or soybean paste to ferment for several days, and then boiled into a soup. It is said that this method not only allows for the odor to be easily eliminated with less spices but also ensures the flavor of the paste permeates the meat, making it tender.

In addition, there are many restaurants that use blood sausage, restaurants that serve it with the bones instead of removing the flesh, restaurants that use red pepper paste, restaurants that use little or no soybean paste and season it with salted fish, etc., etc. This is because Boshintang itself is a food that does not have a single, definitive recipe.

There is also Bosintang made with [beef](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%87%A0%EA%B3%A0%EA%B8%B0 "beef") instead of [dog](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B0%9C%EA%B3%A0%EA%B8%B0 "dog meat") meat. [\[8\]](#fn-8) It is also called Boyangtang or Beef Nutritional Soup. However, since beef is somewhat firmer and tougher, the texture is different, and the taste of the meat and fat is also different, making it a subtle substitute.

Since the ban on dog slaughter, goat soup has been gaining popularity as an alternative to bosintang. It is not uncommon to see restaurants that used to sell dog meat switching to [goat meat](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%97%BC%EC%86%8C%EA%B3%A0%EA%B8%B0 "goat meat") , and some are even converting their business to serve samgyetang or duck soup. This is because goat meat tastes similar to dog meat while having a better image. However, the price is much higher than that of traditional bosintang.

## [4.](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%B3%B4%EC%8B%A0%ED%83%95\#toc) history

Since ancient times , [dogs](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EA%B0%9C "dog") have been considered to be pets that have been with humans, but have also been a great ingredient for many people. The people who eat dogs seemed to have been quite large, not only in Korea but also in [China](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%A4%91%EA%B5%AD "china") and [Maya](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EB%A7%88%EC%95%BC "Maya") .
