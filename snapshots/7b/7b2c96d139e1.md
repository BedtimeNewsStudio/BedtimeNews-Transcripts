The **Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party** ( [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): حزب البعث العربي الاشتراكي __Ḥizb al-Baʿth al-ʿArabī al-Ishtirākī__[\[ˈħɪzbalˈbaʕθalˈʕarabiːalɪʃtɪˈraːkiː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Arabic "Help:IPA/Arabic")), also known simply as the **Baʽath Party** ( [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): [حزب البعث](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D8%AD%D8%B2%D8%A8%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%B9%D8%AB#Etymology_1.2 "wikt:حزب البعث"), [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation "Literal translation")'resurrection party'),[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-2) was a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party "Political party") founded in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria") by [Michel Aflaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Aflaq "Michel Aflaq"), [Salah al-Din al-Bitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_al-Din_al-Bitar "Salah al-Din al-Bitar"), and associates of [Zaki al-Arsuzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaki_al-Arsuzi "Zaki al-Arsuzi"). The party espoused [Ba'athism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'athism "Ba'athism")—an ideology mixing [Arab nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nationalist "Arab nationalist"), [pan-Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arab "Pan-Arab"), [Arab socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_socialist "Arab socialist"), and [anti-imperialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-imperialist "Anti-imperialist") interests. Ba'athism calls for the unification of the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world") into a [single united state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Union "Arab Union"). Its motto, " [Unity, Freedom, Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity,_Freedom,_Socialism "Unity, Freedom, Socialism")", refers to [Arab unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism "Pan-Arabism") and [freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-imperialism "Anti-imperialism") from foreign control and interference as well as supporting [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism "Socialism").

The party was founded by the merger of the [Arab Ba'ath Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Ba'ath_Movement "Arab Ba'ath Movement"), led by Aflaq and al-Bitar, and the [Arab Ba'ath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Ba'ath "Arab Ba'ath"), led by al-ʾArsūzī, on 7 April 1947 as the **Arab Ba'ath Party**. The party quickly established branches in other Arab countries - although it would only hold power in [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq") and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"). In 1953, the Arab Ba'ath Party merged with the [Arab Socialist Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Movement "Arab Socialist Movement"), led by [Akram al-Hourani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akram_al-Hourani "Akram al-Hourani"), to form the **Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party**. The newly formed party was a relative success, and it became the second-largest party in the Syrian parliament in the [1954 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Syrian_parliamentary_election "1954 Syrian parliamentary election"). This, coupled with the increasing strength of the [Syrian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Communist_Party "Syrian Communist Party"),\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] led to the establishment of the [United Arab Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic "United Arab Republic") (UAR), a union made up of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") and Syria, in 1958. The UAR would prove unsuccessful, and was dissolved following the [1961 Syrian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1961 Syrian coup d'état").

Following the break-up of the [UAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic "United Arab Republic"), the Ba'ath Party was reconstituted. However, during the UAR period, military activists had established the Military Committee that took control of the Ba'ath Party away from civilian hands. In the meantime, in Iraq, [the local Ba'ath Party branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Iraq_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Iraq Region") had taken power by orchestrating and leading the [Ramadan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_Revolution "Ramadan Revolution"), only to [lose power a couple of months later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_1963_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "November 1963 Iraqi coup d'état"). The Military Committee, with Aflaq's consent, took power in Syria in the [1963 Syrian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1963 Syrian coup d'état").

A power struggle quickly developed between the civilian faction led by Aflaq, al-Bitar, and [Munīf ar-Razzāz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munif_Razzaz "Munif Razzaz") and the Military Committee led by [Salah Jadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_Jadid "Salah Jadid") and [Hafez al-Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez_al-Assad "Hafez al-Assad"). As relations between the two factions deteriorated, the Military Committee initiated the [1966 Syrian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1966 Syrian coup d'état"), which ousted the National Command led by al-Razzāz, Aflaq, and their supporters. The 1966 coup split the Ba'ath Party between the [Iraqi-dominated Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party_(Iraqi-dominated_faction) "Ba'ath Party (Iraqi-dominated faction)") and the [Syrian-dominated Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party_(Syrian-dominated_faction) "Ba'ath Party (Syrian-dominated faction)"). There are currently no Ba’athist run countries after [the fall of the Ba’ath regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Assad_regime "Fall of the Assad regime") in Syria on 8 December 2024.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

Main article: [History of the Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Ba'ath_Party "History of the Ba'ath Party")

The party was founded on 7 April 1947 [\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-3) as the Arab Ba'ath Party by [Michel Aflaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Aflaq "Michel Aflaq") (an [Antiochian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Antioch "Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch") [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians")), [Salah al-Din al-Bitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_al-Din_al-Bitar "Salah al-Din al-Bitar") (a [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam") [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "Muslim")), and the followers of [Zaki al-Arsuzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaki_al-Arsuzi "Zaki al-Arsuzi") (an [Alawite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawites "Alawites") who later became an atheist) in [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus "Damascus"), Syria, leading to the establishment of the [Syrian Regional Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Syria_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Syria Region").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200366-4) Other regional branches were established throughout the Arab world in the later 1940s and early 1950s, in, among others, [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Iraq_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Iraq Region"),[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolk2006109-5) [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Yemen_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Yemen Region") and [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party "Jordanian Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199098-6) Throughout its existence, the National Command (the body responsible for all-Arab affairs) gave most attention to Syrian affairs.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200366-4) The 2nd National Congress was convened in June 1954 and elected a seven-man National Command; Aflaq, Bitar, and [Akram al-Hourani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akram_al-Hourani "Akram al-Hourani") were elected and represented the Syrian Regional Branch,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEReich199010-7) while [Abdullah Rimawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Rimawi "Abdullah Rimawi") and Abdallah Na'was were elected to represent the Jordanian Branch.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005231-8) The 1954 congress is notable for sanctioning the merger of the [Arab Socialist Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Movement "Arab Socialist Movement") and the Ba'ath Party, which took place supposedly in January 1953, with the newly merged party resuming publications under the name the Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party after 8 April 1954.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaylani197213-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-10)

The Syrian Regional Branch rose to prominence in the 1940s and 1950s; in the [1954 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Syrian_parliamentary_election "1954 Syrian parliamentary election") the Syrian Regional Branch won 22 seats in parliament, becoming the second largest party in the country.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaylani197215-11) 90 percent of Ba'ath Party members who stood for elections were elected to parliament.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaylani197215-11) The failure of the traditional parties, represented by the [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_(Syria) "People's Party (Syria)") and the [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_(Syria) "National Party (Syria)"), strengthened the Ba'ath Party's public credibility.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaylani197216-12) Through this position, the party was able to get two of its members into the cabinet; Bitar was appointed [Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_and_Expatriates_(Syria) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates (Syria)") and Khalil Kallas became Minister of Economics.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaylani197219-13) Its new, strengthened position was used successfully to garner support for Syria's merger with [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser "Gamal Abdel Nasser")'s Egypt, which led to the establishment of the [United Arab Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic "United Arab Republic") (UAR) in 1958.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaylani197219%E2%80%9321-14)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/President_Gamal_Abdul_Nasser_with_Baath_Party_founders_Michel_Aflaq_and_Salah_al-Bitar_in_1958.jpg/250px-President_Gamal_Abdul_Nasser_with_Baath_Party_founders_Michel_Aflaq_and_Salah_al-Bitar_in_1958.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Gamal_Abdul_Nasser_with_Baath_Party_founders_Michel_Aflaq_and_Salah_al-Bitar_in_1958.jpg) [Michel Aflaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Aflaq "Michel Aflaq") (left) and [Salah al-Din Bitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_al-Din_al-Bitar "Salah al-Din al-Bitar") (right) with Egyptian President [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser "Gamal Abdel Nasser") (centre) in 1958. The three leaders were prime advocates for a [pan-Arab union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Union "Arab Union")

On 24 June 1959, [Fuad al-Rikabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuad_al-Rikabi "Fuad al-Rikabi"), the Regional Secretary of the Iraqi Regional Branch, accused the National Command of betraying Arab nationalist principles by conspiring against the UAR.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960271-15) In light of these criticisms, the Ba'ath convened the 3rd National Congress, held 27 August–1 September 1959, attended by delegates from Iraq, Lebanon, Jordan, South Arabia, the Gulf, "Arab South", "Arab Maghreb", Palestine, and Party student organisations in Arab and other universities.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinistry_of_Information197133-16) The congress is notable for endorsing the dissolution of the Syrian Regional Branch, which had been decided by Aflaq and Bitar without inner-party consultation in 1958,[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPodeh1999219-17) and for expelling Rimawi, the Regional Secretary of the Jordanian Regional Branch.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960497-18) Rimawi reacted to his expulsion by forming his own party, the [Arab Socialist Revolutionary Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Revolutionary_Ba'ath_Party "Arab Socialist Revolutionary Ba'ath Party"), which established a rival National Command to compete with the original.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005203-19) The National Command responded to the problems in Iraq by appointing a Temporary Regional Command on 2 February 1960, which appointed [Talib El-Shibib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talib_El-Shibib "Talib El-Shibib") as Regional Secretary,[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960271-15) and on 15 June 1961 the National Command expelled Rikabi from the party.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960271-15)

In Iraq, the Iraqi Regional Branch had supported [Abdul-Karim Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Karim_Qasim "Abdul-Karim Qasim")'s seizure of power and its ensuing abolishment of the Iraqi Monarchy.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200524%E2%80%9325-20) The Iraqi Ba'athists supported Qasim on the grounds that they believed he would enter Iraq into the UAR, enlarging the Arab nationalist republic.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200524%E2%80%9325-20) However, this was proven to be a ruse, and after taking power, Qasim launched an Iraq first policy.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200524%E2%80%9325-20) In retaliation, the Ba'ath Party tried to assassinate Qasim in February 1959, but the operation, involving a young [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein "Saddam Hussein"), failed.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200529-21) Qasim was overthrown in the [Ramadan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_Revolution "Ramadan Revolution") led by young Ba'athist officer [Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Hassan_al-Bakr "Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr"); long suspected to be supported by the American [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") (CIA),[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200540-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-23) however pertinent contemporary documents relating to the CIA's operations in Iraq have remained classified by the U.S. government,[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-25) although the Iraqi Ba'athists are documented to have maintained supportive relationships with U.S. officials before, during, and after the coup.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-27) The Iraqi Regional Branch, when it took power, was so riven by factionalism that its purported allies launched a counter-coup forcing them out of power in November 1963.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200544%E2%80%9346-28)

The 4th National Congress, held in August 1960, criticized the leadership of Aflaq and Bitar, called for the reestablishment of the Syrian Regional Branch and deemphasized the party's commitment to [Arab nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nationalism "Arab nationalism") while emphasizing more the socialist character of the party.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199066-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197224-30) A year later, during the UAR's nadir in Syria, the Syrian General [Abdul Karim al-Nahlawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Karim_al-Nahlawi "Abdul Karim al-Nahlawi") launched [a coup on 28 September 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1961 Syrian coup d'état"), which led to the reestablishment of the Syrian Arab Republic.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-USDoS-31)

### Rule in Syria, infighting, the 1966 coup and split: 1963–1966

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Rule in Syria, infighting, the 1966 coup and split: 1963–1966")\]

The challenges of building a Ba'athist state led to considerable ideological discussion and internal struggle within the party.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeFronzo200961-32) The Iraqi Regional Branch was increasingly dominated by self-described [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism "Marxism") [Ali Salih al-Sa'di](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Salih_al-Sa'di "Ali Salih al-Sa'di").[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeFronzo200961-32) Al-Sa'di was supported in his ideological reorientation by [Hammud al-Shufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammud_al-Shufi "Hammud al-Shufi"), the Regional Secretary of the Syrian Regional Branch;[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-33) Yasin al-Hafiz, one of the party's few ideological theorists; and by certain members of the secret Military Committee.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199087-34) The Marxist wing gained new ground at the 6th National Congress (held in October 1963), in which the Iraqi and Syrian regional branches called for the establishment of "socialist planning",[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAli2004105-35) " [collective farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_farming "Collective farming") run by peasants", "workers' democratic control of the means of production", and other demands reflecting a certain emulation of [Soviet-style socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Ideology of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union").[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHiro1982143-36) Aflaq, angry at this transformation of his party, retained a nominal leadership role, but the National Command as a whole came under the control of the radicals.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAli2004106%E2%80%93107-37)

In 1963, the Ba'ath Party [seized power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1963 Syrian coup d'état") in Syria, and from then on the Ba'ath functioned as the only officially recognized Syrian political party, but factionalism and splintering within the party led to a succession of varying governments and new constitutions.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division200455-38) On 23 February 1966, [a coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1966 Syrian coup d'état") led by [Salah Jadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_Jadid "Salah Jadid"), the informal head of the Military Committee, overthrew Aflaq and Bitar's cabinet.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division200459-39) The coup sprung out of factional rivalry between Jadid's "regionalist" (qutri) camp, which promoted ambitions for a [Greater Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_(region) "Syria (region)"), and the more traditionally [pan-Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism "Pan-Arabism") faction then in power, the "nationalist" (qawmi) faction.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division200459-39) Jadid's supporters were considered to have been more left-wing then Aflaq and his peers.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division2004213-40) Several of Jadid's opponents managed to make their escape, and they fled to [Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut "Beirut"), [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon").[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division200459-39) Jadid moved the party in a more radical direction. Although he and his supporters had not signed onto the victorious far-left line at the 6th Party Congress, they had now moved to adopt its positions.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division2004213-40) The moderate faction, formerly led by Aflaq and al-Bitar, were purged from the party.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division2004213-40)

While it took some years, the 1966 coup resulted in the creation of two competing National Commands, [one Syrian-dominated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party_(Syrian-dominated_faction) "Ba'ath Party (Syrian-dominated faction)") and another [Iraqi-dominated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party_(Iraqi-dominated_faction) "Ba'ath Party (Iraqi-dominated faction)").[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAli2004106%E2%80%93107-37) However, both in Iraq and Syria, the Regional Command became the real centre of party power, and the membership of the National Command became a largely honorary position, often the destination of figures being eased out of the leadership.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAli2004106%E2%80%93107-37) One consequence of the split was that [Zaki al-Arsuzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaki_al-Arsuzi "Zaki al-Arsuzi") took Aflaq's place as the official father of [Ba'athist thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'athism "Ba'athism") in the pro-Syrian Ba'ath movement, while the pro-Iraqi Ba'ath movement still considered Aflaq the _[de jure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_jure "De jure")_ father of Ba'athist thought.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBengio1998218-41)

## Organization

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Organization")\]

The organizational structure of the Ba'ath Party was created at the 2nd National Congress (1954) by amending the party's Internal Regulations (_An-Nidhāmu-d-Dākhilī_), which had been previously approved at the party's 1st National Congress (1947).[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999392-42) The organizational structure ran from top to bottom, and members were forbidden to initiate contacts between groups on the same level of the organisation, i.e., all contacts had to pass through a higher command level.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43)

### National organization

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: National organization")\]

Further information: [National Command of the Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Command_of_the_Ba'ath_Party "National Command of the Ba'ath Party")

The National Command was the ruling organ of the party between sessions of the National Congress and was headed by a Secretary-General.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECommins200465-44) Between National Congresses, the National Command was held accountable by the National Consultative Council (Arabic: _al-majlis al-istishari al-qawmi_).[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972231-45) The National Consultative Council was a forum made up of representatives from the party's regional branches.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972231-45) However, the number of National Consultative Council members was decided by the size of the regional branch.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972231-45) The National Congress elected the National Command, National Tribunal, the party's discipline body, and the Secretary-General, the party leader.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972231-45) The congress delegates determined the party's policies and procedures.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972231-45)

Before 1954, the party was ruled by the Executive Committee, but this organ, along with others, too, was replaced at the 2nd National Congress in 1954.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECommins200465-44) In Ba'athist jargon, "Nation" means the [Arab Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nationalism "Arab nationalism"), because of that, the National Command formed the highest policy-making and coordinating council for the Ba'ath movement throughout the Arab world.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECommins200465-44) The National Command had several bureaus, similar to those of the Regional Command.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972231-45) National Command sessions were held monthly.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972231-45) Of these, the National Liaisons Office was responsible for maintaining contact with the party's Regional Branches.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972148-46)

### Regional organization

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Regional organization")\]

A "region" ( _quṭr_), in Ba'athist parlance, is an Arab state, e.g., Syria, Iraq, or Lebanon.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoueiri2000234-47) Use of the term _region_ reflected the Party's refusal to acknowledge these countries as separate nation-states.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoueiri2000234-47) The Regional Congress, which combined all the provincial branches, was the region's highest authority and elected a Regional Command, the party leadership in a specific region; the Regional Tribunal, the body responsible for discipline inspection; and a Regional Secretary, the regional party leader.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) The Regional Congress was made of delegates from the provincial branches; other members attended, but as observers.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) The Regional Congress was responsible for evaluating the party's performance since the last Regional Congress, while at the same time formulating new policies for the next period, which would last until the next Regional Congress was held.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) How long this period lasted was decided by the Regional Command.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) The Regional Command, similar to the Branch Command, operated through bureaus and met for weekly-sessions.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43)

Below the Regional Commands were _branches_.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoueiri2000234-47) The branch came above the sub-branch; it comprised at least two to five sub-branches[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) and operated at the provincial level.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoueiri2000234-47) The branch held a congress periodically in which it elected a Command and a Secretary (leader).[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) The Command operated through bureaus, such as the Workers Bureau and the Bureau of the Secretariat.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) The _sub-branch_ level constituted three to five sections "and was the lowest level of the party to hold a periodical Congress."[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) Some sub-branches were independent of central authority and elected their own Command and secretaries, while other sub-branches were incorporated into the branches.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) In the latter case, the sub-branch secretary would be appointed by the superior branch.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43)

A _section_, which comprised from two to five divisions, functioned at the level of a large city quarter, a town, or a rural district.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECommins200465-44) It elected its own command, composed of five members, but the sub-branch appointed the command's secretary.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) Beneath the section were _divisions_.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECommins200465-44) A division comprised two to seven _circles_, controlled by a division commander.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECommins200465-44) The lowest level was the circle. It was composed of three to seven members, constituting the basic organizational unit.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECommins200465-44) Such Ba'athist groups occurred throughout the bureaucracy and the military. They functioned as the Party's watchdogs and were an effective form of covert surveillance within a public administration.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoueiri2000234-47)

The Military Organization was made up of branches similar to those in the Ba'ath's civilian sector.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) However, unlike the civilian sector, the Military Organization was controlled by a separate Military Bureau and held periodical Military Congresses.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) The Military Organization and the Civilian Organization converged at the Regional Congress.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43)

### Membership

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Membership")\]

There existed three types of membership categories in the Ba'ath Party: Active member (Arabic: _udw ämil_), Apprentice Member (Arabic: _udw mutadarrib_) and Supporter (Arabic: _firqa_).[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) An Active member had to attend all formal meetings of his party unit, was given the right to vote in party elections,[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) and could run for party office.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43) In the Syrian Regional Branch, a member had to spend 18 months as a Supporter to be promoted to Apprentice status, and then wait another 18 months to be promoted to Active member status.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972230-43)

## Ideology and policy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Ideology and policy")\]

Further information: [Ba'athism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'athism "Ba'athism")

### Foundational Ba'ath: 1947–1960

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Foundational Ba'ath: 1947–1960")\]

#### Arab Nation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Arab Nation")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Ba%27ath_Constitution.jpg/250px-Ba%27ath_Constitution.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ba'ath_Constitution.jpg) Part of the 1947 Ba'ath Party constitution

For more than 2 decades, [Michel Aflaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Aflaq "Michel Aflaq")'s essay compilation titled " _Fi Sabil al-Ba'ath_" (translation: "The Road to Renaissance") was the primary ideological book of the Ba'ath party. The work was published by Aflaq in 1940.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-48) From its very beginning, the party was a manifestation of [Arab nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nationalism "Arab nationalism") thought, with the party describing itself as "The Party of Arab Unity".[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999134-49) The pan-Arab tendencies of the party's predecessor, the [Arab Ba'ath Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Ba'ath_Movement "Arab Ba'ath Movement"), were strengthened in 1945–1947 by recruiting members from [Zaki al-Arsuzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaki_al-Arsuzi "Zaki al-Arsuzi")'s Arab Ba'ath.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999135-50) The first article of the party's constitution stated that: "...the Arabs form one nation. This nation has the natural right to live in a single state. \[As such,\] the Arab fatherland constitutes an indivisible political and economic unit. No Arab can live apart from the others."[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEClaessen201024-51)

To express his heartfelt belief in Arab nationalism, Aflaq coined the term "one Arab nation with an eternal message" ( [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): _ummah arabiyyah wahidah thatu risalah khalidah_).[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199030-52) Party ideology, and Ba'athism in general, was not based on concepts such as the purity of the Arab race or ethnic chauvinism, but on idealistic concepts borrowed from the [enlightenment era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment").[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199030%E2%80%9331-53) According to author Tabitha Petran, the basic tenet of the party's ideology was:[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoaddel2005229-54)

> ...that the Arab nation is a permanent entity in history. The Arab nation is considered, philosophically speaking, not as a social and economic formation, but as a transcendent fact inspiring different forms, one of its highest contributions taking the form of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"). It was not Islam that modeled the peoples of Arabia, the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent "Fertile Crescent"), and [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa"), equipping them with Islamic values, especially the Arabic language and the Arabic culture, but the Arab nation which created Islam. This conception of the Arab nation implicitly advantages the Arab contribution to history. On the other hand, Arab decadence can be overcome through a purifying and spiritual action, not religious but moral.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoaddel2005229-54)

#### Peasant and workers

The early Ba'ath gave very little attention to the problems facing the peasants and workers.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) As the historian [Hanna Batatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna_Batatu "Hanna Batatu") notes, "Aflaq was basically urban in outlook. The peasants never constituted an object of his special concern. In his writing there is scarcely an expression of concentrated interest in the country's husbandsmen."[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) While peasants and the issues they faced are mentioned in some of Aflaq's work, there was scarcely any depth given to them.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) Aflaq never expressed explicit enmity towards traditional landowners.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) Issues such as these would only gain prominence when [Akram al-Hourani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akram_al-Hourani "Akram al-Hourani") became a leading party figure and when the "transitional Ba'athists" took power.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) Of the four members in the 1st Executive Committee, [Wahib al-Ghanim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahib_al-Ghanim "Wahib al-Ghanim") was the only one who paid much attention to the problems of peasants and workers,[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) as the other members (Aflaq, [Salah al-Din al-Bitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_al-Din_al-Bitar "Salah al-Din al-Bitar") and Jalil al-Sayyide) had a [middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class "Middle class") upbringing and upheld middle-class values.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999134%E2%80%93136-56)

The early party organization never cultivated a deep following in rural areas.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) In fact, at the party's founding congress, only one peasant and one worker were present among the 217 delegates.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) Most of the delegates were either school teacher or students attending universities.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) When [Akram al-Hourani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akram_al-Hourani "Akram al-Hourani")'s [Arab Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Movement "Arab Socialist Movement") (ASP) merged with the Ba'ath Party, the majority of ASP members of peasant origin did not join the Ba'ath Party, instead becoming personal followers of Hawrani.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) However, the majority of Ba'ath members were of rural upbringing.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999136-55) The "Transitional Ba'ath", which grew out of the dissolution of the Syrian Regional Branch (1958) and the Military Committee, was more rural in outlook, policy and ideology.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatatu1999144%E2%80%93145-57)

#### "Unity, Freedom, Socialism"

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: \"Unity, Freedom, Socialism\"")\]

Main article: [Waḥda, Ḥurriyya, Ishtirākiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa%E1%B8%A5da,_%E1%B8%A4urriyya,_Ishtir%C4%81kiyya "Waḥda, Ḥurriyya, Ishtirākiyya")

The slogan " [Unity, Freedom, Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa%E1%B8%A5da,_%E1%B8%A4urriyya,_Ishtir%C4%81kiyya "Waḥda, Ḥurriyya, Ishtirākiyya")" is the key tenet in Ba'athist thought.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199462-58) Unity stood for the creation of an independent, strong Arab Nation.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199465-66-59) [Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty "Liberty") did not mean [liberal democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy "Liberal democracy"), but rather freedom from colonial oppression and [freedom of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_speech "Freedom of speech") and [thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_thought "Freedom of thought").[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199466%E2%80%9368-60) Aflaq believed that the Ba'ath Party, at least in theory, would rule, and guide the people, in a transitional period of time without consulting the people,[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199467%E2%80%9368]-61) however he did support intra-party democracy.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199467-62) The last tenet, 'socialism', did not mean socialism as defined in the West, but rather a unique form of [Arab socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_socialism "Arab socialism").[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199469%E2%80%9370-63) According to Ba'athist thought, socialism had originated under the rule of the Prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad "Muhammad").[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199469%E2%80%9370-63) The original interpretation of Arab socialism did not answer questions regarding economic equality or how much [state control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_socialism "State socialism") was necessary, but instead focused on freeing the Arab Nation and its people from colonization and oppression in general.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalem199469%E2%80%9370-63)

### Transitional Ba'ath: 1960–1964

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Transitional Ba'ath: 1960–1964")\]

#### Regionalists versus nationalists

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Regionalists versus nationalists")\]

After the failure of the [United Arab Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic "United Arab Republic") (UAR), a union of Egypt and Syria, the Ba'ath Party was divided into two main factions, the [Regionalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutriyun "Qutriyun") ( [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): _Qutriyyun_) and the Nationalists (pan-Arab) ( [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): _Qawmiyyun_).[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197236-64) When the union with Egypt collapsed, the Ba'ath Party was put in a difficult position, as the party still sought Arab unity but did not oppose the UAR's dissolution and did not want to seek another union with Egypt under [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser "Gamal Abdel Nasser")'s rule.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197236-64) However, being the unionist party that it was, the party's leaders could not state their position on this issue.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197236-64) The end result was that the pro-Arab nationalists within the Ba'ath Party became committed [Nasserists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasserism "Nasserism"), while the more moderate Arab nationalists founded the pro-Nasserite [Socialist Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Unionist_Party_(Syria) "Socialist Unionist Party (Syria)") party.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197236-64) A third group, led by people disenchanted with both Nasser and the union period, remained in the Ba'ath Party but stopped believing in the feasibility of pan-Arabism.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197236-64) On 21 February 1962, the National Command issued a new policy regarding the pan-Arab project by first mentioning the successes and failures of the UAR, but ending the statement by calling for the reestablishment of the UAR as a decentralized [federal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation "Federation") with Nasser's Egypt.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197237-65) Many rank-and-file members opposed this change in policy, with many members being both disenchanted with pan-Arabism and Aflaq's continued party rule.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197237-65)

When the Syrian Regional Branch was reestablished, the majority of its members in the provinces were of communal origins – Druze, Alawi, or Ismaili.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968166-66) The provincial party members had not been told of the Syrian Regional Branch's dissolution, which in fact broke the communication line with provincial branches and the National Command.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968166-66) While it is true that in 1962 the Regionalists supported the slogan adopted at the 5th National Congress, "the renewal of the union with Egypt while taking note of past mistakes", they treated such a slogan as a propaganda slogan rather than a feasible goal.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968166%E2%80%93167-67)

#### The "Arab road to socialism"

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: The \"Arab road to socialism\"")\]

The disillusionment felt among party members on the pan-Arab project, led to the radicalization of the party's interpretation of socialism.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197241-68) Yasin al-Hafiz, a former member of the [Syrian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Communist_Party "Syrian Communist Party"), was an early frontrunner for the party's radicalization.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197241-68) While he didn't oppose the pan-Arab project, he wanted to turn the concept of Arab socialism into a [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_socialism "Scientific socialism") and [revolutionary socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_socialism "Revolutionary socialism") ideology which adapted [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism "Marxism") to local conditions.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197241-68) [Jamal al-Atassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_al-Atassi "Jamal al-Atassi"), who had been a moderate socialist for most of his life, called for the renunciation of Arab socialism in 1963 and the adoption of a "virtually Marxist concept of socialism" by claiming that [class struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_conflict "Class conflict") was the moving force in society.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197241%E2%80%9342-69)

[Hammud al-Shufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammud_al-Shufi "Hammud al-Shufi") became the leader of the party's Marxist faction during his short stint as Syrian Regional Secretary, literally the head of the Syrian Regional Organization.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197277-70) Shufi was able, due to his position as head of the Organization Bureau of the [Regional Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Command_of_the_Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Syria_Region "Central Command of the Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Syria Region"), to recruit several Marxist or Marxist-leaning members to the top of the Syrian Regional party hierarchy.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197277%E2%80%9378-71) Radical socialists led by Ali Salih al-Sadi took control of the [Iraqi Regional Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Iraq_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Iraq Region") in 1963, which led to the official radicalization of the party's ideology.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197282%E2%80%9383-72)

The delegates at the 6th National Congress elected an Ideology Committee that was responsible for writing a charter about the party's ideology.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197284%E2%80%9386-73) The end result was the document _Points of Departure_.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197284%E2%80%9386-73) The document, which was approved by the 6th National Congress, relegated Arab unity to a secondary role and gave socialism prominence.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197284%E2%80%9386-73) Marxist concepts were used interchangeably alongside Ba'athist ones; however, the document was reluctant in explicitly admitting that certain ideas were of Marxist origins.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197287-74) The 6th National Congress borrowed key [Marxist-Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism "Marxism–Leninism") tenets such as " [people's democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_democracy_(Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) "People's democracy (Marxism–Leninism)")" and emphasized the need of a [socialist vanguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguardism "Vanguardism") in-order to:

> "play the role of mediator and leader (even if it is in power) that acts to direct the journey of the masses towards the socialist future in a scientific way and in a democratic style".[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-75)

While the _Points of Departure_ didn't create a break with the party's traditional ideology, it criticized the party's old guard for giving Arab unity primacy over socialism and their failure to turn Ba'athism into a comprehensive theory.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197287-74) While the documents says Arab unity is progressive, the reason for it being important changed.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197288-76) The document stated: "Arab unity is an indispensable basis for the construction of a [socialist economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_economics "Socialist economics")."[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197288-76) Aflaq also believed that Arab unity was only an intermediate goal, but it stood at the centre of classical Ba'athism.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197288-76) In the _Points of Departure_, despite not firmly stating it, the goal of creating a socialist society seemed to be both an immediate goal and the main goal of the party.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197288-76)

The concept of Arab socialism, accused of being narrow-minded and nationalistic, was replaced with the "Arab road to socialism" concept.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197288-76) The _Points of Departure_ criticized the classical Ba'athist view regarding [private ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property "Private property").[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197288-76) Classical Ba'athists supported private ownership as a way to recruit into the party many [petty bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petite_bourgeoisie "Petite bourgeoisie") elements.[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197289-77) The document called for [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization "Nationalization") of the commanding heights of the economy, the slow incorporation of the petty bourgeoisie into the socialist economy and the elimination of the [national bourgeoisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie "Bourgeoisie") and its allied classes.[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197289-77) To safeguard the party from evolving into one supporting [state capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capitalism "State capitalism"), the socialist economy would be controlled by a [vanguard party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguardism "Vanguardism") together with popular participation from the toiler masses.[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197289-77) Major policies in the "Arab road to socialism" included:

> The [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization "Nationalization") of the major branches of the economy with the participation of the toiling masses in the management of the economy, and for the creation of [collective farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_farming "Collective farming") to effect the [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution "Revolution") necessary for the peasants...the Congress stipulated that the changes be directed by a "revolutionary vanguard", the final aim being to establish a "popular democracy" that was to guarantee freedom to the classes which constitute the true people and ensure the country's rapid development. This regime was to center on the party, leading the popular organizations and councils and operating according to the principle of " [democratic centralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_centralism "Democratic centralism")"[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-78)

#### Stance on religion

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Stance on religion")\]

[Militant secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_atheism "State atheism") was emphasized in the " _Declaration of Principles_" manifesto published by the Ba'ath party in 1960; which declared that the party's "educational policy" was to build a "new generation of Arabs that believe in the unity of the nation and the eternity of its mission".[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-79) The manifesto also stated that this envisaged Ba'athist generation would be "committed to [scientific thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science") freed from the shackles of superstition and backward customs" and replace religion with [Arab nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nationalism "Arab nationalism") as their belief system.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-80)

### Neo-Ba'ath: 1964–1966

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Neo-Ba'ath: 1964–1966")\]

Further information: [Neo-Ba'athism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Ba'athism "Neo-Ba'athism")

[Neo-Ba'athism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Ba'athism "Neo-Ba'athism") refers to the dramatic changes that manifested in Ba'athist ideology from 1960 to 1964, and the Military Committee's takeover of the Syrian Regional Branch and the National Command in the period 1964 to 1966.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968170%E2%80%93175-81) The 6th National Congress signified the takeover of the party by an anti-militarist left, which opposed both the traditional leaders in the National Command and the pragmatists in the Military Committee.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968174%E2%80%93175-82) When the anti-military left called for [popular democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_democracy "Popular democracy"), no involvement of the military in national politics and popular struggle, the Military Committee became concerned.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968175-83) In 1965, Ba'athist President [Amin al-Hafiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amin_al-Hafiz "Amin al-Hafiz") imposed the socialist policies adopted in the 6th National Congress; fully nationalizing Syrian industry and vast segments of the private sector, and establishing a centralized [command economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_economy#Command_economy "Planned economy").[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-84)

By 1965 the anti-military leftists began to "spread rumors about the rightist character of the military junta \[Military Committee\] within the party and their subversive efforts to engulf it. There was not a single officer in the party who was not accused of conspiracy and reactionary tendencies."[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968177-85) In collaboration with the National Command, the Military Committee succeeded in expelling the anti-military left from the party at the 7th National Congress.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968179-86) The Military Committee, which now controlled the Syrian Regional Branch, took control of the Ba'ath Party in the [coup of 1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1966 Syrian coup d'état").[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968180-87) The military committee accused the Old Guard of diluting socialist ideology and casting aside " [collective leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_leadership "Collective leadership")".[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-88) According to Middle East expert Avraham Ben-Tzur, "the \[neo-\]Ba'th in its latest variant is a bureaucratic apparatus headed by the military, whose daily life and routine are shaped by the rigid military oppression on the home front, and \[Soviet aid among others\] military aid."[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-Tzur1968181-89)

## Regional branches

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Regional branches")\]

### Iraq

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Iraq")\]

Main article: [Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Iraq Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Iraq_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Iraq Region")

[Fuad al-Rikabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuad_al-Rikabi "Fuad al-Rikabi") founded the Iraqi Regional Branch in 1951[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolk2006109-5) or 1952.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhareebDougherty2004194-90) There are those who trace the branch's founding to Abd ar Rahman ad Damin and Abd al Khaliq al Khudayri in 1947, after their return from the 1st National Congress, which was held in Syria.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-91) Another version is that the branch was established in 1948 by Rikabi and Sa'dun Hamadi, a [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam "Shia Islam") [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim "Muslim").[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShefferMa'oz2002174-92) However, Efraim Karsh and Inari Rautsi contend that the Regional Branch was established in the 1940s, but that it received official recognition as a Regional Branch of the Ba'ath Party in 1952 by the National Command.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKarshRautsi199113-93) What is certain is that Rikabi was elected the Regional Branch's first Regional Secretary in 1952.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShefferMa'oz2002174-92)

The party initially consisted of a majority of Shia Muslims, as Rikabi recruited supporters mainly from his friends and family, but slowly became [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam") dominated.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTENakash2003136-94) The Regional Branch, and other parties of pan-Arab inclination, had difficulties in recruiting Shia members.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDawisha2005174-95) Most Shi'ites considered pan-Arab ideology as a Sunni project, since the majority of Arabs are Sunnis.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDawisha2005174-95)

At the time of [14 July Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_July_Revolution "14 July Revolution") in 1958, which overthrew the [Hashemite monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq "Kingdom of Iraq"), the Regional Branch had 300 members.[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200522-96) The Iraqi Regional Branch supported [Abdul-Karim Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Karim_Qasim "Abdul-Karim Qasim")'s rule on the grounds that he would seek Iraq's entry into the [United Arab Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic "United Arab Republic").[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200524%E2%80%9325-20) Of the 16-members of Qasim's cabinet, 12 of them were Regional Branch members.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200524%E2%80%9325-20) After taking power, Qasim changed his position on the UAR, reverting to the old "Iraq first policy".[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200524%E2%80%9325-20) This turn displeased the Regional Branch and other Arab nationalist groups.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200525%E2%80%9326-97) Due to his policy reversal, the Regional Branch gathered a group, led by [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein "Saddam Hussein"), which tried but failed to assassinate Qasim.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200526-98)

The Regional Branch seized power in the [Ramadan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_Revolution "Ramadan Revolution").[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200539-99) The coup was led by leading Regional Branch member [Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Hassan_al-Bakr "Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr").[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200539-99) The plotters appointed [Abdul Salam Arif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Salam_Arif "Abdul Salam Arif"), a Nasserite, to the [Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Iraq "President of Iraq") while al-Bakr was appointed the [country's Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Iraq "Prime Minister of Iraq").[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200541-100) However, real power was in the hands of Ali Salih al-Sadi, the branch's Regional Secretary.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMufti1996161-101) After taking power, the Regional Branch through its militia, the National Guard, initiated what Iraqi expert Con Coughlin referred to as an "orgy of violence" against [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism") and [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_politics "Left-wing politics") elements.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200541-100) These repressive measures coupled with factionalism within the Regional Branch led to the [November 1963 Iraqi coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_1963_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "November 1963 Iraqi coup d'état") by President Arif and his Nasserite supporters.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200544-102) Iraq expert Malik Mufti believes Aflaq may have supported Arif's coup because it weakened al-Sadi's position within the party and strengthened his own.[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMufti1996165-103) The coup forced the branch to go underground.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200546%E2%80%9348-104) Due to the coup, several leading Ba'athist were jailed, such as al-Bakr and Saddam.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200546%E2%80%9348-104) Despite this, the Regional Branch elected al-Bakr as Regional Secretary in 1964.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoughlin200546%E2%80%9348-104)

### Jordan

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Jordan")\]

Further information: [Arab Ba'ath Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Ba'ath_Progressive_Party "Arab Ba'ath Progressive Party") and [Jordanian Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party "Jordanian Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party")

Following the party's establishment in Syria, Ba'athist ideas spread throughout the Arab world. In Jordan Ba'athist thought first spread to the [East Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjordan_(region) "Transjordan (region)") in the late-1940s, most notably at universities.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005135-105) While the Regional Branch was not formed until 1951, several meetings took place at the universities where students and professors alike would discuss the Ba'athist thought.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005135-105) Despite the ideology being very popular, it took time before the actual Regional Branch was established.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) A group of teachers established the Regional Branch in the city by [Al-Karak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karak "Al-Karak").[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) At the very beginning, the clinic owned by Abd al-Rahman Shuqyar was used as the branch's meeting place.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) Bahjat Abu Gharbiyah became the Regional Branch's first member in the [West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank "West Bank"), and was thus resigned the responsibility of building the party's organization in the area the branch secretary in the West Bank, and was thus responsible in that area.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) In the West Bank, the branch was most active in the cities of [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem") and [Ramallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramallah "Ramallah").[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106)

The 1st Regional Congress was held in 1951 in the home of [Abdullah Rimawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Rimawi "Abdullah Rimawi").[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) The congress mapped out the "future course of the party".[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) The next year, the 2nd Regional Congress was held, this time in Abdallah Na'was' home.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) It elected a Regional Command and appointed Rimawi as the branch's Regional Secretary.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) Shugyar, Gharbiyah and Na'was agreed to serve in the Regional Branch's Central Committee.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) Rimawi and Na'was, his deputy, would prove effective leaders.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) Shortly after the 2nd Regional Congress, the branch launched a successful recruitment campaign in Jordanian and Palestinian neighbourhoods and cities.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) On 28 August 1956 the branch was legalized by a High Court.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136%E2%80%93137-107)

Both Rimawi and Na'was were [elected to Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Jordan "Parliament of Jordan") in the [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Jordanian_general_election "1950 Jordanian general election") and [1951 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Jordanian_general_election "1951 Jordanian general election") as [independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_politician "Independent politician") (the branch was not a legal party at the time).[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005137-108) In the 1951 election, the branch managed to elect three members to parliament.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005135-105) Rimawi was able to retain his seat in parliament until the [1956 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Jordanian_general_election "1956 Jordanian general election").[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106) None of these elections can be considered democratic.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005175-109) Shuqyar, during the 1951 elections, was imprisoned by the authorities because his views were deemed too radical.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005175-109) Less than a month before the election day, the British Embassy in Amman had estimated that Shuqyar would gain an easy victory.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005175-109) However, because of the undemocratic nature of the election, Shuqyar was not elected.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005175-109) As voting patterns would prove, voters who voted for Ba'athist candidates lived in [Irbid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irbid "Irbid") and [Amman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amman "Amman") on the East Bank, and Jerusalem and [Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus "Nablus") on the West Bank.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005136-106)

Shuqyar during a government-imposed exile to Southern Jordan, used his spare time reading Marxist and [Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninism "Leninism") literature.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005137-108) While he never became a communist, Shuqyar began to support communist concepts.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005137-108) On his return from exile he tried to persuade the Regional Branch to join in an electoral front with the [Jordanian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_Communist_Party "Jordanian Communist Party").[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005137-108) However, the Regional Branch leaders Rimawi, Na'was, Gharbiyah and [Munif Razzaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munif_Razzaz "Munif Razzaz") opposed such an idea, and because of it, Shuqyar left the Ba'ath Party.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005137-108)

Rimawi and Na'was were elected to the National Command at the 2nd National Congress (held in 1952).[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2005231-8) At the 6th and 7th National Congress, the Regional Branch elected Razzaz to the National Command.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972227-110)

### Lebanon

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Lebanon")\]

Main articles: [Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Lebanon Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Lebanon_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Lebanon Region") and [Socialist Arab Lebanon Vanguard Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Arab_Lebanon_Vanguard_Party "Socialist Arab Lebanon Vanguard Party")

The Lebanese Regional Branch was formed in 1949–1950.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeddon200485-111) During the existence of the UAR, the Regional Branch was split into two factions, those supporting Nasser and those opposing him.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960497-18) However, in April 1960, the UAR denied the Regional Branch organ _As Sahafäh_ access into the UAR-ruled Syria.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960497-18)

The Regional Branch was strongest in the city of [Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli,_Lebanon "Tripoli, Lebanon").[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960356-112) In the [1960 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Lebanese_general_election "1960 Lebanese general election"), [Abd al-Majid al-Rafei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Majid_al-Rafei "Abd al-Majid al-Rafei") was just a few votes short of being elected to parliament.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960356-112) However, a persistent problem for him during his election campaign was the vocal criticism of him and the Regional Branch by the [Lebanese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Communist_Party "Lebanese Communist Party").[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960356-112) In Tripoli the Communists supported the candidacy of [Rashid Karami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Karami "Rashid Karami"), to ensure themselves of a Regional Branch victory.[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960348-113) On 17 July 1961 a group of rival Ba'athists led by Rimawi opened fire on several of the Regional Branch's members.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOron1960378-114)

During the UAR years, the same factional lines that developed in the Syrian Regional Branch came to the Lebanese Regional Branch.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197223-115) At the 4th National Congress (held in Lebanon), which was mainly attended by delegates representing Lebanon, several resolutions with a pronounced anti-Nasser tone were approved.[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197223%E2%80%9324-116) At the same time, criticism of Aflaq and Bitar was severe, both their leadership records and their ideology were criticized.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197224-30) A resolution was approved, which stated that the party leaders (Aflaq and al-Bitar among others) had too hastily entered into a union with Egypt, had wrongly dissolved the Syrian Regional Branch in 1958, and had given pan-Arabism primacy when socialism was more important. The resolution also affirmed the need to use a more Marxist lens rather than a Ba'athist one to analyze the current situation, and the need for the party to strengthen their positions among the workers, peasants, artisans, and shopkeepers.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197224-30) Because of the position of the Lebanese Regional Branch, Aflaq at the 5th National Congress invited enough Iraqi Regional Branch delegates to neutralize the Lebanese delegates.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197237-65) However, at the same time, the Lebanese Regional Branch opposed Hawrani and his faction.[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich197239-117) At the 6th National Congress, the Lebanese Regional Branch elected Jubrän Majdalani and Khalid al-Ali to the National Command.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972227-110)

At the 7th National Congress, the National Command in collaboration with the Military Committee removed leftists, such as those found in the Lebanese Regional Branch, from leadership positions, and expelled them from the party in the most severe cases.[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972106-118) The Lebanese Regional Branch managed to elect three members to the National Command at the 7th National Congress; Majdalani, al-Ali and Abd al-Majid Rafi.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972227-110)

### Libya

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Libya")\]

Main article: [Libyan Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party "Libyan Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party")

The Regional Branch was founded in the 1950s[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESimmons1993166-119) by Amr Taher Deghayes.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWright1981227-120) Ba'athism was a major political force in Libya following the establishment of the [United Arab Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic "United Arab Republic"). Many intellectuals were attracted to Ba'athist ideology during the later years of the [Kingdom of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Libya "Kingdom of Libya"). However, with help from Nasserist propaganda, several Ba'athists changed affiliation and became Nasserists instead.[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWright198194-121) The growth of these pan-Arab ideologies concerned the government, which led to the incarceration of several Nasserist and Ba'athist military officers in the early sixties.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWright198196-122) The Ba'athist were accused of working to overthrow "the political, economic and social system" of the Kingdom; the sentences ranged from everything to eight months to two years.[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESimmons1993161-123) By 1964, the Libyan Regional Branch had only managed to establish one-level below the Regional Command, the branch-level.[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDevlin197918-124) Syrian specialist John Devlin estimated that the Libyan Regional Branch had been 50 and 150 members in 1964.[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDevlin197918-124)

### Syria

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Syria")\]

Main article: [Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Syria Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Syria_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Syria Region")

Syrian politics took a dramatic turn in 1954 when the military government of [Adib Shishakli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adib_Shishakli "Adib Shishakli") was overthrown and the democratic system restored. The Ba'ath, now a large and popular organisation, won 15 out of 142 parliamentary seats in the [Syrian election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Syrian_parliamentary_election "1954 Syrian parliamentary election") that year, becoming the second-largest party in parliament. Aside from the [Syrian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Communist_Party "Syrian Communist Party") (SCP), the Ba'ath Party was the only party able to organise mass protests among workers.[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPeretz1994413-125) The party was supported by the [intelligentsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsia "Intelligentsia") due to their pro-Egyptian and [anti-imperialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-imperialism "Anti-imperialism") stance along with their advocation of social reform.[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinerStanley2009149-126)

The Ba'ath faced considerable competition from ideological competitors, notably the [Syrian Social Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Social_Nationalist_Party "Syrian Social Nationalist Party") (SSNP), which supported the establishment of a [Greater Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_(region) "Syria (region)"). The Ba'ath Party's main adversary was the SCP, whose support for class struggle and internationalism was anathema to the Ba'ath.[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division2004211-127) In addition to parliamentary-level competition, all these parties (as well as [Islamists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamism "Islamism")) competed in street-level activity and sought to recruit support among the military.[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division2004210%E2%80%93211-128)

By the end of 1957, the SCP was able to weaken the Ba'ath Party to such an extent that the Ba'ath Party drafted a bill in December that called for a union with Egypt, a move that proved to be very popular. The Ba'ath leadership dissolved the party in 1958, gambling that the illegalisation of certain parties would hurt the SCP more than it would the Ba'ath.[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division2004211%E2%80%93212-129)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Hawrani_Aflaq_1957.jpg/250px-Hawrani_Aflaq_1957.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hawrani_Aflaq_1957.jpg) [Akram al-Hourani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akram_al-Hourani "Akram al-Hourani") (left) with [Michel Aflaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Aflaq "Michel Aflaq"), 1957.

A [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1961 Syrian coup d'état") in [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus "Damascus") in 1961 brought the UAR to an end.[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division200452%E2%80%9353-130) Sixteen prominent politicians signed a statement supporting the coup, among them al-Hawrani and [Salah al-Din al-Bitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_al-Din_al-Bitar "Salah al-Din al-Bitar") (who later retracted his signature).[\[131\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPodeh1999152%E2%80%93153-131) Following the UAR's dissolution, the Ba'ath Party was reestablished at the 1962 congress.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFederal_Research_Division200455-38) The Military Committee did not show itself to the civilian wing of the party at this congress.[\[132\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200368-132) During the congress, Aflaq and the Military Committee, through [Muhammad Umran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Umran "Muhammad Umran"), made contact for the first time; the committee asked for permission to initiate a coup d'état; Aflaq supported the conspiracy.[\[133\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199075-133)

Following the success of the [Ramadan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_Revolution "Ramadan Revolution"), led by the Ba'ath Party's [Iraqi Regional Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Iraq_Region "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Iraq Region"), the Military Committee hastily convened to hatch a coup against [Nazim al-Qudsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazim_al-Qudsi "Nazim al-Qudsi")'s presidency.[\[134\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199076%E2%80%9378-134) The [8 March Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1963 Syrian coup d'état") proved successful, and a Ba'athist government in Syria was established.[\[134\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199076%E2%80%9378-134) The plotters first order was to establish the [National Council for the Revolutionary Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_for_the_Revolutionary_Command "National Council for the Revolutionary Command") (NCRC), consisting entirely of Ba'athists and Nasserists, and controlled by military personnel rather than civilians from the very beginning.[\[135\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199078-135)

While the Ba'ath Party had attained power, there was significant infighting.[\[136\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200368%E2%80%9369-136) The Military Committee, which was itself a tiny minority of the already small Ba'ath Party membership, ruled by force.[\[136\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200368%E2%80%9369-136) The Ba'ath Party lacked a popular base, as it had only 2,500 members by mid-1963. Even if membership expanded, the authoritarian rule it had introduced would only increase.[\[136\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200368%E2%80%9369-136)

The civilian wing was plagued by infighting between the radical socialist and moderate faction, while the military stood more unified.[\[137\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200369-137) Whatever the case, the [Syrian Regional Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Command_of_the_Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Syria_Region "Central Command of the Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party – Syria Region") slowly amassed its powers by weakening the National Command.[\[137\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200369-137) This all came to a head in the [1966 Syrian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Syrian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1966 Syrian coup d'état").[\[137\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorge200369-137)

The party officially indefinitely suspended all activities in Syria on 11 December 2024, following the [Fall of the Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Assad_regime "Fall of the Assad regime").[\[138\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-138)

### Others

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ba%27ath_Party&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Others")\]

Following the Ba'ath Party's founding, regional branches were established in [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait "Kuwait"), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia") and [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman "Oman") .[\[139\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoldman200260-139) Not long after it established branches in [North Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yemen "Kingdom of Yemen") and [South Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_South_Arabia "Protectorate of South Arabia").[\[140\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972169-140) In [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia "Tunisia"), a [Regional Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Ba'ath_Movement "Tunisian Ba'ath Movement") was established in the 1950s, but was forced underground for much of its existence.[\[141\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-underground-141) The Saudi Regional Branches elected Ali Ghannäm to represent them at the 7th National Command.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabinovich1972227-110) While its currently unknown which side the Saudi Ba'ath took after the 1966 split, it published a newspaper, _Sawt al-Tal'iyya_, from 1973 to 1980. It was an ardent critic of the [Saudi royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Saud "House of Saud") and [American imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_imperialism "American imperialism"). The majority of its members were [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam "Shia Islam") [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims "Muslims").[\[142\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-142) In late 1963, Ba'ath cells were being established in Sudan, and there were even rumours that a Ba'ath cell had been established in Egypt.[\[143\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-FOOTNOTESeale199090-143) A [regional Ba'ath Party branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_of_Algeria "Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party of Algeria") was established in [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria "Algeria") in 1988 after the one-party system ended.[\[144\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%27ath_Party#cite_note-baathflagbook-144)
