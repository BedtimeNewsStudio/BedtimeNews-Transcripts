Birgit Prinz

**Birgit Prinz** (born 25 October 1977) is a German former [footballer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football"), two-time [FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup") champion and three-time [FIFA World Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year "FIFA World Player of the Year"). In addition to the [German national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team "Germany women's national football team"), Prinz played for [1\. FFC Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eintracht_Frankfurt_(women) "Eintracht Frankfurt (women)") in the [Frauen-Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frauen-Bundesliga "Frauen-Bundesliga") as well as the [Carolina Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Courage "Carolina Courage") in the [Women's United Soccer Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_United_Soccer_Association "Women's United Soccer Association") (WUSA), the first professional women's league in the United States. Prinz remains one of the game's most prolific [strikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_(association_football) "Forward (association football)") and is the second [FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup") all-time leading scorer with 14 goals (second only to [Marta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Vieira_da_Silva "Marta Vieira da Silva") from Brazil). In 2011, she announced the end of her active career.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) She currently works as a sport psychologist for the men's and women's teams of [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga "Bundesliga") club [TSG 1899 Hoffenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSG_1899_Hoffenheim "TSG 1899 Hoffenheim").[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)

## Club career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Birgit_Prinz&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Club career")\]

Prinz began her career at Dörnigheimer SV. She made her [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu%C3%9Fball-Bundesliga_(women) "Fußball-Bundesliga (women)") debut for [FSV Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FSV_Frankfurt "FSV Frankfurt"), where she played from 1993 to 1998. During that time Prinz won two Bundesliga titles and two [German Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frauen_DFB_Pokal "Frauen DFB Pokal"). In 1997 and 1998 she was the Bundesliga top scorer. In 1998, she moved to local rivals [1\. FFC Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eintracht_Frankfurt_(women) "Eintracht Frankfurt (women)"), where she has had her biggest success at club level. In 13 seasons at the club, Prinz won six Bundesliga and eight German Cup titles. She also won the Bundesliga top-scorer award twice more in 2001 and 2007. Prinz won the [UEFA Women's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Champions_League "UEFA Women's Champions League") three times with Frankfurt, in the [2001–02](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_UEFA_Women's_Cup "2001–02 UEFA Women's Cup"), [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_UEFA_Women's_Cup "2005–06 UEFA Women's Cup") and [2007–08 seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_UEFA_Women's_Cup "2007–08 UEFA Women's Cup"). She also reached the final in 2004, but lost to the Swedish side [Umeå IK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ume%C3%A5_IK "Umeå IK").[&#8202;](#ref-back-dfb-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-dfb)

For two seasons, Prinz joined [Carolina Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Courage "Carolina Courage") in the professional women's league [WUSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_United_Soccer_Association "Women's United Soccer Association") in the United States. During her short stint in America she claimed the 2002 WUSA Championship. After the [2003 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup"), Prinz declined an offer from [AC Perugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perugia_Calcio "Perugia Calcio") to play in Italy's men's [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A "Serie A"), fearing her transfer would be used as a publicity stunt and she would end up on the bench.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)

In her time at FFC Frankfurt, Prinz won many personal awards, including a record eight [German Female Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_footballer_of_the_year "German footballer of the year") awards from 2001 to 2008.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jahres-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-jahres) She was named the [FIFA World Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year "FIFA World Player of the Year") in 2003, 2004 and 2005. For four consecutive years from 2007 to 2010 she came second, behind Brazil's [Marta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Vieira_da_Silva "Marta Vieira da Silva").[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

## International career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Birgit_Prinz&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: International career")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Birgit_Prinz.jpg/250px-Birgit_Prinz.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Birgit_Prinz.jpg) Prinz playing for [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team "Germany women's national football team") in 2011

At the age of 16, Prinz made her debut for the [Germany national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team "Germany women's national football team") in July 1994 against [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_women's_national_soccer_team "Canada women's national soccer team"). She came on after 72 minutes and scored the game-winner in the 89th minute.[&#8202;](#ref-back-focus-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-focus) One year later, she won her first major title at the [1995 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_1995 "UEFA Women's Euro 1995"), scoring in the final. In the same year, she was named to Germany's squad for the [1995 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "1995 FIFA Women's World Cup"), where they lost to Norway in the final match.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dfb-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-dfb) She remains the youngest player ever to appear in a World Cup Final.[&#8202;](#ref-back-focus-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-focus)

For the next decade, Prinz had one of the most successful international careers in women's football. She won four more [UEFA European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Championship "UEFA Women's Championship") in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_1997 "UEFA Women's Euro 1997"), [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_2001 "UEFA Women's Euro 2001"), [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_2005 "UEFA Women's Euro 2005") and [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Euro_2009 "UEFA Women's Euro 2009"). At the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics "Football at the Summer Olympics") she won bronze three times with the German team, in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics "Football at the 2000 Summer Olympics"), [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics "Football at the 2004 Summer Olympics") and [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics "Football at the 2008 Summer Olympics"). At the [2003 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup"), Prinz helped Germany win its first World Cup title in the women's game. She was honoured as the tournament's best player and top-scorer. Prinz became the women's national team captain at the end of 2003, and remained until her retirement. Four years later, at the [2007 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "2007 FIFA Women's World Cup"), she captained the team to Germany's second World Cup title; she was awarded the Silver Ball as the second-best player at the tournament.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dfb-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-dfb)

Prinz holds several national and international records. With 14 goals, she is the [second all-time leading goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Cup#Top_scorers_of_all_time "FIFA Women's World Cup") at [FIFA Women's World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup"). From 2008 until 2012, Prinz and Brazil's [Cristiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiane_(footballer) "Cristiane (footballer)") both held the [tournament record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_women's_Olympic_football_tournament_records "List of women's Olympic football tournament records") of ten goals at the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics "Football at the Summer Olympics"), although Cristiane has now surpassed Prinz. For the German national team Prinz appeared 214 times and scored 128 goals, and is the team's [most capped player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team#Most_capped_players "Germany women's national football team") and [top goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team#Top_goalscorers "Germany women's national football team").[&#8202;](#ref-back-dfb-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-dfb)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Birgit_Prinz&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Prinz is a trained [physical therapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_therapist "Physical therapist").[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) In 2010, she graduated with her master's degree in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology") from the [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt "Goethe University Frankfurt").[&#8202;](#ref-back-spox-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-spox) Since January 2012, she has worked as a sport psychologist in the youth academy, women's U-17 and women's Bundesliga teams at TSG 1899 Hoffenheim.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

List of international goals scored by Birgit Prinz

### At World Cup and Olympic Tournaments

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Birgit_Prinz&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: At World Cup and Olympic Tournaments")\]

Prinz competed in five [FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "FIFA Women's World Cup"):
[Sweden 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "1995 FIFA Women's World Cup"),
[USA 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "1999 FIFA Women's World Cup"),
[USA 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup"),
[China 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "2007 FIFA Women's World Cup")
and [Germany 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup "2011 FIFA Women's World Cup");
and four [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics "Football at the Summer Olympics"):
[Atlanta 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament "Football at the 1996 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament"),
[Sydney 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament "Football at the 2000 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament"),
[Athens 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament "Football at the 2004 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament"),
and [Beijing 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament "Football at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament").
Altogether she played in 43 matches and scored 24 goals at those nine global tournaments.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fifaps_bp-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-fifaps_bp) With Germany, Prinz is a two-time world champion from USA 2003 and China 2007, and a runner-up from Sweden 1995, as well as a three-time bronze medalist from Sydney 2000, Athens 2004 and Beijing 2008.
