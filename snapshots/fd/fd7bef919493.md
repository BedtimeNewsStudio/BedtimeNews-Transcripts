Turkey is building a spaceport in Somalia while preparing its first lunar spacecraft for a planned 2027 launch from the United States, with President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan outlining progress on both projects at a space congress on Monday.

Erdoğan spoke at the 77th International Astronautical Congress (IAC) in Antalya, a city on Turkey’s Mediterranean coast. Turkey is hosting the congress for the first time October 5-9 under the theme “The World Needs More Space,” bringing together representatives from space agencies, industry and research institutions.

The Somali launch facilities are intended to give Turkey independent access to space and contribute to Africa’s space sector, Erdoğan told participants. He described the project as part of Turkey’s National Space Program, which covers lunar exploration and the infrastructure needed to launch spacecraft.

“The space race cannot be a competition between just a few countries,” Erdoğan said, pledging to continue efforts to make Turkey a participant in the space industry.

Construction predates Erdoğan’s remarks in Antalya. Industry and Technology Minister Mehmet Fatih Kacır announced on December 30, 2025, that feasibility and design work had been completed and construction of the first phase had begun on land allocated to Turkey by Somalia.

The facility’s first phase was intended to become ready for launches in early 2027, Turkish Space Agency (TUA) President Yusuf Kıraç indicated in December 2025. That date was presented as a target.

Investment in the project is expected to exceed €200 million, according to Spanish defense news website Escudo Digital.

Turkey’s lunar spacecraft has completed manufacturing, assembly and integration and is undergoing tests designed to simulate conditions during launch and in space. The spacecraft has more than 80 percent domestic content and is targeted for launch in the first half of 2027, according to the Scientific and Technological Research Council of Turkey (TÜBİTAK).

The lunar mission will use a SpaceX launch vehicle, Kıraç explained in May. After testing and final preparations, the spacecraft is to be shipped to Cape Canaveral in Florida, according to TÜBİTAK.

The mission will send an uncrewed spacecraft into orbit around the Moon to conduct research for at least three months before a controlled descent toward the lunar surface.

Turkey’s National Space Program, unveiled in 2021, covers lunar exploration, independent access to space, satellite development and a spaceport. Turkey launched its first domestically developed communications satellite, Türksat 6A, in 2024, the same year Alper Gezeravcı became the first Turkish citizen to travel to space.

Somalia’s location near the Equator offers advantages for some launches. Earth’s surface moves fastest at the Equator, giving rockets launched eastward an additional boost that can reduce fuel needs or allow them to carry more cargo.

Somalia’s Indian Ocean coastline, low air and maritime traffic and weather conditions also offer advantages for launch safety and scheduling, Kacır explained in December. Launching over the ocean can reduce risks to populated areas.

The spaceport expands a partnership that already covers military training and maritime security. Turkey opened its Turkish-Somali Military Training Center (TURKSOM) in Mogadishu, Somalia’s capital, in 2017 to train Somali troops. The countries signed a 10-year defense and economic cooperation agreement in Ankara in February 2024 to support Somalia’s navy and maritime security.

Turkey has also expanded energy cooperation with Somalia. Its Oruç Reis seismic research vessel conducted offshore surveys, and the Çağrı Bey drilling vessel reached Somalia in April to carry out Turkey’s first overseas deep-sea exploration drilling operation.
