# Elpida agrees to take over Mitsubishi’s DRAM business

By Faith Hung  10.03.2002 0

TOKYO — Confirming weeks of speculation, Elpida Memory Inc. today said it is planning to take over the DRAM business of Mitsubishi Electric Corp. and has formed a foundry alliance with Taiwan's Powerchip Semiconductor Corp.

Elpida, a joint venture between NEC Corp. and Hitachi Ltd., said today that it's expecting to integrate Mitsubishi's DRAM operations by April 2003. The move will allow Tokyo-based Elpida to become the world's fourth-biggest DRAM maker, outpacing Infineon Technologies AG, it said.

“The target of Elpida is to reach 15% of market share,” from 8.5% in 2001, Tokumasa Yasui, an executive vice president of Elpida, told reporters in Taipei.

The consolidation comes as soft demand and excessive supply drive down DRAM average selling prices, leaving players in the U.S., Taiwan, Japan and others with heavy losses.

Elpida has been in a tough situation since it was established in 1999. One major factor is that its share in the volatile market isn't big enough, Yasui said.

For Mitsubishi, the situation is the same because its share is less than 5%, lower than the 10% or more that some analysts said would require for a player to stay in the market. “Without the minimum share size, it's difficult to stay in business,” said Warren Lau, an analyst with HSBC Securities Asia Ltd., Taipei. “It's a good thing for Mitsubishi to exit the market.”

Currently, Samsung Electronics and Micron Technology, Boise, Idaho, control more than 50% of the market combined, followed by debt-ridden Hynix Semiconductor, whose share exceeds 10%, some analysts said.

As part of the agreement, Mitsubishi will let Elpida acquire all of Mitsubishi's standard DRAM devices and modules that are currently produced on its 0.15- micron and smaller process technologies. “This transition makes good business sense considering current market conditions,” Koichi Nagasawa, executive vice president of Mitsubishi, said in a statement.

Separately, Elpida will use the 0.13-micron and 0.12-micron capacity of Powerchip starting April, and develop the more advanced 0.11-micron and 0.10-micron processes with Powerchip, a DRAM supplier to Mitsubishi for the last eight years.

“This partnership combines Elpida's R&D capability and Powerchip's strength in low-cost manufacturing,” said Michael Tsai, president of Powerchip.

Powerchip is planning to make 15,000 per month using 0.13-micron technology next year at its first 300mm wafer facility in Hsinchu, Taiwan. Right now, the company runs an 8-in. wafer fab, which has a maximum monthly capacity of 40,000 wafers. Tsai declined to reveal how much of its capacity will be used by Elpida.

Elpida has a 12-in. fab in Hiroshima, Japan. The facility is scheduled to enter an initial production of 3,000 wafers per month in the first quarter of 2003, following a pilot production this month, Yasui said. Elpida will gradually increase the output to 10,000 — 15,000 wafers, depending on market condition, he said.

Powerchip said that it will continue to make system LSI products for Mitsubishi.
