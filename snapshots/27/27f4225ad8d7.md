# The actors union secured a $120 million in streaming bonuses for members and forced Netflix and studios to open their black box of data

![](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/46785d52dfeb92e5d81793bca0f95178db390411ade0463e3d111239159b1e19/lightyear_networkapi/resizefit_w1200%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Ffortune_175%2F4776eb15a679872b492f01981acc0690)

- [NFLX+2.16%](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NFLX/ "NFLX")

The tentative agreement reached between actors and Hollywood studios after a four-month strike includes a big nod to the streaming era.

As part of the contract, actors on hit streamed shows would get far bigger bonuses. Previously, these types of performance-based payments, which could be substantial for movies released in theaters and for traditional TV shows, were negligible for actors on even the most popular streaming programs.

This represents a significant step forward for actors, whose work is increasingly produced by or for streaming services. Early in the strike actors from popular streamed shows on the likes of [Netflix](https://fortune.com/company/netflix/) shared on social media the small size of their streaming residuals. Many of those actors wondered how shows that were either critically acclaimed or hits with viewers (or sometimes both) could pay them so little. [Kimiko Glenn](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/notes-on-hollywood/orange-is-the-new-black-signalled-the-rot-inside-the-streaming-economy), who had a supporting role on one of Netflix's first original series, _Orange is the New Black_, was among the first to go viral when she shared a TikTok video showing a foreign residual check that amounted to $27.30. _Breaking Bad_ star Aaron Paul said he hadn't [earned any money](https://fortune.com/2023/09/05/aaron-paul-netflix-breaking-bad-residuals/) from Netflix carrying the show, for which he won three Emmy awards.

Streaming companies, which have quickly gained a [major foothold in Hollywood](https://fortune.com/longform/netflix-versus-hollywood/), have closely guarded their viewership data. They have often reported metrics like subscribers, but they have [rarely offered glimpses](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/streamers-data-sharing-1235045041/) about which shows and movies are most popular on their services. In recent years, some streaming services like Netflix and Max, owned by Warner Bros. [Discovery](https://fortune.com/company/discovery-insurance/), have begun releasing [top 10 lists](https://www.vulture.com/2023/08/max-top-10-streaming-ratings.html), but even those are comparative and don't show detailed viewer numbers. In the past, Hollywood relied on third-party measurement companies like Nielsen to provide ratings to gauge, among other things, how much actors and writers should earn in residual payments. However, with streaming, that money largely doesn't reach actors.

Residual payments were established in 1960, the last time both the writers and actors went on strike simultaneously. During that strike the two unions negotiated a [framework for contemporary residuals](https://fortune.com/2023/07/19/what-are-residuals-hollywood-writers-actors-strike-streaming/). Streamers technically pay residuals, but they are much smaller than an actor would earn for television reruns or DVD sales.

In the [tentative agreement](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/11/08/business/media/actors-strike-deal.html?searchResultPosition=2) with the actors union, the studios agreed to a data transparency clause for evaluating residuals. Streamers would have to share data including the total number of hours users spent watching individual shows in the U.S. and abroad, and the running time of the program in question. That data will still be kept private and be subject to confidentiality agreement, according to a [summary](https://deadline.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/SAG-AFTRA-TV-Theatrical-Summary-Agreement.pdf) of the contract.

Story Continues

Actors would also be eligible for a $120 million streaming bonus fund that would provide [$40 million annually](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2023/nov/10/contract-for-hollywood-actors-includes-40m-yearly-in-streaming-bonuses) to them during the three-year contract. In the current agreement. actors on streamed shows would earn a bonus if the show was watched by more than 20% of the platform's total viewers within the first 90 days of release. If a show meets the threshold, 75% of the payout would go directly to the actors. The remaining 25% would go into a fund jointly administered by the actors union and studios to be disbursed at a later date on whatever they decide. Fran Drescher, president of SAG-AFTRA, the actors union, acknowledged only "a thimble worth of shows" would benefit from the new arrangement.

Securing additional payments for even just the most popular streaming shows was a major change, Drescher [said](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=joEbGiDdDpM) at a news conference last week. "It's what we said in the beginning to them \[the studios\]," she said. "It didn't matter the mechanism, it didn't matter the amount. What mattered was that we got into another pocket—and we did."

The tentative agreement will be voted on by the actors union's full membership starting on Tuesday and ending Dec. 5.

While the contract represents significant gains for actors in terms of receiving payments from streaming services, the union did fall short of its initial stated goals. It had originally asked for a 2% of streaming service revenue, then dropped that number to 1%, then proposed a per subscriber fee that would have amounted to 57 cents per user. Netflix's co-CEO Ted Sarandos called that " [a bridge too far](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/sagaftra-subscriber-fee-amptp-proposal-1235623414/)." Included in the initial offer of a per subscriber payment to the union was the proposal of a jointly managed fund to administer payments, which ended up in the tentative deal, albeit under different terms.

During her news conference Drescher said she initially had doubts about whether SAG-AFTRA had made sufficient gains for its members. But when she considered that the union had established a new precedent for the industry she was reenergized. "Suddenly I started to get my mojo back," she said, realizing the current deal opened the door for further gains in the future.

# CNA Financial Q3 Earnings Preview: Investors Focus on Insurance Pricing Pressure and Underwriting Margins

Fiona Craig

November 2, 20266 min read

  - [CNA-0.69%](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CNA/ "CNA")

![Paperwork ©Shutterstock](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/0f28507613a9b336fcac94d4d103f924c61f0f52d503f40d22d30c7c26dbb584/lightyear_networkapi/resizefit_w1200%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Finvestorshub_458%2F2bde8cfc4b55843c4643e84810d5aed8.png)

CNA Financial Corporation (NYSE:CNA) is scheduled to report its third-quarter earnings before the US stock market opens on Monday, with investors assessing whether the Chicago-based insurer can maintain profitability as pricing conditions weaken across several commercial insurance segments.

The results will provide an indication of how effectively CNA is managing underwriting risks, maintaining customer retention and adjusting its portfolio as competition increases in the property and casualty insurance market.

Analysts expect earnings of $1.24 per share on revenue of $3.02 billion. The earnings forecast represents an improvement from the previous quarter's $1.19 per share but a 17.3% decline compared with the same period last year.

Revenue is projected to fall 20.9% year over year, reflecting the company's decision to reduce exposure to less attractive insurance risks as market conditions change.

## CNA Financial Earnings Estimates and Share Price Performance

Market expectations suggest that CNA could deliver a sequential improvement in earnings despite facing a more challenging pricing environment.

The consensus forecast of $1.24 per share compares with the $1.19 reported in the second quarter, although the anticipated year-over-year decline indicates continued pressure on profitability.

The company's shares recently traded at $48.87, within their 52-week range of $41.53 to $55.71.

The sole analyst covering CNA has assigned the stock a Sell rating and a $45 price target, suggesting potential downside of approximately 7.9% from the quoted share price.

CNA trades at a forward price-to-earnings ratio of 11.35 times, while projected earnings-per-share growth of negative 10% points to near-term challenges.

With a market capitalisation of approximately $13.22 billion, the insurer enters the earnings announcement following a period of operational improvement, although investors remain focused on the sustainability of its recent profitability gains.

## Combined Ratio Remains a Key Measure of Underwriting Performance

One of the most closely watched figures in CNA's third-quarter results will be its combined ratio, which measures underwriting expenses and claims relative to earned premiums.

A combined ratio below 100% indicates that an insurer is generating an underwriting profit before investment income.

CNA reported a combined ratio of 92.8% in the second quarter, compared with 90.6% a year earlier.

The increase indicated a narrowing underwriting margin, raising questions about whether further competitive pricing pressure could affect profitability.

Commercial property insurance premiums declined 1.2% in the first quarter of 2026, ending a sequence of 33 consecutive quarters of increases.

The shift reflects changing conditions in the commercial insurance market, where additional underwriting capacity and stronger competition have begun to influence pricing.

Investors will be looking for evidence that CNA's underwriting discipline and selective approach to accepting risks can help protect margins despite lower premium rates in certain business lines.

## New Business Growth and Customer Retention Under Scrutiny

CNA's ability to generate profitable new business will be another important consideration in the earnings report.

During the second quarter of 2026, the company recorded $718 million in new business, which it described as a record level.

Customer retention stood at 83%, indicating that CNA maintained a substantial proportion of its existing insurance relationships while attracting new accounts.

The third-quarter results will show whether this momentum continued as pricing conditions became more competitive.

However, the quality and profitability of new business will be as important as the volume of premiums generated.

Strong new business growth accompanied by weaker underwriting margins could limit the financial benefits of expanding the insurance portfolio.

Investors will therefore assess whether CNA has maintained its underwriting standards while pursuing opportunities in the commercial insurance market.

## Portfolio Changes Contribute to Expected Revenue Decline

The anticipated 20.9% year-over-year decline in revenue will also be a central focus of the earnings announcement.

CNA has been adjusting its business mix by increasing its emphasis on specialty insurance products and reducing exposure to accounts that offer less attractive returns.

The strategy is intended to improve the quality of the company's underwriting portfolio rather than prioritise premium growth across all business categories.

However, the projected revenue contraction raises questions about the pace of the transition and its impact on overall financial performance.

Investors will be looking for indications that CNA can preserve profitability while selectively reducing its exposure to lower-margin insurance business.

Management's commentary on pricing trends, portfolio composition and underwriting opportunities will help clarify the direction of the company's strategy.

## Second-Quarter Earnings Beat Provides Comparison

CNA enters the third-quarter announcement following stronger-than-expected results in the previous reporting period.

In the second quarter of 2026, the insurer reported earnings of $1.19 per share, exceeding analysts' expectations of $1.01.

The result represented a positive earnings surprise of approximately 17.8%.

Revenue reached $4.73 billion, substantially above market forecasts.

However, the revenue comparison was influenced by non-insurance warranty operations, which can generate significant fluctuations between reporting periods.

This variation means that headline revenue movements may not fully reflect underlying trends in CNA's core insurance activities.

The company also recorded 41% growth in diluted earnings per share over the preceding year, reflecting improvements in its financial performance.

The third-quarter results will provide further evidence of whether those gains can be sustained as commercial insurance pricing becomes less favourable.

## Commercial Insurance Market Faces Changing Conditions

The broader property and casualty insurance industry is experiencing a transition in 2026 as pricing pressures increase across several commercial lines.

After an extended period of premium increases, competition and additional insurance capacity have created more favourable conditions for some commercial insurance buyers.

The decline in property insurance rates reflects this shift, potentially limiting premium growth for insurers operating in competitive segments.

At the same time, underwriting risks remain significant.

Social inflation, including rising litigation costs and larger liability claims, continues to affect insurers' exposure to potential losses.

Emerging risks and changing claims patterns also create uncertainty around future underwriting profitability.

For CNA, the challenge is to balance competitive pricing with disciplined risk selection while maintaining the margins achieved during the previous period of stronger insurance market conditions.

## What Investors Will Watch in CNA's Q3 Results

CNA's third-quarter announcement will provide an important update on the company's performance as the commercial insurance pricing cycle changes.

Investors will focus on the combined ratio, new business volumes, customer retention and the profitability of recently written policies.

The expected revenue decline will also attract attention, particularly as the company continues shifting towards specialty insurance and reducing exposure to less profitable accounts.

Management's assessment of market pricing, underwriting conditions and future growth opportunities could influence expectations for the remainder of 2026 and into 2027.

Although CNA has demonstrated improved earnings performance over the past year, the latest results will help determine whether its underwriting strategy can maintain profitability in a more competitive market.

American Integrity Insurance Group (NYSE:AII) more than doubled its third-quarter net income as growth in voluntary policies and a reduction in the share of business ceded to reinsurers boosted the property insurer's retained premiums.

Net income rose to $28.4 million, or $1.44 per diluted share, from $13.2 million, or $0.67 per diluted share, a year earlier. Net premiums earned increased 94.5% to $101.2 million, while gross premiums written rose 22.7% to $293.3 million.

## Reinsurance changes lift retained premiums

CFO Brian Foley said the increase in net premiums earned reflected both underlying business growth and the company's decision to reduce its non-catastrophe quota share cession to 25% from 40%, effective Jan. 1. The change allows American Integrity to retain more premiums, but also leaves it responsible for more losses and reduces the ceding commission income available to offset expenses.

## Growth in Florida and beyond

Policies in force increased 18.6% to more than 481,000, and in-force premium rose 17.4% to nearly $1.1 billion. Policy retention improved to 84.9%, from 84.4% in the second quarter and 82.8% a year earlier.

President Jon Ritchie said the company wrote approximately 8,000 new voluntary policies in Florida's Tri-County region, up from just over 200 a year earlier. Outside that region, it wrote approximately 8,600 new voluntary policies on middle-aged homes, compared with fewer than 600 a year earlier.

Bob Ritchie said improved market conditions and Florida's legislative reforms have supported the company's expansion in middle-aged homes, a segment where it had reduced participation during the state's litigation crisis. He said the average premium per policy remained broadly stable year over year, with business mix and an inflation guard adjustment helping offset modest rate reductions elsewhere in the portfolio.

Voluntary new business policies in Georgia, South Carolina and North Carolina rose 20% year over year, Jon Ritchie said. American Integrity did not participate in a Citizens take-out during the quarter because the available policies did not meet its underwriting and profitability standards. Jon Ritchie said Hurricane Isaias, a fourth-quarter event, is expected to produce a loss of up to $35 million based on preliminary information. The company's catastrophe excess-of-loss program attaches at $35 million. He described the estimate as conservative but cautioned that claims were still at an early stage and the estimate could change.

American Integrity does not currently expect the storm to materially alter its outlook for the June 1, 2027, reinsurance renewal. Jon Ritchie said the company continues to anticipate favorable pricing, while noting that terms will depend on industry losses, available capacity and conditions at renewal. He also expects the company's $35 million retention exhaustion point to rise in proportion to portfolio growth.

The insurer ended the quarter with $391.2 million in shareholders' equity and book value of $19.96 per share. Foley said it fully repaid its outstanding surplus note debt during the quarter and ended the period debt-free.

## Revenue growth

Insurers earn revenue three ways. The core insurance business itself, often called underwriting and represented in the income statement as premiums earned, is one way. Investment income from investing the "float" (premiums collected upfront not yet paid out as claims) in assets such as fixed-income assets and equities is the second way. Fees from various sources such as policy administration, annuities or other value-added services are the third. Fidelity National Financial struggled to consistently increase demand as its $14.91 billion of revenue for the trailing 12 months was close to its revenue five years ago. That underwhelming result suggests it is a low-quality business.
