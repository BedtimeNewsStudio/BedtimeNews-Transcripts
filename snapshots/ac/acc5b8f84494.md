Scouting for Boys

_**Scouting for Boys: A handbook for instruction in good citizenship**_ is a book on [Boy Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouting "Scouting") training, published in various editions since 1908. Early editions were written and illustrated by [Robert Baden-Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Baden-Powell,_1st_Baron_Baden-Powell "Robert Baden-Powell, 1st Baron Baden-Powell") with later editions being extensively rewritten by others. The book was originally a manual for self-instruction in observation, tracking and [woodcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodcraft "Woodcraft") skills as well as self-discipline and self-improvement, about the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") and duty as citizens with an eclectic mix of anecdotes and unabashed personal observations and recollections. It includes references to the author's own exploits. It is based on his boyhood experiences, his experience with the [Mafeking Cadet Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafeking_Cadet_Corps "Mafeking Cadet Corps") during the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War "Second Boer War") at the [siege of Mafeking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mafeking "Siege of Mafeking"), and on his experimental [camp on Brownsea Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsea_Island_Scout_camp "Brownsea Island Scout camp"), England.

## History

_Scouting for Boys_ (1908) was Baden-Powell's rewrite of his earlier book _Aids to Scouting_ (1899)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bp1899-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-bp1899) with many youth training ideas openly taken from _The Birch Bark Roll of the Woodcraft Indians_ (1906)[&#8202;](#ref-back-seton1906-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-seton1906) written by [Ernest Thompson Seton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Thompson_Seton "Ernest Thompson Seton"), who later became the Chief Scout of the [Boy Scouts of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scouts_of_America "Boy Scouts of America").[&#8202;](#ref-back-arrow-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-arrow)[&#8202;](#ref-back-birch-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-birch) _Aids to Scouting_ was mostly a written explanation of the military scouting and self-reliance skills lessons Baden-Powell had learned from [Frederick Russell Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Russell_Burnham "Frederick Russell Burnham"), the British Army Chief of Scouts, but following the [siege of Mafeking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mafeking "Siege of Mafeking") this military handbook unexpectedly became popular with many youth groups and educators, like [Charlotte Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Mason "Charlotte Mason"), in Britain.[&#8202;](#ref-back-arrow-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-arrow)[&#8202;](#ref-back-masonaids-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-masonaids)[&#8202;](#ref-back-masonaids2-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-masonaids2) At Mafeking, Baden-Powell's adjutant had recruited and trained boys aged 12–15 as cadets and during the siege they acted as postmen, messengers, and later to carry the wounded, to free men for fighting. Upon his return to England, following the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War "Second Boer War"), Baden-Powell learned some British schools had been using _Aids to Scouting_ to teach [observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation "Observation") and [deduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive_reasoning "Deductive reasoning"). In 1906, Seton discussed youth training ideas with Baden-Powell and shared with him a copy of _The Birch Bark Roll of the Woodcraft Indians_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-birch-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-birch) Soon after, Baden-Powell decided to revise _Aids to Scouting_ into a book for boys.[&#8202;](#ref-back-arrow-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-arrow) Several friends supported Baden-Powell, including Sir [William Alexander Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Alexander_Smith_(Boys'_Brigade) "William Alexander Smith (Boys' Brigade)"), founder of the [Boys' Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys'_Brigade "Boys' Brigade"), [Cyril Arthur Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Arthur_Pearson "Cyril Arthur Pearson"), who owned newspapers and printing presses, and the novelist [Maria Fetherstonhaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Fetherstonhaugh "Maria Fetherstonhaugh"), who provided a quiet Wimbledon house where he could write.[&#8202;](#ref-back-notestobook2004-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-notestobook2004)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) Baden-Powell wrote a draft, then called _Boy Patrols_, which he used and tested with 22 boys for one week at [camp on Brownsea Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsea_Island_Scout_camp "Brownsea Island Scout camp") in the summer of 1907, where Pearson's literary editor [Percy Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Everett "Percy Everett") assisted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-arrow-3)[\[4\]](#refnote-arrow)

_Scouting for Boys_ was published in six fortnightly instalments of approximately 70 pages each, from January to March 1908. They were produced by Pearson's printer, Horace Cox. These six publications were a success and, as planned, were issued in book form on 1 May 1908. Although _Aids to Scouting_ strongly influenced the book, _Scouting for Boys_ presents Scouting from the perspective of outdoorsmen and explorers rather than military men, and it adds the Scout Oath, Scout Law, honours and games for youth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-arrow-4)[\[4\]](#refnote-arrow)[&#8202;](#ref-back-birch-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-birch) The book was revised and an enormous variety of editions were published. Many of these editions were edited by others and, far beyond mere editing, whole sections were written by authors other than Baden-Powell. The book was a best seller upon release, and, in its various editions, is claimed to have become one of the best-selling books in history. _Scouting for Boys_ has been translated into many languages. In 1948, editions of the book were still selling 50,000 copies annually. Only in 1967 was a decline noted by the publisher and in the last decades of the 20th century the book came to be seen as a period curiosity even by the Scout Movement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-notestobook2004-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-notestobook2004) It is claimed to be the fourth bestselling book of the 20th century.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)[&#8202;](#ref-back-hislop-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-hislop) A realistic estimate is that  approximately 4million copies of the UK edition have been sold. Extrapolating this to 87 different language editions worldwide, historic world sales of _Scouting for Boys_ can be estimated at 100 to 150million copies since 1908.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jeal-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-jeal)[&#8202;](#ref-back-censusdata-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-censusdata)

In her introduction to the 2005 edition, [Elleke Boehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elleke_Boehmer "Elleke Boehmer") criticises the book saying "the text was deeply scored through with a contemporary class prejudice which would have been off-putting to non-middle-class readers, as captured in the sharp aphorism that bees form a 'model community, for they respect their Queen and kill their unemployed' (p. 117) Character observation in many ways meant reading for the signs of working-class poverty."[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

## Editions

_Scouting for Boys_ has been published in over thirty consecutive editions by London based [C. Arthur Pearson Ltd.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Arthur_Pearson_Ltd. "C. Arthur Pearson Ltd."), and it is translated to all the major languages of the world. Estimatedly, over 100million books have been printed, making it rank high in the [list of best-selling books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_books "List of best-selling books").[&#8202;](#ref-back-arrow-5)[\[4\]](#refnote-arrow)[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) The internet page www.scoutingforboysroundtheworld.org has identified more than 300 different editions and included them in a database accessible via this internet page. Users can also add missing editions to the database themselves.

## Contents

All parts of the six installments in 1908 have the title _Scouting for Boys_ in big capitals. With a listed price of ' [4d](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_pence "Old pence"). net', it was affordable to many boys, many of whom would have been at work, as the school-leaving age was 14. Authorship is attributed thus: 'by B-P (Lieut. Gen. Baden Powell C.B.)' (sic).

Most chapters start with hints to instructors. All chapters have [campfire yarns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campfire_yarn "Campfire yarn"), appealing to boys, most contain sections with games and activities, and they close with recommendations for books to read.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rohrer-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-rohrer)

## Copyright status

[Copyright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright "Copyright") to _Scouting for Boys_ expired on 31 December 2011, at the end of the 70th year after the death of its author on 8 January 1941 and the book is now in the [public domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_domain "Public domain"). Baden-Powell had given his [copyright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright "Copyright") to the book to [The Scout Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scout_Association "The Scout Association") which owned the copyright in the UK, until it expired.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) The [Boy Scouts of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scouts_of_America "Boy Scouts of America") was granted a copyright licence by Baden-Powell himself for their _[Boy Scout Handbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scout_Handbook "Boy Scout Handbook")_, written during the BSA's formal founding in 1910.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jeal-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-jeal)
