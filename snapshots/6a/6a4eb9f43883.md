The **2021 South African unrest**, also known as the **July 2021 riots**,[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:5-23) the **Zuma unrest**[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Felix-24) or **Zuma riots**,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:6-25) was a wave of civil unrest that occurred in [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa")'s [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal") and [Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng "Gauteng") provinces from 9 to 18 July 2021, sparked by the imprisonment of former President [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma "Jacob Zuma") for [contempt of court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contempt_of_court "Contempt of court").[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-za1-21):52 Resulting protests against the incarceration triggered wider rioting and looting, much of it said to be undertaken by people not in support of Zuma[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-26) and fuelled by [job layoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termination_of_employment "Termination of employment") and economic inequality worsened by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_South_Africa "COVID-19 pandemic in South Africa") policies.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-28) The unrest began in the province of [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal") on the evening of 9 July,[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-unrestbegins-29) and spread to the province of [Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng "Gauteng") on the evening of 11 July,[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-30)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-31) and was the worst episode of violence the country experienced since the end of [Apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid").[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-32)

Zuma was taken into custody after declining to testify at the [Zondo Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zondo_Commission "Zondo Commission"), an inquiry into allegations of corruption during his term as president from 2009 to 2018.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-33) The [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa "Constitutional Court of South Africa") reserved judgment on Zuma's application to rescind his sentence on 12 July 2021.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-34)[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-35)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-reserved-36) The South African government reported that 354 people had died in the riots.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-za1-21) As of 12 August 2022, 5,500 people had been arrested, in connection with the unrest.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:00-22)

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

### Economy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Economy")\]

More than half of South Africa's population lives in poverty, with an unemployment rate of 32%.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-37) According to the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank"), [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_inequality_in_South_Africa "Wealth inequality in South Africa") has increased since 1994 in South Africa, rendering it the most unequal country in the world.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-38)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-39)

### Jacob Zuma's legal battle

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Jacob Zuma's legal battle")\]

Main article: [Jacob Zuma corruption charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma_corruption_charges "Jacob Zuma corruption charges")

Former South African president [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma "Jacob Zuma") was [charged with corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma_corruption_charges "Jacob Zuma corruption charges") in March 2018, in connection with the [South African Arms Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Arms_Deal "South African Arms Deal"), known as the "Strategic Defence Package" worth R30 billion (equal to US$2.5 billion or £2 billion).[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-40) The legal battle has continued since, with Zuma's legal team attempting to have the charges dismissed and appealing for more time to prepare. During the trial proceedings, Zuma was repeatedly absent from the court, citing medical reasons and lack of funds.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-41)

### Imprisonment of Jacob Zuma

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Imprisonment of Jacob Zuma")\]

See also: [Jacob Zuma §Zondo Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma#Zondo_Commission "Jacob Zuma")

On 29 June 2021, Zuma was sentenced to 15 months in prison for contempt of court, after he refused to appear at [a commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zondo_Commission "Zondo Commission") his government appointed to investigate alleged corruption during his time in office, and engaged in what the judges characterised as a "politically motivated smear campaign" against the country's judiciary.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-42) The case ultimately proceeded to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa "Constitutional Court of South Africa").[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:4-43) He was given until the end of 4 July to hand himself in, after which the [South African Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Police_Service "South African Police Service") would be obliged to arrest him. On 3 July, the court agreed to hear his application on 12 July.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-44) If Zuma refused to surrender by 4 July, the police were given until 7 July to arrest him.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-45) Supporters had gathered near his home with weapons to prevent his arrest[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Estcourt-46) but he handed himself over to the police on 7 July,[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-47)[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-48) and was jailed at the [Estcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estcourt "Estcourt") Correctional Centre.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Estcourt-46)

On 8 July 2021, the Minister of Justice and Correctional Services [Ronald Lamola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Lamola "Ronald Lamola") announced that Zuma would be eligible for parole upon serving a quarter of his 15-month sentence.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-49) Zuma challenged his detention on 9 July in the Pietermaritzburg High Court on the grounds of health but this was rejected.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:4-43) His imprisonment led to violent protests by his supporters in KwaZulu-Natal, which quickly developed into widespread looting in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-50)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-auto-1)

### Riots and looting

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Riots and looting")\]

On 9 July 2021, the same day Pietermaritzburg High Court upheld his conviction and prison sentence, the unrest began.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-unrestbegins-29)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-unrest-51) Public violence, burglary, and malicious damage to property were reported in parts of KwaZulu-Natal, with at least 28 people being arrested and a highway being blocked.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-unrest-51) The riots continued on the evening of 11 July, when multiple news sources indicated reports of gunshots and explosions heard at local malls and residential areas.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-52)

The violence quickly escalated, and by the morning of 12 July, multiple companies and malls were forced to close following widespread looting and violence.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-53) An incident occurred at Southgate Spar, a supermarket near a township in [Pietermaritzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietermaritzburg "Pietermaritzburg"), as an unnamed 15-year-old boy was shot in the chest with a rubber bullet fired by police in the midst of the unrest and later succumbed to his wounds.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:3-54) The incident played a part in escalating the unrest.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:3-54)

354 people died due to the unrest according to the South African government,[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-za1-21) while 5,500 were arrested by 12 August 2022.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:00-22) In KwaZula-Natal, 2,763 people had been arrested by 24 July 2021.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:7-55)

### Racial tensions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Racial tensions")\]

See also: [Anti-Indian sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Indian_sentiment "Anti-Indian sentiment")

In the [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_Africans "Indian South Africans")-majority township of [Phoenix, KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix,_KwaZulu-Natal "Phoenix, KwaZulu-Natal"), some of the residents had armed themselves to fight off looters, due to the absence of police forces. This in turn stoked racial tensions between black and [Indian South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_African "Indian South African") citizens, with several racially motivated attacks reported to have taken place.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-56)[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:7-55) [India's Minister of External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_External_Affairs_(India) "Minister of External Affairs (India)") [S. Jaishankar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Jaishankar "S. Jaishankar") raised the issue of the safety of people of Indian origin with the South African [Minister of International Relations and Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_International_Relations_and_Cooperation "Minister of International Relations and Cooperation") [Naledi Pandor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naledi_Pandor "Naledi Pandor"), who assured him that the government was trying its best to restore law and order.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-57)

[Police Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Police_(South_Africa) "Minister of Police (South Africa)") [Bheki Cele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bheki_Cele "Bheki Cele") stated that the main motive behind the Phoenix riots was criminal and that racial issues were secondary. He confirmed that 20 people had died in the town in the unrest. He also warned people against falling for [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news "Fake news") designed to increase racial tensions.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-58) An investigation by the _[Daily Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Maverick "Daily Maverick")_ newspaper found that vigilantes in Phoenix and surrounding areas had targeted suspected looters and even ordinary civilians, committing brutal acts on them. Then- [KwaZulu-Natal Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_KwaZulu-Natal "Premier of KwaZulu-Natal") [Sihle Zikalala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihle_Zikalala "Sihle Zikalala") stated that 38 people had been killed by vigilantes in Phoenix, [Inanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inanda,_KwaZulu-Natal "Inanda, KwaZulu-Natal") and [Verulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verulam,_KwaZulu-Natal "Verulam, KwaZulu-Natal"). Racial tensions in the area, it was discovered, were stoked by false rumours of a massacre of between 350 and 500 black people, allegedly by Indians in Phoenix. The newspaper also reported that some incidents of murders of both black people and Indians under investigation were believed by the police to have credible evidence of racial motivations.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:7-55) These incidents of violence were linked by some commentators to memories of the [Durban riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban_riots "Durban riots") in 1949.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-59)

On 3 August, Cele stated that the rioting in Phoenix did have a partially racial nature. He explained that on 12 July, some residents had set up roadblocks and started patrolling the streets after rioting in Durban. Some of the vigilantes had started racially profiling people, mainly black people, and restricting their entry. When one truck containing looted items was stopped, the four occupants, who were black citizens, fired at the vigilantes, who shot back and killed one of them. Cele stated that the police believes this led to further violence between the different communities. Many vehicles were stopped and looted at the roadblocks, while the passengers were also assaulted.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-60)

Incidents of [xenophobic attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophobia_in_South_Africa "Xenophobia in South Africa") targeting foreign truck drivers[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Grootes-61) and foreigners were also reported during the unrest.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Felix-24)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-62)

### Incitement

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Incitement")\]

Police Minister [Bheki Cele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bheki_Cele "Bheki Cele") stated that the security cluster was looking at ten to twelve people who were fueling the riots through social media.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-63) According to [State Security Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_Security_(South_Africa) "Minister of State Security (South Africa)") [Ayanda Dlodlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayanda_Dlodlo "Ayanda Dlodlo"), they are investigating information as to whether senior former agents in the [intelligence agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Security_Agency_(South_Africa) "State Security Agency (South Africa)") and senior ANC members aligned to former president Jacob Zuma are responsible for igniting the recent violence in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-64)

Jacob Zuma's daughter, Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla, is allegedly among those who encouraged the looting and violence in order to secure the release of her father as an unverified Twitter account under her name encouraged people to protest.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-65)[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-66)

In November 2021 the expert panel into the July 2021 civil unrest concluded that the riots were at least to some extent organized rather than spontaneous:

_"The submissions we received point to elements of organisation behind the looting of the malls, combined with opportunistic looting. \[...\]. It is clear that the planners wanted the looting to look like they were spontaneous; a phenomenon that we have coined 'organised spontaneity'."_[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-za1-21)

### State response

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: State response")\]

Initially, the [South African Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Police_Service "South African Police Service") (SAPS) was deployed in the Nkandla district to control the number of protests in the area.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-67)[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-68)

Over the weekend, as the [South African Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Police_Service "South African Police Service") (SAPS) battled to contain the large-scale looting and damage to infrastructure,[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-69) pressure mounted on government to deploy the army.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-70)

On the morning of Monday, 12 July 2021, the [South African National Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force "South African National Defence Force") (SANDF) was deployed in [Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng "Gauteng") and [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal "KwaZulu-Natal"), as part of [Operation Prosper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Defence_Force#Domestic_operations "South African National Defence Force").[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-71)[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-72)[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-OperationNames-73)

On 12 July 2021, president [Cyril Ramaphosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Ramaphosa "Cyril Ramaphosa") addressed the riots, saying that the acts of public violence have been "rarely seen" in democratic South Africa. Ramaphosa referred to the riots as opportunistic acts of violence, citing the lack of grievance, nor any political cause, that can justify the destruction by the protestors. He highlighted the [Constitution of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_South_Africa "Constitution of South Africa"), which guarantees the rights of everyone to express themselves, but stated that the victims of the violence unfolding are the workers, truck drivers, business owners, and the parents of those who have died and have all done nothing wrong. He went on to discuss the impact of the riots on the [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccination_in_South_Africa "COVID-19 vaccination in South Africa") vaccine rollout, stating that it has been drastically disrupted after prior setbacks. He also noted how the economy of the country would face further challenges due to food and medication insecurity resulting from the riots. The deployment of SANDF to assist with ending the unrest was also discussed by him.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-74)

On the same day, the [Constitutional Court of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa "Constitutional Court of South Africa") reserved its previous judgment and rejected Zuma's bid to rescind his prison sentence.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-75) As a result of the decision, Zuma is required to remain imprisoned.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-reserved-36)

On 14 July 2021, the [Minister of Defence and Military Veterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_and_Military_Veterans "Minister of Defence and Military Veterans") [Nosiviwe Mapisa-Nqakula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nosiviwe_Mapisa-Nqakula "Nosiviwe Mapisa-Nqakula") said that the SANDF had increased the deployed number of troops to 25,000.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-SANDF_DeploymentIncrease-76)

Following the unrest, President Ramaphosa announced on 18 July that the government would explore introducing a [basic income grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income "Universal basic income").[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-77)

### Public response

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Public response")\]

As a result of the state response failing to quell the unrest, [private security companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_security_companies "Private security companies"), taxi drivers, armed civilians and vigilante groups had taken on law enforcement duties by protecting businesses and communities from rioting and looting. Petrol stations, shopping malls, supermarkets and communication infrastructure were guarded by some members of the public, often with the support of business owners and corporations.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-78) Vigilante groups protecting suburbs and businesses worked in an organised manner to arrest looters and to hand them over to authorities, albeit in an often violent manner.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-79)

#### Roads

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Roads")\]

Road closures on both the [N3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N3_(South_Africa) "N3 (South Africa)") and [N2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N2_road_(South_Africa) "N2 road (South Africa)") have affected the transportation of goods from the east coast into provinces in the north.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-80)[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-81)[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-82) This affects the transportation of goods to landlocked countries in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") including [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana "Botswana"), [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe") and [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia").[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-83)[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:8-84)

Multiple logistics and fuel companies declared forced temporary closure of their operations in KZN, citing fears of continued looting, hijackings, truck burnings, and social unrest that could further impact business operations, adding to more costs sustained from the looting and damage to property.[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-85)[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-86) During the riots there were reports of xenophobic attacks targeting foreign truck drivers along the N3.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Grootes-61)

The unrest coincided with the worst of the violence during the [2021 Cape Town taxi conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Cape_Town_taxi_conflict "2021 Cape Town taxi conflict") which was believed to be unrelated to the events in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng.[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-87)

#### Container ports

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Container ports")\]

The container ports of [Richards Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richards_Bay "Richards Bay") and Durban ceased operations. Containers in the port of Durban were plundered. After several attacks on trucks, the N3 Highway, which links port Durban with Johannesburg, was closed on 10 July.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:0-88)

The unrest coincided with the [Transnet ransomware attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnet_ransomware_attack "Transnet ransomware attack") that also contributed to shutting down the Durban container port.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-89)[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-90) The ransomware attack is believed to have been unrelated to the unrest.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-91)

#### Rail

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Rail")\]

State-owned logistics operator [Transnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnet "Transnet") declared [force majeure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_majeure "Force majeure") on 14 July on a rail line linking Johannesburg to the coast.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-92)

### Supply chain disruptions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Supply chain disruptions")\]

#### Food shortages

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Food shortages")\]

[![A surviving grocery store's queue reaches 800m long shortly after reopening.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/15_July%2C_Queue_for_Gillitts_Checkers_reaches_800m_long_by_9am_in_the_morning..jpg/250px-15_July%2C_Queue_for_Gillitts_Checkers_reaches_800m_long_by_9am_in_the_morning..jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:15_July,_Queue_for_Gillitts_Checkers_reaches_800m_long_by_9am_in_the_morning..jpg) A surviving grocery store's queue reaches 800m long shortly after reopening.

On 12 July 2021, the Consumer Goods Council of South Africa warned that widespread looting, destruction and closures would lead to food shortages across the country.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-93) By 14 July 2021, damage to transport infrastructure had caused food shortages, leading to queues outside grocery stores, and prevented harvesting and distribution of fresh produce.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:8-84) On 15 July 2021, the largest food manufacturer in South Africa, [Tiger Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Brands "Tiger Brands"), suspended bakery operations in affected areas due to damaged infrastructure and facilities while also noting that it had suffered a loss of stock in excess of R150 million.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-TigerBrands-94) Some stores and petrol stations in Johannesburg noted that their shelves were getting emptied and supplies were dwindling due to panic buying.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-JhbPanicBuying-95) A Durban journalist noted that while bread had returned to their area, people were paying significantly more for it and were queueing for many hours at a time.[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-DBN_BreadGouging-96) Stores were also placing limits on how much customers could buy while community-run barricades reportedly turned people away if they could not prove they were from those communities.[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-DBN_BreadGouging-96)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/SAUnrestPanicBuying.jpg/250px-SAUnrestPanicBuying.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SAUnrestPanicBuying.jpg) Empty shelves in a grocery store due to [panic buying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_buying "Panic buying") during the 2021 South African unrest

#### Fuel shortages

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Fuel shortages")\]

The Sapref oil refinery, which is a joint venture between [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell "Royal Dutch Shell") and [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP "BP"), is the largest oil refinery in Sub Saharan Africa.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-SaprefForceMajeure-97) It supplies approximately one third of South Africa's fuel needs and its operations were suspended due to a declaration of [force majeure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_majeure "Force majeure").[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-SaprefForceMajeure-97) On 15 July 2021, the [Department of Mineral Resources and Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mineral_Resources_and_Energy "Department of Mineral Resources and Energy") placed prohibitions on the citizens' ability to buy fuel in portable containers and canisters, citing concerns that stockpiling would exacerbate the situation.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-FuelShortage-98)

#### Medical supply shortages

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Medical supply shortages")\]

[Clicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Clicks_Holdings_Limited "New Clicks Holdings Limited") and [Dis-Chem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dis-Chem "Dis-Chem"), the two largest pharmaceutical retailers in South Africa, reported supply disruptions due to looting. The provision of clinical medicine to hospitals was also disrupted.[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-99) As of 16 July, it has been estimated that approximately 50% of patients with chronic illness in KwaZulu-Natal lack an adequate supply of necessary medicine.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-100) Healthcare providing facilities in Pietermaritzburg were placed under severe pressure due to many members of staff being unable to get to their posts, while those that did get there had to deal with an influx of trauma patients.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-PMBMedicalShortage-101) A junior doctor from Pietermaritzburg said that the disruptions in the supply chain resulted in an inability to restock medication, [PPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment "Personal protective equipment"), blood, and syringes.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-PMBMedicalShortage-101)

### Financial

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Financial")\]

#### Economic

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Economic")\]

The South African rand weakened as much as 2% on 12 July, the most it has since 25 February.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-102) Based on a preliminary analysis on 13 July, the [South African Special Risks Insurance Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_on_Finance_(South_Africa) "Standing Committee on Finance (South Africa)") (SASRIA) estimated that total losses due to damage and looting may run into "billions of rand".[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-103) In September 2021 it stated that the nationwide insurance claims could range between R20—25 billion, making it the most expensive riot in the world.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-104)

On 19 July 2021, officers of the [eThekwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EThekwini_Metropolitan_Municipality "EThekwini Metropolitan Municipality") municipality and KwaZulu-Natal provincial government provided a recovery roadmap to the [Toyota Motor Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota "Toyota"). Reportedly, the unrest had led the multinational to doubt the security and viability of its investments in the province.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-105)

Initial estimates by the South African Property Owners Association (SAPOA) on 13 July put the loss to the [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP "GDP") of [Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban "Durban") in excess of R20 billion.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-106) Then-acting [Minister in the Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_in_the_Presidency "Minister in the Presidency") [Khumbudzo Ntshavheni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khumbudzo_Ntshavheni "Khumbudzo Ntshavheni") cited SAPOA estimates on 20 July to say that the unrest could cost a loss of R50 billion ($3.4 billion) to South Africa's national economy.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:2-107) The damage to KwaZulu-Natal's economy alone was estimated to be R20 billion ($1.4 billion) according to Ntshavheni.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Winning-108) 40,000 businesses and 50,000 traders were affected overall in KwaZulu-Natal, while stock worth R1.5 billion was lost and 150,000 jobs were stated to be at risk.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:2-107)

A survey carried out by the [eThekwini Metropolitan Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EThekwini_Metropolitan_Municipality "EThekwini Metropolitan Municipality") and the Durban Chamber of Commerce and Industry in 2022 however estimated the losses in Durban alone to be R70 billion[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:5-23) (equivalent to US$4.89 billion).[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-109) This included R40 billion worth of sales and stock, R20 billion worth of machinery and equipment, and damage worth R15 billion to property. The survey further stated that 9,500 jobs were placed at risk and 16,000 business were affected.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-110)

A July 2021 preliminary report meanwhile estimated losses suffered by the province of Gauteng at R3.5 billion, according to the province's then- [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_Gauteng "Premier of Gauteng") [David Makhura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Makhura "David Makhura"). He also added that 14,500 jobs were estimated to have been lost and 30 shopping malls were looted. Although no factories were affected in the riots, small-scale businesses were significantly affected.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-111)

#### Destruction of property

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Destruction of property")\]

By Monday afternoon on 12 July, more than 200 [shopping centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_center "Shopping center") had been looted, with several in Soweto having been ransacked.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:6-25) On 14 July, the SA Pharmacy Council noted that 90 pharmacies were completely destroyed with the bulk of those in KwaZulu-Natal being hit the hardest.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-SAPC_Looting-112) In Durban, during a 14 July media briefing, [the mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mxolisi_Kaunda "Mxolisi Kaunda") of [eThekwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EThekwini_Metropolitan_Municipality "EThekwini Metropolitan Municipality") announced that up to 45,000 businesses were affected with 129,000 jobs at stake and over R16 billion (nearly $1 billion or £793 million) in damages to stock, property, and equipment.[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-DBN_AftermathCosts-113)

[ICASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Communications_Authority_of_South_Africa "Independent Communications Authority of South Africa") announced that 113 [network towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_site "Cell site") had been vandalised, resulting in the disruption of [cellular networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_network "Cellular network").[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-CellTowers-114) The [National Professional Teachers' Organisation of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Professional_Teachers'_Organisation_of_South_Africa "National Professional Teachers' Organisation of South Africa") decried the looting and vandalism of 32 KwaZulu-Natal schools, one of which burned down, as contemptible.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-DestructionOfSchools-115) In [Ladysmith, KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladysmith,_KwaZulu-Natal "Ladysmith, KwaZulu-Natal"), a [crematorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crematorium "Crematorium") was completely destroyed.[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-LadysmithCrematorium-116)

According to Ntshavheni, 161 shopping malls, 11 warehouses and eight factories in KwaZulu-Natal suffered significant damages.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Winning-108) More than 200 shopping centres suffered looting or damage according to her, while 100 malls were subject to arson attacks, 161 liquor outlets and distributors were damaged, 1,400 ATMs and 90 pharmacies were damaged and about 300 banks and post offices were vandalized.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:2-107)

#### Suspension of services

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Suspension of services")\]

Many of South Africa's banks had to shut down their ATMs, branches, and other facilities in response to the unrest.[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-BanksClosing-117)[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-StdBankDamages-118) Over 300 [Capitec Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitec_Bank "Capitec Bank") branches and ATMs were closed,[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-BanksClosing-117) [Nedbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nedbank "Nedbank") closed 226 branches as well as 59 Boxer outlets,[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-BanksClosing-117) [ABSA Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absa_Bank_Limited "Absa Bank Limited") closed approximately 375 branches,[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-BanksClosing-117) and [Standard Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Bank "Standard Bank") had to close 81 branches in KwaZulu-Natal and 116 in Gauteng after 33 of their branches and 220 ATMs were affected.[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-StdBankDamages-118) In Pietermaritzburg, ambulances and other medical staff feared being attacked and were unwilling to go into dangerous areas, and in some cases they were not able to get to emergencies.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-PMBMedicalShortage-101)

### COVID-19

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: COVID-19")\]

#### COVID-19 vaccine rollout crisis

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: COVID-19 vaccine rollout crisis")\]

Main article: [COVID-19 vaccination in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccination_in_South_Africa "COVID-19 vaccination in South Africa")

Following the impact of the looting and destruction to property, multiple [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_South_Africa "COVID-19 pandemic in South Africa") vaccine rollout sites were closed to prevent looting and property destruction.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-119) These precautions slowed the vaccination roll-out while the country was still battling its third wave of infection.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-auto2-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-auto1-16) The SA Pharmacy Council's Vincent Tlala said that COVID-19 vaccines were among the looted items from pharmacies that were affected.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-SAPC_Looting-112)

On 15 July 2021, the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization")'s Africa director [Matshidiso Moeti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matshidiso_Moeti "Matshidiso Moeti") warned that South Africa would see an increase in cases due to the disruptions across KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-WHO_Africa-120) She also noted that while the vaccination programme was aiming for 300,000 vaccines a day before the unrest unfolded, they had only done under 154,000 on 14 July.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-WHO_Africa-120) A junior doctor from Pietermaritzburg mentioned that they were running out of PPE for their Covid wards in the midst of the third wave.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-PMBMedicalShortage-101)

#### Superspreader event

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Superspreader event")\]

[Epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology "Epidemiology") Professor [Quarraisha Abdool Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarraisha_Abdool_Karim "Quarraisha Abdool Karim") cautioned that the ongoing riots could be a [superspreader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superspreading_event "Superspreading event") due to large numbers of people gathering in small and confined places.[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-Covid19_SuperSpreader-121)\[ _[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\]

### Effect on pending legislation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Effect on pending legislation")\]

At the time that the unrest took place, the [Parliament of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_South_Africa "Parliament of South Africa") was considering a Firearms Control Amendment Bill, which would have amended [firearm regulations in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms_regulation_in_South_Africa "Firearms regulation in South Africa") to remove self-defence as a legal ground for gun ownership by civilians. Various civil society organisations, from the [South African Institute of Race Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Institute_of_Race_Relations "South African Institute of Race Relations") and the Institute for Accountability in Southern Africa to SA Gun Owners Association and Free State Agriculture, and political parties like the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_(South_Africa) "Democratic Alliance (South Africa)"), believed that the bill would fail to pass constitutional muster and had to be rejected as the unrest had highlighted the necessity of an armed citizenry in a country where the state's security forces were unable to provide protection and law and order.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-122)[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-123)[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-124)[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-125)

## Reactions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Reactions")\]

### Domestic

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Domestic")\]

- The [ANC Veterans' League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_National_Congress_Veterans'_League "African National Congress Veterans' League")'s president [Snuki Zikalala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuki_Zikalala "Snuki Zikalala") denounced the violence and looting that took place while he voiced support and approval for the deployment of the SANDF to reinforce the SAPS.[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-ANCVL-126)
- [ActionSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ActionSA "ActionSA") leader [Herman Mashaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Mashaba "Herman Mashaba") announced a separate [class action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_action "Class action") lawsuit to be laid against the government and the ANC for failing to take swift action against the riots and looting. Mashaba said that "we believe that there is more than sufficient jurisprudence that merits this a strong test case, and assists us in holding the government to account for its willful failure to ensure proper law enforcement and the protection of lives, livelihoods and property".[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-127)
- Opposition party leader [John Steenhuisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Steenhuisen "John Steenhuisen") of the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_(South_Africa) "Democratic Alliance (South Africa)") announced that the party would be laying criminal charges against Jacob Zuma's children, [Duduzane Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duduzane_Zuma "Duduzane Zuma") and Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla, for allegedly inciting the violence and looting.[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-128)
- On 15 July 2021, [Misuzulu Ka Zwelithini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misuzulu_Zulu "Misuzulu Zulu"), reigning King of the Zulu nation, issued a statement calling for calm and a return to order.[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-129)
- The leader of [Freedom Front Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Front_Plus "Freedom Front Plus"), [Pieter Groenewald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Groenewald "Pieter Groenewald"), said those responsible for the unrest should face charges of [sedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedition "Sedition"), among others. He added that South Africa needed to learn necessary lessons from the looting and violence, especially the need for rural development. Finally, he stated that the intelligence services failed the country.[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-130)
- On 16 July 2021, the Thabo Mbeki Foundation released a statement claiming that the violent unrest is part of a counterrevolutionary insurgency seeking " [state capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capture "State capture")."[\[131\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-131)
- On 26 July 2021, [Julius Malema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Malema "Julius Malema"), the leader of the [EFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Freedom_Fighters "Economic Freedom Fighters"), criticised Ramaphosa's decision to deploy the SANDF to quell the unrest, and threatened to join in. "No soldiers on our streets! Otherwise, we are joining. All fighters must be ready." he said. He also rejected the claim that the unrest constituted an "insurrection".[\[132\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-132)

## Aftermath

### Clean-up operations

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=33 "Edit section: Clean-up operations")\]

By 15 July 2021, volunteer groups in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal had begun repairing damaged property, donating food and providing transport to affected communities.[\[143\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-143)

### State of disaster

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=34 "Edit section: State of disaster")\]

On 29 July 2021, citing the violence and looting that had caused widespread damage, KwaZulu-Natal premier Sihle Zikalala declared a state of disaster for the province, which decision he claimed was arrived at by the [provincial executive council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_(South_Africa) "Executive Council (South Africa)") on 28 July.[\[144\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-144)

### Criminal justice

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2021_South_African_unrest&action=edit&section=35 "Edit section: Criminal justice")\]

Police are investigating suspects and have arrested one unnamed individual as of 16 July 2021.[\[145\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-145) 682 individuals face prosecution on allegations of public violence, looting, and destruction of infrastructure as of 19 July 2021.[\[146\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-146)

Thulani Dlomo, the former head of the State Security Agency special operations unit and a loyal supporter of Zuma, is reported to be under investigation for inciting unrest.[\[147\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-147) Radio personality and Zuma supporter [Ngizwe Mchunu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngizwe_Mchunu "Ngizwe Mchunu") was also arrested for incitement to commit public violence. Bonginkosi Khanyile was also charged with incitement of violence, and due to appear in the Durban Magistrates Court on 22 October 2021.[\[148\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-148)

ANC [West Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Rand_District_Municipality "West Rand District Municipality") councillor Clarence Tabane was charged with incitement to commit public violence.[\[149\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-149) Bruce Nimmerhoudt, a mayoral candidate for the [Patriotic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Alliance_(South_Africa) "Patriotic Alliance (South Africa)") party, appeared in court on charges of alleged incitement to commit public violence,[\[150\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-150) and was charged under the Terrorism and Related Activities Act (Terrorism).[\[151\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-151) ANC ward councillor Solly Seloane from [Daveyton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daveyton "Daveyton"), [Ekurhuleni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Ekurhuleni_Metropolitan_Municipality "City of Ekurhuleni Metropolitan Municipality") was arrested for public violence and theft after CCTV footage appeared to show him stealing clothes from [Markham and Totalsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foschini_Group "Foschini Group") stores during the unrest.[\[152\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-152)

On 4 August evidence from multiple [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") groups was handed over to the SAPS by [GOOD party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_(political_party) "Good (political party)") representative [Brett Herron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Herron_(politician) "Brett Herron (politician)") to assist with investigations into the cause of the unrest.[\[153\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:1-153) The Daily Maverick stated that the evidence directly linked 26 individuals, allegedly from within the ANC and government, with instigating, organising, and coordinating the unrest following Zuma's imprisonment.[\[153\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-:1-153)

On 28 August, the [Hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawks_(South_Africa) "Hawks (South Africa)") arrested two suspects in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal on suspicion of using social media to incite public violence. Both suspects were expected to appear in court on 30 August 2021.[\[154\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-154)

On 15 November, the [South African Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Human_Rights_Commission "South African Human Rights Commission") began an inquiry into the causes and consequences of the July unrest. It will examine allegations of racially motivated attacks and killings, and the actions of the security forces. The inquiry is expected to run until December 2021.[\[155\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-155)[\[156\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-156)

On 30 January 2025, Jacob Zuma's daughter [Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duduzile_Zuma-Sambudla "Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla") was arrested on terrorism charges on suspicion of inciting violence on social media that contributed to the 2021 unrest.[\[157\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest#cite_note-157)
