Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d4/Logo_RCEP.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Logo_RCEP.png) Logo of the RCEP

The **Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership** ( **RCEP**[/ˈɑːrsɛp/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [_AR-sep_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is a [free trade agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreement "Free trade agreement") among the Asia-Pacific countries of [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei"), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia"), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand"), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), and [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Initiator-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-Initiator) The 15 member countries account for about 30% of the [world's population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") (2.2 billion people) and 30% of [global GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_world_product "Gross world product") ($29.7 trillion), making it the largest [trade bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_bloc "Trade bloc") in history.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) Signed in November 2020, RCEP is the first free trade agreement among the [largest economies in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_countries_by_GDP "List of Asian countries by GDP") (excluding India), including China, Indonesia, Japan, and South Korea.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCMP_What_is_RCEP-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-SCMP_What_is_RCEP)

The RCEP was conceived at the 2011 [ASEAN Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Summit "ASEAN Summit") in [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali "Bali"), Indonesia, while negotiations formally launched during the 2012 ASEAN Summit in Cambodia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Challenges-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-Challenges) India, which took part in the initial negotiations but later decided to opt out, was invited to join the bloc at any time. Any other country or separate customs territory in the region can accede to the pact from 1 July 2023 onward.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sign1-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-Sign1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sign2-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-Sign2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) The treaty was formally signed on 15 November 2020 at the virtual ASEAN Summit hosted by Vietnam. For the first ten ratifying countries, the trade pact took effect on 1 January 2022.[&#8202;](#ref-back-aus_nz-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-aus_nz)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

The RCEP includes a mix of high-,[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[note 1\]](#refnote-15) middle-,[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[note 2\]](#refnote-16) and low-income countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[note 3\]](#refnote-17) It is expected to eliminate about 90% of the [tariffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariff "Tariff") on imports between its signatories within 20 years of coming into force, and establish common rules for [e-commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-commerce "E-commerce"), trade, and intellectual property.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCMP_What_is_RCEP-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-SCMP_What_is_RCEP) Several analysts predicted that it would offer significant economic gains for signatory states, boost [post-pandemic economic recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic "Economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic"), as well as "pull the economic centre of gravity back towards Asia, with China poised to take the lead in writing trade rules for the region," leaving the United States behind in economic and political affairs in the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[note 4\]](#refnote-28) Reactions from others were neutral or negative,[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[note 5\]](#refnote-41) with some analysts saying that the economic gains from the trade deal would be modest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnbc_A_coup-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-cnbc_A_coup)[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj._Asias_Huge-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-wsj._Asias_Huge)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mahadevan_Nugroho_pp._3148–3167-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-Mahadevan_Nugroho_pp._3148–3167)[&#8202;](#ref-back-hind_India_has-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-hind_India_has)

## Contents

RCEP aims to lower tariffs, increase investment, and facilitate freer movement of goods around the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:Ma&Kang-1)[\[37\]](#refnote-:Ma&Kang):176 It includes unified [rules of origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_of_origin "Rules of origin") throughout the bloc, which may facilitate international supply chains and trade within the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYTimes_China-Led_Trade_Pact-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-NYTimes_China-Led_Trade_Pact)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest) It also prohibits certain tariffs. It does not focus on labour unions, [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection "Environmental protection"), or [government subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_subsidies "Government subsidies").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYTimes_China-Led_Trade_Pact-1)[\[70\]](#refnote-NYTimes_China-Led_Trade_Pact)

The RCEP contains 20 chapters discussing trade within many sectors, including (but not restricted to):

1. Goods and services
2. Investment
3. Government Procurement
4. Standards and Technical Regulations
5. Intellectual Property Rights
6. E-Commerce

The RCEP contains tariff schedules that intend to eliminate tariffs by 92 percent over a course of 20 years. The tariff schedules include the complete elimination of tariffs and quotas on over 65% of the goods trade within the RCEP bloc.[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-77) Tariff concessions included within the document will have varying effects based on different members political and economic context. For members that are already heavily liberalized, concessions will be lower than those with higher existing tariffs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-:1)

Under the RCEP there is a high level of protection within the agricultural sectors. Crops and meat products experience the highest level of protection, especially when compared to global trade dynamics outside of the RCEP. Although agriculture will still experience high levels of protection, the RCEP will have significant contributions, with a current tariff reduction of 12.8 percent occurring within 8.4% of agricultural products.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[73\]](#refnote-:1)

Within the manufacturing sector, motor vehicles, apparel and leather, non metallic minerals, and textiles experience the highest levels of tariff protection. Conversely, extractive products and natural resources will experience little to no protection, facing zero or near zero tariff rates.[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-79)

The RCEP is not as comprehensive as the [Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_and_Progressive_Agreement_for_Trans-Pacific_Partnership "Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership"), another free trade agreement in the region that includes some of the same countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCMP_What_is_RCEP-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-SCMP_What_is_RCEP) The RCEP "does not establish unified standards on labour and the environment, or commit countries to open services and other vulnerable areas of their economies."[&#8202;](#ref-back-ektgam-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-ektgam)

The tariffs schedule just for Japan is 1,334 pages long.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ektgam-1)[\[75\]](#refnote-ektgam)

## Projected value

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/2020_RCEP-15%27s_share_of_global_GDP.svg/960px-2020_RCEP-15%27s_share_of_global_GDP.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2020_RCEP-15's_share_of_global_GDP.svg) 2020 RCEP-15's share of global GDP (%)

The combined GDP of potential RCEP members surpassed the combined GDP of [Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Pacific_Partnership "Trans-Pacific Partnership") members in 2007. It was suggested that continued economic growth, particularly in China and Indonesia, could see total GDP in the original RCEP membership grow to over US$100 trillion by 2050, roughly double the project size of TPP economies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-:0) On 23 January 2017, President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") signed a memorandum withdrawing the United States from the TPP, a move which was seen to improve the chances of success for RCEP.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-82)

According to a 2020 projection, the agreement is expected to enlarge the global economy by US$186 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest-1)[\[71\]](#refnote-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest) According to Peter Petri and Michael Plummer of the [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution "Brookings Institution"), the RCEP could add $209 billion annually to world incomes, and $500 billion to world trade by 2030, and that "new agreements will make the economies of North and Southeast Asia more efficient, linking their strengths in technology, manufacturing, agriculture, and natural resources."[&#8202;](#ref-back-broo_RCEP_A-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-broo_RCEP_A) According to computer simulations in a related paper also by Petri and Plummer published by the [Peterson Institute for International Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterson_Institute_for_International_Economics "Peterson Institute for International Economics") (PIIE), the RCEP will raise global national incomes in 2030 by an annual $147 billion and $186 billion respectively, "yield especially large benefits for China, Japan, and South Korea and losses for the United States and India", and "will be especially valuable because it strengthens East Asian interdependence, raising trade among members by $428 billion and reducing trade among nonmembers by $48 billion".[&#8202;](#ref-back-PIIE_US_Decouples-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-PIIE_US_Decouples)

The simulations in Petri and Plummer's PIIE paper showed that the RCEP and the [Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_and_Progressive_Agreement_for_Trans-Pacific_Partnership "Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership") (CPTPP) would together more than offset the global negative economic effects of the China-United States trade war, but not the individual losses of China and the United States. Moreover, the incremental value of the CPTPP will be reduced by the trade war (from $147 billion to $121 billion) while the value of RCEP will be increased (from $186 billion to $209 billion). The PIIE working paper also stated that "RCEP will be economically significant with or without India, and indeed more significant than the CPTPP, with especially important benefits for China, Japan, and Korea" and that "RCEP will reorient trade and economic ties away from global linkages toward regionally focused relationships in East Asia."[&#8202;](#ref-back-PIIE_US_Decouples-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-PIIE_US_Decouples)

According to the [Asian Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Development_Bank "Asian Development Bank") (ADB), the RCEP is "relatively comprehensive in coverage" and combines existing deals, which brings Asia a step closer to a region-wide trading bloc. The agreement further liberalises goods and services trade, establishes common rules of origin for all goods traded, establishes commitments regarding government procurement, and aims to establish open and competitive markets. Though the degree of liberalisation within RCEP is not as deep as in the CPTPP, RCEP members are projected to gain $174 billion in real income by 2030, equivalent to 0.4% of the members' aggregate GDP. China, Korea and Japan will benefit the most, with likely gains of $85 billion for China, $48 billion for Japan, and $23 billion for Korea. Other significant RCEP gains will accrue to Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ADB_contribution-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-ADB_contribution) Due to their RCEP membership, Japan and Korea would accrue gains amounting to 1% of their GDP while Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam and Brunei would all accrue gains of 0.5% of GDP or higher.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bangkok_Post_trade-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-Bangkok_Post_trade)

New Zealand's [Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_and_Trade_(New_Zealand) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (New Zealand)") stated that the RCEP is projected to add $186 billion to the world economy and increase New Zealand's GDP by around $2.0 billion. The RCEP members took 56% of New Zealand's total exports, representing 61% of New Zealand's goods exports (worth $36.6 billion) and 45% of New Zealand's services exports (worth $11.8 billion). According to the ministry, "RCEP delivers improved market access for New Zealand services exporters and investors in some RCEP markets that go beyond existing FTAs. Under RCEP, New Zealand services exporters and investors will, for the first time, benefit from market access commitments from China and ASEAN countries that are not party to the CPTPP".[&#8202;](#ref-back-NZ_MFAT-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-NZ_MFAT) Following the signing of the RCEP, New Zealand and China signed a deal to further expand their existing free-trade agreement. The expanded deal provides for tariffs to be either removed or cut on many of New Zealand's mostly commodities-based exports, ranging from dairy to timber and seafood, while compliance costs will also be reduced. The deal also opens up sectors such as aviation, education and finance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-China-NZ_expand-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-China-NZ_expand)

[Huang Qifan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huang_Qifan "Huang Qifan"), former mayor of [Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing "Chongqing"), stated that with the implementation of RCEP, China and RCEP countries will form an increasingly close industrial chain and [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain "Supply chain") connection, and in the future, there will be a variety of [production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_(economics) "Production (economics)") and [marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing "Marketing") models such as "in China, for China", "in China, for RCEP", "in RCEP, for the world". At that time, China's large [market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_(economics) "Market (economics)") superimposed RCEP large market, China's [dual circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_circulation "Dual circulation") superimposed RCEP great circulation, "will profoundly change the world industrial chain supply chain layout".[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-85)

## RCEP and COVID-19

### Impacts of COVID-19 on RCEP members

The [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") impacted virtually every economy, happening right as the RCEP trade deal was meant to start. The pandemic caused trade costs to increase at borders, creating barriers on [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade") to goods and services. According to the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank"), issues of high fiscal debt, [supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain "Supply chain") bottlenecks, the widening skill gap, an increase in poverty, and global supply value chain resilience were challenges in economic recovery.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:02-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-:02)

Trends in pandemic responses within RCEP members mirrored global ones, with RCEP members with more [developed markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_market "Developed market") having both a quicker response and recovery from economic shocks than [emerging markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_market "Emerging market"). This was found to be due to emerging markets lack of risk resistance, which is predicted to strengthen under regional integration under the RCEP agreement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-87)

#### RCEP mitigation of economic shocks

The regional integration of RCEP will result from the contents of the agreement, including rules based trade and investment, open [market access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_access "Market access"), increased economic cooperation, and having [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") members central to the agreement. These measures have worked to alleviate the economic shocks of the pandemic, and are expected to assist in mitigating effects of the US-China trade war on East Asia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:02-1)[\[81\]](#refnote-:02) An analysis conducted by Itakura (2022) shows positive trends in growth of GDP for all RCEP members through the 2030s.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Asia Global Institute report: RCEP Trade 2022

Trade between RCEP members in 2022 revealed post-pandemic trade dynamics and reflected the increasing integration that the RCEP intended to create. The Asia Global Institute reports that in 2022, trade between RCEP members increased by 8 percent, lagging behind trade between RCEP members and non-RCEP members with an increased 8.6% growth in trade. All members experienced a rebound in trade post COVID-19, arriving at pre-Covid trade levels by 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-:12)

Raw materials, such as salt, nickel, and chemicals, significantly increased in trade between RCEP members. This could be a result of both [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") being members, with China being the lead importer of nickel and Indonesia being the lead global exporter. This is also representative of the [Electric Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle_industry_in_China "Electric vehicle industry in China") industry prevalent within RCEP members. There was a dramatic increase in tobacco exports within RCEP members of 150% in 2022, with China individually experiencing a 900% growth of exports.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-1)[\[83\]](#refnote-:12)

Although RCEP is the first agreement to create a free trade agreement between the large economies of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_China "Economy of China"), [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_South_Korea "Economy of South Korea"), and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Japan "Economy of Japan"), 2022 trends show a preference for the big three to trade with other RCEP members rather than each other. Trade volume between the big three declined between the years of 2020 to 2023 as trade between RCEP members as a conglomerate grew.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-2)[\[83\]](#refnote-:12)

The [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") countries within RCEP were not observed to experience gains in trade. In 2022, 20% of ASEAN's trade occurred between ASEAN members, and 30% came from what is referred to as the non-ASEAN 5, consisting of [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Indonesia "Economy of Indonesia"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Malaysia "Economy of Malaysia"), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Philippines "Economy of the Philippines"), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Singapore "Economy of Singapore"), and [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Thailand "Economy of Thailand").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-3)[\[83\]](#refnote-:12)

## Reactions

### Positive

When the RCEP was signed, Chinese premier [Li Keqiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Keqiang "Li Keqiang") declared it "a victory of multilateralism and free trade".[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign-2)[\[7\]](#refnote-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign) Singaporean prime minister [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong") called it "a major step forward for our region" and a sign of support for free trade and economic interdependence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest-2)[\[71\]](#refnote-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest) Vietnamese prime minister [Nguyễn Xuân Phúc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Xu%C3%A2n_Ph%C3%BAc "Nguyễn Xuân Phúc") said the RCEP will be conducive to economic recovery and bring prosperity to enterprises and people in all countries concerned.[&#8202;](#ref-back-146-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-146) Cambodian Prime Minister [Hun Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hun_Sen "Hun Sen") stated that the RCEP has great potential to address major challenges as well as has a crucial role in maintaining [prosperity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosperity "Prosperity") and [political stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_stability "Political stability") in the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-147)

Several analysts\[ _[according to whom?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")_\] predicted that it would help stimulate the economies of signatory states amid the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), as well as "pull the economic centre of gravity back towards Asia, with China poised to take the lead in writing trade rules for the region", leaving the U.S. behind in economic and political affairs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign-3)[\[7\]](#refnote-SCMP_China_declares_victory_as_15_nations_sign)[&#8202;](#ref-back-USDecline-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-USDecline)[&#8202;](#ref-back-TimeUS-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-TimeUS)

[Mohamed Azmin Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Azmin_Ali "Mohamed Azmin Ali"), Minister of International Trade and Industry of Malaysia, said the RCEP would encourage local businesses to enter global markets and would increase Malaysia's exports. He stated that RCEP signatories would enjoy preferential treatment due to the removal of tariff and non-tariff trade barriers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-malaysianreserve_global-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-malaysianreserve_global)

[Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo "Joko Widodo"), President of Indonesia, stated that the signing of the RCEP was a historic day that signalled Indonesia's strong commitment to multilateralism. [Agus Suparmanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agus_Suparmanto "Agus Suparmanto"), Indonesian Minister of Trade, said that the RCEP could boost Indonesia's exports to signatory countries by 8-11% and boost investment into Indonesia by 18-22%, and expressed confidence that the trade pact would benefit Indonesian business.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jakartaglobe_benefits-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-jakartaglobe_benefits)

[Hiroaki Nakanishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroaki_Nakanishi "Hiroaki Nakanishi"), Chairman of the [Japan Business Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Business_Federation "Japan Business Federation"), said the RCEP will help expand trade and investment in the region, and will bring further prosperity and stability, which is very important for achieving a free and open international economic order.[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-148)

[Moon Jae-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Jae-in "Moon Jae-in"), the [President of South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_South_Korea "President of South Korea"), praised the RCEP as an unprecedented mega regional trading agreement and expressed confidence that it will "contribute to the recovery of multilateralism and the development of free trade around the world, beyond the region". Moon also stated that he expects the RCEP to open the world's biggest e-commerce market.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Korea_herald_moon_sign-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-Korea_herald_moon_sign) The Korean Chamber of Commerce and Industry welcomed the conclusion of the RCEP, expecting that it would "expand a new free trade bloc and serve as the basis for revitalizing the Asia-Pacific regional economic markets". The RCEP will benefit Korean companies by removing tariffs on several Korean imports in signatory states, especially in steel, automobiles and electronics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Korea_herald_firms_benefit-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-Korea_herald_firms_benefit)

[Kishore Mahbubani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishore_Mahbubani "Kishore Mahbubani"), Singapore's former permanent representative to the United Nations and former President of the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council"), stated that the "future of Asia will be written in four letters, RCEP" and that India did a major geopolitical favour to China by withdrawing from the RCEP, just as the United States did by withdrawing from the CPTPP. Mahbubani added that with India and the United States absent, "a massive economic ecosystem centered on China is evolving in the region".[&#8202;](#ref-back-mahbubani_fp-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-mahbubani_fp)

According to Peter Petri and Michael Plummer at the [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution "Brookings Institution"), the agreement represented "a triumph of ASEAN's middle-power diplomacy" and would lead to significant increases in world incomes and trade by 2030, even though it "says nothing at all about labor, the environment, or state-owned enterprises". They added, "However, ASEAN-centered trade agreements tend to improve over time."[&#8202;](#ref-back-broo_RCEP_A-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-broo_RCEP_A)

### Criticism

In 2016, the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation "Electronic Frontier Foundation") described the first draft of RCEP's intellectual property provisions as containing "quite simply the worst provisions on copyright ever seen in a trade agreement". They praised the second draft for removing some of the most problematic provisions similar to the ones in the Trans-Pacific Partnership, but described as unnecessary the new [broadcasting rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting_rights "Broadcasting rights") provision, which gives broadcasters exclusive control over content that has been broadcast, even if they do not hold any copyright in said content. They wrote that a prohibition on reproduction of broadcast content was "such an extreme proposal that it is currently considered off the table" in the proposed [Protection of Broadcasts and Broadcasting Organisations Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_of_Broadcasts_and_Broadcasting_Organisations_Treaty?action=edit&redlink=1 "Protection of Broadcasts and Broadcasting Organisations Treaty").[&#8202;](#ref-back-EFF-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-EFF)

India pulled out of the deal in November 2019, primarily due to concerns of dumping of manufactured goods from China and agricultural and dairy products from Australia and New Zealand, potentially affecting its own domestic industrial and farming sectors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-indi_India_decides-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-indi_India_decides) Due to India's withdrawal, there are concerns that China may dominate RCEP.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest-3)[\[71\]](#refnote-FT_Asia-Pacific_countries_sign_one_of_the_largest) ASEAN leaders stated that India was welcome to return and join the bloc. Any other state may join RCEP 18 months after it comes into force.[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-150)

_[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ reported in November 2019 that the tariff-related liberalisations from RCEP would be modest, calling it a " [paper tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_tiger "Paper tiger")". A comprehensive study into the deal shows that it would add just 0.08% to China's 2030 GDP without India's participation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj._Asias_Huge-1)[\[32\]](#refnote-wsj._Asias_Huge)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mahadevan_Nugroho_pp._3148–3167-1)[\[33\]](#refnote-Mahadevan_Nugroho_pp._3148–3167)

Human rights groups said RCEP could negatively affect small farmers, lead to more land conflicts, and make workers in poorer countries worse off. Rashmi Banga, a senior economist at [UNCTAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNCTAD "UNCTAD"), said that implementing RCEP at a time of crisis will make poorer countries in Southeast Asia even more vulnerable, adding, "Most Asean nations will see rising imports and declining exports. That will worsen their balance of trade and weaken their fiscal position."[&#8202;](#ref-back-reut_Fears_world's-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-reut_Fears_world's)

Former [Australian prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_prime_minister "Australian prime minister") [Malcolm Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Turnbull "Malcolm Turnbull") said that despite the "hoopla", the RCEP was "a really low-ambition trade deal that we shouldn't kid ourselves on", adding, "It's a very old fashioned trade deal. It's low ambition. It's been affected largely for strategic reasons."[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc_What_is-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-bbc_What_is)[&#8202;](#ref-back-scmp_China-Australia-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-scmp_China-Australia)

[CNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNBC "CNBC") reported that the economic benefits from RCEP would be modest and could take years to materialise, with analysts from [Citi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup "Citigroup") suggesting that RCEP was "a coup for China" non-economically. The Citi report also said that India is one of the biggest losers from RCEP, adding, "exclusion will likely make India less attractive as an alternative production base versus ASEAN."[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnbc_A_coup-1)[\[31\]](#refnote-cnbc_A_coup)

Indian external affairs minister [Subrahmanyam Jaishankar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subrahmanyam_Jaishankar "Subrahmanyam Jaishankar") said it is not in India's interest to join the RCEP, as the trade deal would have "fairly immediate negative consequences" for India's economy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-hind_RCEP_would-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-hind_RCEP_would)[&#8202;](#ref-back-econ_India_pulled-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-econ_India_pulled) Zia Haq, associate editor at _[Hindustan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Times "Hindustan Times")_, said India has "rightly shunned" the RCEP because at the moment it cannot take advantage of free-trade agreements. He went on to say, "India fears the RCEP will also limit its policy-making room in areas such as foreign investment." He said that according to some analysts, there will be limited gains from the RCEP without India, which is Asia's second-largest economy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-hind_India_has-1)[\[34\]](#refnote-hind_India_has)

Yen Huai-shing, deputy director at the [Chung-Hua Institution for Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chung-Hua_Institution_for_Economic_Research "Chung-Hua Institution for Economic Research"), wrote on _[Taipei Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Times "Taipei Times")_ that the RCEP is "not to have a strong impact" on Taiwan. She said that most observers did not expect the RCEP to provide a high degree of openness, and that it provides no [dispute settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispute_resolution "Dispute resolution") mechanism when handling certain trade issues such as [meat inspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_inspection "Meat inspection") regulations, adding, "In other words, they are more symbolic than binding."[&#8202;](#ref-back-taip_RCEP_not-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-taip_RCEP_not)

According to Patricia Ranald at the [Australian Institute of International Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Institute_of_International_Affairs "Australian Institute of International Affairs"), RCEP has limited gains from trade and ignores labour, human rights, and environmental sustainability issues. She said, "Despite claims about the benefits of common standards, the RCEP has no commitments to internationally recognised labour rights and environmental standards which Australia and other RCEP governments have endorsed through the United Nations and the [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization "International Labour Organization")."[&#8202;](#ref-back-inte_RCEP_Has-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-inte_RCEP_Has)

The Center for Environmental Concerns outlined the harmful environmental implications of increased trade within the RPEC bloc. The CEC states that the lack of provisions on environmentally critical activities and areas will affect data collection and information dissemination critical to environmentally dependent activities. The CEC expresses concern that extractive resource industries experiencing near-zero tariff levels will increase in competition, leading to a higher demand for timber, with devastating effects of deforestation and habitat destruction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:03-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-:03)

The CEC also finds that increased trade resulting from RPEC will lead to an increased demand in energy and emissions, with the RPEC containing no provisions promoting the use of renewable energy. Concerns of increasing emissions follow from predictions of energy intensive sectors (steel, cement, and chemicals) becoming more competitive, as well as an increasing use of fossil fuels to sustain production and transportation processes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:03-1)[\[149\]](#refnote-:03)

The lack of environmental provisions have also prompted scientists to estimate the impacts of trade within the RCEP bloc on emissions contributing to climate change. In 2018, almost 40% of global emissions resulted from RCEP member countries. As RCEP is expected to increase trade and benefit all members, it is also expected to increase emission production. It is estimated that a tariff reduction on all goods traded within the RCEP could result in RCEP members being responsible for 75.4 percent of increased global emissions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-155-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-155)

[Salvatore Babones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Babones "Salvatore Babones") commented on _[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy "Foreign Policy")_ that, by 2030, the world's economy would be expected to grow around 40% and the RCEP may add 0.2% to it, the scale of a "rounding error". He said that some main exports to China, such as Japan's machinery and Australia's iron ore, are already tariff-free. Babones said that with the signing of RCEP, "China may score propaganda points by posing as the guardian of the international system, but the system itself is increasingly bypassing China."[&#8202;](#ref-back-fore_Cutting_Through-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-fore_Cutting_Through)

Some analysts and economists said the RCEP is unlikely to benefit its developing member countries in the short term. Kate Lappin, Asia Pacific regional secretary at [Public Services International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Services_International "Public Services International"), said that the pact has no provisions for improving labour rights, adding, "The agreement might not be good for governments and workers, but still deliver profits for foreign investors."[&#8202;](#ref-back-reut_RCEP_unlikely-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-reut_RCEP_unlikely)
