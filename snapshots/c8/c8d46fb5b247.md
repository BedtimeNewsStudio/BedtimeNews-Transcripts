Wespath Benefits and Investments

**Wespath Benefits and Investments** (formerly known as **General Board of Pension and Health Benefits**) is an American non-profit pension agency affiliated with the [United Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Methodist_Church "United Methodist Church"). Wespath supervises and administers [retirement plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retirement_plan "Retirement plan"), investment funds, health and welfare benefit plans for active and retired [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy "Clergy") and lay employees of the Church. In accordance with its fiduciary duties, Wespath administers benefit plans and invests using [Christian finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_finance "Christian finance") principles.

As of 2018[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Wespath_Benefits_and_Investments&action=edit), it held approximately $28 billion in assets for over 100,000 participants and over 120 United Methodist-affiliated institutions.

## History

Established in 1908 its current head office is in Glenview, Illinois.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)

## Sustainable Investment

Wespath follows the _United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights_ for [socially responsible investing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socially_responsible_investing "Socially responsible investing").[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)

It identifies several countries as having "prolonged and systematic patterns of human rights violations,"[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) including the [Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Republic "Central African Republic"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"), and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) A total of 44 companies are excluded from its investment funds including all of Israel's major banks. The board also excludes a further nine companies due to their contribution to [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming "Global warming").[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)
