# AI Bot Pluto Invades StarCraft Ladder and Defeats Professional Players

StarCraft: Brood War has gained a rather unusual competitor on its official ladder, as the AI bot Pluto appeared among human players and began defeating several professional players, using extremely unconventional strategies and seemingly superhuman unit control.

Pluto was originally developed for this year's Computer Olympiad StarCraft AI Competition, where it produced a dominant performance and won the tournament after defeating PurpleWave 4-1 in the final. Unlike traditional StarCraft bots that largely rely on predefined strategies, Pluto is a neural network with around 315 million parameters that learned to play through reinforcement learning and self-play. The same model can play different races and matchups while making decisions for individual units throughout a game.

However, Pluto was never supposed to be playing against real players on the official ladder. According to members of the Brood War AI community, an unknown individual took the bot, modified it to work with StarCraft: Remastered, and placed it on the ladder without permission from its creator. The bot played under the name **^333^** and at one point climbed as high as fifth place on the global leaderboard.

The situation is even more controversial because the community claims that the unauthorized version of Pluto was additionally modified to use a maphack. This is particularly important because the original Pluto was trained using only the information that a player would normally have access to through the fog of war. Its developers have even explained that giving the model additional information could potentially confuse it, as it was trained to make decisions without having access to it.

Because of this, the ladder results should not be treated as a completely accurate demonstration of the original Pluto's capabilities. Nevertheless, its games attracted significant attention because of the way the bot plays. Pluto defeated several well-known Brood War professionals, including Larva, Iris, Ret and Paralyze, with some of the games also being analyzed by prominent StarCraft commentators such as Artosis.

One of the most interesting matches came against Protoss player Paralyze. Playing Terran, Pluto used an extremely unusual combination of Marines, Medics, Tanks, Ghosts, Wraiths and even a Firebat. Instead of relying on a conventional Terran strategy, the bot simultaneously fought in multiple areas of the map, precisely micro-managed its units and used Ghosts to Lockdown key enemy units. At one point, it even accidentally used Lockdown on its own Siege Tank, showing that its exceptional control does not necessarily translate into equally strong decision-making in every situation.

Against Zerg player Larva, Pluto went even further away from conventional strategy. It built as many as six Barracks and essentially relied on huge numbers of Marines and Medics, while skipping the Missile Turrets and traditional attack and armor upgrades normally associated with the matchup. Rather than trying to outplay Larva through standard economic or technological play, the bot used its micro to surround and neutralize Mutalisks before taking control of the game.

Its displayed APM has also attracted considerable attention. During some games, Pluto reached several thousand actions per minute, although its actual decision-making speed is not nearly that extreme. According to explanations from the AI community, the model makes one major decision every six frames, which corresponds to roughly 240 actions per minute. When a single command controls multiple units, the game registers each individual unit command as a separate action, causing the displayed APM to reach several thousand.

Interestingly, despite its impressive micro, Pluto also has clear weaknesses. AI researcher Evan "evanthebouncy" Chen described it as extremely strong in terms of its "instinct," while pointing out issues with building placement, long-term planning and certain unusual situations. Community members have also noted that the bot was not trained for some units and abilities, leaving potential opportunities for human players to exploit.

The incident has caused frustration among members of the StarCraft AI community, who emphasize that the bot's appearance on the ladder was not authorized. According to community reports, the case was reported to Blizzard with a request for the account to be removed.

This is not the first time an AI has played on a StarCraft ladder. In 2019, DeepMind, working with Blizzard, allowed AlphaStar to anonymously play against humans on the European StarCraft II ladder as part of an official and controlled experiment. Players were able to participate in the experiment, and AlphaStar eventually reached Grandmaster level.

Pluto's case, however, is completely different. Its appearance on the ladder was not part of an official developer experiment, but rather the result of an unauthorized modification of a bot that was originally created for an AI competition.

Pluto therefore represents an interesting new development for StarCraft AI, although its ladder performances should currently be viewed with some caution because of the way the bot was placed on the server and the allegations surrounding the use of a maphack. What is clear is that this year's COG StarCraft AI Competition demonstrated significant progress in reinforcement learning for Brood War, while Pluto's games against professional players offered a bizarre glimpse of what StarCraft looks like when it is played by a system that approaches decision-making and micro-management in a fundamentally different way from humans.
