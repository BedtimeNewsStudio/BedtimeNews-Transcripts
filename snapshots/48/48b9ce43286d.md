**Field Harris** [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBE "CBE") (September 18, 1895 – December 21, 1967) was a highly decorated [lieutenant general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_general_(United_States) "Lieutenant general (United States)") in the [United States Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps "United States Marine Corps"), who commanded the Marine Aviation Units during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") and 1st Marine Aircraft Wing during the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)

## Early years

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Field_Harris&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early years")\]

Thomas Field Harris was born on September 18, 1895, in [Versailles, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles,_Kentucky "Versailles, Kentucky"), the son of Andrew Thomas and Lena _Field_ Harris.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000)

He attended the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy "United States Naval Academy") at [Annapolis, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapolis,_Maryland "Annapolis, Maryland"), and graduated in 1917.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000) He was subsequently appointed a second lieutenant in the Marine Corps on March 30 of that year.

His first assignment was for a brief period aboard the [USS _Nevada_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Nevada_(BB-36) "USS Nevada (BB-36)") and subsequently was assigned to the Third Provisional Brigade at [Guantánamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guant%C3%A1namo_Bay "Guantánamo Bay"), [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba"). Harris stayed in this capacity until April 1919.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000) His next service assignment was at [Naval Station Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Cavite "Naval Station Cavite"), [Philippine Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Islands "Philippine Islands"), where he participated in the shore patrol duty. Field was transferred back to the United States in June 1922, when he assigned to the [Judge Advocate General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_Advocate_General's_Corps "Judge Advocate General's Corps") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") While there he graduated from [George Washington University School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University_School_of_Law "George Washington University School of Law").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000) Subsequently, he was assigned to battleship [USS _Wyoming_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wyoming_(BB-32) "USS Wyoming (BB-32)"), where he was appointed a commanding officer of the Marine detachment.

Field later attended the advanced one-year course at [Marine Corps Base Quantico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Quantico "Marine Corps Base Quantico") and then began flight training at the [Naval Air Station Pensacola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Pensacola "Naval Air Station Pensacola"), [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida"). He was designated a Naval Aviator on April 13, 1929.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-4)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000) His first duties as a flyer were at [Naval Air Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_North_Island "Naval Air Station North Island"), [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego"), where he served as a commanding officer and executive officer of an aircraft squadron within West Coast Expeditionary Force. Field then attended the course of instructions at [Air Corps Tactical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Corps_Tactical_School "Air Corps Tactical School") at [Langley Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley_Field "Langley Field") and subsequently served within shore duty in [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti "Haiti") and sea duty aboard the aircraft carrier [USS _Lexington_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Lexington_(CV-2) "USS Lexington (CV-2)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-5)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000) Field's next service assignment was at [Headquarters Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headquarters_Marine_Corps "Headquarters Marine Corps") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), where he served in the aviation section. He also attended the [Naval War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_War_College "Naval War College") at [Newport, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island "Newport, Rhode Island"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-6)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000) where he graduated from the Senior course in May 1939.

## World War II

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Field_Harris&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: World War II")\]

At the beginning of the War, Field served still in [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo "Cairo"), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"), as assistant naval attaché. He had the opportunity to study the Royal Air Force's support of [Britain's Eighth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Army_(United_Kingdom) "Eighth Army (United Kingdom)") in its desert operations. After that, he went to the South Pacific and was chief of staff, aircraft, on Guadalcanal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-7)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000)

## Korean War and retirement

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Field_Harris&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Korean War and retirement")\]

Major General Harris was commanding general of 1st Marine Aircraft Wing during the Korean War. His son, Lieutenant Colonel [William Frederick Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Frederick_Harris "William Frederick Harris"), USMC, was lost on December 7, 1950, in the breakout at the [Battle of Chosin Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chosin_Reservoir "Battle of Chosin Reservoir"). General Harris retired July 1953 and subsequently worked as librarian for the Kentucky State Law Library.

General Harris died in 1967.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Simmons_2000-8)[\[3\]](#refnote-Simmons_2000)
