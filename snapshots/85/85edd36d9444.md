The **Hsiung Feng IIE** ( **HF-2E**; [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"):雄風二E巡弋飛彈; lit.'Brave Wind IIE Cruise Missile') is a [surface-to-surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-to-surface_missile "Surface-to-surface missile") [land-attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-attack_missile "Land-attack missile") [cruise missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missile "Cruise missile") system developed by the [National Chung-Shan Institute of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Chung-Shan_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology "National Chung-Shan Institute of Science and Technology") (NCSIST) in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan").

## Development

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hsiung_Feng_IIE&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Development")\]

According to the _Taiwan Defense Review_ (TDR), the HF-2E land attack cruise missile is not a derivative of the Hsiung Feng 2 [HF-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HF-2 "HF-2") anti-ship missile, as it is often incorrectly reported. The use of the "HF-2E" designation is the primary cause of this confusion and was intentional [misinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misinformation "Misinformation") in order to divert attention away from the true nature of the project, which is that of a long range offensive cruise missile. The HF-2E is actually a completely different design and is said to serve a similar function in Taiwan's military as the [US Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy "US Navy")'s [Tomahawk cruise missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_cruise_missile "Tomahawk cruise missile").[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-tdr2) Its launch weight is reported to be in the 3,300–3,500lb (1,500–1,600kg) range, including its solid rocket booster.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1) It is essentially a tactical land attack cruise missile designed for use against military target sets, particularly air-defense fire units and command-and-control facilities, and its relatively small warhead size and the rather limited number of missiles planned for procurement clearly suggest that this is not a "first strike" weapon.[&#8202;](#ref-back-def1-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-def1)

The project was first announced in 2001. Following several test firings in 2004 and early 2005 at the [Jiu Peng Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiu_Peng_Missile_Range "Jiu Peng Missile Range") in southeastern Taiwan, the baseline HF-2E (Block I) completed its operational evaluation (OPEVAL) in 2005, the missile flying a low-altitude circuit off Taiwan's southeastern coastline between Pingtung and Lanyu Island. An improved HF-2E missile was reportedly tested by CSIST at Jui Peng Missile Range on 2 February 2007.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1) In 2017 the missiles received an extensive upgrade to increase their effectiveness against naval targets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AP_2017-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-AP_2017)

## Description

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hsiung_Feng_IIE&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Description")\]

The baseline HF-2E Block I land attack cruise missile (LACM) is said to be powered by an indigenously-developed Taiwanese turbofan engine believed to be rated in the 800lbf (3.6kN) thrust range and developed by CSIST partially based on technology and experience from the Microturbo 078 turbojet engine used on Hsiung Feng 2 ( [HF-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HF-2 "HF-2")) anti-ship cruise missiles. When equipped with a standard unitary high-explosive 1,000lb (450kg)-class warhead, it is said to have a maximum range of 700km. Other types of warhead are said to be in development, such as cluster [submunitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submunitions "Submunitions") and a hard target penetrator warhead. TDR also reported that through modification of the existing engine and combined with the redesign and reduction of the missile's control and electronic systems, CSIST was able to free up enough internal space/weight in the missile to allow it to carry additional fuel and extend its range to over 2,000km,[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1) although a _Defense News_ report claimed the other version was only an 800km range missile.[&#8202;](#ref-back-def1-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-def1) The ultimate objective is to develop a missile that has an objective range of over 5,000km (3,100mi), using a technologically advanced Taiwanese power plant with superior fuel efficiency and mission endurance, and possibly a more advanced and lighter miniaturized warhead.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1)

The HF-2E Block I missile uses [inertial guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_guidance "Inertial guidance") with [global positioning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_positioning_system "Global positioning system") (GPS) and [TERCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TERCOM "TERCOM") updates. For [terminal guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_guidance "Terminal guidance"), it uses [infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing "Infrared homing") ( [Imaging infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermographic_camera "Thermographic camera")) with an autonomous digital target recognition system. The IIR terminal seeker is used for target acquisition and to positively identify an optimal aim point. The target image is then compared against digitized files in the memory of the on-board guidance computer ( [DSMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSMAC "DSMAC") terminal guidance). The HF-2E block I missile's cruising speed is high subsonic, typically in the Mach0.75– Mach0.85 (571–647mph; 919–1,041km/h) range. When the missile approaches hostile territory, it would descend to an altitude of about 15–30m (49–98ft). On its final approach to its target, the missile would climb up to avoid any physical barriers and to allow its IIR seeker to acquire the target and identify an optimal aim point before plunging down onto the designated impact point. The report in Taiwan Defense Review, credited the HF-2E block I missile with a pre-terminal accuracy of around 15m.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-4)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1)

The primary difference between the two main variants is the engine. The longer ranged variant has a [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan "Turbofan") engine. The shorter range variant was first tested in 2004 with the longer ranged variant following in 2007 however both were put into mass production.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

## Production and fielding

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hsiung_Feng_IIE&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Production and fielding")\]

The low-rate production of the Block I missile was to have started in July 2005 and using funds originally allocated for its R&D, at least five missiles were built. The unit cost per missile was estimated to be at US$3.08 million (2003 US$ dollar value).[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-5)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1) Another report made in 2006 claimed that three batteries comprising twenty-four mobile launchers and forty-eight missiles were in the final stages of testing and may be fielded within two years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) The HF-2E began low volume production in the Project ChiChun (戟隼, jǐ zhǔn, _lance hawk_). A _[Taipei Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Times "Taipei Times")_ news report claimed that President Ma Ying Jeou ordered the production of 500 to 1000 HF-2E missiles in 2008.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tai1-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-tai1) It was approved and cleared to enter full volume/serial production in 2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) It supposedly can deliver a 440lb (200kg) unitary warhead to a range of 600km (370mi).[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) The [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") has reported a range of 1,500km.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AP_2017-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-AP_2017)

The HF-2E missile is primarily deployed operationally in ground-mobile launchers. The launcher vehicle carries the missiles in protective aluminum box launchers, with wings and control fins retracted, conceptually similar to the trailer-mounted mobile launchers for Tien Kung [Sky Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Bow "Sky Bow") series surface-to-air missiles and [HF-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HF-2 "HF-2") coastal defense missiles. The launchers are normally based in hardened shelters at military installations, with deployment to remote, pre-surveyed launch sites during alert situations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-6)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1) The [SCMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCMP "SCMP") has reported that the HF-IIE is closely watched by the PLA's [Rocket Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Artillery_Corps "Second Artillery Corps").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

In 2022 annual combined production of the HF-2E and Hsiung Sheng was 81 units, the two missiles share a production line.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Taiwan_News_Hsiung_Sheng-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-Taiwan_News_Hsiung_Sheng)

In 2025 it was reported that the navy planned to upgrade the [Kee Lung-class destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kee_Lung-class_destroyer "Kee Lung-class destroyer") with HF-2E missiles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

## Hsiung Sheng

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hsiung_Feng_IIE&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Hsiung Sheng")\]

The improved variant has been designated the **Hsiung Sheng** ( [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters"):雄昇; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters "Simplified Chinese characters"):雄升; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): _Xióng Shēng_). It is designed to be able to strike critical targets in distant Chinese cities like [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan "Wuhan") and [Qingdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao "Qingdao").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Taiwan_News_Hsiung_Sheng-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-Taiwan_News_Hsiung_Sheng)

In January 2021 the [ROCAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_Air_Force "Republic of China Air Force") took delivery of the first batch of improved HF-2E missiles. The range is reported to be in excess of 1,200km however the number of missiles deployed is confidential.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) The Hsiung Sheng carries either a unitary or fragmentation warhead.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Taiwan_News_Hsiung_Sheng-2)[\[12\]](#refnote-Taiwan_News_Hsiung_Sheng) The unitary high explosive warhead is designed to target bunkers and command centers while the fragmentation warhead is designed to target airfields.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

## General characteristics

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hsiung_Feng_IIE&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: General characteristics")\]

- Primary Function: [Land-attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-attack_missile "Land-attack missile") [cruise missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missile "Cruise missile")
- Power Plant: [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan "Turbofan") engine[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-7)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1) with [solid rocket booster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_rocket_booster "Solid rocket booster") for sustained cruise flight
- Range: Alternately reported to be 600km,[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) 1,500km,[&#8202;](#ref-back-AP_2017-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-AP_2017) and 2,000km.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-8)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1)
- Top Speed: Mach0.75– Mach0.85 (571–647mph; 919–1,041km/h)
- Guidance: [Inertial navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system "Inertial navigation system") and commercial [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS "GPS") with in-flight waypoints and corrected by [terrain contour matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain_contour_matching "Terrain contour matching") and forward-looking imaging infrared (IIR) seeker with [automatic target recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_target_recognition "Automatic target recognition") for [terminal guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_guidance "Terminal guidance")[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr1-9)[\[3\]](#refnote-tdr1)
- Launch Weight: 3,000–3,500lb (1,400–1,600kg) including solid rocket booster[&#8202;](#ref-back-tdr2-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-tdr2)
- Accuracy: Within 10m
- Warhead: 200kg (440lb)
- Date deployed: Unknown
