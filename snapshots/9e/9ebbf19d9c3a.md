AMSTERDAM – Profits at Dutch electronics company Philips made a major recovery in 1997 with the company bringing in a net income of 5.7 billion guilders ($2.9 billion), up from a loss of $303 million in the previous year.

Turnover climbed 10% in 1997 to $39.2 billion, with income from operations jumping from $929 million in 1996 to $2.5 billion in 1997. Profit from ordinary business activities shot up to $1.7 billion, from $371 million in 1996.

The remarkable turnaround has been put down to a combination of factors, including a no-nonsense selloff of Philips’ loss-making activities, among them its media interests. Philips still owns a 75% share of Polygram, its only remaining media company.

Sales of Philips 50% share in United & Philips Communications to joint venture partner United Intl. Holdings, and sale of the group’s smart card activities and Teletext U.K. also helped boost the profit picture, as did more favorable exchange rates.

Philips president Cor Boonstra, the man behind the company’s new rough-and-tough, perform-or-get-out strategy is already said to be casting about for a replacement. Boonstra stepped up to his position in October 1996, but warned he wouldn’t be staying long.

Now reports in the Dutch press are circulating that Boonstra is grooming Roel Pieper, former VP of U.S. firm Compaq, for the position.

Philips has declined to confirm the reports.

Pieper stepped down from his post at the world’s biggest producer of personal computers last month after meeting with Boonstra in December and will be elected to the Philips executive board in March. Pieper has a history of putting into play aggressive marketing strategies, an area Boonstra has said needs to be pursued with vigor at Philips if it wants to remain a market leader.
