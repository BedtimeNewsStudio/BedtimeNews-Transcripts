**Clarence William Nelson II** (born September 29, 1942) is an American politician, attorney, and former astronaut who served from 2001 to 2019 as a [United States senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") from Florida and from 2021 to 2025 as the [administrator of the National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_NASA "Administrator of NASA") (NASA). A member of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"), Nelson served from 1979 to 1991 as a [U.S. representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") from Florida's [Space Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Coast "Space Coast"), and from 1972 to 1978 as a member of the [Florida House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_House_of_Representatives "Florida House of Representatives"). In January 1986, he became the second sitting member of Congress to fly in space, after Senator [Jake Garn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Garn "Jake Garn"), when he served as a [payload specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload_specialist "Payload specialist") on mission [STS-61-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-61-C "STS-61-C") aboard the [Space Shuttle Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia "Space Shuttle Columbia"). Before entering politics, he served in the [United States Army Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Reserve "United States Army Reserve") during the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)

Nelson retired from Congress in 1990 to [run for governor of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Florida_gubernatorial_election "1990 Florida gubernatorial election"), but was unsuccessful. He was later elected [Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner, and Fire Marshal of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer,_Insurance_Commissioner,_and_Fire_Marshal_of_Florida "Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner, and Fire Marshal of Florida"), serving from 1995 to 2001. In 2000, Nelson [was elected to the U.S. Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_Senate_election_in_Florida "2000 United States Senate election in Florida") seat that had been vacated by retiring Republican senator [Connie Mack III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connie_Mack_III "Connie Mack III") with 51% of the vote. He was [reelected in 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_United_States_Senate_election_in_Florida "2006 United States Senate election in Florida") with 60% of the vote[&#8202;](#ref-back-2006vote-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2006vote) and [in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_election_in_Florida "2012 United States Senate election in Florida") with 55% of the vote. Nelson [ran in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_Senate_election_in_Florida "2018 United States Senate election in Florida") for a fourth term, but narrowly lost to then-Governor [Rick Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Scott "Rick Scott").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters18-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Reuters18) In May 2019, Nelson was appointed to serve on NASA's advisory council.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASACouncil-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-NASACouncil)

In the U.S. Senate, Nelson was generally considered a centrist and a [moderate Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democrats_(United_States) "New Democrats (United States)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-tampabay-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-tampabay)[&#8202;](#ref-back-orlandoweekly-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-orlandoweekly)[&#8202;](#ref-back-tampabay-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-tampabay)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sentinel-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-sentinel) He supported [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-:5) lowering taxes on lower and middle income families,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-:6) expanding environmental programs and regulation,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-:7) protecting the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act "Affordable Care Act"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-:8) and [expanding Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act#Expanding_Medicaid "Affordable Care Act").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-:9) Nelson chaired the Senate Aging Committee from 2013 to 2015, and served as ranking member of the Senate Commerce Committee from 2015 to 2019.

On March 19, 2021, President [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") announced his intention to nominate Nelson to the position of [Administrator of NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_NASA "Administrator of NASA").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:10-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-:10) On April 29, the Senate confirmed Nelson by unanimous consent. He was sworn in by Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") on May 3. Nelson left [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") on January 20, 2025, at the end of Biden's presidency.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)

## Early life and education

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")\]

Nelson was born on September 29, 1942, in Miami, Florida, the only child of Nannie Merle and Clarence William Nelson.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fourh-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-fourh) His father was a real estate investor and a lawyer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-:12) He is of Scottish, Irish, English, and Danish descent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) His father died of a heart attack when Nelson was 14 and his mother of [ALS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALS "ALS") when he was 24.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)
Nelson grew up in [Melbourne, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne,_Florida "Melbourne, Florida"), where he attended [Melbourne High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_High_School_(Melbourne,_Florida) "Melbourne High School (Melbourne, Florida)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-officialbio-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-officialbio)

Nelson attended [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists "Baptists") and [Episcopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States) "Episcopal Church (United States)") churches, but later was [baptized through immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersion_baptism "Immersion baptism") in a Baptist church. He served as International President of Kiwanis-sponsored [Key Club International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Club "Key Club") (1959–1960).[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) In 2005, he joined the First Presbyterian Church in Orlando.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

Nelson attended the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida "University of Florida"), where he was a member of [Florida Blue Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Blue_Key "Florida Blue Key") and [Beta Theta Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Theta_Pi "Beta Theta Pi") social fraternity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24) He transferred to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") after two years at the University of Florida.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-:12) At Yale he would be roommates with [Bruce Smathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Smathers "Bruce Smathers"), the son of Florida senator [George Smathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Smathers "George Smathers"). Nelson received a Bachelor of Arts with a major in political science from Yale University in 1965 and a [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor "Juris Doctor") from the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia") in 1968.[&#8202;](#ref-back-whorunsgov-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-whorunsgov)

In 1965, during the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), Nelson joined the [United States Army Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Reserve "United States Army Reserve"). He served on active duty from 1968 to 1970, attaining the rank of [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(United_States_O-3)#U.S._Army "Captain (United States O-3)"), and he remained in the Army until 1971. Nelson was admitted to the [Florida Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Florida_Bar "The Florida Bar") in 1968, and began practicing law in Melbourne in 1970. In 1971, he worked as legislative assistant to Governor [Reubin Askew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reubin_Askew "Reubin Askew").[&#8202;](#ref-back-whorunsgov-1)[\[25\]](#refnote-whorunsgov)

## Space Shuttle _Columbia_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Space Shuttle Columbia")\]

In 1986, Nelson became the second sitting member of Congress (and the first member of the House) to travel into space. He went through NASA training with fellow "congressional observer" Senator [Jake Garn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Garn "Jake Garn"), who flew on [STS-51-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-51-D "STS-51-D") in 1985. Nelson served as a [payload specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload_specialist "Payload specialist") on [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") [_Columbia_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia "Space Shuttle Columbia")'s [STS-61-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-61-C "STS-61-C") mission from January 12 to 18, 1986. Coincidentally, STS-61-C's pilot was [Charles Bolden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bolden "Charles Bolden"), who also went on to serve as a NASA administrator. [Gregory Jarvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Jarvis "Gregory Jarvis") was originally scheduled to be on this flight, but he was reassigned to [STS-51-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-51-L "STS-51-L") and replaced by Nelson;[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26) Jarvis, along with the six others on STS-51-L, died in the [Space Shuttle _Challenger_ disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster"), ten days after STS-61-C's mission ended.

In 1988, Nelson published a book about his space flight experience, _Mission: An American Congressman's Voyage to Space_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

## Early political career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Early political career")\]

### Florida Legislature

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Florida Legislature")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Bill_Nelson_in_1972.jpg/250px-Bill_Nelson_in_1972.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bill_Nelson_in_1972.jpg) Nelson in 1972 as a Florida State Representative

In 1972, Nelson was elected to the [Florida House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_House_of_Representatives "Florida House of Representatives") as the member from the 47th district, representing much of [Brevard County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brevard_County,_Florida "Brevard County, Florida") and portions of [Orange County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County,_Florida "Orange County, Florida") and [Seminole County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole_County,_Florida "Seminole County, Florida").[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) He won reelection in 1974 and 1976.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapobio-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-wapobio)

As a representative of [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne,_Florida "Melbourne, Florida"), Nelson was one of the representatives who opposed the demolition of the Old Capitol of Florida.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

### U.S. House of Representatives

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: U.S. House of Representatives")\]

Nelson was [elected to the U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections#Florida "1978 United States House of Representatives elections") in 1978 in the open [9th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida's_9th_congressional_district "Florida's 9th congressional district") after the five-term Republican incumbent, [Louis Frey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Frey "Lou Frey"), chose to run for governor of Florida rather than for reelection.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)

In [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections "1980 United States House of Representatives elections"), Nelson was reelected to that district, which encompassed all of Brevard and part of Orange County. He was [redistricted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistricting "Redistricting") to the [11th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida's_11th_congressional_district "Florida's 11th congressional district"), encompassing all of Brevard and parts of Orange, [Indian River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_River_County,_Florida "Indian River County, Florida"), and [Osceola counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osceola_County,_Florida "Osceola County, Florida"); he [won reelection in 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections "1982 United States House of Representatives elections"), [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections "1984 United States House of Representatives elections"), [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections "1986 United States House of Representatives elections"), and [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections "1988 United States House of Representatives elections"). He remained a member of the U.S. House of Representatives until 1991.

Nelson chaired the [House Space Subcommittee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Science_Subcommittee_on_Space_and_Aeronautics "United States House Science Subcommittee on Space and Aeronautics") for six years[&#8202;](#ref-back-nasa-bill-nelson-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-nasa-bill-nelson) as a key member of the [House Committee on Science, Space and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Science,_Space,_and_Technology "United States House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology").[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapo-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-wapo)[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapo-1)[\[34\]](#refnote-wapo) His district included [Cape Canaveral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral "Cape Canaveral") and its [space facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Space_Force_Station "Cape Canaveral Space Force Station"). In 1988, Bill Nelson criticized President Reagan's policy to export American satellites for launch on China's [Long March rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_(rocket_family) "Long March (rocket family)"). Nelson called this an "inconsistent administration policy." Nelson stated that Reagan "wanted to build up commercial space ventures, and on the other hand, he is cutting off the commercial space ventures at the knees with these export licenses."[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapo-2)[\[35\]](#refnote-wapo)

### 1990 gubernatorial election

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 1990 gubernatorial election")\]

In 1990, Nelson [ran unsuccessfully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Florida_gubernatorial_election "1990 Florida gubernatorial election") for the Democratic nomination for [governor of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Florida "List of governors of Florida"). His primary rival was former U.S. Senator [Lawton Chiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawton_Chiles "Lawton Chiles"). During the campaign, the younger Nelson tried to highlight Chiles' age and use of [Prozac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluoxetine "Fluoxetine") to treat his depression, but this proved to be an unpopular strategy, and Nelson lost by a wide margin, getting 30.5% of the vote to Chiles' 69.5%. Chiles went on to win the general election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapo-3)[\[36\]](#refnote-wapo)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-nyt)

### Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner and Fire Marshal

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner and Fire Marshal")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Governor_Bush_and_the_Cabinet_-_Tallahassee%2C_Florida.jpg/250px-Governor_Bush_and_the_Cabinet_-_Tallahassee%2C_Florida.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Governor_Bush_and_the_Cabinet_-_Tallahassee,_Florida.jpg) Governor [Jeb Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeb_Bush "Jeb Bush") and Nelson in 1999

In 1994, Nelson announced his intention to seek the office of [Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner, and Fire Marshal of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer,_Insurance_Commissioner,_and_Fire_Marshal_of_Florida "Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner, and Fire Marshal of Florida"). He won the election with 52% of the vote over State Rep. Tim Ireland's 48%. In 1998, he won re-election to the office, again defeating Ireland.

In 2000, Nelson announced that he would be running for the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") seat held by retiring Republican [Connie Mack III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connie_Mack_III "Connie Mack III").[&#8202;](#ref-back-election-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-election) Florida's [resign-to-run law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resign-to-run_law "Resign-to-run law") compelled Nelson to submit his resignation as Treasurer, Insurance Commissioner and Fire Marshal early in 2000 when he began to campaign for the U.S. Senate. He chose January 3, 2001, as the effective date of his resignation, as that was the date on which new senators would be sworn in.[&#8202;](#ref-back-election-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-election)

In [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_Senate_elections "2000 United States Senate elections"), Nelson ran as a Democrat for the U.S. Senate seat vacated by retiring Republican senator [Connie Mack III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connie_Mack_III "Connie Mack III"). #### 2006

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: 2006")\]

Main article: [2006 United States Senate election in Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_United_States_Senate_election_in_Florida "2006 United States Senate election in Florida")

Following the [2004 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_elections "2004 United States elections"), in which Republican [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") was re-elected and the Republican Party increased its majority in both the House and the Senate, Nelson was seen as vulnerable. He was a Democrat in a state that Bush had won, though by a margin of only five percentage points.[&#8202;](#ref-back-watimes-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-watimes)

Evangelical Christian activist [James Dobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dobson "James Dobson") declared that Democrats, including Nelson, would be "in the 'bull's-eye'" if they supported efforts to block Bush's judicial nominees.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-nyt) Nelson's refusal to support efforts in Congress to intervene in the [Terri Schiavo case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terri_Schiavo_case "Terri Schiavo case") was seen as "a great political issue" for a Republican opponent to use in mobilizing Christian conservatives against him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapo-4)[\[41\]](#refnote-wapo)

[Katherine Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Harris "Katherine Harris"), the former Florida secretary of state and two-term U.S. representative, defeated three other candidates in the September 5 Republican primary. Harris' role in the [2000 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election "2000 United States presidential election") made her a polarizing figure. Many Florida Republicans were eager to reward her for her perceived party loyalty in the Bush-Gore election, while many Florida Democrats were eager to vote against her for the same reason.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sunsentinel-images-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-sunsentinel-images) In May, when the party found itself unable to recruit a candidate who could defeat Harris in the primary, many Republican activists admitted that the race was already lost.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sptimes-may-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-sptimes-may)

Nelson focused on safe issues, portraying himself as a bipartisan centrist problem-solver.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sunsentinel-images-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-sunsentinel-images)
He obtained the endorsement of all 22 of Florida's daily newspapers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nakedpols-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-nakedpols) Harris failed to secure the endorsement of [Jeb Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeb_Bush "Jeb Bush"), who publicly stated that she could not win; the [United States Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Chamber_of_Commerce "United States Chamber of Commerce"), which had supported her in her House campaigns, did not endorse her in this race.[&#8202;](#ref-back-endorsements-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-endorsements)

As the election approached, polls showed Harris trailing Nelson by 26 to 35 points.[&#8202;](#ref-back-gonesouth-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-gonesouth) Nelson transferred about $16.5 million in campaign funds to other Democratic candidates,[&#8202;](#ref-back-nelsonrolls-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-nelsonrolls) and won the election with 2,890,548 votes to Harris's 1,826,127 votes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-house-stats-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-house-stats) He won 57 out of the state's 67 counties.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

#### 2018

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: 2018")\]

Main article: [2018 United States Senate election in Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_Senate_election_in_Florida "2018 United States Senate election in Florida")

Nelson ran for reelection in 2018. He ran unopposed in the Democratic Party primary on August 28[&#8202;](#ref-back-taldemocrat-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-taldemocrat)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nytimes-fl-election-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-nytimes-fl-election) and faced Florida Governor [Rick Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Scott "Rick Scott") (a Republican) in the general election on November 6. The extremely tight race—with a margin of less than 0.25% separating Nelson and Scott—triggered a manual recount, per state law.[&#8202;](#ref-back-floridatoday-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-floridatoday) The recount showed that Scott had defeated Nelson by 10,033 votes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters18-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-Reuters18)

A paper by scholars at the MIT Election Data and Science Lab concluded that the design of Broward County's 2018 general election ballots may have resulted in Nelson receiving 9,658 fewer votes than he otherwise would have, narrowing Scott's margin of victory but not changing the result. The study found that many voters did not see the U.S. Senate race on the lower left side of the ballot and, as a result, did not vote in that race.[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-54)

## Post-Senate activities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Post-Senate activities")\]

On May 28, 2019, Nelson was appointed to serve on NASA's advisory council. Nelson was a member-at-large of the council, which advises on all major program and policy issues before the agency. [NASA Administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_NASA "Administrator of NASA") [Jim Bridenstine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bridenstine "Jim Bridenstine") praised his appointment, saying, "Nelson is a true champion for human spaceflight and will add tremendous value as we go to the Moon and on to Mars."[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASACouncil-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-NASACouncil)

Nelson endorsed former [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States "Vice President of the United States") [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") for President of the United States in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-55)

## Political positions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Political positions")\]

Nelson was often considered a [moderate Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democrats_(United_States) "New Democrats (United States)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-63) He styled himself as a [centrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism "Centrism") during his various campaigns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tampabay-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-tampabay) During his 2018 reelection campaign, challenger Rick Scott called Nelson a "socialist"; [PolitiFact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PolitiFact "PolitiFact") called the assertion "pants-on-fire" false.[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64) According to ratings by the _[National Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Journal "National Journal")_, Nelson was given a 2013 composite score of 21% conservative and 80% liberal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-:1) In 2011, he was given composite scores of 37% conservative and 64% liberal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[65\]](#refnote-:1)

Nelson has a lifetime conservative rating of nearly 30% from the [American Conservative Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Conservative_Union "American Conservative Union").[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-66) Conversely, the [Americans for Democratic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_for_Democratic_Action "Americans for Democratic Action") gave Nelson a 90% liberal quotient for 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67) In the [115th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/115th_United_States_Congress "115th United States Congress"), Nelson was more conservative than 93% of other congressional Democrats.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-68) [GovTrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GovTrack "GovTrack"), which analyzes a politician's record, places him near the Senate's ideological center and GovTrack placed him among the most moderate senators in 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-69)

The only Florida Democrat in statewide office in 2017, Nelson was described by [Politico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politico "Politico") in March of that year as "a Senate indicator species... an institutional centrist". Politico wrote that the Democratic Party "is shifting left and so is he".[&#8202;](#ref-back-politico-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-politico)

In July 2017, Nelson had a 53% approval rating and 25% disapproval rating, with 22% of survey respondents having no opinion on his job performance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-71) [FiveThirtyEight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FiveThirtyEight "FiveThirtyEight"), which tracks congressional votes, shows that Nelson had voted with President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s positions 42.5% of the time as of June2018[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit).[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-72)

### Economic issues

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Economic issues")\]

#### Trade

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Trade")\]

In 2005, Nelson was among 10 Democrats who voted in favor of the [Dominican Republic–Central America Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic%E2%80%93Central_America_Free_Trade_Agreement "Dominican Republic–Central America Free Trade Agreement") (CAFTA) on its 55–45 passage in the Senate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-73)

#### Tax policy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Tax policy")\]

On several occasions, Nelson voted to reduce or eliminate the [estate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_tax_in_the_United_States "Estate tax in the United States"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-74) notably in June 2006, when he was among four Democrats voting for a failed (57–41) cloture motion on a bill to eliminate the tax.[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-75)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2e/Nelson_Hurricane_Hunter.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nelson_Hurricane_Hunter.jpg) Nelson works with government storm trackers during a hurricane-hunter flight into the center of [Hurricane Charley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Charley "Hurricane Charley") in August 2004[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-76)

Nelson voted against a Republican plan to extend the [Bush tax cuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_tax_cuts "Bush tax cuts") to all taxpayers. Instead, he supported extending the tax cuts for those with incomes below $250,000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Comparison-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-Comparison) Nelson voted for the [Buffett Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffett_Rule "Buffett Rule") in April 2012. Of his support for the Buffett Rule, Nelson said he voted to raise the minimum tax rate on incomes over $1 million per year to 30% to reduce the budget deficit and to make the tax code fairer: "In short, tax fairness for deficit reduction just makes common sense."[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-78)

In 2011, Nelson voted to end Bush-era tax cuts for those earning over $250,000, but voted for $143 billion in tax cuts, unemployment benefits, and other economic measures.[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-79)[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-80)

In 2013, Nelson advocated tax reform, which he defined as "getting rid of special interest tax breaks and corporate subsidies" and gaining "simplicity, fairness, and economic growth".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-:6)

Nelson and [Susan Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Collins_(politician) "Susan Collins (politician)") introduced legislation in 2015 that would "make it easier for smaller businesses to cut administrative costs by forming multiple-employer 401(k)-style plans."[&#8202;](#ref-back-81-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-81)

#### Government spending

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Government spending")\]

Nelson voted for the [American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Recovery_and_Reinvestment_Act_of_2009 "American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009"), often referred to as economic stimulus, proposed by President Obama.[&#8202;](#ref-back-STAUG-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-STAUG) In August 2011, he voted for a bill to increase the [debt limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_limit "Debt limit") by $400 billion. Nelson said that while the bill was not perfect, "this kind of gridlock doesn't do anything." Nelson voted against Congressman [Paul Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ryan "Paul Ryan")'s budget.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Comparison-1)[\[77\]](#refnote-Comparison)

#### Consumer affairs

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Consumer affairs")\]

In May 2013, Nelson asked the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission "Federal Trade Commission") and the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Financial_Protection_Bureau "Consumer Financial Protection Bureau") to investigate why consumers who carried out a real-estate short sale were having their credit scores lowered to the same degree as those who went through [foreclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreclosure "Foreclosure"). Nelson suggested a penalty if the issue was not addressed within 90 days.[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-83)

Nelson was interested in product safety issues and was often engaged in oversight and criticism of the [U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Consumer_Product_Safety_Commission "United States Consumer Product Safety Commission"). He repeatedly opposed Trump's nominee to lead the commission.[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-84)

#### Flood insurance

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Flood insurance")\]

Nelson voted in favor of the Biggert–Waters Flood Insurance Reform Act of 2012, which required the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Flood_Insurance_Program "National Flood Insurance Program") to raise insurance rates for some properties at high risk of flooding to better reflect true flood risk costs and keep the program solvent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-85)[&#8202;](#ref-back-tampabay-3)[\[86\]](#refnote-tampabay) In 2014, after an outcry by Florida property owners facing steep flood insurance-rate hikes,[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-87) Nelson supported legislation that would provide retroactive refunds for taxpayers who had experienced large hikes in their flood-insurance rates due to the sale or purchase of a home. The proposal would also cap average annual premium increases at 15 to 18 percent and allow insurance-rates subsidies based on current flood maps.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-88)

#### Earmarks

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Earmarks")\]

In 2010, PolitiFact found that Nelson had flip-flopped on the issue of [earmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earmark_(politics) "Earmark (politics)"), pushing for a moratorium on the practice after saying that "earmarks were an important part of creating jobs and growing Florida's economy."[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-89)

### Terrorism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Terrorism")\]

In September 2014, Nelson said the U.S. should hit back at the [Islamic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State "Islamic State") (ISIS) immediately, because "the U.S. is the only one that can put together a coalition to stop this group that's intent on barbaric cruelty."[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-90)

He supported the "Denying Firearms and Explosives to Dangerous Terrorists Act." Introduced in 2013 and again in 2015, it would keep guns from people with suspected terrorist links.[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-91)

Standing outside the Orlando [Pulse nightclub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_(nightclub) "Pulse (nightclub)") immediately after the June 2016 [massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_nightclub_shooting "Pulse nightclub shooting") there, Nelson called [Omar Mateen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Mateen "Omar Mateen") a "lone wolf", and when asked if it was an act of [jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihad "Jihad"), said he could not confirm that.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-92) Shortly afterward, citing intelligence sources, Nelson said there was apparently "a link to [Islamic radicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_fundamentalism "Islamic fundamentalism")" and perhaps ISIS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-93)[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-94) He later said on the Senate floor that "terrorists... want to divide people", but that Mateen had instead "brought people together".[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-95) After the massacre, Nelson and [Barbara Mikulski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Mikulski "Barbara Mikulski") supported an increase in FBI funding.[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-96) A year after the massacre, Nelson attended a memorial at which he reiterated that it had "united Orlando and it united the country".[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-97)

Nelson supported the Terrorist Firearms Prevention Act of 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-98)

In August 2017, the _[Miami Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Herald "Miami Herald")_ urged Nelson to back [Lindsey Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsey_Graham "Lindsey Graham")'s [Taylor Force Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Force_Act "Taylor Force Act"), which would block U.S. subsidies to the [Palestinian Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Authority "Palestinian Authority"), which gives monetary assistance to "Palestinian prisoners, former prisoners and families of 'martyrs.'" Nelson did vote for the bill, which passed overwhelmingly.[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-99)

### Health care

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Health care")\]

In March 2010, Nelson voted for the [Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act "Affordable Care Act") (also known as Obamacare)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-:8) and the [Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Care_and_Education_Reconciliation_Act_of_2010 "Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act of 2010"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-100) which passed and were signed into law by President Obama.

In 2014, Nelson called for the expansion of [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid "Medicaid").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-:9)

In 2016, Nelson called the House Zika bill "a disaster", complaining that it would take "$500 million in health care funding away from Puerto Rico" and limit access to "birth control services needed to help curb the spread of the virus and prevent terrible birth defects."[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-101) In 2017, he wrote a letter to the [Center for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention "Centers for Disease Control and Prevention") (CDC) asking it to prioritize [Zika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zika_virus "Zika virus") prevention.[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-102)

In September 2017, Nelson and [Susan Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Collins_(politician) "Susan Collins (politician)") introduced the Reinsurance Act of 2017, an effort "to stabilize the health insurance marketplace". It would provide $2.25 billion to "reduce risk for insurance companies by providing funds to insurers for high-risk enrollees" and "help keep premiums in check".[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-103)

### Immigration

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Immigration")\]

In January 2017, Nelson wrote President Trump a letter protesting his immigration [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13769 "Executive Order 13769"). "Regardless of the constitutionality or legality of this [Executive Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order "Executive order")," he wrote, "I am deeply concerned that it may do more harm than good in our fight to keep America safe." U.S. success in the fight against terrorism, he argued, "depends on the cooperation and assistance of Muslims who reject radicalism and violence. Whether intended or not, this Executive Order risks alienating the very people we rely upon in the fight against terror."[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-104)

### Space exploration and NASA

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Space exploration and NASA")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/President_Obama_Visits_Kennedy_Space_Center.jpg/250px-President_Obama_Visits_Kennedy_Space_Center.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Obama_Visits_Kennedy_Space_Center.jpg) President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") and Nelson visit Kennedy Space Center in April 2010[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Transition_Authorization_Act_Signing.jpg/250px-Transition_Authorization_Act_Signing.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Transition_Authorization_Act_Signing.jpg) Nelson looks on as President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") signs the NASA Transition Authorization Act of 2017

In March 2010, Nelson complained that President Obama had erred in canceling NASA's [Constellation program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation_program "Constellation program").[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-105) He argued against the $6 billion [development of the Commercial Crew Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_the_Commercial_Crew_Program "Development of the Commercial Crew Program") the Obama administration proposed and for a NASA-developed heavy lift rocket built on Constellation's inheritance, which was later included in the [NASA Authorization Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Authorization_Act_of_2010 "NASA Authorization Act of 2010") and became SLS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-106) 11 years later, [Charles Bolden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bolden "Charles Bolden") (NASA administrator in 2010) said that Nelson's skepticism was common in Congress at the time and refused to call him an opponent of commercial crew.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-107)

On July 7, 2011, it was reported that Nelson said Congress "starved" the space program of funding for several years, but suggested that the situation was turning around and called on the Obama administration to push for NASA funding.[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-108) In September 2011, Nelson and Senator [Kay Bailey Hutchison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kay_Bailey_Hutchison "Kay Bailey Hutchison") led the push to continue the development of Constellation's [Ares V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ares_V "Ares V") SLV in the form of [Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System "Space Launch System").[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-109)[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-110)

In 2016, Nelson brokered a bipartisan compromise ending import of Russian [RD-180](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RD-180 "RD-180") rocket engines.[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-111)

In 2017 and 2018, Nelson sought to prevent [Jim Bridenstine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bridenstine "Jim Bridenstine"), Trump's nominee to head NASA, from being confirmed in the Senate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-:0) Although Bridenstine had no formal qualifications in science or engineering, he refuted the [scientific consensus on climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_consensus_on_climate_change "Scientific consensus on climate change").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[112\]](#refnote-:0) Bridenstine was ultimately confirmed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-113-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-113)

During his own confirmation hearing in 2021, Nelson reversed his earlier stances on the Commercial Crew Program and desirability of a NASA administrator without STEM education, and praised Bridenstine (who had endorsed him earlier).[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-114)

In June 2021, Nelson said of the future of [U.S.-Russian cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93United_States_relations "Russia–United States relations") in the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") (ISS): "For decades, upwards now of 45 plus years \[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[we've cooperated with\] Russians in space, and I want that cooperation to continue. Your politics can be hitting heads on Earth, while you are cooperating" in space.[\[115\]](#refnote-115)

### LGBT rights

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: LGBT rights")\]

On December 18, 2010, Nelson voted in favor of the [Don't Ask, Don't Tell Repeal Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Ask,_Don't_Tell_Repeal_Act_of_2010 "Don't Ask, Don't Tell Repeal Act of 2010"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-116-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-116)[&#8202;](#ref-back-117-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-117) which established a legal process for ending the policy that prevented gay and lesbian people from serving openly in the [U.S. Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces").

On April 4, 2013, Nelson announced that he no longer opposed same-sex marriage. He wrote, "The civil rights and responsibilities for one must pertain to all. Thus, to discriminate against one class and not another is wrong for me. Simply put, if The Lord made homosexuals as well as heterosexuals, why should I discriminate against their civil marriage? I shouldn't, and I won't."[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-:5)

### Foreign policy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: Foreign policy")\]

#### Iraq War

#### Iran

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=34 "Edit section: Iran")\]

In July 2017, Nelson voted in favor of the [Countering America's Adversaries Through Sanctions Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countering_America's_Adversaries_Through_Sanctions_Act "Countering America's Adversaries Through Sanctions Act") that placed [sanctions on Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_sanctions_against_Iran "United States sanctions against Iran") together with [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") and [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea").[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-119)

#### Israel

In March 2017, Nelson co-sponsored the [Israel Anti-Boycott Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Anti-Boycott_Act "Israel Anti-Boycott Act"), Senate Bill 720, which permits U.S. states to enact laws that would require contractors to sign a pledge saying that they will not boycott Israeli goods or their contracts will be terminated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-121)

In December 2017, Nelson supported President Trump's decision to [recognize Jerusalem as Israel's capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_recognition_of_Jerusalem_as_capital_of_Israel "United States recognition of Jerusalem as capital of Israel").[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-122)

#### Venezuela

#### Cuba

### Gun control

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=40 "Edit section: Gun control")\]

In 2012, the [National Rifle Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rifle_Association "National Rifle Association") (NRA) gave Nelson an "F" grade for his support of [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_control "Gun control").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NRA1004-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-NRA1004) He advocated new gun control laws, including an [assault weapons ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_weapons_legislation_in_the_United_States "Assault weapons legislation in the United States"), a [ban on magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-capacity_magazine_ban "High-capacity magazine ban") over ten rounds, and a proposal that would require [universal background checks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_background_check "Universal background check") for people buying guns at [gun shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_shows_in_the_United_States "Gun shows in the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-130)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bell1004-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-Bell1004)

In response to the 2016 [Pulse nightclub shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_nightclub_shooting "Pulse nightclub shooting") in [Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida "Orlando, Florida"), Nelson expressed remorse that the Feinstein Amendment, which would have banned the sale of guns to people on the [terrorist watch list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_Screening_Database "Terrorist Screening Database"), and a Republican proposal to update background checks and to create an alert for law enforcement when a person is placed on the terrorist watch list, had failed to pass the Senate. He said: "What am I going to tell the community of Orlando that is trying to come together in the healing? Sadly, what I am going to have to tell them is that the NRA won again."[&#8202;](#ref-back-Griffin1004-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-Griffin1004) Both he and [Marco Rubio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Rubio "Marco Rubio") supported the bills.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Leary1004-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-Leary1004)

In October 2017, after the [Las Vegas shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Las_Vegas_shooting "2017 Las Vegas shooting"), Nelson and [Dianne Feinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Feinstein "Dianne Feinstein") sponsored a bill to ban [bump stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bump_stock "Bump stock") for assault weapons. "I'm a hunter and have owned guns my whole life", he said. "But these automatic weapons are not for hunting, they are for killing."[&#8202;](#ref-back-134-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-134)

Nelson spread misinformation via [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") after the [Parkland high school shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkland_high_school_shooting "Parkland high school shooting") in 2018, falsely claiming that shooter Nikolas Cruz wore a gas mask and tossed smoke grenades as he shot people. After an April 2018 shooting in Liberty City, Nelson claimed that assault weapons had been used in the shooting, when in fact handguns were used.[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-135)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sarasota-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-sarasota)

### Environment

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=42 "Edit section: Environment")\]

Nelson and Mel Martinez co-sponsored a 2006 bill banning oil drilling off Florida's Gulf Coast. In 2017 he said he wanted the ban to continue to 2027, but that it was "vigorously opposed by the oil industry." Along with 16 Florida congress members from both parties, he urged the Trump administration to keep the eastern Gulf of Mexico off limits to oil and gas drilling. "Drilling in this area," they wrote, "threatens Florida's multibillion-dollar tourism-driven economy and is incompatible with the military training and weapons testing that occurs there."[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[11\]](#refnote-:7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-138-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-138)[&#8202;](#ref-back-139-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-139)

In 2011, Nelson co-sponsored the RESTORE Act, which directed money from [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP "BP") fines to states affected by the [_Deepwater Horizon_ oil spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_Horizon_oil_spill "Deepwater Horizon oil spill").[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[140\]](#refnote-140)

On June 27, 2013, Nelson co-sponsored what became the [Harmful Algal Bloom and Hypoxia Research and Control Amendments Act of 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmful_Algal_Bloom_and_Hypoxia_Research_and_Control_Amendments_Act_of_2014 "Harmful Algal Bloom and Hypoxia Research and Control Amendments Act of 2014"), which reauthorized and modified the Harmful Algal Bloom and Hypoxia Research and Control Act of 1998 and [authorized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authorization_bill "Authorization bill") the [appropriation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appropriations_bill_(United_States) "Appropriations bill (United States)") of $20.5 million annually through 2018 for the [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Oceanic_and_Atmospheric_Administration "National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration") (NOAA) to mitigate the harmful effects of [algal blooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_bloom "Algal bloom") and [hypoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxia_(environmental) "Hypoxia (environmental)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-1254cbo-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-1254cbo)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ThisWkHill-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-ThisWkHill)

In 2015, after Gov. [Rick Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Scott "Rick Scott") directed Florida officials to stop using the terms "climate change" and "global warming," Nelson introduced an amendment to prevent federal agencies from censoring official communications on climate change. It "fell to a point of order after a 51-49 vote, though Senator Marco Rubio (R-FL) joined Nelson in supporting the amendment."[&#8202;](#ref-back-143-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-143)[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-144)

### Hurricanes

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=43 "Edit section: Hurricanes")\]

After [Hurricane Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Maria "Hurricane Maria") in 2017, Nelson and Rubio agreed that Trump had taken too long to send the U.S. military to Puerto Rico to take part in relief efforts. "For one week we were slow at the switch," Nelson said in San Juan. "The most efficient organization in a time of disaster is an organization that is already capable of long supply lines in combat. And that's the U.S. military."[&#8202;](#ref-back-145-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-145) After Hurricane Maria led many Puerto Ricans to flee to Florida, Nelson encouraged them to register to vote there.[&#8202;](#ref-back-146-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-146)

Nelson was criticized for sending campaign fundraising emails in the wake of [Hurricane Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma "Hurricane Irma").[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-147)[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-148)[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-149)

### Supreme Court

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=44 "Edit section: Supreme Court")\]

Nelson opposed and [filibustered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filibuster "Filibuster") the nomination of [Neil Gorsuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Gorsuch "Neil Gorsuch") to the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-150)[&#8202;](#ref-back-politico-1)[\[70\]](#refnote-politico)

### Security and surveillance

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=45 "Edit section: Security and surveillance")\]

In 2007, Nelson was the only Democrat on the Senate Intelligence Committee to vote against an amendment to withhold funds for the use by the CIA of [torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_interrogation_techniques "Enhanced interrogation techniques") on terrorism suspects. His vote, combined with those of all Republican members of the committee, killed the measure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-151)

In January 2018, Nelson voted to reauthorize the [Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Intelligence_Surveillance_Act "Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act"), which allows the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency "National Security Agency") to extend a program of warrantless spying on internet and phone networks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-152) In 2015, he had called for a permanent extension of the law.[&#8202;](#ref-back-153-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-153)

## Controversies

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=46 "Edit section: Controversies")\]

### The far side of the Moon

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=47 "Edit section: The far side of the Moon")\]

During a congressional hearing, when queried about China’s lunar ambitions targeting the moon's far side, Nelson mistakenly said, "They are going to have a lander on the far side of the moon, which is the side that is always in dark" (the far side does receive sunlight).[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-cnn) The incident fueled debate amid a burgeoning space race narrative,[&#8202;](#ref-back-mashable-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-mashable) with China’s comprehensive lunar initiatives, including the [Chang'e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program "Chinese Lunar Exploration Program") missions, and the [Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_program "Artemis program") program, both vying to send astronauts back to the moon.

### Campaign donations from Saudi Arabia

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=48 "Edit section: Campaign donations from Saudi Arabia")\]

Nelson received campaign contributions from [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia")'s lobbyists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-voa-0)[\[156\]](#refnote-voa) In June 2017, he voted to support Trump's $110 billion [arms deal with Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_United_States%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_arms_deal "2017 United States–Saudi Arabia arms deal").[&#8202;](#ref-back-thehill-0)[\[157\]](#refnote-thehill) In March 2018, Nelson voted against Bernie Sanders's and Chris Murphy's bill to end U.S. support for the [Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi-led_intervention_in_the_Yemeni_civil_war "Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war").[&#8202;](#ref-back-miaminewtimes-0)[\[158\]](#refnote-miaminewtimes)

### Russian hack claim

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bill_Nelson&action=edit&section=49 "Edit section: Russian hack claim")\]

On August 7, 2018, Nelson claimed that Russian operatives had penetrated some of Florida's election systems ahead of the 2018 midterm elections; the claim was contentious during his 2018 reelection bid.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[159\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[160\]](#refnote-:2) He said that more detailed information was classified.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tampabay-4)[\[161\]](#refnote-tampabay) At the time, fact-checkers did not have evidence to backup Nelson's claims,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[162\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sherman-0)[\[163\]](#refnote-sherman) but later that August, "three people familiar with the intelligence" told NBC News "that there is a classified basis for Nelson's assertion" because "VR Systems had been penetrated in August 2016 by hackers working for" [GRU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRU_(Russian_Federation) "GRU (Russian Federation)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-nbc-0)[\[164\]](#refnote-nbc)

A government official familiar with the intelligence told [The McClatchy Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McClatchy "McClatchy") that Russian hackers had penetrated some of Florida's county voting systems in 2016. [DHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Homeland_Security "United States Department of Homeland Security") spokesperson Sarah Sendek said that the agency has "not seen any new compromises by Russian actors of election infrastructure."[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[160\]](#refnote-:2) _The Tampa Bay Times_ reported that leaders of the Senate Intelligence Committee had told Nelson of a penetration of some of Florida's voter registration databases in 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-2)[\[160\]](#refnote-:2)

Department of Homeland Security Secretary [Kirstjen Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirstjen_Nielsen "Kirstjen Nielsen") and FBI director [Christopher Wray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Wray "Christopher A. Wray") denied Nelson's claims in a letter to Florida election officials.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sentinel-1)[\[165\]](#refnote-sentinel)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sarasota-1)[\[136\]](#refnote-sarasota) Amid the criticism, Nelson defended his assertions, saying that Senate Intelligence Committee leaders [Mark Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Warner "Mark Warner") and [Richard Burr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Burr "Richard Burr") had instructed him and fellow Florida Senator Marco Rubio to warn the Florida secretary of state about Russian interference.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-3)[\[160\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[159\]](#refnote-:3) Warner and Burr neither confirmed nor denied Nelson's claim that Florida's systems had been penetrated, while Rubio took "a line on the controversy similar to Burr and Warner's".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-2)[\[159\]](#refnote-:3) The Foundation for Accountability and Civic Trust, a conservative watchdog group, filed an ethics complaint against Nelson, saying that he "discussed classified information or made it up".[&#8202;](#ref-back-tampabay-5)[\[166\]](#refnote-tampabay)

Special Counsel Robert Mueller's investigation on Russian interference in the 2016 election, which concluded in 2019, found that Russian intelligence officials "sent spearphishing emails to over 120 email accounts used by Florida county officials responsible for administering the 2016 U.S. election" and that "at least one Florida county" was successfully penetrated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sentinel-2)[\[167\]](#refnote-sentinel) In August 2018, federal authorities said they saw no signs of any "new or ongoing compromises" of state or local election systems.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ap-0)[\[168\]](#refnote-ap) In May 2019, Governor [Ron DeSantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_DeSantis "Ron DeSantis") said that voter databases in two counties had been successfully penetrated ahead of the 2016 presidential election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ap-1)[\[169\]](#refnote-ap)
