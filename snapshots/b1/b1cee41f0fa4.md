# Imee Marcos

Imee Marcos

Senator of the Philippines since 2019 (born 1955)

Not to be confused with her adopted sister, [Aimee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aimee_Marcos "Aimee Marcos").

In this [Filipino name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_name "Filipino name") for [married women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wife "Wife"), the birth [middle name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_name "Middle name") or maternal [family name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname "Surname") is _Romualdez_, the birth surname or paternal family name is _Marcos_, and the [marital name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_and_married_names "Maiden and married names") is _Manotoc_.

**Maria Imelda Josefa Remedios** " **Imee**" **Romualdez Marcos-Manotoc**[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) (locally[\[ˈaɪmiˈmaɾkɔs\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Tagalog "Help:IPA/Tagalog"); born November 12, 1955) is a Filipino politician and film producer and film actress serving as a [senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Philippines "Senate of the Philippines") since 2019. She previously served as governor of [Ilocos Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte "Ilocos Norte") from 2010 to 2019 and as the [representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_the_Philippines "House of Representatives of the Philippines") of Ilocos Norte's [2nd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte's_2nd_congressional_district "Ilocos Norte's 2nd congressional district") from 1998 to 2007. She is a daughter of dictator and president [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos") and convicted former first lady [Imelda Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos "Imelda Marcos"), and the older sister of the current president, [Bongbong Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos "Bongbong Marcos").[&#8202;](#ref-back-everydayHistory-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-everydayHistory)

Imee Marcos' political career began during [her father's martial law regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos "Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos"), becoming chairperson of the [Kabataang Barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Kabataan#Predecessors "Sangguniang Kabataan") (KB) Foundation in 1977[&#8202;](#ref-back-VFPahayag-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-VFPahayag) and a member of the [Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa "Batasang Pambansa") in 1984. It was during her KB term that activist [Archimedes Trajano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes_Trajano "Archimedes Trajano") was abducted, tortured, and murdered in 1977 shortly after publicly questioning her appointment to the office.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JamainePunzalan20161122-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-JamainePunzalan20161122) With her interest in media, she produced various film projects such as [Nonoy Marcelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonoy_Marcelo "Nonoy Marcelo")-directed propaganda films: the 1977 documentary _[Da Real Makoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Real_Makoy "Da Real Makoy")_[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) and the 1978 television film _[Tadhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadhana_(film) "Tadhana (film)")_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-:13) the first Philippine animated feature film; she helped establish the [Metro Manila Popular Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila_Popular_Music_Festival "Metro Manila Popular Music Festival") in 1978,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Conference-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-Conference)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Billboard_report_on_5th_contest-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-Billboard_report_on_5th_contest) and was made director general of the [Experimental Cinema of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_Cinema_of_the_Philippines "Experimental Cinema of the Philippines") in 1982. After her family was ousted from power in the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution "People Power Revolution") of 1986, she and her husband [Tommy Manotoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Manotoc "Tommy Manotoc") were exiled in [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco").[&#8202;](#ref-back-JamainePunzalan20161122-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-JamainePunzalan20161122)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

After the death of Ferdinand Marcos in 1989, President [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino "Corazon Aquino") allowed the Marcoses to return to the Philippines in 1991.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JackieDent20121123-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-JackieDent20121123) Imee ran for political office in 1998, and won three terms in the House of Representatives and three terms as governor of Ilocos Norte. She was elected to the Senate in the [2019 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Philippine_general_election "2019 Philippine general election") for a six-year term. She ran for re-election in the 2025 midterm elections, and won placing 12th, securing a second term.[&#8202;](#ref-back-GuardianMay2019-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-GuardianMay2019)[&#8202;](#ref-back-victimcard-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-victimcard)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bulwark-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-bulwark)

Imee Marcos's conviction in the 1993 _[Trajano v. Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajano_v._Marcos "Trajano v. Marcos")_ case (978 F 2d 493) before the U.S. district court in Honolulu is noted in U.S. legal circles for exposing the weaknesses of the [act of state doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_state_doctrine "Act of state doctrine"), allowing for similar suits to be filed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SocialTransformations-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-SocialTransformations)[&#8202;](#ref-back-UnitedStatesCourts-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-UnitedStatesCourts)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Steinhardt1995-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-Steinhardt1995)

She has been linked to the [stolen wealth of her family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolen_wealth_of_the_Marcos_family "Stolen wealth of the Marcos family"), identified as a beneficiary of various Marcos offshore holdings as revealed in the [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers "Panama Papers")[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) and the findings in the court convictions of her mother Imelda Marcos.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) These holdings were defined as "ill-gotten wealth" by the [Supreme Court of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines "Supreme Court of the Philippines"), and are the subject of repatriation efforts by the [Presidential Commission on Good Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Commission_on_Good_Government "Presidential Commission on Good Government").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Davies20160507-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-Davies20160507)[&#8202;](#ref-back-PCGG30-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-PCGG30) Amidst the growing rift between the Marcos and Duterte families during her brother Bongbong's administration, she has frequently sided with the Dutertes and their allies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28)[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)

## Early life

Imee Marcos was born Maria Imelda Josefa Remedios Romualdez Marcos on the morning of November 12, 1955 at Our Lady of Lourdes Hospital in [Mandaluyong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaluyong "Mandaluyong") (then a municipality in [Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_(province) "Rizal (province)"))[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30) to [Imelda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos "Imelda Marcos") and [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos").[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeCVHoR-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-imeeCVHoR) She has three other siblings: [Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos "Bongbong Marcos"), the current [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Philippines "President of the Philippines");[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32) [Irene Marcos-Araneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Marcos-Araneta "Irene Marcos-Araneta");[&#8202;](#ref-back-op-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-op) and [Aimee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aimee_Marcos "Aimee Marcos"), who was adopted.

She turned ten years old the day afterwards her father was elected in 1965. In an interview with her family-backed _Filipinas Magazine_ in 1999, she admitted that she was uncomfortable living in the palace because it was too confining, very formal, and fixed. She also added that it is "not necessarily the most appropriate place to bring up a kid but it was quite nice".[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34)

While residing at the palace, Marcos asserted that she attended 'regular' schools in Manila. However, she had to discontinue her education due to the challenges faced by the First Family in going out caused by protest rallies outside Malacañang. These rallies, met with military assaults resulting in numerous Filipino deaths, were a response to her family's enduring [conjugal dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugal_dictatorship "Conjugal dictatorship") that spanned over two decades.[&#8202;](#ref-back-special-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-special)

## Education

During the [2019 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Philippine_general_election "2019 Philippine general election"), Marcos's educational background has been steeped in controversy. Under her parents' conjugal dictatorship, Marcos graduated from at least four schools (including three universities), she graduated as " _[Cum Laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors "Latin honors")_" and "class valedictorian" in two public occasions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-auto)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rappler.com-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-rappler.com)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-auto1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-news.abs-cbn.com-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-news.abs-cbn.com)

### Primary and secondary education

- Marcos attended the _Institucion Teresiana_ (now [Saint Pedro Poveda College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pedro_Poveda_College "Saint Pedro Poveda College")) in Quezon City from Kindergarten through Grade 4 where she earned first honors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeCVHoR-1)[\[31\]](#refnote-imeeCVHoR)
- She transferred to the [Convent of Our Lady of Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_College_San_Lorenzo "Assumption College San Lorenzo") at [Herran Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Gil_Street "Pedro Gil Street") in Manila for Grade 5 to first year high school, where she also earned first honors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeCVHoR-2)[\[31\]](#refnote-imeeCVHoR)
- Marcos later transferred to the "American School" (now [International School Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_Manila "International School Manila")) in Makati.[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeCVHoR-3)[\[31\]](#refnote-imeeCVHoR)

#### Falsification of Santa Catalina high school graduation

Marcos claimed that she graduated as class valedictorian from Santa Catalina Convent (now called [Santa Catalina School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Catalina_School "Santa Catalina School")) in [Monterey, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey,_California "Monterey, California").[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-auto1)

On 21 March 2019, the assistant head of the Santa Catalina school, John Aimé, stated the following: "'(Imee Marcos) attended our school for a brief period in the fall of 1972 — she is _not_ a graduate".[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-2)[\[38\]](#refnote-auto1)

### Undergraduate education

In 1973, Imee Marcos enrolled at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), where she took a variety of courses in religion and politics though she did not declare an [academic major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_major "Academic major").[&#8202;](#ref-back-AsburyParkPress-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-AsburyParkPress)

Marcos's stay at Princeton was marred with controversy with black and Asian students (Asian-American Students Association - AASA) protesting her admission for allowing the daughter of a dictator to study at the university and as a potential threat to students who opposed the Marcos regime.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AsburyParkPress-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-AsburyParkPress)[&#8202;](#ref-back-princetonianFalseClaimGraduation-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-princetonianFalseClaimGraduation)

- She withdrew from Princeton in 1976, returned in 1977, and then withdrew for the last time in 1979.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EsquireImeeGuns-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-EsquireImeeGuns)[&#8202;](#ref-back-JelineMalasig20181023-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-JelineMalasig20181023) She did not receive any degree from Princeton University.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AsburyParkPress-2)[\[40\]](#refnote-AsburyParkPress)[&#8202;](#ref-back-EsquireImeeGuns-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-EsquireImeeGuns)[&#8202;](#ref-back-JelineMalasig20181023-1)[\[43\]](#refnote-JelineMalasig20181023)[&#8202;](#ref-back-falseprincetondegree-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-falseprincetondegree)
- According to a Princeton alumnus Richard Klein in the August 1983 issue of _[Town Topics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Topics_(newspaper) "Town Topics (newspaper)")_, Marcos had flunked out.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dearBrookeTownTopics-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-dearBrookeTownTopics)

In the book _Some Are Smarter Than Others_, author Ricardo Manapat reveals that after the [EDSA revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_revolution "EDSA revolution"), investigators from the [Presidential Commission on Good Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Commission_on_Good_Government "Presidential Commission on Good Government") found out that Marcos's tuition, [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") 10,000 monthly allowance, and the 18th-century estate she stayed in while studying at Princeton was paid for using taxpayer money that could be traced partly to the intelligence funds of the Office of the President, and partly to some of the 15 bank accounts that the Marcoses had secretly opened in the US under assumed names.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Manapat1991-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-Manapat1991)

#### Falsified claims of graduation from Princeton University

Imee Marcos's time as a student at Princeton became a public issue once again in 2018, when she filed her candidacy for the Philippine Senate in the 2019 elections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EsquireImeeGuns-2)[\[42\]](#refnote-EsquireImeeGuns)

Marcos claimed in numerous venues, including a campaign leaflet and her official website, that she had graduated from Princeton. This resulted in social media uproar which brought up old news articles to show that Imee Marcos had not.[&#8202;](#ref-back-falseprincetondegree-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-falseprincetondegree)

On her campaign website, Marcos uploaded an official biography that claimed that she was "one of the first female graduates from an [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League "Ivy League") School—Princeton University, graduating with honors,"[&#8202;](#ref-back-IRMCVPDF-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-IRMCVPDF) but this claim was quickly disproved by news reports and on social media.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EsquireImeeGuns-3)[\[42\]](#refnote-EsquireImeeGuns)[&#8202;](#ref-back-JelineMalasig20181023-2)[\[43\]](#refnote-JelineMalasig20181023) In addition, she had stated in her curriculum vitae during her stay in the House of Representatives that she had graduated with honors from Princeton with an "Independent Major in Religion and Politics".[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeCVHoR-4)[\[31\]](#refnote-imeeCVHoR)

In a later interview with news anchor [Tina Marasigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Marasigan "Tina Marasigan") on [DZMM TeleRadyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DZMM_TeleRadyo "DZMM TeleRadyo"), she was asked whether she really graduated from Princeton or not, and whether her status as a Princeton graduate could be proven. She evaded the question and answered:[&#8202;](#ref-back-princetonDZMM-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-princetonDZMM)

> Yes, correct: it really is the season -- it's election season even though it hasn't been declared by COMELEC. This was also done to Bongbong and to others we know. My record speaks for itself. I think, performance-wise, we can see that we know what we are doing and we were able to help many. Most of all, we have many solutions that can help the multitude. I believe that this is the first generation in which we can really end hunger and poverty. The science is already there for agriculture, the medicine is also there so that no one will die from infections and other diseases. So we really have no excuse. There is also [big data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_data "Big data") and technology so we can monitor each and every poor person in the Philippines. Let us end poverty in this decade.[&#8202;](#ref-back-princetonDZMMOrigFilipinoQuote-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-princetonDZMMOrigFilipinoQuote)

The [ABS-CBN News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN_News "ABS-CBN News") network noted that the video went viral as Marcos opted not to answer the question.
[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48) This was seconded by [InterAksyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterAksyon "InterAksyon"), pointing out that Marcos "avoided answering the question and instead diverted the topic to the scholastic records of her brother Bongbong Marcos."[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49)

- In January 2019, the deputy spokesperson of Princeton university, Michael Hotchkiss, stated that their university records "do not show that Ms Marcos was awarded a degree."[&#8202;](#ref-back-falseprincetondegree-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-falseprincetondegree)
- On 26 February 2019, the student newspaper of Princeton, _[The Daily Princetonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Princetonian "The Daily Princetonian")_, reported that Marcos falsely claims that she graduated from the university. In an e-mail message to _The Daily Princetonian_, Hotchkiss repeated the statement: "Our records do not show that Ms. Marcos was awarded a degree."[&#8202;](#ref-back-princetonianFalseClaimGraduation-1)[\[41\]](#refnote-princetonianFalseClaimGraduation)

### Time at the University of the Philippines, College of Law

After her stay at Princeton, she then enrolled at the [University of the Philippines College of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_College_of_Law "University of the Philippines College of Law") on 1979 as a day student, originally part of batch 1983.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EsquireImeeGuns-4)[\[42\]](#refnote-EsquireImeeGuns) Accordingly, her admission was controversial because the student body protested the fact that she had failed to meet the normal requirement of having a college degree, while they had to go through a stringent admissions process.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tasteFakeNews-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-tasteFakeNews)

- According to [University of the Philippines Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UP_Cebu "UP Cebu"), Professor Madrileña de la Cerna, Marcos did not take 35 units worth of courses at the College of Law by the time she was supposed to graduate. Despite the missing units, the college faced political pressure to allow her to graduate, but the faculty, led by Ms. [Haydee Yorac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haydee_Yorac "Haydee Yorac"), refused to approve her graduation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tasteFakeNews-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-tasteFakeNews)

#### Staged ceremony, mock graduation and honorary claims

A few days after the University of the Philippines held its recognition ceremonies for 1983, a televised recognition ceremony held at the [Meralco Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meralco_Theater "Meralco Theater") showed Imee Marcos graduating, and being honored _[magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude "Magna cum laude")_ even though she had not actually graduated. The ceremony was pushed by her parents, [Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos") and [Imelda Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos "Imelda Marcos"), who ruled the Philippines under their conjugal dictatorship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tasteFakeNews-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-tasteFakeNews)[&#8202;](#ref-back-EsquireImeeGuns-5)[\[42\]](#refnote-EsquireImeeGuns)

In the book _Some Are Smarter Than Others_ published in 1991 and written by Ricardo Manapat, the author detailed, "It appeared that Imee never had the proper qualification to enter the school and that her name never appeared in the list of approved graduates nor among the candidates endorsed for graduation by a committee."[&#8202;](#ref-back-rappler.com-1)[\[37\]](#refnote-rappler.com)

Marcos claimed in her curriculum vitae during her time at the House of Representatives that she graduated with a bachelor's degree from the UP College of Law. She also claimed that she graduated _magna cum laude_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeCVHoR-5)[\[31\]](#refnote-imeeCVHoR)

- The Executive Vice President of University of the Philippines, [Teodoro Herbosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teodoro_Herbosa "Teodoro Herbosa"), declared: "There is no record of her graduation from UP nor any honors or academic distinctions received with the University Registrar's office".[&#8202;](#ref-back-falseImeeMarcosCumLaudeULaw-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-falseImeeMarcosCumLaudeULaw)

A viral Facebook post later circulated in the social platform, claiming that Imee graduated from the University of the Philippines in 1983 as class valedictorian. The post included an alleged yearbook, which had Marcos in it.[&#8202;](#ref-back-noRecordYearbookEntryImee-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-noRecordYearbookEntryImee) The yearbook's authenticity was later proven to be false, as Marcos did not appear in any authenticated UP yearbook from 1983.[&#8202;](#ref-back-noRecordYearbookEntryImee-1)[\[51\]](#refnote-noRecordYearbookEntryImee) Coincidentally, Marcos's eldest son Borgy was also born in 1983, making it impossible for Marcos to graduate from UP Law on time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-viralUPLawYearbookNot1983-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-viralUPLawYearbookNot1983)

According to former UP Law dean Froilan Bacungan in the book _The Turning Point: Twenty-six accounts of February events in the Philippines_:[&#8202;](#ref-back-marcosPRStuntUPLawDean-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-marcosPRStuntUPLawDean)

> "There was indeed some kind of a ceremony held which looked as if she graduated. I was there. It was a little bit PR that, strictly speaking, we should not have participated in. It borders in fact on a little bit of misrepresentation. No less than the Chief Justice of the Supreme Court graced the occasion...
>
> ...I allowed her to enter the College of Law in spite of the fact that she couldn't present a certificate proving that she had a bachelor's degree which was the basic requirement...
>
> Imee had sounded very confident that she would submit the certificate. Maybe she was not telling the truth --- how can I know? Four years afterwards, when she could be considered for graduation, we discovered that she never submitted her \[&#8202;](#ref-back-marcosPRStuntUPLawDean-2)[undergraduate\] diploma. She was not given a bachelor of law degree and that meant she could not take the bar examination".[\[4\]](#refnote-marcosPRStuntUPLawDean)

### Falsification of Master in Management degree

The [Asian Institute of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Institute_of_Management "Asian Institute of Management") refused to provide any public information on whether Marcos was enrolled or had graduated from their school, as Marcos has claimed through her government resume that she also earned a "MA Management and Business Administration (MA MBA)" from the school.

- On 22 March 2019, the Registrar and Student Enterprise Director of the school, Bryan Magbutay, officially declared the following: "In our fifty years of providing world class programs, we _never_ offered an MA—MBA".[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeMarcosAIMDegreeTrueFalse-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-imeeMarcosAIMDegreeTrueFalse)

## Career under the Marcos administration

### Declaration of martial law

Marcos and her siblings ( [Ferdinand Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos "Bongbong Marcos") and [Irene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Marcos "Irene Marcos")) were studying overseas before Marcos signed [Proclamation No. 1081](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._1081 "Proclamation No. 1081") on September 21, 1972. The siblings stayed there until they ended their studies, but would come home for Christmas and summer holidays[&#8202;](#ref-back-special-1)[\[35\]](#refnote-special) She turned 18 - the Philippines' age of majority, fourteen months after the declaration of Martial Law,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Vernise-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-Vernise) and played various political roles in the Martial Law administration - as chairperson of the Kabataang Barangay and as assemblyman to the [Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa "Batasang Pambansa"). She was already 30 when the People Power revolution finally deposed the Marcos administration in 1986.

#### Kabataang Barangay Foundation

After returning from Princeton, Marcos entered the world of politics as chairperson of the Kabataang Barangay Foundation in September 1977.[&#8202;](#ref-back-special-2)[\[35\]](#refnote-special)[&#8202;](#ref-back-VFPahayag-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-VFPahayag) On the same day she became chairperson, student [Archimedes Trajano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes_Trajano "Archimedes Trajano") of [Mapúa Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map%C3%BAa_University "Mapúa University") was found brutally murdered after questioning her appointment at his school's open forum the day prior. She would later be convicted on the torture and killing of Trajano in later life.[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-57)

#### Assemblyman for Ilocos Norte to the Batasang Pambansa

On June 30, 1984, she won a seat as one of two assemblyman to the [Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa "Batasang Pambansa") for Ilocos Norte (the other assemblyman was [Antonio V. Raquiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Raquiza "Antonio Raquiza")) under the wing of her father's [dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship "Dictatorship"). She formally held this role until the Batasang Pambansa was dissolved in the aftermath of the [1986 EDSA Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_EDSA_Revolution "1986 EDSA Revolution"), which ousted their family from power.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-bio)

#### National Media Production Center and Experimental Cinema of the Philippines

From 1979 to 1986, she was consultant to the minister of the [National Media Production Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Media_Production_Center "National Media Production Center") in Quezon City. From 1982 to 1985, she was director general of the [Experimental Cinema of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_Cinema_of_the_Philippines "Experimental Cinema of the Philippines") (ECP) after recommendation from her parents, who were controlling the country through a [conjugal dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugal_dictatorship "Conjugal dictatorship").[&#8202;](#ref-back-isite-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-isite) She served as co-producer of the films _The Boatman_, _Brutal_, and _Scorpio Nights_, but was not listed as producer or co-producer of other ECP films.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JelineMalasig20181102-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-JelineMalasig20181102)

In a 2002 interview with Philippine cinema academician [Joel David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_David "Joel David"), Marcos confirmed that she had "half-jokingly" named the " _Experimental Cinema of the Philippines_" as a reference to [Centro Sperimentale di Cinematografia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_Sperimentale_di_Cinematografia "Centro Sperimentale di Cinematografia"), the institution founded during the dictatorship of [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini") \- a reference to a cinema institution put up under a prior dictatorship which Professor David surmised to also be a tease aimed at Imee's parents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-61)

As the mid-1980s approached, the collapse of the Philippine economy forced government to reduce the budget of the ECP, and to raise funds, it began screening what critics deemed skin flicks, which were called "Bomba" or "Bold" films in the local slang.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JojoDevera2012-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-JojoDevera2012)\[ _[self-published source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Self-published_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] At around this time, Imee Marcos promoted Johnny Litton, who had been deputy director general to chief executive officer of the ECP under her, and Litton's decision to screen extremely explicit films such as Scorpio Nights (Regal Films), Company Of Women (Athena Productions, Inc.) and Hubo (lit. " _Naked_" FLT Films International) were scored by industry critics. By 1985 ECP was producing "about twenty sexually explicit quickies" which they hoped to screen at the three screening rooms of the National Film Center. The films were assured of a neat profit despite the few screening venues.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JojoDevera2012-1)[\[62\]](#refnote-JojoDevera2012)\[ _[self-published source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Self-published_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\]

#### Unexplained Marcos wealth

See also: [Unexplained wealth of the Marcos family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unexplained_wealth_of_the_Marcos_family "Unexplained wealth of the Marcos family")

The Marcoses got much criticism during the last part of Ferdinand Marcos's reign because of their conspicuous spending,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Manapat1991-1)[\[44\]](#refnote-Manapat1991)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Seagrave,_Sterling_1988-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-Seagrave,_Sterling_1988) which was far beyond their legal means, as expressed in the Ferdinand and Imelda Marcos's legally-required Statements of Assets Liabilities and Net Worth (SALN).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Robles2016-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-Robles2016)[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-65)

Some of the specific things Imee Marcos enjoyed were her own " [Marcos Mansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Mansions "Marcos Mansions")" in Baguio and in the Metro Manila area,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Manapat1991-2)[\[44\]](#refnote-Manapat1991) and her own purchases of Marcos jewels just like her mother.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Manapat1991-3)[\[44\]](#refnote-Manapat1991)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Robles2016-1)[\[64\]](#refnote-Robles2016)

In one instance recounted by British journalist Caroline Kennedy, Imee Marcos effectively used [Philippine Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Airlines "Philippine Airlines") flights as a courier for breastmilk when she was traveling in Europe but had left her child in the Philippines. According to Kennedy, Imee had explained that she "expressed her milk every day and then Daddy sent a Philippine Airlines plane to wherever she was and it would bring the milk back."[&#8202;](#ref-back-Manapat1991-4)[\[44\]](#refnote-Manapat1991)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Robles2016-2)[\[64\]](#refnote-Robles2016) Kennedy noted that this coincided with a time when many Europe-based Philippine expats complained of a lot of flight delays and cancellations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Robles2016-3)[\[64\]](#refnote-Robles2016)

All this was aside from the uber-VIP treatment that the Marcoses regularly received. For example, when Imee Marcos enrolled in the University of the Philippines, the university broke its own policy of not having airconditioned classrooms and made sure the lecture halls Imee had classes in were equipped with AC.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Seagrave,_Sterling_1988-1)[\[63\]](#refnote-Seagrave,_Sterling_1988)

### Other roles before the 1986 revolution

#### Other media work

From 1975 to 1986, Marcos produced the television shows _Kulit Bulilit_ and _Kaluskos Musmos_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio-1)[\[58\]](#refnote-bio) She was also a consultant/writer of the [Children's Television Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Workshop "Sesame Workshop") for Asia and New York (1977–1979).[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio-2)[\[58\]](#refnote-bio) She also collaborated as a producer with cartoonists [José Zabala-Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Zabala-Santos "José Zabala-Santos") and his nephew [Nonoy Marcelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonoy_Marcelo "Nonoy Marcelo") to work for the first-ever Philippine full-length animated film titled _[Tadhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadhana_(film) "Tadhana (film)")_ ("Destiny"), which originally conceived as a [television pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_pilot "Television pilot"), premiered on Philippine television during the anniversary of [Martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law "Martial law") in 1978.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-:12)

Marcos was once a columnist of _[Manila Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bulletin "Manila Bulletin")_ (then known as _Bulletin Today_) in Manila, publisher of the Filipino Film Review, publisher and editor of the Kabataang Barangay Foundation, Makati, Metro Manila, and special consultant to the chairperson of the board of [BBC-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banahaw_Broadcasting_Corporation "Banahaw Broadcasting Corporation"), [RPN 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Philippines_Network "Radio Philippines Network"), and [IBC 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Broadcasting_Corporation "Intercontinental Broadcasting Corporation").[&#8202;](#ref-back-isite-1)[\[59\]](#refnote-isite) She was also the producer of _Metro_ Magazine (1975–1986).[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio-3)[\[58\]](#refnote-bio)

## Torture and murder of Archimedes Trajano

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Imee_and_Ferdinand_Marcos_during_the_latter%27s_21_year_reign_as_Philippine_president.jpg/500px-Imee_and_Ferdinand_Marcos_during_the_latter%27s_21_year_reign_as_Philippine_president.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Imee_and_Ferdinand_Marcos_during_the_latter's_21_year_reign_as_Philippine_president.jpg) Imee Marcos being supported by her father, [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos").

The torture and killing of [Archimedes Trajano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes_Trajano "Archimedes Trajano") was attributed to Imee Marcos, then the National Chairman of the Kabataang Barangay. "On August 31, 1977, Archimedes Trajano, a 21-year-old student of Mapua Institute of Technology, attended an open forum with Imee Marcos, 21-year-old daughter of the dictator. Her father had appointed her National Chairman of the Kabataang Barangay youth organization. When Trajano questioned her about her appointment, Imee apparently became irritated. Her guards seized Trajano and dragged him away. His body was found hours later, and it has severe bruises, wounds and lifeless: he had been severely tortured and beaten to death."[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeUSCourtTrajano-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-imeeUSCourtTrajano)

Nine years after the killing of Archimedes Trajano, his mother, Agapita Trajano, pressed charges against Imee Marcos and her accomplices for "false imprisonment, kidnapping, wrongful death, and a deprivation of rights" of her son. Marcos and her lawyers did not deny that Trajano was tortured—instead, they argued that as agents of the state, the soldiers who killed Trajano were immune from suit in a foreign state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-whyWeShouldWorry-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-whyWeShouldWorry) The Honolulu district court awarded $2.5 million in punitive damages, $1.25 million for mental anguish to Agapita Trajano and $246,966 in attorney's fees and costs against Imee Marcos for the murder of Archimedes Trajano, by Marcos's personal bodyguards.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-69) Imee Marcos responded by saying, " _Yes, Archimedes Trajano was tortured and killed but it's none of your business._"[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-70)

## 1986 ouster and life in exile

Main articles: [Assassination of Ninoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ninoy_Aquino "Assassination of Ninoy Aquino") and [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution "People Power Revolution")

Increasing unrest springing from the economic collapse of the Philippines in the years after the assassination of [Senator Benigno Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino "Ninoy Aquino") in 1983 came to a head in February 1986, when the EDSA Revolution succeeded in unseating the Marcoses from Malacañang palace.[&#8202;](#ref-back-StuartSantiago1995-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-StuartSantiago1995)

Fearful of a scenario in which Marcos's presence in the Philippines would lead to a civil war,[&#8202;](#ref-back-StuartSantiago1995-1)[\[71\]](#refnote-StuartSantiago1995) the Reagan administration flew Marcos and a party of about 80 individuals[&#8202;](#ref-back-DavidHolley19860228-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-DavidHolley19860228) \- the extended Marcos family and a number of close associates[&#8202;](#ref-back-APmanifest19860311-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-APmanifest19860311) \- from the Philippines to Hawaii despite Marcos's objections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-StuartSantiago1995-2)[\[71\]](#refnote-StuartSantiago1995) Imee and her family were on the flight with her parents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DavidHolley19860227-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-DavidHolley19860227)

The exiles stayed at [Hickam Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickam_Air_Force_Base "Hickam Air Force Base") at the expense of the US Government. A month later, they moved into a pair of residences in [Makiki Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makiki_Heights "Makiki Heights"), [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu "Honolulu"), which were registered to [Antonio Floirendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Floirendo "Antonio Floirendo") and Bienvenido and Gliceria Tantoco.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DavidHolley19860228-1)[\[72\]](#refnote-DavidHolley19860228)

Marcos would eventually die in exile in 1989.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Richburg&Branigin19890929-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-Richburg&Branigin19890929)

President Corazon Aquino eventually allowed the Marcoses, including Imee, to return to the Philippines in order to face various charges.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SethMydans19911104-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-SethMydans19911104) News reports from the period record that Marcos supporters organized a crowd from Manila's slums to welcome the Marcoses on their return.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SethMydans19911104-1)[\[76\]](#refnote-SethMydans19911104)

## Post-exile career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8e/Imee_Marcos_-_August_2013.jpg/500px-Imee_Marcos_-_August_2013.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Imee_Marcos_-_August_2013.jpg) Imee Marcos in August 2013

### Return to politics

Further information: [The Kingmaker (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kingmaker_(film) "The Kingmaker (film)")

Twelve years after her family's exile, Marcos returned to politics. She ran as Congresswoman of the [2nd District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte's_2nd_congressional_district "Ilocos Norte's 2nd congressional district") of [Ilocos Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte "Ilocos Norte") and won. She assumed office on June 30, 1998. She was then reelected in 2001 and 2004. Her term ended on June 30, 2007.

In the 2010 elections, she ran as governor of Ilocos Norte against her cousin, [Michael Marcos Keon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Marcos_Keon "Michael Marcos Keon"), who was the governor during that time. She defeated her cousin in the elections. Imee had 196,160 votes while Keon had 86,005 votes. She assumed office on June 30, 2010. She was re-elected in 2013, unopposed. She was reelected in the 2016 elections, securing a third consecutive and final term.

During the third Sulong Pilipinas Convention in 2016, President Rodrigo Duterte named Imee as one of his campaign contributors, saying that she borrowed money to fund his campaign. However, Duterte's Statement of Contributions and Expenditures (SOCE) does not include her as a donor. Imee denied the claim saying that she just helped him get votes from the Ilocos Region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-77)[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-78)

On January 25, 2019, Marcos tweeted that [Ilocos Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte "Ilocos Norte")'s poverty incidence rate lessened under her governorship; however, the very same tweet showed poverty incidence rate actually increased during her rule. Poverty _reduction_ rate was 17% in 2006, while it dropped to 5% in 2015.[&#8202;](#ref-back-pinoyakoblog.com-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-pinoyakoblog.com)\[ _[self-published source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Self-published_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] The tweet was afterwards deleted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-abogado.com.ph-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-abogado.com.ph)

### Establishment of the Marcos political dynasty

Main article: [Marcos dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_dynasty "Marcos dynasty")

Imee Marcos's entrance into politics, beginning with her term as congresswoman of the 2nd District of [Ilocos Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte "Ilocos Norte") in 1998, saw her taking over the position previously held by her brother, [Bongbong Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos "Bongbong Marcos"), who became governor of Ilocos Norte that same year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AFP20161109-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-AFP20161109) In the context of their mother [Imelda Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos "Imelda Marcos")'s similar return to politics as congresswoman in Leyte in 1995,[&#8202;](#ref-back-AFP20161109-1)[\[81\]](#refnote-AFP20161109) journalists and academics noted that the Marcoses had cemented a political dynasty after their return from exile,[&#8202;](#ref-back-SydneyMorningHerald20121124-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-SydneyMorningHerald20121124)[&#8202;](#ref-back-AFP20161109-2)[\[81\]](#refnote-AFP20161109) despite the explicit anti-dynasty provision in Article II Section 26 of the [1987 Constitution of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Constitution_of_the_Philippines "1987 Constitution of the Philippines").[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-83)

Saying that this was a common occurrence because of the way Philippine society is structured, Imee Marcos asserted in a November 2012 interview with the _[Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Morning_Herald "Sydney Morning Herald")_[&#8202;](#ref-back-SydneyMorningHerald20121124-1)[\[82\]](#refnote-SydneyMorningHerald20121124) that:

> "It's pretty feudal in the Philippines still, even though we like to fool ourselves."[&#8202;](#ref-back-SydneyMorningHerald20121124-2)[\[82\]](#refnote-SydneyMorningHerald20121124)

Political scientist Ramon Casiple, in an interview with the _[South China Morning Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Morning_Post "South China Morning Post")_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-PurpleChrystylRomero20170924-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-PurpleChrystylRomero20170924) noted:

### Unexplained wealth

Imee Marcos was named in the Offshore Leaks – [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers "Panama Papers"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-85) along with her three sons, Fernando Martin, Matthew Joseph, Ferdinand Richard Michael,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-:6) her sister [Irene Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Marcos "Irene Marcos"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-Offshore_Leaks-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-Offshore_Leaks)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ICIJ-IM-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-ICIJ-IM)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-:7) her brother-in-law Gregorio Maria Araneta III,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[90\]](#refnote-:7) and her estranged husband Tommy Manotoc's relatives Ricardo Gabriel Kalaw Manotoc and Teodoro Kalaw Manotoc.[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-91)[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-92) According to records uncovered by the [International Consortium of Investigative Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Consortium_of_Investigative_Journalists "International Consortium of Investigative Journalists"), Imee and her three sons are beneficiaries of the Sintra Trust, which was formed in June 2002 in the [British Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Virgin_Islands "British Virgin Islands"). Other documents, the latest dated 2010, also name Imee as a financial adviser for the Sintra Trust as well as ComCentre Corporation, which was formed in January 2002 and is still in operation. She is also identified as a "master client" for the M Trust, which was formed in July 1997 and closed July 2009. However, these three offshore accounts do not appear in Imee's [Statement of Assets, Liabilities and Net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_of_Assets,_Liabilities_and_Net_worth "Statement of Assets, Liabilities and Net worth") (SALN).[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-93)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-1)[\[86\]](#refnote-:6) The SALN is required of all public officials and employees, and should include all of the officials' assets and liabilities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-94)

### Misuse of tobacco funds

Main article: [Ilocos Norte Tobacco Excise Tax funds controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte_Tobacco_Excise_Tax_funds_controversy "Ilocos Norte Tobacco Excise Tax funds controversy")

On March 2, 2017, House Majority Leader [Rodolfo Fariñas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Fari%C3%B1as "Rodolfo Fariñas"), along with Pampanga representatives [Aurelio Gonzales Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelio_Gonzales_Jr. "Aurelio Gonzales Jr.") and [Juan Pablo Bondoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pablo_Bondoc "Juan Pablo Bondoc"), filed House Resolution 882, which requested the House Committee on Good Government to "conduct an inquiry" about the misuse of PHP66.45 million from Ilocos Norte's share of excise tax funds to obtain more than 110 units of motor vehicles. The resolution cited that the "highly irregular purchase" violated 2 republic acts—RA No. 7171, "An Act to Promote the Development of the Farmer in the Virginia Tobacco Producing Provinces,"[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-95) and RA No. 9184, "Government Procurement Reform Act[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-96) --and 1 presidential decree: PD No. 1445, Government Auditing Code of the Philippines.[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-97)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-:0) The investigation began on May 2, 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-:1)

The vehicles were said to be for distribution to the different barangays and municipalities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[98\]](#refnote-:0) Marcos justified the purchase as a response to the multiple requests of farmers asking for vehicles,[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-100) although Fariñas had pointed out that RA 7171 does not include purchasing vehicles as a means to utilize the excise tax. He also pointed out that a barangay captain from Laoag City received vehicles, despite the fact that there are no tobacco farms in that area.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[99\]](#refnote-:1) Other barangay captains have also complained that the vehicles were not duly registered under the Land Transportation Office (LTO), or filed as government property, so costs for oil and gas could not be reimbursed by the barangay budget.[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-101)

At a July 24, 2017 hearing, six local officials confessed that they have access to tobacco excise tax funds used to pay 70 mini-trucks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-:2) Pedro Agcaoili, Eden Batulayan, Josephine Calajete, Encarnacion Gaor, Genedine Jambaro, and Evangeline Tabulog[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-103) also acknowledged that their signatures were written on pertinent documents, a fact that they had previously denied.[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-104) Marcos also admitted that the said mini-trucks were bought from a direct contractor, and was not up for public bidding, as was the protocol when government property is procured.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[102\]](#refnote-:2)

Upon continued investigation and hearings, the House committee discovered that all the purchased vehicles "were overpriced by PHP21,450,000."[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-:4) The direct contractor who sold 40 units of mini cab was identified as Mark Chua, who is also currently Marcos's long-time boyfriend, who overpriced the mini cabs by PHP7,800,000 overall.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[105\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[106\]](#refnote-:4) The committee also verified the complaints from barangay captains that the vehicles did not have registrations from the LTO. Committee Report No. 638 therefore concluded and recommended that charges be filed against Marcos, Chua, and several local officials.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-2)[\[105\]](#refnote-:3) As of July 2018, another "fact-finding investigation" initiated by the [Office of the Ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ombudsman_of_the_Philippines "Ombudsman of the Philippines") is underway. If they find enough cause, the Ombudsman will then open a formal investigation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-107)

### As beneficiaries of illegal Swiss foundations

In the conviction of her mother Imelda Marcos for seven counts of graft in November 2018,[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-108) the [Sandiganbayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandiganbayan "Sandiganbayan") anti-graft court found that illegal Swiss foundations were used to earn from investments and interests to benefit Imelda and Ferdinand Marcos and their beneficiaries Imee Marcos, Bongbong Marcos, and Irene Marcos-Araneta.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-:5)

The Sandiganbayan's 5th Division convicted former Imelda Marcos for creating and maintaining seven private foundations in Switzerland while holding government positions from 1968 to 1986. Imee and her siblings were named as beneficiaries of two of the illegal foundations: the Trinidad Foundation and the Xandy Foundation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[\[109\]](#refnote-:5)

Marcos filed her certificate of candidacy on October 16, 2018, accidentally arriving at the same time as human rights advocate [Chel Diokno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chel_Diokno "Chel Diokno"), who was also filing his candidacy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-COMELECConfrontation-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-COMELECConfrontation) Because Chel Diokno's crowds supported his human rights advocacy, political chants quickly transitioned into protest chants concerning the Marcos martial law legacy, including the traditional protest chants "Never Again to Martial Law" and "Marcos Hitler Diktador Tuta!" On November 10, 2018, the anti-graft court [Sandiganbayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandiganbayan "Sandiganbayan") found that she and her siblings benefited from the illegal Swiss foundations that her mother, [Imelda Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos "Imelda Marcos"), created and maintained.[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-112)[&#8202;](#ref-back-113-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-113) On November 25, 2018, Marcos proposed to expand the [Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantawid_Pamilyang_Pilipino_Program "Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program") (4Ps) by providing a monthly income to fishermen and farmers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-114) Marcos also proposed a rescue package to support coconut farmers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-115)

Despite the senatorial campaign not yet in effect, Marcos began a premature senatorial campaign in numerous provinces.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] On January 17, 2019, [Cebu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_City "Cebu City") mayor [Tomas Osmeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomas_Osme%C3%B1a "Tomas Osmeña") ordered the taking down of political tarpaulins of Marcos during the [Sinulog Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinulog_Festival "Sinulog Festival"); the mayor criticized Marcos for 'politicizing' a religious festivity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-116-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-116)

In her campaigns, she noted that she was a graduate of [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-117-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-117) However, Princeton deputy university spokesperson Michael Hotchkiss stated that Imee's claim was false and completely fabricated, as she never graduated from Princeton University.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JelineMalasig20181023-3)[\[43\]](#refnote-JelineMalasig20181023)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[36\]](#refnote-auto) At the same time, Imee's claim that she was a cum laude from the [University of the Philippines Diliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Diliman "University of the Philippines Diliman") was found to be fabricated. UP Executive Vice President Teodoro Herbosa noted that "there is no record of Imee's graduation from UPD (University of the Philippines Diliman) _nor_ any honors or academic distinctions received with the University Registrar's office."[&#8202;](#ref-back-rappler.com-2)[\[37\]](#refnote-rappler.com) During a February 9, 2019, senatorial debate, Marcos called on the government to remove term limits in elections, sparking criticism.[&#8202;](#ref-back-gmanetwork.com-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-gmanetwork.com) Term limit removal was used by her father, the late former President Ferdinand Marcos, in the 1970s which led to a 21-year brutal regime.[&#8202;](#ref-back-gmanetwork.com-1)[\[118\]](#refnote-gmanetwork.com) On March 6, 2019, amid Imee's fake degrees controversy, President Duterte's daughter, [Sara Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte "Sara Duterte"), backed Marcos, stating, "Honesty should not be an election issue," effectively yielding to Imee's fabricated claims.[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-119) Marcos has also claimed she graduated from [Santa Catalina School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Catalina_School "Santa Catalina School") as "class valedictorian" for more than four decades. On March 21, 2019, Santa Catalina School assistant head of school John Aimé disproved Imee's claims, stating: 'Imee Marcos attended our school for a brief period in the fall of 1972, she is _not_ a graduate.'[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-3)[\[38\]](#refnote-auto1) After Imee's third educational attainment claim was disproved, the [Asian Institute of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Institute_of_Management "Asian Institute of Management") (AIM)'s Registrar and Student Enterprise Director, Bryan Magbutay, reiterated, "In our 50 years of providing world class programs we _never_ offered an MA MBA," effectively disproving Imee's fourth educational attainment claim.[&#8202;](#ref-back-news.abs-cbn.com-1)[\[39\]](#refnote-news.abs-cbn.com)

In another campaign meet-up, Marcos stated that she was "too young" to have any power during her father's [dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship "Dictatorship"), and that she should not be blamed for the things she did due to her youth. However, records show that she was already 30 years old when her family was ousted from power in 1986.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DharelPlacido20161121-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-DharelPlacido20161121)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ferdinandMarcosDaughterMoveOn-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-ferdinandMarcosDaughterMoveOn)

On January 25, 2019, Marcos tweeted that [Ilocos Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte "Ilocos Norte")'s poverty incidence rate lessened under her governorship; however, the very same tweet showed poverty incidence rate actually increased during her rule. Poverty _reduction_ rate was 17% in 2006, while it dropped to 5% in 2015.[&#8202;](#ref-back-pinoyakoblog.com-1)[\[79\]](#refnote-pinoyakoblog.com)\[ _[self-published source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Self-published_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] The tweet was afterwards deleted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-abogado.com.ph-1)[\[80\]](#refnote-abogado.com.ph) In April 2019, Marcos stated that she intends to make working abroad a "matter of choice", however, she dodged questions on her father's dictatorship - which led to the country's economic collapse, forcing Filipinos to work abroad in the first place.[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-122)

On April 11, 2019, after China again reiterated its claims in the [South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Sea "South China Sea"), Marcos announced that she "trusts China",[&#8202;](#ref-back-philstar.com-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-philstar.com) falsely claiming that China never invaded [any Philippine territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough_Shoal "Scarborough Shoal").[&#8202;](#ref-back-philstar.com-1)[\[123\]](#refnote-philstar.com)[&#8202;](#ref-back-124-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-124) She also blamed the Philippines, falsely claiming that Filipinos started the territorial conflicts with China,[&#8202;](#ref-back-philstar.com-2)[\[123\]](#refnote-philstar.com) earning criticism from Filipinos.[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-125)

After the [2019 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Philippine_Senate_election "2019 Philippine Senate election"), she placed eighth and won one of the twelve contested seats. #### 18th Congress (2019–2022)

On July 22, 2019, Marcos was appointed as chair of the [Philippine Senate Cultural Communities Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_Senate_committees "List of Philippine Senate committees").

On August 6, 2019, Marcos filed a bill that would make men also liable for committing [adultery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adultery "Adultery").[&#8202;](#ref-back-128-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-128) Following the controversial transfer of ₱42 billion by the [Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_(Philippines) "Department of Health (Philippines)") to the [Department of Budget and Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Budget_and_Management "Department of Budget and Management")-Procurement Service (DBM-PS) for the purchase of [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") [personal protective equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment "Personal protective equipment"), in September 2021, Marcos filed a bill seeking to abolish the DBM-PS and the [Philippine International Trading Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_International_Trading_Corporation?action=edit&redlink=1 "Philippine International Trading Corporation") to "address systemic corruption" in the DBM-PS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-129-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-129) In September 2021, the [Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Offshore_Gaming_Operator "Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator") was signed into law by President Duterte. Senator Imee Marcos voted in favor of the law, which regulated and legalized POGOs, which are gambling corporations. The illegal activities of the POGOs were known since 2016, and were widely exposed to the public even before the law that legalized them was enacted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-130)[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-131)

In the [19th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Congress_of_the_Philippines "19th Congress of the Philippines"), Marcos was named as chair of four Senate committees: [Cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_Committee_on_Cooperatives "Philippine Senate Committee on Cooperatives"); [Electoral Reforms and People's Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_Committee_on_Electoral_Reforms_and_People's_Participation "Philippine Senate Committee on Electoral Reforms and People's Participation"); [Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_Committee_on_Foreign_Relations "Philippine Senate Committee on Foreign Relations"); and [Social Justice, Welfare and Rural Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Senate_Committee_on_Social_Justice,_Welfare_and_Rural_Development "Philippine Senate Committee on Social Justice, Welfare and Rural Development").[&#8202;](#ref-back-132-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-132) In December 2022, Marcos filed "fruit salad" bills promoting and protecting the Philippine's pineapple, [moringa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moringa_oleifera "Moringa oleifera"), [dragon fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_fruit "Dragon fruit"), and mango industries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-133-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-133)

In June 2023, Marcos filed a Senate resolution seeking to investigate a "request" by the United States government for the Philippines to temporarily house Afghan refugees. Marcos maintained the request lacked transparency and said some refugees, who are "supporters of the US and possibly, even former employees of the US government or US companies", pose a threat to public safety and national security.[&#8202;](#ref-back-134-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-134)[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-135)

In November 2023, Marcos reaffirmed her support and loyalty to former president [Rodrigo Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Duterte "Rodrigo Duterte") and Vice President [Sara Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte "Sara Duterte") regarding the issue of confidential funds,[&#8202;](#ref-back-136-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-136) while stating that the Philippines does not need to cooperate with the [International Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court "International Criminal Court") (ICC) as it will "cause great shame for the Filipinos" citing the courts are working, free and strong.[&#8202;](#ref-back-137-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-137)

On October 2, 2024, Marcos would file her certificate of Candidacy for re-election as senator for the [2025 Senate elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Philippine_Senate_election "2025 Philippine Senate election"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-138-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-138) she initially declined to be a part of her Brother's Senatoial slate, [Alyansa para sa Bagong Pilipinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alyansa_para_sa_Bagong_Pilipinas "Alyansa para sa Bagong Pilipinas") back on September 28, 2024,[&#8202;](#ref-back-139-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-139) but she would later be reintroduced back in the slate on February 11, 2025, during their Proclamation rally.[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[140\]](#refnote-140)

In March 2025, Marcos would lead a Senate investigation regarding the circumstances surrounding the [arrest of Rodrigo Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest_of_Rodrigo_Duterte "Arrest of Rodrigo Duterte"), which she stated had "deeply divided the nation."[&#8202;](#ref-back-141-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-141)

On March 26, 2025, Marcos would withdraw again from Alyansa after the arrest of Rodrigo Duterte, saying that she could "no longer stand on the same platform with the rest of the Alyansa senatorial candidates."[&#8202;](#ref-back-142-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-142)

on April 14, 2025, Vice President [Sara Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte "Sara Duterte") endorsed Marcos's re-election bid in a 31-second political advertisement which the two were dressed in black, slammed the current state of the Philippines.[&#8202;](#ref-back-143-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-143)

On May 10, 2025, Marcos and former fellow Alyansa mate, [Camille Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Villar "Camille Villar"), were adopted by Oppositional senatorial slate, [DuterTen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuterTen "DuterTen"), led by [PDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partido_Demokratiko_Pilipino "Partido Demokratiko Pilipino") as guest candidates.[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-144)

#### 20th Congress (2025–present)

In 2026, Marcos made claims on the senate floor that the 2028 Presidential elections in the Philippines would be cancelled. However that was disproven when President BongBong Marcos stated that he could not violate the 1987 Philippine constitution related to elections and would step down.[&#8202;](#ref-back-146-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-146)

## Party affiliation

Marcos formerly belonged to the [Kilusang Bagong Lipunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilusang_Bagong_Lipunan "Kilusang Bagong Lipunan") (KBL), the political party of her father.[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeCVHoR-6)[\[31\]](#refnote-imeeCVHoR) In 2009, she and her family joined the alliance of the [Nacionalista Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacionalista_Party "Nacionalista Party") in support of its [2010 presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Philippine_presidential_election "2010 Philippine presidential election") candidate, then-Senator [Manny Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Villar "Manny Villar"). Incidentally, her father used to be a member of Nacionalista before he founded KBL in 1978.[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-147)

## Martial law denialism

Main article: [Historical distortion regarding Ferdinand Marcos §Historical denialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_distortion_regarding_Ferdinand_Marcos#Historical_denialism "Historical distortion regarding Ferdinand Marcos")

As with other Marcos family members who have stayed in the public eye since their return to the Philippines,[&#8202;](#ref-back-CMFR20160310-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-CMFR20160310)[&#8202;](#ref-back-MerlinaHernandoMalipot20180907-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-MerlinaHernandoMalipot20180907)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NikDeYnchausti20160923-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-NikDeYnchausti20160923) Imee Marcos has received significant criticism[&#8202;](#ref-back-PhilipJamilla20180830-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-PhilipJamilla20180830) for instances of [historical revisionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_revisionism "Historical revisionism"), and the [denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denialism "Denialism") or trivializing of the human rights violations and economic plunder that took place during the Marcos administration, and of the role she played in the administration.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NikDeYnchausti20160923-1)[\[150\]](#refnote-NikDeYnchausti20160923)[&#8202;](#ref-back-PhilipJamilla20180830-1)[\[151\]](#refnote-PhilipJamilla20180830)

Prominent examples of statements by Imee Marcos which have received such criticism include her 2016 statement that she was "too young" to have any power during her father's administration (although she was already 30 years old when her father was ousted in February 1986),[&#8202;](#ref-back-DharelPlacido20161121-1)[\[120\]](#refnote-DharelPlacido20161121) and her 2018 assertion that critics should just "move on" regarding the crimes and excesses of the martial law era.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ferdinandMarcosDaughterMoveOn-1)[\[121\]](#refnote-ferdinandMarcosDaughterMoveOn)

### "Move on" remark regarding martial law abuses

On August 21, 2018, the anniversary of the assassination of Senator [Benigno Aquino Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_Jr. "Benigno Aquino Jr.") who had flown back to the Philippines to face her father, Imee Marcos told Filipinos to "move on" from the [abuses, murders and massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship "Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship") during her father's dictatorship. Imee Marcos stated, "The millennials have moved on, and I think people at my age should also move on as well".[&#8202;](#ref-back-ferdinandMarcosDaughterMoveOn-2)[\[121\]](#refnote-ferdinandMarcosDaughterMoveOn)

To this, various sectors of the youth protested, releasing statements such as "She has no right to claim what our stand is on the issue. Not in our name, Imee Marcos," "The millennials, the youth in general, have not moved on and we will never move on from the Marcoses' crimes against the Filipino people. Not until justice has been served to the thousands of Filipinos who were killed, abducted and tortured under their reign, we will not move on",[&#8202;](#ref-back-millenialsTellImeeMarcos-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-millenialsTellImeeMarcos) "Binubuo pa lang ako ng magulang ko may utang na ako. Paano ako nagmo-move on Imee Marcos?" (I was still being formed by my parents in the womb, already I had debts. How can I move on, Imee Marcos?),[&#8202;](#ref-back-theseMillenialsHavent-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-theseMillenialsHavent) "When your family's in jail, when you return what you stole, when Marcos is taken out of Libingan ng mga Bayani, then we move on," and "The gall of Imee Marcos to ask why many have not "moved on" from the turbulent past they caused is like asking someone who got robbed to just think of the stolen item as donation. Isauli niyo mga ninakaw ninyo! (Return what you stole!)"[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeMarcosDrawsIre-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-imeeMarcosDrawsIre)

In a statement before media, Marcos said "...what I've heard is that there are calls for an apology tantamount on admission, which we would never do" and stated that she did not know what her family should admit to in the first place.[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeMarcosApologizesInadvertently-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-imeeMarcosApologizesInadvertently)

### Family denial

Imee Marcos denies that human rights abuses occurred during her family's regime, calling them political accusations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-politicalAccusations-0)[\[156\]](#refnote-politicalAccusations) In a press briefing, she said " _Kung ang dinedemand ay admission (of guilt) ay palagay ko hindi pwede 'yun_" (If what is demanded is an admission of guilt, I don't think that's possible). When asked why, she replied: " _Bakit kami mag-aadmit sa hindi namin ginawa?_" (Why would we admit to something we did not do?). She added, "As we all know, these are political accusations that have not been proven in court".[&#8202;](#ref-back-politicalAccusations-1)[\[156\]](#refnote-politicalAccusations) This is despite having been convicted before a U.S. district court through the 1993 _[Trajano v. Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajano_v._Marcos "Trajano v. Marcos")_ case (978 F 2d 493).[&#8202;](#ref-back-157-0)[\[157\]](#refnote-157)[&#8202;](#ref-back-158-0)[\[158\]](#refnote-158)[&#8202;](#ref-back-imeeUSCourtTrajano-1)[\[67\]](#refnote-imeeUSCourtTrajano)

## Personal life

Marcos is the eldest daughter of Ferdinand Marcos and Imelda Romuáldez Marcos. Her full siblings are [Bongbong Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos "Bongbong Marcos"), [Irene Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Marcos "Irene Marcos"), and [Aimee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aimee_Marcos "Aimee Marcos"). She also has a number of half-siblings who are not as known by the public at large.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ReferenceA-0)[\[160\]](#refnote-ReferenceA)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Manapat1991-5)[\[44\]](#refnote-Manapat1991) This includes three siblings which her father had with [Carmen Ortega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Ortega "Carmen Ortega") of the Ortega clan of La Union, who was his [common-law wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common-law_marriage "Common-law marriage") before he married Imelda Romuáldez as a political strategy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-161-0)[\[161\]](#refnote-161)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ReferenceA-1)[\[160\]](#refnote-ReferenceA)

Marcos was married to golfer and former professional basketball coach [Tommy Manotoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Manotoc "Tommy Manotoc"). Their marriage was controversial since the Philippines did not have a [divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce "Divorce") law and Manotoc's October 1981 divorce obtained in the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic "Dominican Republic") from wife [Aurora Pijuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_Pijuan "Aurora Pijuan") was not recognized under Philippine law.[&#8202;](#ref-back-marcosSonInLawApology-0)[\[162\]](#refnote-marcosSonInLawApology) Marcos and Manotoc have three sons: Fernando Martín ("Borgy"), a [commercial model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_(person) "Model (person)") and [club DJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_jockey#Club_DJs "Disc jockey"); [Ferdinand Richard Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Richard_Michael?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ferdinand Richard Michael") ("Mike"), a lawyer; and [Matthew Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Manotoc "Matthew Manotoc") ("MJ"), a [sports agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_agent "Sports agent") and incumbent governor of [Ilocos Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte "Ilocos Norte") since June 30, 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BarbaraMaeDacanay20120314-0)[\[163\]](#refnote-BarbaraMaeDacanay20120314)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio-5)[\[58\]](#refnote-bio) She had two stepchildren during her marriage to Manotoc, including [ABS-CBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN "ABS-CBN") reporter and news anchor TJ Manotoc.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CathyBabao20180123-0)[\[164\]](#refnote-CathyBabao20180123)

On October 19, 2000, a 27-year-old film producer from [Leyte, Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte,_Leyte "Leyte, Leyte") named Cesar Bendigo who was allegedly in a romantic relationship with Marcos was killed in an ambush by [New People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People's_Army "New People's Army") rebels; Marcos last visited his residence on August 24, 2000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-165-0)[\[165\]](#refnote-165)

Since her [legal separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_separation "Legal separation") from Manotoc, Marcos has been in a long-term relationship with Singaporean and ethnic [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") businessman and resident [Mark Chua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte_Tobacco_Excise_Tax_funds_controversy "Ilocos Norte Tobacco Excise Tax funds controversy"), since the 1990s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-166-0)[\[166\]](#refnote-166) Marcos and Chua have been linked to the [₱](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_peso "Philippine peso") 64.5 million [Ilocos Norte Tobacco Excise Tax Funds Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Norte_Tobacco_Excise_Tax_funds_controversy "Ilocos Norte Tobacco Excise Tax funds controversy").[&#8202;](#ref-back-167-0)[\[167\]](#refnote-167)[&#8202;](#ref-back-168-0)[\[168\]](#refnote-168)
