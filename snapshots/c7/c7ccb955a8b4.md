**Timothy Mellon** (born July 22, 1942) is an American businessman, the grandson of [Andrew Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mellon "Andrew Mellon"), and an heir to the [Mellon banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellon_Financial "Mellon Financial") fortune.[&#8202;](#ref-back-boon-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-boon) He is a major donor to the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)").

Mellon's net worth has been estimated at between $700 million and $4 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-:2) As of June 2024, _[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")_ estimated the [Mellon family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellon_family "Mellon family")'s net worth at $14.1 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)

## Early life and education

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")\]

Born on July 22, 1942, in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh"), Mellon is the son of [Paul Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Mellon "Paul Mellon") and his first wife, Mary Conover Brown, and the grandson of [U.S. Treasury Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Treasury "United States Secretary of the Treasury") [Andrew Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mellon "Andrew Mellon").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Singh-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-Singh) His mother died in 1946.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Singh-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-Singh)

According to Mellon's memoir, he grew up in a Virginia mansion and a private plane shuttled him to boarding school in Massachusetts. He attended The Fenn School in [Concord, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_Massachusetts "Concord, Massachusetts"), and [Milton Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Academy "Milton Academy") in [Milton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton,_Massachusetts "Milton, Massachusetts").[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-0)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato) As a kid, he was considered "gregarious" and "brilliantly mathematically minded", according to [Burton Hersh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Hersh "Burton Hersh"), author of _The Mellon Family.[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-1)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato)_

Mellon graduated from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in 1964 with a bachelor's degree in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science") with membership in [Pi Sigma Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_Sigma_Alpha "Pi Sigma Alpha").[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-2024-becker-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-wp-2024-becker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ydn-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-ydn) As an undergraduate, he was a member of [Trumbull College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumbull_College "Trumbull College") and the [Scroll and Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scroll_and_Key "Scroll and Key") secret society.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) He enrolled in [Yale Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_Law_School "Yale Law School") but dropped out after three days,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-:5) instead earning a master's degree in [city planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning "Urban planning") from Yale in 1966.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4)

A May 1971 _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ article called him "a quiet Yale graduate with two college degrees who has applied computer techniques to city planning."[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13) Mellon later rebuked his Yale education as "pseudo-scientific sociological claptrap" and "Progressive dogma".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0)

## Career and life events

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career and life events")\]

After graduation, Mellon worked at the Yale computer center for three years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-:5) In the 1960s, he founded a software company called Vertical Systems, which sold software for [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM "IBM") computers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-:6)

In 1977, Mellon founded Perma Treat, a treated wood business that produced railroad ties for [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak "Amtrak") and [Conrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrail "Conrail").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-2)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-:6) In 1981, he formed [Guilford Transportation Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Systems "Pan Am Systems") (GTI),[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-2024-becker-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-wp-2024-becker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) a railroad holding company named for his adopted hometown of [Guilford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilford,_Connecticut "Guilford, Connecticut").[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) That year, GTI purchased the [Boston & Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_and_Maine_Corporation "Boston and Maine Corporation") railroad in April and the [Maine Central Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Central_Railroad "Maine Central Railroad") in May,[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18) with government approval for the Boston & Maine completed in 1983.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) GTI also purchased the [Delaware & Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_and_Hudson_Railway "Delaware and Hudson Railway") (D&H) with government approval in 1984;[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) the D&H was sold in 1988.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-:7)

In 1998, GTI purchased the bankrupt [Pan American World Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_World_Airways "Pan American World Airways")' brand rights for $28.5 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-3)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4) The company was reorganized as [Pan Am Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Systems "Pan Am Systems") and operated the subsidiary [Boston-Maine Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston-Maine_Airways "Boston-Maine Airways") until it was shuttered in 2008 by the [Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_transportation "Department of transportation") for falsifying financial records and lack of financial fitness.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23) GTI, renamed [Pan Am Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Railways "Pan Am Railways") in 2006,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[22\]](#refnote-:7) was announced for sale by Mellon in 2020 and sold to the [CSX Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSX_Corporation "CSX Corporation") in 2022 for $600 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0)

In 1999, Mellon purchased the [Goodspeed Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodspeed_Airport "Goodspeed Airport") in [East Haddam, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Haddam,_Connecticut "East Haddam, Connecticut"), for $2.33 million;[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24) he sold it in 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)

In 2002, Mellon stepped down as a trustee of the [Andrew W. Mellon Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_W._Mellon_Foundation "Andrew W. Mellon Foundation") after 21 years on its board.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-2024-becker-2)[\[7\]](#refnote-wp-2024-becker)

In July 2024, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ reported that Mellon was responsible for the 2012 removal and subsequent 2013 return of the [Narragansett Runestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Runestone "Narragansett Runestone").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-3)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0)

### Search for Amelia Earhart

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Search for Amelia Earhart")\]

In 2012, Mellon donated over $1 million to [The International Group for Historic Aircraft Recovery (TIGHAR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TIGHAR "TIGHAR"), a nonprofit organization, to assist its efforts to find [Amelia Earhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_Earhart "Amelia Earhart")'s plane and remains. In 2013, he sued TIGHAR for racketeering, alleging that it engaged in deceit by soliciting his money to search for Earhart's plane.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26) Mellon claimed that the plane had been found in 2010, before he made his donation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

In 2014, U.S. District Judge [Scott W. Skavdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_W._Skavdahl "Scott W. Skavdahl") granted TIGHAR's motion for summary judgment after recognizing that even Mellon's own experts were unable to confirm his allegations about the 2010 photographs that he claimed showed the plane. Skavdahl concluded:

> Defendants represented to Plaintiff they were planning another expedition in their continued quest to find the wreckage of Amelia Earhart's airplane. Upon reading about Defendants' efforts, Plaintiff contacted Defendants and expressed his interest in supporting the expedition with a monetary contribution. That's exactly what the parties then did. No false representations were made. Regardless, no rational trier of fact could find Defendants falsely represented they had not found Earhart's plane by embarking on another expedition in hopes of finding conclusive evidence to prove it. No matter how convinced or sincere Plaintiff is in his subjective belief and opinion that Amelia Earhart's airplane was or should have been discovered prior to the making of his donation, that belief and opinion is insufficient to create a genuine dispute of material fact.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28)

Mellon appealed to the [United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Tenth_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit"), which affirmed the district court's ruling without holding oral argument. The Tenth Circuit concluded that the lack of actionable falsity precluded Mellon's claims.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)

## Politics

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Politics")\]

### Views and autobiography

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Views and autobiography")\]

Mellon is a [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)"), though in the past he supported and donated to liberal causes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-4)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4) He voted for President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson"), [George McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern "George McGovern"), and [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter") before becoming a [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") during [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan")'s presidency.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-2024-becker-3)[\[7\]](#refnote-wp-2024-becker)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-5)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4)

He is a supporter of President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump").[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-2)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-6)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4) He is anti-tax and anti-union.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-4)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-:2) He has criticized teacher unions and the introduction of women studies, LGBT studies, and African American studies into higher education.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-auto) He was a listener of [Rush Limbaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_Limbaugh "Rush Limbaugh").[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-3)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato)

Mellon faced media attention after comments describing his political views in his 2015 self-published autobiography " _panam.captain"_ came to light.[&#8202;](#ref-back-donor-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-donor)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[30\]](#refnote-auto) In it, he calls [social safety net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_safety_net "Social safety net") programs "slavery Redux" and those receiving benefits "slaves of a new master, Uncle Sam".[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-4)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato) He also writes that black people became "even more belligerent" after social programs expanded in the 1960s and '70s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-donor-1)[\[31\]](#refnote-donor)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-2)[\[30\]](#refnote-auto) Mellon defended his comments in the book and did not apologize.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-7)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4)

### Political donations

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Political donations")\]

From 1996 to 2017, Mellon gave $350,000 in political donations to [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush"), [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain "John McCain"), and [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney").[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-5)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato)[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-2024-becker-4)[\[7\]](#refnote-wp-2024-becker) From January 2020 to June 2024, he gave $227 million to federal candidates and political committees, nearly all Republicans.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-5)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0) As of August 2024, he had donated $165 million in the 2024 elections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-os-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-os)

As of 2024 reporting, he had donated to only two [Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"): [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez "Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez"), for her [2018 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_New_York's_14th_congressional_district_election "2018 New York's 14th congressional district election"), and [Tulsi Gabbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsi_Gabbard "Tulsi Gabbard"), for her [2020 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsi_Gabbard_2020_presidential_campaign "Tulsi Gabbard 2020 presidential campaign").[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-3)[\[30\]](#refnote-auto) He said he donated to Ocasio-Cortez to disrupt the Democratic Party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-8)[\[12\]](#refnote-:4)

#### 2010–2021

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: 2010–2021")\]

In 2010, Mellon donated $1.5 million to [Arizona Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Governor "Arizona Governor") [Jan Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Brewer "Jan Brewer")'s defense fund to help cover the costs of legal challenges against [Arizona SB 1070](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_SB_1070 "Arizona SB 1070"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-arizdonation-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-arizdonation) the broadest and strictest anti- [illegal immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigration_to_the_United_States "Illegal immigration to the United States") measure in the U.S. at the time of its passage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34)[&#8202;](#ref-back-boon-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-boon) The fund's biggest donation, it received national and international attention and spurred considerable controversy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)

In the [2018 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_elections "2018 United States elections"), Mellon was a major political donor, especially to the Republican-aligned [Congressional Leadership Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Leadership_Fund "Congressional Leadership Fund").[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37) In April 2020, he donated $10 million to [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s super PAC [America First Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_First_Action "America First Action").[&#8202;](#ref-back-boon-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-boon) According to [OpenSecrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenSecrets "OpenSecrets"), in 2020 and 2022, he was the 6th- and 5th-most prolific donor in the U.S., spending $60 million and $47 million, respectively, to support Republican candidates and causes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)

In August 2021, Mellon donated $53.1 million in stock to the state of Texas for construction of the [Mexico–United States barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_barrier "Mexico–United States barrier") walls.[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39)

#### 2024 presidential campaign

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: 2024 presidential campaign")\]

In the second quarter of 2023, Mellon donated $5 million to American Values 2024, a super PAC affiliated with the [Robert F. Kennedy Jr. 2024 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy_Jr._2024_presidential_campaign "Robert F. Kennedy Jr. 2024 presidential campaign"); that, together with a large donation from billionaire [Gavin de Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_de_Becker "Gavin de Becker"), accounted for 97% of donations the PAC received through the end of June 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40) By March 2024, Mellon's donations to the PAC totaled $20 million,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-:1) and by July 2024 it had increased to $25 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-6)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0)

Between April 2023 and March 2024, Mellon donated $15 million to [MAGA Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_America_Great_Again_Inc. "Make America Great Again Inc.") (Make America Great Again), a Trump-affiliated [super PAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_PAC "Super PAC").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[41\]](#refnote-:1) On May 31, 2024, he gave MAGA Inc. $50 million, one of the largest disclosed donations ever.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42)[&#8202;](#ref-back-donor-2)[\[31\]](#refnote-donor) By July 2024, Mellon had given $75 million toward supporting Trump's campaign,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-7)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0) and by October, the amount donated to the PAC had reached $150 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43)

Mellon was the second-largest individual donor in the [2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election"), spending $197 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)

#### Post-2024

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Post-2024")\]

In the first half of 2025, Mellon donated an additional $2 million to MAGA Inc.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-:12)

During the [2025 United States federal government shutdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_government_shutdown "2025 United States federal government shutdown"), Mellon donated $130 million to help pay members of the [United States Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces"). _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ noted that the donation would equal approximately $100 per service member.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-:3)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Timothy_Mellon_in_March_2015.jpg/250px-Timothy_Mellon_in_March_2015.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Timothy_Mellon_in_March_2015.jpg) Mellon (left) receives a framed photograph of the [USCGC _Mellon_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Mellon "USCGC Mellon"), a ship named after his grandfather, [Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mellon "Andrew Mellon").

_The New York Times_ has called Mellon a "reclusive billionaire". His nephew has called him the "most private Mellon there is" and "a highly private individual" to those who know him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[46\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-6)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato)

His inheritance from the [Mellon family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellon_family "Mellon family") was estimated at $100 million by [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-8)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0) His net worth is estimated to be between $700 million and $4 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-2)[\[2\]](#refnote-:2) He owns properties in Wyoming and Connecticut.[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-7)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato)

A private pilot, he has logged over 11,500 [flight hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_time "Flight time").[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-2024-becker-5)[\[7\]](#refnote-wp-2024-becker)

### Marriages

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Marriages")\]

On June 8, 1963 in [Morristown, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morristown,_New_Jersey "Morristown, New Jersey"), Mellon married Susan Crawford Tracy, the daughter of a [Esso Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esso "Esso") executive.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47)[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-8)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato)[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49) The couple moved to [Guilford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilford,_Connecticut "Guilford, Connecticut"). They divorced by the early 1980s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Singh-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-Singh)

In the early 1980s, Mellon married Louise Whitney, a childhood friend. They divorced in the early 1990s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Singh-3)[\[5\]](#refnote-Singh)[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-9)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato) He then married Patricia Trenary Freeman, the divorcee of a former classmate who was the best man at his first wedding. They then divorced and remarried.[&#8202;](#ref-back--Reginato-10)[\[6\]](#refnote--Reginato) They moved from Guilford to [Saratoga, Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratoga,_Wyoming "Saratoga, Wyoming"), in 2005.[&#8202;](#ref-back-boon-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-boon)

### Legal issues

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Timothy_Mellon&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Legal issues")\]

Mellon has been involved in a number of legal issues, often over relatively small dollar amounts. Testifying in a 2014 civil case, he estimated he had been deposed 15 to 20 times, and that he did not know how many lawsuits he was then involved in.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-9)[\[14\]](#refnote-:0)

In 2004, he was sued by [Connecticut Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Attorney_General "Connecticut Attorney General") [Richard Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Blumenthal "Richard Blumenthal") for cutting down trees in a conserved wetland and was fined.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-2024-becker-6)[\[7\]](#refnote-wp-2024-becker)
