# Intel Delays 10-nm Volume Production Until 2019

By [Dylan McGrath](https://www.eetimes.com/author/dylan-mcgrath/ "Posts by Dylan McGrath")  04.27.2018 9

SAN FRANCISCO — Intel announced the appointment of yet another big-name chip architect and provided more evidence that its multi-year quest to diversify beyond the PC is finally paying dividends. But the company also pushed volume production of 10-nm chips from late this year to next year, saying that progress in improving yields is slower than expected.

In a conference call with analysts following its first quarter earnings announcement, Intel CEO Brian Krzanich said that Intel is shipping 10-nm products in low volumes and that yields are improving but that the rate of improvement is slower than the company expected.

“As a result, volume production is moving from the second half of 2018 into 2019,” said Krzanich. “We understand the yield issues and have defined improvements for them, but they will take time to implement and qualify.”

Krzanich said that the yield issues are tied to defects resulting from the amount of multi-patterning required for 10 nm, the last node before Intel will begin using extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography at 7 nm.

Intel reported a 9% increase in first-quarter sales and raised its sales target for the year to $67.5 billion from $65 billion. The company also said that non-PC products accounted for 49% of its sales in the first quarter, a new high water mark for the company that has spent more than a decade pushing to diversify beyond its core PC business.

Intel said that Jim Keller, a 20-year design veteran whose career has included prominent roles with Apple and AMD, will join the company as a senior vice president leading its silicon engineering group. Intel hired Keller away from Tesla, where he had most recently been vice president of autopilot and low-voltage hardware.

Keller's appointment comes five months after Intel [lured Raj Koduri away from AMD](https://eetimes.com/document.asp?doc_id=1332577) and installed him as chief architect of its core and visual computing group to lead its edge computing initiative.

Among the milestones of his career, Keller led the development of Apple's original A4 custom mobile processor and its successor, the A5. He also led the development of AMD's Zen architecture while serving as AMD's chief cores architect.

Intel's first-quarter sales increased to $16.1 billion, up from $14.8 billion in the first quarter of 2017. Intel reported a net income for the quarter of $4.5 billion, up 50% compared with the first quarter of 2017.

Intel said that its memory business had record revenue of more than $1 billion in the first quarter, an increase of 20% from the year-ago quarter.

IanD    2018-04-30 08:08:04

Which is why I said "the fine-pitch metal layers" -- meaning, the bottom couple plus vias and MOL for Intel 10nm. This is still a much bigger step for cobalt than any of the foundries are doing at 7nm.

Copper at 7nm is OK even for high clock rates (we've got circuits running at >10GHz designed for 10 year lifetime at maximum Tj) so long as you design/choose the libraries carefully (not the ultra-compact 6T mobile libraries) and do power-aware placement and thorough EM checks -- of course cobalt gives an advantage, but this doesn't mean copper is dead at 7nm.

At 5nm and below it gets more and more difficult to keep RC low when using copper because of the damascene thickness.
