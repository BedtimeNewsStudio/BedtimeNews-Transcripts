# Ineos 1:59 Challenge

Ineos 1:59 Challenge

2019 attempt to beat a 2-hour marathon

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/b/b1/INEOS_159_Challenge_logo.svg/500px-INEOS_159_Challenge_logo.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:INEOS_159_Challenge_logo.svg) Logo of the event

The **Ineos 1:59 Challenge**, held on 12 October 2019, was an attempt by Kenyan athlete [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge "Eliud Kipchoge") to break [the two-hour mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-hour_marathon "Two-hour marathon") for running the [marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon "Marathon") distance, which he achieved in a time of 1:59:40.2. The event was specifically created for Kipchoge and held in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna"), Austria to help market the Nike ZoomX [Vaporfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Vaporfly_and_Tokyo_2020_Olympics_controversy "Nike Vaporfly and Tokyo 2020 Olympics controversy") 4%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) Kipchoge had previously attempted to run a two hour marathon at the [Breaking2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking2 "Breaking2") event organised by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_inc. "Nike, inc.") in 2017, but he fell short by 25 seconds. He then went on to run a [world record marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_world_record_progression "Marathon world record progression") at the [2018 Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Berlin_Marathon "2018 Berlin Marathon") before British chemicals company [Ineos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos "Ineos") announced the attempt in May 2019. 41 [pacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker_(running) "Pacemaker (running)"), rotating in and out in groups of 7, assisted Kipchoge throughout the attempt.

Due to the presence of the pacemakers, delivery of hydration by bicycle, and the lack of open competition, the achievement was not eligible to be ratified as a marathon world record, and was not recognized as such by [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics "World Athletics"). The two-hour barrier was first broken under record-eligible conditions by [Sabastian Sawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabastian_Sawe "Sabastian Sawe") in the [2026 London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_London_Marathon "2026 London Marathon"), with a time of 1:59:30.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-:0)

## Background

Main articles: [Marathon world record progression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_world_record_progression "Marathon world record progression") and [Breaking2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking2 "Breaking2")

The possibility of a sub-two-hour marathon had attracted increasing discussion since about 2011,[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) at which time the world record was 2:03:59 held by Ethiopian runner [Haile Gebrselassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Gebrselassie "Haile Gebrselassie"). Gebrselassie said in 2011 that the two-hour mark could be broken in 20 to 25 years.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Athletics manager Glenn Latimer believed that the record would plateau around the 2:02 mark, an opinion shared by Kenyan runner [Samuel Wanjiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wanjiru "Samuel Wanjiru"). Extrapolating [the fall in marathon world record times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_world_record_progression "Marathon world record progression"), Francois Peronnet of the [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal "University of Montreal") predicted that the two-hour mark would be broken in 2028.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) In 2017, _[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")_ reported that the average decrease in the marathon record was 8 seconds per year for the previous 50 years and 9.4 seconds per year for the previous 20 years. If the record continued to reduce at either of these rates, then the – barrier would be crossed in 2040 or 2036, respectively. It also stated that the range of peak ability was between 1:57:58 and 2:00:47.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Economist-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-Economist) A 2019 study by Simon Angus of [Monash University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University "Monash University") predicted that the barrier would be crossed in May 2032 with an odds of success level of 1 in 10 and that the peak ability is 1:58:05.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

### Breaking2

[Breaking2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking2 "Breaking2") was a project run by sports equipment manufacturer [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc. "Nike, Inc.") announced in December 2016 with the goal of breaking the two-hour mark over the marathon. Three runners, [Zersenay Tadese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zersenay_Tadese "Zersenay Tadese"), [Lelisa Desisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelisa_Desisa "Lelisa Desisa"), and [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge "Eliud Kipchoge") were to attempt the feat, assisted by a team of pacemakers, scientists, engineers, physicians and trainers. The world record at the time was held by [Dennis Kimeto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Kipruto_Kimetto "Dennis Kipruto Kimetto"), set at the [2014 Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Berlin_Marathon "2014 Berlin Marathon") where he ran 2:02:57, while Kipchoge, whom Ed Caesar of _[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_(magazine) "Wired (magazine)")_ described as "the best marathoner in the world" had a personal best of 2:03:05.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) Tadese and Desisa had marathon personal bests of 2:10:41 and 2:04:45, respectively, while Tadese had the fastest half-marathon best of the three at 58:23. The reason for Tadese's selection was his perceived potential based on assessments made by the support team.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

Nike announced the race would take place at the [Monza Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monza_Circuit "Monza Circuit") near [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monza "Monza"), Italy, where Kipchoge ran a half marathon personal best of 59:17 during a test event.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) The course was 2.4 kilometres (1.5mi) long which meant the run would consist of about 17.5 laps around the course.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wired2-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-Wired2)

The attempt took place on 6 May 2017 at 5:45 a.m. [Central European Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time "Central European Time") ( [UTC+1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+1 "UTC+1")).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wired2-1)[\[11\]](#refnote-Wired2) Groups of six pacemakers ran in a diamond formation around the three runners and aimed to go through every 5 kilometres (3.1mi) in 14:13. For the first 5-kilometre section split, they were one second off the pace, going through in 14:14, and they later went through the 10 kilometres (6.2mi) mark in 28:21. Around 50 minutes into the race, Desisa dropped out of the group before Tadese and Kipchoge went together through halfway in 59:57. Tadese soon dropped off the pace leaving Kipchoge alone to pass through 30 kilometres (19mi) in 1:25:20 and 40 kilometres (25mi) in 1:54:00. Falling behind the target pace, Kipchoge would need to increase his pace in order to run under 2 hours. However, he lost 10 seconds on the final lap, leaving him short of the goal by 25 seconds in a time of 2:00:25. Tadese finished in 2:06:51 and Desisa in 2:14:10.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wired3-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-Wired3)

Although faster than Kimetto's record of 2:02:57, Kipchoge's time could not be ratified by the [IAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics "World Athletics") (now known as World Athletics) as the attempt included the use of interchangeable pacemakers. Caesar believed that factors contributing to the failure of the attempt were the temperature and humidity on the day, which were both higher than the ideal range.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wired3-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-Wired3) A 2012 study found that the ideal temperature was 4°C (39°F) and that a 10 °C increase resulted in a 1.4% decrease in speed, while the temperature during the attempt was 11°C (52°F).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Economist-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-Economist)

### Events after Breaking2

Later in 2017, Kipchoge competed in the [Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Berlin_Marathon "2017 Berlin Marathon") alongside [Kenenisa Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenenisa_Bekele "Kenenisa Bekele") (the defending champion), [Wilson Kipsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Kipsang "Wilson Kipsang"), [Vincent Kipruto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Kipruto "Vincent Kipruto"), [Guye Adola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guye_Adola "Guye Adola"), [Mosinet Geremew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosinet_Geremew "Mosinet Geremew"), and [Felix Kandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Kandie?action=edit&redlink=1 "Felix Kandie"). Kipchoge won in a time of 2:03:32, his second fastest time after his 2:03:05 performance at the [2016 London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_London_Marathon "2016 London Marathon"). The victory marked his eighth win out of the previous nine marathons he had competed in, excluding Breaking2.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) Kipchoge followed this success with a victory in the [2018 London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_London_Marathon "2018 London Marathon"). Pacemakers were instructed to and passed through the halfway mark in 1:01:00, faster than the world record pace following splits of 13:48 and 28:19 through 5 kilometres and 10 kilometres, respectively. In the 24th mile, Kipchoge left Ethiopian [Shura Kitata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shura_Kitata "Shura Kitata") to win in a time of 2:04:17.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) Kipchoge next raced in the [2018 Berlin Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Berlin_Marathon "2018 Berlin Marathon") held on 16 September 2018. He stated that his preparation was "entirely concentrated" on the race and was "confident" that he could beat his personal best. Also racing were the likes of Tadese and Kipsang.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) Kipchoge completed the first 10 kilometres in 29:01 and the second in 28:55 before passing through the halfway mark in 61:06 before the last pacemaker soon dropped out. He then ran solo for the rest of the race, running a negative split and a 60:34 second half. He crossed the line in a world record time of 2:01:39, 78 seconds faster than Kimeto's previous world record.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18) He next raced in the [2019 London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_London_Marathon "2019 London Marathon") in April 2019. A group of nine went through 10 kilometres (6.2mi) in 29:01 and halfway in 61:37 before Kipchoge increased the pace and "one by one" his competitors dropped out of the group. Through the 24th mile, only Geremew managed to stay with him. Kipchoge ran the next two miles in 4:26 and 4:30 to win the race 2:02:37, while Geremew finished second in 2:02:55. This was Kipchoge's second fastest time behind his Berlin world record and also the second fastest marathon in history as well as his fourth London Marathon victory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)

On 26 April 2026, [Sabastian Sawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabastian_Sawe "Sabastian Sawe") and [Yomif Kejelcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomif_Kejelcha "Yomif Kejelcha") became the first individuals to run a record-eligible marathon in under two hours, at the [2026 London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_London_Marathon "2026 London Marathon").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-:0)

## Summary

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/London_Marathon_2018_%2841594080862%29.jpg/500px-London_Marathon_2018_%2841594080862%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:London_Marathon_2018_(41594080862).jpg) Kipchoge at the 2018 London Marathon

On 6 May 2019, in a press release on their website, British multinational chemicals company [Ineos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos "Ineos") announced that Kipchoge would be attempting a marathon in the autumn of 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) Ineos has invested in sports ventures such as cycling team [Ineos Grenadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos_Grenadiers "Ineos Grenadiers") (bought from [Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Group "Sky Group") in 2019, a team which had won 8 [Grand Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_(cycling) "Grand Tour (cycling)") since 2012),[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) and [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One "Formula One") through the part ownership and principal sponsorship of [Mercedes-Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_in_Formula_One "Mercedes-Benz in Formula One").[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) The attempt was scheduled to take place on the 6 May 2019, the 65th anniversary of the "historic" [four-minute mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-minute_mile "Four-minute mile") by British runner [Roger Bannister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Bannister "Roger Bannister").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Vienna-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-Vienna)

### Course

The attempt was to take place in the [Prater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prater "Prater"), a park in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna"), Austria. The city was chosen to host the run after an "extensive search of venues fitting Eliud's criteria" with factors such as [time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_zone "Time zone"), weather, and number of spectators considered.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Vienna-1)[\[23\]](#refnote-Vienna) A [preprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preprint "Preprint") published in October 2019 claimed that the oxygen uptake on the Vienna course would "fluctuate by no more than 2% of the oxygen uptake rates on a flat, straight course" and that it would "incur a penalty of less than 5 seconds compared to a perfectly level and straight course".[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

### The attempt

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/9/90/Kipchoge%27s_Marathon_Speeds.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kipchoge's_Marathon_Speeds.png) Comparison of Kipchoge's speeds in Breaking2, the 2018 Berlin Marathon world record, and the Ineos 1:59 Challenge (Vienna).[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)

For the attempt Kipchoge was joined by forty-one [pacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker_(running) "Pacemaker (running)"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26) who rotated twice each lap and ran in a V-formation, rather than the diamond formation chosen for the previous attempt. Kipchoge was placed at the bottom of the formation with two pacemakers running behind him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wired-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-Wired) Each lap of the course featured two 4.3-kilometre (2.7-mile) out-and-back stretches of Hauptallee with the turning points coming at the [Lusthaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusthaus_(Vienna) "Lusthaus (Vienna)") and [Praterstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praterstern "Praterstern") roundabouts at either end of the avenue, in the [Prater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prater "Prater") park. The entire route inclines only 2.4 metres (7.9 feet). Spectators were present for the attempt.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28)

The organizers planned to run the event on Saturday, 12 October 2019, but they had a reserve window of eight days in case of poor weather conditions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29) The attempt was run on 12 October starting at 08:15 [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time "Central European Time"). Organizers allowed a start time between 05:00 and 09:00, but chose 08:15 to maximize viewership. The weather conditions were expected to be dry with a temperature of 9°C (48°F) at the start, rising to 12°C (54°F) at the finish.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

The organizers of the attempt added many techniques during the run which cumulatively assisted Kipchoge and the pacemakers:

- Pacing lasers guided the pacemakers and the main runner, thus allowing them to run at a precise pace and meaning energy was not lost in unwanted acceleration.
- The route was carefully chosen to ensure that no effort would be wasted on battling the wind or on directional or incline changes. This was achieved by the fact that most of the course was lined with tall trees reducing wind, and the course was very flat.
- The location of the race was chosen because its time zone was close to that of [Kaptagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptagat "Kaptagat"), Kenya, where Kipchoge trains. This meant Kipchoge would not be affected by jet lag or have his sleeping and eating patterns disrupted.
- The route was picked to be at low altitude, to increase oxygen in the air and thus help performance.
- Kipchoge wore an improved version of Nike's previously unreleased [Vaporfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Vaporfly_and_Tokyo_2020_Olympics_controversy "Nike Vaporfly and Tokyo 2020 Olympics controversy") Next% running shoes, claimed to improve running economy by 4 percent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35) The shoes were not banned by the IAAF, and the top 10 men in the [Chicago Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Marathon "Chicago Marathon") (held the next day) wore Vaporflys. It was reported that Kipchoge and [Brigid Kosgei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigid_Kosgei "Brigid Kosgei"), who broke the women's world record in Chicago, wore bespoke versions of the shoe, with the model called AlphaFLY. The Nike Vaporfly that they wore has a carbon-fibre plate fitted in its chunky foam sole which supposedly helps propel the wearer forward. A group of athletes complained to the IAAF about the shoes, leading the governing body to create a working party to look at the issue. However, Kipchoge had set his world record wearing them a year earlier, as did [Abraham Kiptum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Kiptum "Abraham Kiptum") when he set the half-marathon record. In addition, the five fastest times over the distance were all set by runners wearing these shoes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)
- A V-shaped formation of pacemakers shielded the runner from wind resistance. An earlier attempt used a differently shaped diamond formation. Kipchoge was placed at the bottom of the formation with two pacemakers running behind him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wired-1)[\[27\]](#refnote-Wired)
- Hydration was provided by a team coordinator on a bicycle, and not via the usual water station method, in order to save time.

The Breaking2 attempt had been held behind closed doors at Monza with just a few press and Nike employees present. Kipchoge missed the presence of a crowd there and requested that the public be allowed to attend the Ineos 1:59 Challenge.[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39)
