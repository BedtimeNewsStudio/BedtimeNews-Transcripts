**Amado Carrillo Fuentes** ([/fuˈɛntəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); December 17, 1954 – July 4, 1997) was a Mexican [drug lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_lord "Drug lord"). He seized control of the [Juárez Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju%C3%A1rez_Cartel "Juárez Cartel") after assassinating his boss [Rafael Aguilar Guajardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Aguilar_Guajardo "Rafael Aguilar Guajardo").[&#8202;](#ref-back-frontera-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-frontera)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gonzalez-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Gonzalez) Amado Carrillo became known as " _El Señor de Los Cielos_" ("The Lord of the Skies"), because of the large fleet of jets he used to transport drugs. He was also known for [laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_laundering "Money laundering") money via [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia"), to finance this fleet.

He died in July 1997, in a Mexican hospital, after undergoing extensive [plastic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_surgery "Plastic surgery") to change his appearance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6) In his final days, Carrillo was being tracked by Mexican and U.S. authorities.

Amado Carrillo Fuentes was assessed to be worth around $25 billion at the time of his death.

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Amado_Carrillo_Fuentes&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Carrillo was born to Walter Vicente Carrillo Vega and Aurora Fuentes in Guamuchilito, [Navolato, Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navolato,_Sinaloa "Navolato, Sinaloa"), Mexico. He had eleven siblings.

Carrillo was the nephew of [Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Fonseca_Carrillo "Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo"), also known as "Don Neto", the [Guadalajara Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara_Cartel "Guadalajara Cartel") leader. Amado got his start in the drug business under the tutelage of his Uncle Ernesto and later brought in his brothers, and eventually his son, Vicente José Carrillo Leyva.

Carrillo's father died in April 1986. Carrillo's brother, Cipriano Carrillo Fuentes, died in 1989 under mysterious circumstances.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Amado_Carrillo_Fuentes&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

Initially, Carrillo was part of the [Guadalajara Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara_Cartel "Guadalajara Cartel"), sent to [Ojinaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojinaga "Ojinaga"), Chihuahua to oversee the [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine") shipments of his Uncle, [Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Fonseca_Carrillo "Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo") ("Don Neto"), and to learn about border operations from [Pablo Acosta Villarreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Acosta_Villarreal "Pablo Acosta Villarreal") ("El Zorro de Ojinaga"; "The Ojinaga Fox") and [Rafael Aguilar Guajardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Aguilar_Guajardo "Rafael Aguilar Guajardo"). Later, Carrillo worked with [Pablo Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Escobar "Pablo Escobar") and the [Cali Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali_Cartel "Cali Cartel") smuggling drugs from [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia") to Mexico and the United States. He also worked with " [El Chapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Chapo "El Chapo")" (Joaquín Guzmán Loera), the [Arellano Félix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arellano_F%C3%A9lix "Arellano Félix") family, and the [Beltrán Leyva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltr%C3%A1n_Leyva "Beltrán Leyva") organization.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-usdoj.gov-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-usdoj.gov)

During his tenure, Carrillo reportedly built a multibillion-dollar drug empire. It was estimated that he may have made over $25 billion in revenue over the course of his career.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

## Death

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Amado_Carrillo_Fuentes&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Death")\]

The pressure to capture Carrillo intensified among U.S. and Mexican authorities after people in [Morelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morelos "Morelos") state began silent marches against Governor Jorge Carrillo Olea and his presumed complacency with drug-related violence. Carrillo Fuentes owned a house three blocks from the governor's official residence and regularly held narco-fiestas in the [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality "Municipality") of [Tetecala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetecala "Tetecala").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) Governor Carrillo Olea was forced to resign and was arrested; this type of pressure may have convinced Carrillo Fuentes to undergo facial [plastic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_surgery "Plastic surgery") and [abdominal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdomen "Abdomen") surgery [liposuction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liposuction "Liposuction") to change his appearance. Subsequently, on July 4, 1997, Carrillo decided to move forward with the operation at Santa Mónica Hospital in [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City "Mexico City"). During his operation however, some complications arose, which were either caused by a certain medication or a malfunctioning respirator. Carrillo died due to said complications in the early hours of July 5, 1997. His family shared a different story, having told reporters that Carrillo had suffered a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack "Heart attack") whilst under [anesthesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anesthesia "Anesthesia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

Two of Carrillo Fuentes's bodyguards were in the operating room during the procedure. On November 7, 1997, the two surgeons who performed Carrillo's surgery were found dead, encased in concrete inside steel drums, with their bodies showing signs of [torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torture "Torture"). A third body was also found in a matching barrel, just as mangled as the other two, and is presumed to have been a third doctor who assisted in the surgery. This body however, is unidentifiable, and not confirmed to have been the third surgeon.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) On November 8, Carrillo's [corpse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadaver "Cadaver") was displayed for a [wake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_(ceremony) "Wake (ceremony)") at the [Federal Judicial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Judicial_Police "Federal Judicial Police")'s [morgue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgue "Morgue") in Mexico City.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

## Juárez Cartel after Carrillo

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Amado_Carrillo_Fuentes&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Juárez Cartel after Carrillo")\]

On the night of August 3, 1997, at around 9:30p.m., four drug traffickers walked into a restaurant in [Ciudad Juárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Ju%C3%A1rez "Ciudad Juárez"), pulled out their guns, and opened fire on five diners, killing them instantly.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6restaurante-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-6restaurante) Police estimated that more than 100 bullet casings were found at the crime scene. According to a report issued by the _[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")_, four men went to the restaurant carrying at least two [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47 "AK-47") automatic rifles while others stood at the doorstep.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6restaurante-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-6restaurante)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

On their way out, the gunmen claimed another victim,[&#8202;](#ref-back-sharp-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-sharp) [Armando Olague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Olague?action=edit&redlink=1 "Armando Olague (page does not exist)"), a prison official and off-duty law enforcement officer who was gunned down outside the restaurant after he had walked from a nearby bar to investigate the shooting. Reportedly, Olague had run into the restaurant from across the street with a gun in his hand to check out the commotion. It was later determined that Olague was also a known [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant "Lieutenant") of the Juárez Cartel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sharp-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-sharp)

Mexican authorities declined to comment on the motives behind the killing, stating the shootout was not linked to Carrillo's death. Nonetheless, it was later stated that the perpetrators were gunmen of the [Tijuana Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_Cartel "Tijuana Cartel").[&#8202;](#ref-back-6restaurante-2)[\[17\]](#refnote-6restaurante)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)

Although confrontations between drug traffickers were common in Ciudad Juárez, they rarely occurred in public places. What happened in the restaurant threatened to usher in a new era of border crime in the city.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sharp-2)[\[19\]](#refnote-sharp)

In Ciudad Juárez, the Office of the Mexican Attorney-General ( [PGR](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procuradur%C3%ADa%20General%20de%20la%20Rep%C3%BAblica%20(M%C3%A9xico) "es:Procuraduría General de la República (México)")) seized warehouses that they believed the cartel used to store weapons and cocaine. They also seized over 60 properties all over Mexico belonging to Carrillo and began an investigation into his dealings with police and government officials. Officials also froze bank accounts amounting to $10 billion belonging to Carrillo.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) In April 2009, Mexican authorities arrested Carrillo's son, [Vicente Carrillo Leyva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Carrillo_Leyva "Vicente Carrillo Leyva").[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

## Funeral

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Amado_Carrillo_Fuentes&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Funeral")\]

Carrillo was given a large and lavish, expensive funeral in Guamuchilito, Sinaloa. In 2006, [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Sonora "Governor of Sonora") [Eduardo Bours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Bours "Eduardo Bours") asked the federal government to tear down Carrillo's mansion in [Hermosillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermosillo "Hermosillo"), Sonora.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)
