Panel on Environmental Affairs

Background brief on the North East New Territories Landfill 

prepared by the Legislative Council Secretariat 

Purpose 

1. This paper provides background information on the North East New Territories (“NENT”) Landfill. It also gives a brief account of the views and concerns expressed by Members when issues relating to this landfill were discussed by relevant committees of the Legislative Council (“LegCo”) in recent years. 

Background 

Strategic landfills in Hong Kong 

2.  Landfills are part of the waste management chain in Hong Kong. Hong Kong had operated 13 landfills which are now closed and some have already been restored for community greening and activities. Today, there are three strategic landfills, namely, the Southeast New Territories (“SENT”) Landfill at Tseung Kwan O, NENT Landfill at Ta Kwu Ling, and the West New Territories (“WENT”) Landfill at Tuen Mun. 1 Their locations were chosen in light of the development needs of various districts and transport optimization in terms of emissions and costs for waste arising from all over the city. They are equipped with state-of-art liners, leachate collection and treatment systems, landfill gas management systems, and surface and ground water management systems.    

> 1The NENT Landfill and WENT Landfill receive municipal solid waste, construction waste and special waste, whereas the SENT Landfill receives only construction waste since June 2016. Among these three landfills, the WENT Landfill is the only one that can receive waste transported by sea.

- 2 -

North East New Territories (“NENT”) Landfill 

3. The NENT Landfill commenced operation in June 1995. The contractual arrangement and financial details of this facility are given in Appendix 1 . As the NENT Landfill was anticipated to be exhausted, and large-scale waste-to-energy (“WtE”) facilities had yet to come on stream, 2 the Administration sought the approval of the Finance Committee on 12 December 2014 to extend the NENT Landfill by about 70 hectares of land, at an estimated cost of $7,510 million in money-of-the-day prices for the design, construction and restoration of the extension project. 3 The estimated operating life of the landfill extension is about 10 years, which may vary according to future development such as the extent of waste reduction. 

Vision of “Zero Landfill” under the Waste Blueprint for Hong Kong 2035 

4.  In February 2021, the Environment Bureau (renamed as the Environment and Ecology Bureau from 1 July 2022) announced the Waste Blueprint for Hong Kong 2035 (“the Waste Blueprint”), setting out the vision of “Waste  Reduction ‧Resources Circulation ‧Zero Landfill”, and related strategies, goals and measures to tackle the challenge of waste management up to 2035. To achieve this vision, the Administration sets out two main goals under the Waste Blueprint: 

(a) medium-term goal - to gradually reduce the per capita municipal solid waste (“MSW”) disposal rate by 40-45% through implementing MSW charging, together with the launch of other policies and legislation, waste reduction and recycling initiatives, as well as publicity and education campaigns, and raise the recovery rate to about 55% by implementing multiple measures promoting waste separation and complementing with WtE or waste-to-resources (“WtR”) facilities; and 

(b) long-term goal - to develop adequate WtE facilities, so as to move away from the reliance on landfills for MSW disposal and achieve “Zero Landfill” in around 2035. By then, there should only be limited non-incinerable and non-recyclable waste that requires direct disposal at landfills.                

> 2WtE is the process of generating energy in the form of electricity and/or heat from the primary treatment of waste, or the processing of waste into a fuel source.
> 3The contract of NENT Landfill extension was tendered in 2020 and awarded in January 2022. According to the Administration’s paper for seeking the funding approval of the Finance Committee on the project in December 2014, the extension was then estimated to be completed in around 2028 (including about two years of restoration works after the landfill’s exhaustion). - 3 -

Development of waste-to-energy facilities 

5. To achieve the goal of moving away from the reliance on landfills for MSW disposal by around 2035, the Administration is on one hand promoting waste reduction at source and various means of recycling, and on the other hand 

expediting the development of sufficient  WtE  facilities as set out below, with a view to converting unavoidable and non-recyclable MSW into resources comprehensively: 

(a)  the Integrated Waste Management Facilities Phase 1 (“I·PARK1”), which is now being built near Shek Kwu Chau, will be the first  WtE facility that adopts advanced incineration technology to treat MSW in  Hong Kong. This facility is targeted for commissioning in 2025 with a  daily  treatment capacity of 3 000 tonnes of MSW; 

(b) the investigation and design study for the development of the Integrated Waste Management  Facilities Phase 2 (“ I·PARK2 ”) in Tsang Tsui middle ash lagoon in Tuen Mun will be completed in 2025. The works contract for I·PARK2 is expected to be tendered in the second quarter of 2025, with an aim to commence operation in early 2030s with a daily treatment capacity of not less than 4 000 tonnes of MSW; and 

(c) as  promulgated in the 2022 Policy Address, the Administration will study developing a third modern WtE incinerator (i.e. Integrated Waste Management Facilities Phase 3 (“I·PARK3”)) in the Northern Metropolis to tie in with the future urban development of Hong Kong. 

Major views and concerns expressed by Members 

6.  Members discussed issues relating to landfills (including the NENT Landfill) and development of WtE facilities at the meetings of the Panel on Environmental Affairs (“EA Panel”) in connection with the Administration’s waste management strategies and relevant policy initiatives under Policy Addresses, and at the special meetings of the Finance Committee to examine the Estimates of Expenditure in recent years. Related issues were raised by Members when the Subcommittee to Study Policy Issues Relating to Municipal Solid Waste Charging, Recovery and Recycling discussed the development of WtE/WtR infrastructure. Members’ major views and concerns are summarized in the ensuing paragraphs. - 4 -

Management of North East New Territories Landfill 

Odour control 

7. Members expressed concern about the persistent odour problem associated with the operation of NENT Landfill, and conveyed the grievances of local residents and those in some districts of Shenzhen towards this facility. They requested the Administration to strengthen management of the NENT Landfill with a view to minimizing its odour nuisance. 

8. The Administration advised that a number of measures had been put in place to improve the operational standard of the NENT Landfill. For example, the operating area of the landfill had been reduced and deodourizers were regularly sprayed there to reduce the odour concentration. The landfilling process had also been improved according to up-to-date international standards and experience to reduce odour emissions in the process. The Administration had also increased the provision of manpower for conducting regular inspections and checks on the capping layers to fix the leaks caused by rupture; and shortened the operating hours of the landfill. As for the leachate storage lagoon in the non-operating area, while they were uncovered and might give off foul smell in the past, sealing works had been carried out to minimize the odour. 

9. The Administration also advised that apart from ongoing management/supervision by the subject team concerned and monitoring by the Environmental Compliance Division of the Environmental Protection Department (“EPD”), an independent consultant had been appointed to monitor the performance of the NENT Landfill’s operator. The Secretary for Environment and Ecology had also written to the global headquarters of the contractor urging the latter to strengthen its landfill management. Regionally, EPD had all along been actively maintaining close communication and exchanging views with the Shenzhen Municipal Government on the operational management of the NENT Landfill. Shenzhen and Hong Kong would continue to maintain close liaison on related issues. 

Environmental impact on local districts arising from transportation of waste to landfills 

10. Members expressed concern about the traffic impact and other nuisances arising from transportation of waste to landfills on the local districts concerned. Referring to the some 400 and 70 vehicle trips made by refuse collection vehicles and container trucks daily on average to transport waste from the Shatin Transfer Station to the NENT Landfill, Members requested the Administration to consider changing the mode of transportation from land transport to sea transport from this refuse transfer station (“RTS”). - 5 -

11. The Administration advised that RTSs were set up in various places as refuse collection points. Refuse from RTSs was basically all containerized and would not cause pollution or other problems. The Administration concurred that the distribution of RTSs would be crucial to minimizing the pollution and nuisance caused by refuse transfer. In this connection, the Administration would review afresh the locations of RTSs in connection with the development of WtE facilities. 

Development of waste-to-energy infrastructure 

12. As landfilling was not a sustainable mode of waste management,  Members criticized the Administration for its slow progress in developing a network of modern WtE incinerators for moving away from reliance on landfills. Some Members urged the Administration to close down NENT Landfill as early as possible and shelve the extension project. The Administration was requested to study ways to shorten the project timeframe for developing I·PARK2 and I·PARK3 by drawing reference from the development of similar facilities in the Mainland and increase the handling capacity of these facilities. 

13. The Administration advised that it would strive to achieve the “Zero Landfill” goal by adopting a three-pronged approach, namely further promoting waste reduction through the implementation of MSW charging, enhancing recycling support, and developing WtE and WtR facilities. To shorten the construction time of I·PARK1, prefabricated components were used as far as possible in place of onsite construction, and low-noise construction activities were carried out round the clock. For I‧PARK 2, the Administration would carry out the environmental impact assessment and various tasks of pre-construction works in parallel. While the current target was to commence the operation of  I‧PARK 2in early 2030s, the Administration would liaise with the relevant trades and contractors to explore viable measures for expediting the construction of 

I‧PARK 2. The Administration pointed out that before the full commissioning of adequate WtE facilities, Hong Kong still needed landfills for MSW disposal. As the NENT Landfill was expected to be exhausted by 2025- 2026, there was a need to carry out the extension project to increase its capacity during the transition period. Upon the commissioning of I·PARK2, NENT Landfill would cease receiving MSW. 

14. The Administration further advised that it had also been driving the development of other downstream WtE/WtR infrastructures over the past ten years, so as to establish a more comprehensive waste treatment chain and facilitate the transformation of waste into resources, thereby laying the foundation of the development of a circular economy. These facilities included, for example, the T‧PARK (a sludge treatment facility which adopted advanced incineration technology to treat sewage sludge from sewage treatment works), and O ‧PARKs 

(Organic Resources Recovery Centres that converted food waste into electricity and other resources). - 6 -
