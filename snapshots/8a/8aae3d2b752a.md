## What is Pumped Storage Hydropower?

Pumped storage hydropower (PSH) is a type of hydroelectric energy storage. It is a configuration of two water reservoirs at different elevations that can generate power as water moves down from one to the other (discharge), passing through a turbine. The system also requires power as it pumps water back into the upper reservoir (recharge). PSH acts similarly to a giant battery, because it can store power and then release it when needed. The Department of Energy's "Pumped Storage Hydropower" video explains how pumped storage works.

The first known use cases of PSH were found in Italy and Switzerland in the 1890s, and PSH was first used in the United States in 1930. Now, PSH facilities can be found all around the world! According to the [2024 edition of the Hydropower Market Report](https://hydropowermarketreport.ornl.gov/), PSH currently accounts for 88% of all utility-scale energy storage in the United States. America currently has 43 PSH plants and has the potential to add enough new PSH plants to more than double its current PSH capacity.

### Open-Loop Versus Closed-Loop Pumped Storage Hydropower

PSH can be characterized as open-loop or closed-loop. Open-loop PSH has an ongoing hydrologic connection to a natural body of water. With closed-loop PSH, reservoirs are not connected to an outside body of water.

![A chart showing a open-loop pumped storage hydropower system. From top, water sits in an upper reservoir, then travels through a penstock or tunnel into a powerhouse. It passes through a generator/motor, then through a turbine/pump into a lower reservoir](/sites/default/files/styles/full_article_width/public/2024-02/WPTO-Pumped-Storage-Hydropower-web-GR-FY24-tns_open-loop-w150.jpg?itok=HloiYsvR)

Open-loop pumped storage hydropower systems connect a reservoir to a naturally flowing water feature via a tunnel, using a turbine/pump and generator/motor to move water and create electricity.

![A chart showing a closed-loop pumped storage hydropower system. From top, water sits in an upper reservoir, then travels through a penstock or tunnel into a powerhouse. It passes through a generator or motor, then through a turbine or pump into a lower reservoir.](/sites/default/files/styles/full_article_width/public/2024-02/WPTO-Pumped-Storage-Hydropower-web-GR-FY24-tns_closed-loop-w150.jpg?itok=T9CR_nLn)

Closed-loop pumped storage hydropower systems connect two reservoirs without flowing water features via a tunnel, using a turbine/pump and generator/motor to move water and create electricity.

The Hydropower and Hydrokinetic Office (H2O) invests in innovative PSH technologies and research to understand and determine the value of the potential benefits of existing and prospective advanced PSH facilities. Through the [HydroWIRES Initiative](https://www.energy.gov/eere/water/hydrowires-initiative), H2O is currently working on projects designed to evaluate and expand hydropower and PSH’s contribution to grid resilience and reliability.
