## Highlights

In 2025, the median annual equivalised disposable income was 22 638 PPS per inhabitant in the EU, with over a quarter (6 274 PPS) received as social transfers from governments (e.g. pensions, social benefits, etc.).

The at-risk-of-poverty rate anchored to 2019 decreased to 13.0% in 2025, down from 16.5% in 2019.

In 2025, the Gini coefficient for the EU was 29.2, with the highest levels of inequality in terms of disposable income in the EU reported in Bulgaria (37.7), Latvia (35.6) and Lithuania (35.5), and lowest in Slovakia (23.0), Belgium (23.4) and Czechia (24.0).

In 2025, the only EU region with a share of people at risk of poverty exceeding 40% was in France’s overseas departments, while 28 regions had rates below 10%.

This article focuses on income distribution and poverty risk in the [European Union (EU)](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:EU_enlargements "Glossary:EU enlargements") in 2025. It examines median disposable income, the role of social transfers, regional disparities in poverty risk, and income inequality as measured by the [Gini coefficient](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Gini_coefficient "Glossary:Gini coefficient").

All figures are based on [EU statistics on income and living conditions (EU-SILC)](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:EU_statistics_on_income_and_living_conditions_(EU-SILC) "Glossary:EU statistics on income and living conditions (EU-SILC)") available from [Eurostat's online database](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/data/database). EU-SILC data are available for all EU countries, as well as for most of the EFTA and [candidate countries](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Candidate_countries "Glossary:Candidate countries").

## Income distribution

**Median annual disposable income in 2025 highest in Luxembourg and lowest in Hungary**

In 2025, the [median annual disposable income](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Equivalised_disposable_income "Glossary:Equivalised disposable income") was 22 638 PPS ( [purchasing power standard](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Purchasing_power_standard_(PPS) "Glossary:Purchasing power standard (PPS)")) per inhabitant in the EU. Across the EU countries, it ranged from 37 673 PPS in Luxembourg and 32 385 PPS in Austria to 11 958 PPS in Hungary and 12 861 PPS in Romania.

Figure 1 shows the contribution of [social transfers](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Social_transfers "Glossary:Social transfers") to disposable income. This information is split between transfers for pensions and other social transfers, for example, social security benefits and social assistance that aim to alleviate or reduce the risk of poverty.

In 2025, in the EU, social transfers (including pensions) accounted for 6 274 PPS of the median disposable income, 1 694 PPS from transfers other than pensions.

Among the EU countries, there were considerable variations in the contribution made by social transfers to median disposable income in 2025. The largest transfers were observed in Luxembourg, where social transfers (including pensions) increased the median disposable income by 11 104 PPS per inhabitant, followed by Austria (9 356 PPS) and France (8 123 PPS).

In 2025, social transfers excluding pensions contributed more than 2 500 PPS per inhabitant to median disposable income in Luxembourg (3 689), Belgium (2 829 PPS), Austria (2 766 PPS), Ireland (2 675 PPS).

**The at-risk-of-poverty rate in the EU decreased between 2019 and 2025**

The at-risk-of-poverty rate anchored to 2019 decreased across the EU, reaching 13.0% in 2025, compared to 16.5% in 2019 (see Figure 2). This decline reflects an overall improvement in living standards for low-income groups. It suggests that the real equivalised disposable income of these groups, adjusted for inflation, has increased compared to the 2019 baseline, lifting many out of poverty. The largest reductions were observed in Romania (down 17.4 [percentage points (pp)](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Percentage_point "Glossary:Percentage point") to 6.4%) and Bulgaria (down 15.3 pp to 7.3%). The smallest decreases were recorded in Austria (down 1.4 pp to 11.9%) and Denmark (down 1.0 pp to 11.5%).

However, some countries recorded increases in their anchored at-risk-of-poverty rates. Finland saw its rate rise by 0.9 pp to 12.5%, while Germany experienced an increase of 2.1 pp, reaching 16.9% in 2025. France reported a more significant rise of 3.3 pp to 16.9%.

## Income inequality

Median disposable income provides a measure of average living standards, but it does not capture the distribution of income within the population. The [Gini coefficient](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Gini_coefficient) gives the extent to which the distribution of income within a country deviates from a perfectly equal distribution. A Gini value of 100 means that only one person receives all the income in the country, while a Gini value of 0 means that income is distributed equally across the population.

**Income inequality as measured by Gini coefficient above the EU average in 13 EU countries**

In 2025, the Gini coefficient for the EU was 29.2, as shown in Map 1. The highest income disparities among the EU countries were recorded in Bulgaria (37.7), Latvia (35.6) and Lithuania (35.5). In Hungary (29.5) and Malta (29.2) the Gini coefficients were closest to the EU average. Income was more evenly distributed in Slovakia, Belgium, Czechia, Slovenia and Poland, where the Gini coefficient was less than 25.0.

## Data sources

The data used in this article are derived from [EU statistics on income and living conditions (EU-SILC)](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:EU_statistics_on_income_and_living_conditions_(EU-SILC) "Glossary:EU statistics on income and living conditions (EU-SILC)"). EU-SILC data are compiled annually and are the main source of statistics that measure income and living conditions in Europe. It is also the main source of information used to link different aspects relating to the living conditions of households and individuals.

The reference population for the information presented in this article is all private [households](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Private_household "Glossary:Private household") and their current members residing in the territory of an EU Member State (or non-EU member country) at the time of data collection. Persons living in collective households and in institutions are generally excluded from the target population. The data for the EU are population-weighted averages of national data. The reference time for information on income is 2024, while the other households’ characteristics are referenced to 2025.

## Context

To consider differences in household size and composition and thus enable comparisons of income levels across countries and population groups, the concept of [equivalised disposable income](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Equivalised_disposable_income "Glossary:Equivalised disposable income") is used. This measure is based on the total net (also referred to as disposable) household income divided by the number of “equivalent adults” in the household, using a standard (equivalence) scale. Eurostat employs the “modified OECD scale”, which assigns a weight of 1.0 to the household head, 0.5 to each additional adult (aged 14 and over), and 0.3 to each child (aged under 14). The weights are summed to calculate an equivalised household size, which is then used to divide the total household income and determine the equivalised disposable income attributed to each household member.

For the anchored [at-risk-of-poverty](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:At-risk-of-poverty_rate "Glossary:At-risk-of-poverty rate") rate, the equivalised disposable income is compared to a fixed poverty threshold, which is calculated using the income distribution of the base year (2019). This anchored poverty line remains constant over time but is adjusted for inflation to maintain its real value. The anchored poverty threshold enables comparisons across years, providing a consistent baseline to assess changes in living standards for low-income groups.

The median of the equivalised disposable income distribution is typically used in the EU as a key measure for analysing standards of living within each economy. It is simply the income level that divides the population into 2 groups of equal size: one group encompasses half the population with a level of disposable income above the median and the other group encompasses the half of the population with a level of disposable income below the median. The use of the median (rather than the arithmetic mean) avoids any potential distortion that may be caused by the existence of extreme values, such as a few extremely rich households that may raise the arithmetic mean.

The at-risk-of-poverty rate is part of the [at risk of poverty or social exclusion rate (AROPE)](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:At_risk_of_poverty_or_social_exclusion_(AROPE) "Glossary:At risk of poverty or social exclusion (AROPE)"). The AROPE ratio is the share of the total population which is at risk of poverty or social exclusion. The headline target [(EU2030 target)](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:The_European_Pillar_of_Social_Rights_Action_Plan_(EU_2030_targets) "Glossary:The European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan (EU 2030 targets)") on poverty and social inclusion of [the European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:The_European_Pillar_of_Social_Rights_Action_Plan_(EU_2030_targets) "Glossary:The European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan (EU 2030 targets)") is to reduce the number of people at risk of poverty or social exclusion by at least 15 million by 2030. Progress towards this target is monitored through the AROPE rate, published by Eurostat.
