# KH-11 KENNEN

KH-11 KENNEN

Type of American spy satellite

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/KH-11_KENNEN_01.jpg/500px-KH-11_KENNEN_01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KH-11_KENNEN_01.jpg) A conceptual drawing based upon Hubble Space Telescope (HST) layout.[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/KH-11_KENNEN_02.jpg/500px-KH-11_KENNEN_02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KH-11_KENNEN_02.jpg) A conceptual drawing based upon Hubble Space Telescope (HST) layout with internal views.

The **KH-11 KENNEN**[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NRO20160322-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-TSR20120130-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-CIA-RDP83M00914R000700040112-1-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-CIA-RDP74B00415R000100010065-5-4) (later renamed **CRYSTAL**,[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Richelson-5) then **Evolved Enhanced CRYSTAL System**, and codenamed **1010**[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Yenne-6):82 and **Key Hole**[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Yenne-6):82) is a type of [reconnaissance satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_satellite "Reconnaissance satellite") first launched by the American [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office "National Reconnaissance Office") (NRO) in December 1976. Manufactured by [Lockheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Corporation "Lockheed Corporation") in [Sunnyvale, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyvale,_California "Sunnyvale, California"), the KH-11 was the first American spy satellite to use [electro-optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optoelectronics "Optoelectronics") [digital imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_imaging "Digital imaging"), and to offer real-time optical observations.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-7)

Later KH-11 satellites have been referred to by outside observers as KH-11B or KH-12, and by the names "Advanced KENNEN", "Improved Crystal" and "Ikon". Official budget documents refer to the latest generation of electro-optical satellites as _Evolved Enhanced CRYSTAL System_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-FY2013NIP-8) The [Key Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Hole "Key Hole") series was officially discontinued in favor of a random numbering scheme after repeated public references to [KH-7 GAMBIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-7_Gambit "KH-7 Gambit"), [KH-8 GAMBIT 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-8_Gambit_3 "KH-8 Gambit 3"), [KH-9 HEXAGON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-9_Hexagon "KH-9 Hexagon"), and KH-11 KENNEN satellites.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-9)

The capabilities of the KH-11 are highly classified, as are the images they produce. The satellites are believed to have been the source of some imagery of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") and China made public in 1997;\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] images of [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan") and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") made public in 1998 related to the response to the [1998 U.S. embassy bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_U.S._embassy_bombings "1998 U.S. embassy bombings");[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-MERVIN_2000_pp._770%E2%80%93776-10) and a 2019 photo, provided by then-President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"),[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-brumfiel20190830-11) of a failed [Iranian rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safir_(rocket) "Safir (rocket)") launch.

## Program history

Before KENNEN, [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office "National Reconnaissance Office") spy satellites such as [KH-9 HEXAGON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-9_HEXAGON "KH-9 HEXAGON") took photographs on [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographic_film "Photographic film"), which was dropped to Earth in capsules. The satellites' useful life ended when they ran out of film or capsules.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-day20170130-12)

The Film Read-Out [KH-7 GAMBIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-7_Gambit "KH-7 Gambit") (FROG) served as NRO Program A's competitor to NRO Program B's initial [electro-optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-optical "Electro-optical") imagery (EOI) satellite.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-13) After a precursor EOI study under the codeword Zoster, [President Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") on 23 September 1971 approved the development of an EOI satellite codenamed Zaman.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-14) In November 1971, this codeword was changed to Kennen, which is [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English "Middle English") for "to perceive".[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-16) Initial director of the ZAMAN/KENNEN Program Group was Charles R. "Charlie" Roth; he was succeeded in October 1975 by Rutledge P. (Hap) Hazzard.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-17)

The KENNEN system transmits its imagery as data through the [Satellite Data System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Data_System "Satellite Data System") (SDS), a network of [communications satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_satellite "Communications satellite").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Richelson-5)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-18) These digital images were initially processed at a secret National Reconnaissance Office facility dubbed [Area 58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_58 "Area 58") at [Fort Belvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Belvoir "Fort Belvoir") in [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia "Virginia").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-associatepublishers_quasar-20)

In 1999, NRO selected [Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing "Boeing") as the prime contractor for the [Future Imagery Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Imagery_Architecture "Future Imagery Architecture") (FIA) program, which aimed to replace the KH-11 satellites by a more cost-effective constellation of smaller, more capable reconnaissance satellites. After the failure of the FIA in 2005, NRO ordered two more KH-11s from Lockheed.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-DN20090602-21) [USA-224](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA-224 "USA-224"), the first of these, was launched in early 2011 two years ahead of the initial schedule estimate.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NRO20110907-22)

## Design

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/HST_Lockheed_Integration.jpg/500px-HST_Lockheed_Integration.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:HST_Lockheed_Integration.jpg) The Hubble Space Telescope integration at Lockheed.[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fe/KH-11Block3TMAdynamictestunit.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KH-11Block3TMAdynamictestunit.png) A Dynamical Test Unit of KH-11 (unconfirmed) Three Mirror Assembly.

### Initial design specifications

According to [Lew Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Allen "Lew Allen"), the initial key design elements were specified by [Edwin H. Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_H._Land "Edwin H. Land"). They included i) solid state focal plane array, ii) integrated circuits for complex data processing, iii) large, fast optics with a 2.54m (100in) diameter f/2 primary mirror, iv) gigabit/s data link, v) long on-orbit operational lifetime for the imaging satellites, and vi) communication satellites to facilitate close-to-realtime downlink of the images.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Allen_19741127-23)

### Size and mass

KH-11s are believed to resemble the [Hubble Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope "Hubble Space Telescope") in size and shape, as they were shipped in similar containers. Their length is believed to be 19.5 meters, with a diameter of up to 3 meters (120in).[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Richelson-5)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Wade-24) A [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") history of the Hubble,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-25) in discussing the reasons for switching from a 3-meter main mirror to a 2.4-meter (94in) design, states: "In addition, changing to a 2.4-meter mirror would lessen fabrication costs by using manufacturing technologies developed for military spy satellites".

Different versions of the KH-11 vary in mass. Early KH-11s were reported to be comparable in mass to HEXAGON,[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NYTimes19850113-26) i.e. about 12,000kg (26,000lb). Later blocks are believed to have a mass of around 17,000kg (37,000lb)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-FA8811-19-R-0002-27) to 19,600kg (43,200lb).[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-28)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Richelson-5)

### Propulsion module

It has been reported that KH-11s are equipped with a [hydrazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrazine "Hydrazine")-powered propulsion system for orbital adjustments. In order to increase the orbital lifetime of KH-11s, plans existed for refuelling the propulsion module during service visits by the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle").[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NYTimes19850113-26) It has been speculated that the propulsion module is related to Lockheed's [Satellite Support Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Support_Bus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Satellite Support Bus") (SSB), which had been derived from the [Satellite Control Section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-9_Hexagon "KH-9 Hexagon") (SCS) developed by Lockheed for KH-9.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-TheSpaceReview20110207-29)

### Optical Telescope Assembly

A [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA "CIA") history states that the primary mirror on the first KH-11s measured 2.34 meters (92in), but sizes increased in later versions.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Richelson-5) NRO led the development of a computer controlled mirror polishing technique, which was subsequently also used for the polishing of the primary mirror of the Hubble Space Telescope.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-GJTenet20000927-30)

Later satellites had larger mirrors, with a diameter of around 2.9 to 3.1 meters (110 to 120in).[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-31) _[Jane's Defence Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Defence_Weekly "Jane's Defence Weekly")_ indicates that the secondary mirror in the [Cassegrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassegrain_reflector "Cassegrain reflector") [reflecting telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflecting_telescope "Reflecting telescope") system could be moved, allowing images to be taken from angles unusual for a satellite. Also, there are indications that the satellite can take images every five seconds.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Imaging sensors and camera modes

The initial KH-11 camera system offered frame and strip modes.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-32) The focal plane was equipped with an array of light-sensitive silicon diodes, which converted brightness values to electrical signals. The packaging density was sufficiently high (several hundred diodes per inch) to match the ground sample distance of the [CORONA satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_(satellite) "Corona (satellite)"). The recorded digital signal was encrypted and transmitted to a ground station in near real time, and written to film by means of a laser in order to recreate the recorded image.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-COS099312-33) The first [charge-coupled device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge-coupled_device "Charge-coupled device") (CCD) detectors for KH-11 were developed by [Westinghouse Electric Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_Corporation "Westinghouse Electric Corporation") at their Baltimore facility in the later 1970s.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-OralHistoriesRobertKohler-34) KH-11 Block II might have been the first reconnaissance satellite equipped for imaging with an 800 × 800 pixels CCD.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-35) Later block satellites may include [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence "Signals intelligence") capabilities and greater sensitivity in broader [light spectrums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_spectrum "Electromagnetic spectrum") (probably into [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared "Infrared")).[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-CBOPublication528-36)

### Communications

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/KENNEN_InitialConfig_1977Jan.png/500px-KENNEN_InitialConfig_1977Jan.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KENNEN_InitialConfig_1977Jan.png) KENNEN Initial Configuration with 1 imaging and 2 relay satellites (January 1977)

Communication to and data downloads from KH-11 satellites are routed through a constellation of communication relay satellites in higher orbits. The initial communications relay payload is believed to have operated at a frequency of 60GHz, as radio emission at this frequency is blocked by Earth's atmosphere, and thus not detectable from the ground. Launch of the initial two [Satellite Data System satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Data_System "Satellite Data System") occurred in June and August 1976, i.e. ahead of the first launch of a KH-11 satellite in late 1976.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-TheSpaceReview20180267-37)
One of the initial on-orbit challenges were failures of the [Traveling-wave tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling-wave_tube "Traveling-wave tube"), which amplified the communications signals sent from the imaging satellite to the relay satellites, and from the relay satellites to the ground stations. During crossings of the [ionosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionosphere "Ionosphere"), ions could build up on the outside of the tubes, which were operated at 14,000 volts. This resulted in repeated sparking and deposition of carbon traces inside the tubes, ultimately shorting them out. The issue could be abated by changing the orbiting satellite's orientation during crossing of the ionosphere, and was finally solved by better shielding of the tubes in follow-up satellites.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-OralHistoriesRobertKohler-34)
Ground stations for the receipt of KH-11 data have been reported to be located in [Fort Belvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Belvoir "Fort Belvoir"), VA, the former [Buckley Air National Guard Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckley_Air_National_Guard_Base "Buckley Air National Guard Base"), CO, and [Kapaun Air Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapaun_Air_Station "Kapaun Air Station"), Germany.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-HistoricalDictAirIntel-38)

### Resolution and ground sample distance

A perfect 2.4-meter (94in) [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror "Mirror") observing in the visual spectrum (i.e. at a wavelength of 500nm) has a [diffraction limited resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_resolution#Single_telescope "Angular resolution") of around 0.05 [arcsec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_trigonometric_functions "Inverse trigonometric functions"), which from an orbital altitude of 250km (160mi) corresponds to a [ground sample distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_sample_distance "Ground sample distance") of 6cm (2.4in). [Operational resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_seeing "Astronomical seeing") should be worse due to effects of the [atmospheric turbulence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fried_parameter "Fried parameter").[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-39) Astronomer [Clifford Stoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Stoll "Clifford Stoll") estimates that such a telescope could resolve up to "a couple inches. Not quite good enough to recognize a face".[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-stoll1989-40)

### KH-11 generations

Five generations of U.S. electro-optical reconnaissance have been identified:[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-sfr20090622-41)[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-nsf20110120-42)

#### Block I

Block I refer to the original KH-11 satellite, of which five were launched between 19 December 1976 and 17 November 1982.

#### Block II

The three Block II satellites are in the open literature referred to as KH-11B, the alleged _DRAGON_ codename, or _CRYSTAL_, and are believed to be capable of taking infrared images in addition to optical observations.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-GS20070424-43) The first or second Block II satellite was lost in a launch failure.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-nsf20110120-42)

#### Block III

Four Block III satellites, commonly called KH-12 or _Improved CRYSTAL_ were launched between November 1992 and October 2001. The name "Improved CRYSTAL" refers to the "Improved Metric CRYSTAL System" (IMCS). _Metric_ describes the capability to fix [Datum references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datum_reference "Datum reference") (markings) in an image relative to the [World Geodetic System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Geodetic_System "World Geodetic System") for mapping purposes.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-F-2019-00076_C05119876-45) Another improvement was an eightfold increase in the download rate compared to earlier models to facilitate improved real-time access and increased area coverage.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-46) From Block III on, the typical lifetime of the satellites increased to about 15 years, possibly related to a higher lift-off mass, which facilitates larger fuel reserves for countering atmospheric drag.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-seesat_Dec-2018_0058-47)

#### Block IV

Three electro-optical satellites launched in October 2005, January 2011, and August 2013 are attributed to Block IV.

#### Block V

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/DeltaIVHeavy_NROL82_launch.jpg/500px-DeltaIVHeavy_NROL82_launch.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:DeltaIVHeavy_NROL82_launch.jpg) Launch of NROL-82 on Delta IV Heavy

A new generation of clandestine communications satellites launched to inclined geosynchronous orbits have led to speculations that these are in support of Block V electro-optical satellites scheduled for launch in late 2018 (NROL-71) and 2021 (NROL-82).[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-48) The two satellites have been built by Lockheed Martin Space Systems, have a primary mirror with a diameter of 2.4meters, and are evolutionary upgrades to the previous blocks built by Lockheed.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-spacenews_20091020-49)

Based on the published hazard areas for the launch, an orbital inclination of 74° has been deduced for NROL-71. This could indicate that NROL-71 is targeted for a [Type II Multi Sun-Synchronous Orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_II_Multi_Sun-Synchronous_Orbit?action=edit&redlink=1 "Type II Multi Sun-Synchronous Orbit"),[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-MSSO_20181207-50) which would enable the satellite to study the ground at a range of local hour effects (shadow direction and length, daily activities, etc.).[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-seesat_Dec-2018_0040-51)[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-nsfw_20181207-52)

## Derivatives

The _[Misty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misty_(satellite) "Misty (satellite)")_ satellite is believed to have been derived from the KH-11, but modified to make it [invisible to radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_technology "Stealth technology"), and hard to detect visually. The first Misty satellite, [USA-53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA-53 "USA-53"), was released by the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") [_Atlantis_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Atlantis "Space Shuttle Atlantis") on mission [STS-36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-36 "STS-36") in 1990. The [USA-144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA-144 "USA-144") satellite, launched on 22 May 1999 by a [Titan IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_IV "Titan IV") B from [Vandenberg Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandenberg_Space_Force_Base "Vandenberg Space Force Base") may have been a second Misty satellite,[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-53) or an [Enhanced Imaging System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_Imaging_System "Enhanced Imaging System") spacecraft. The satellites are sometimes identified as KH-12s.

In January 2011, [NRO donated to NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_National_Reconnaissance_Office_space_telescope_donation_to_NASA "2012 National Reconnaissance Office space telescope donation to NASA") two space Optical Telescope Assemblies with 2.4 meters (94in) diameter primary mirrors,[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-WP20120604-54)[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NYT20120604-55)[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-Dressler20120604-56)[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-57) similar in size to the Hubble Space Telescope, yet with steerable secondary mirrors and shorter focal length (resulting in a wider field of view). These were initially believed to be KH-11 series "extra hardware", but were later attributed to the cancelled [Future Imaging Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Imaging_Architecture "Future Imaging Architecture") program.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-58) The mirrors are to be used by NASA as the primary and spare for the [Roman Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Grace_Roman_Space_Telescope "Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope").

## Compromises

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Janeskh11leakedphoto.jpg/500px-Janeskh11leakedphoto.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Janeskh11leakedphoto.jpg) A KH-11 image of the [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Shipyard "Black Sea Shipyard") of a [Kiev-class aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev-class_aircraft_carrier "Kiev-class aircraft carrier"), as published by Jane's in 1984.[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/2019-08-29_Safir_launch_failure.jpg/500px-2019-08-29_Safir_launch_failure.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2019-08-29_Safir_launch_failure.jpg) An image (resolution ~10 cm/px) of the damaged launch pad at [Imam Khomeini Spaceport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Khomeini_Spaceport "Imam Khomeini Spaceport") after a rocket explosion on 29 August 2019, speculated as being taken by a KH-11.

In 1978, a young CIA employee named [William Kampiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kampiles "William Kampiles") was accused of selling a KH-11 System Technical Manual describing design and operation to the Soviets. Kampiles was convicted of espionage and initially sentenced to 40 years in prison.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-59)[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-60) Later, this term was reduced, and after serving 18 years, Kampiles was released in 1996.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-jonathonpollard_kampiles-61)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-bop_kampiles-62)

In 1984 [Samuel Loring Morison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Loring_Morison "Samuel Loring Morison"), an intelligence analyst at the Naval Intelligence Support Center, forwarded three classified images taken by KH-11 to the publication _[Jane's Defence Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Defence_Weekly "Jane's Defence Weekly")_. In 1985, Morison was convicted in Federal Court on two counts of espionage and two counts of theft of government property, and was sentenced to two years in prison.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-63) He was pardoned by President [Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") in 2001.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-lewis-64)

In 2019 [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"), as President of the United States, [tweeted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_on_social_media "Donald Trump on social media") a classified image of the aftermath of a failed test of Iran's [Safir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safir_(rocket) "Safir (rocket)") rocket,[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-brumfiel20190830-11) which some believe was taken from the [USA-224](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA-224 "USA-224") satellite.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-65)[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-66)

In [Seymour Hersh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Hersh "Seymour Hersh")'s book _The Samson Option: Israel's Nuclear Arsenal & American Foreign Policy_ Ari Ben-Menashe says that Israel had stolen images from the KH-11 in order to target missiles at the Soviet Union.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-67)

## KH-11 missions

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/KH-11_Keyhole_constellation%2C_September_2013.png/500px-KH-11_Keyhole_constellation%2C_September_2013.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KH-11_Keyhole_constellation,_September_2013.png) All KH-11 Keyhole satellites on orbit, orbital constellation status of September 2013.

Nine KH-11 satellites were launched between 1976 and 1990 aboard [Titan-3D and Titan-34D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_(rocket_family) "Titan (rocket family)") launch vehicles, with one launch failure. For the following five satellite launches between 1992 and 2005, a [Titan IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_IV "Titan IV") launch vehicle was used. The three most recent launches since 2011 were carried out by [Delta IV Heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_IV_Heavy "Delta IV Heavy") launch vehicles. The KH-11 replaced the [KH-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-9 "KH-9") film return satellite, among others, the last of which was lost in a liftoff explosion in 1986.

All KH-11 satellites are in either of two standard planes in [Sun-synchronous orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit "Sun-synchronous orbit"). As shadows help to discern ground features, satellites in a standard plane east of a noon/midnight orbit observe the ground at local afternoon hours, while satellites in a western plane observe the ground at local morning hours.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-GS20070425-68)[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-satobs20090713-69)[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-SatTrackCam1-70) Historically launches have therefore been timed to occur either about two hours before or one hour after local noon (or midnight), respectively.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-nsf20110120-42) The orbits are such that ground-tracks repeat after a certain number of days, currently each four days for the primary satellites in the East and West orbital plane.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-SatTrackCam2-71)

The constellation consists of two primary and two secondary satellites (one primary and one secondary per plane). The orbital planes of the two primary satellites in the East and West plane are separated by 48° to 50°. The orbital plane of the secondary satellite in the East plane is located 20° to the east of the primary satellite, while the orbital plane of the secondary satellite in the West plane is located 10° to the west of the primary satellite.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-SatTrackCam2-71)[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-SatTrackCam3-72)

## Cost

Estimated unit costs, including launch and in 1990 dollars, range from [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") 1.25to[US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") 1.75billion (inflation adjusted $3.08to$4.31billion in 2025).[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-CBOPublication528-36)

According to US Senator [Kit Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_Bond "Kit Bond") initial budget estimates for each of the two legacy KH-11 satellites ordered from Lockheed in 2005 were higher than for the latest [_Nimitz_-class aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimitz_class_aircraft_carrier "Nimitz class aircraft carrier") (CVN-77)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-DN20090602-21) with its projected procurement cost of $6.35billion as of May2005.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NHC20050525-106) In 2011, after the launch of [USA-224](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA-224 "USA-224"), DNRO [Bruce Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_A._Carlson "Bruce A. Carlson") announced that the procurement cost for the satellite had been $2billion under the initial budget estimate, which would put it at about $4.4billion (inflation adjusted $6.3billion in 2025).[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NRO20110907-22)

In April 2014, the NRO assigned a "worth more than $5billion" to the final two legacy KH-11 satellites.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-11_KENNEN#cite_note-NROPR201402-107)
