**Hardinge Bridge** is a steel railway [truss bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truss_bridge "Truss bridge") over the [Padma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_River "Padma River") located at [Ishwardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishwardi_Upazila "Ishwardi Upazila"), [Pabna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pabna "Pabna") and [Bheramara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bheramara "Bheramara"), [Kushtia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushtia "Kushtia") in Bangladesh. It is named after [Lord Hardinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hardinge,_1st_Baron_Hardinge_of_Penshurst "Charles Hardinge, 1st Baron Hardinge of Penshurst"), who was the [Viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy "Viceroy") of India from 1910 to 1916. The bridge is 1.8km (1.1mi) long.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Structurae-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-Structurae)

## Construction

Construction of the [through truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_truss "Through truss") bridge began in 1910,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Structurae-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-Structurae) though it was proposed at least 20 years earlier. It was constructed by Braithwaite and Kirk Company, [West Bromwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich "West Bromwich"), England, based on design of Sir [Alexander Meadows Rendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Meadows_Rendel "Alexander Meadows Rendel"). It was completed in 1912,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Structurae-2)[\[2\]](#refnote-Structurae) and trains started moving on it in 1915.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Engineering_News-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Engineering_News) Lord Hardinge officially opened the bridge on 4 March of that year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-:0)

## Brief history

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Ruppur_Nuclear_Power_Plant_%2C_Ruppur%2C_Pabna.jpg/500px-Ruppur_Nuclear_Power_Plant_%2C_Ruppur%2C_Pabna.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ruppur_Nuclear_Power_Plant_,_Ruppur,_Pabna.jpg) [Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooppur_Nuclear_Power_Plant "Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant"), Hardinge Bridge, and [Lalon Shah Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalon_Shah_Bridge "Lalon Shah Bridge") in the same frame on a moonlit night[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Construction_of_Hardinge_Bridge_1911.jpg/500px-Construction_of_Hardinge_Bridge_1911.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Construction_of_Hardinge_Bridge_1911.jpg) Hardinge Bridge construction (1910)

The construction of a railway bridge over the Padma was proposed in 1889 by the Eastern Bengal Railway for easier communication between [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta "Calcutta") and the then [Eastern Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal "East Bengal") and [Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam "Assam"). In 1902, Sir FJE Spring prepared a report on the bridge. A technical committee reported that a bridge could be constructed at Sara crossing the lower [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges") between the Paksey and [Bheramara Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bheramara_Upazila "Bheramara Upazila") stations on the broad gauge railway from [Khulna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khulna "Khulna") to [Parbatipur Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parbatipur_Upazila "Parbatipur Upazila"). The construction of the bridge started in 1910 and finished two years later.[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) The bridge comprises 15 steel trusses. The main girders are modified "Petit" type.

The most difficult task of the operation was to prevent bank erosion and to make the river flow permanently under the bridge. For this, two guide banks of the "Bell- [bund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunding "Bunding")" type, named after J. R. Bell were built on either side, each extending 910 metres (3,000ft) upstream and 300 metres (1,000ft) downstream from the bridge.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Engineering_News-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-Engineering_News) The ends of the river banks were curved inward and heavily pitched with stone.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Engineering_News-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-Engineering_News)

Hardinge Bridge was severely damaged during the [Liberation War of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_War_of_Bangladesh "Liberation War of Bangladesh") of 1971. On 13 December 1971, the [Indian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Air_Force "Indian Air Force") bombed the 4th guarder from the Paksey side. As the [Pakistani army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_army "Pakistani army") was on retreat towards [Jessore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessore_(city) "Jessore (city)") (their last stronghold), Hardinge Bridge was strategically very important. The allied force damaged the bridge. The Japanese government helped to reconstruct the bridge,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-:0) which was reopened to public passage on 12 October 1972.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

It is the second longest railway bridge in [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Another bridge named [Lalon Shah Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalon_Shah_Bridge "Lalon Shah Bridge") for road transport beside the Hardinge Bridge has recently been constructed.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]
