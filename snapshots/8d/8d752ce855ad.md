The **Sultanate of Sug (Sulu)** ( [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language "Tausug language"): _Kasultanan sin Sūg_; [Central Sama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Sama_language "Central Sama language"): _Kasultanan Sūg_ or _Kasultanan Sūk_; [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language "Malay language"): _Kesultanan Suluk_; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language "Filipino language"): _Kasultanan ng Sulu_ or _Sultanato ng Sulu_) was a [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam "Sunni Islam") [Tausūg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taus%C5%ABg_people "Tausūg people")[\[b\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-17) [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_state "Sovereign state") that ruled the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago "Sulu Archipelago"), coastal areas of [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City"), and certain portions of [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan "Palawan") in today's [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), alongside parts of present-day [Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabah "Sabah"), [North Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kalimantan "North Kalimantan") and in north eastern parts of Borneo and Berau.

The sultanate was founded either on November 17, 1405 or 1457[\[c\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-22) by [Johore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johore "Johore")-born explorer and Sunni [religious scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_scholar "Religious scholar") [Sharif ul-Hashim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_ul-H%C4%81shim_of_Sulu "Sharif ul-Hāshim of Sulu"), the 1st Sultan of Sulu. Paduka _Mahasari Maulana al Sultan Sharif ul-Hashim_ became his full [regnal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regnal_name "Regnal name"); _Sharif-ul Hashim_ is his abbreviated name. He settled in Indanan, Buansa, [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu "Sulu").[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-23)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Genealogy_of_Sultan_Sharif_Ul-Hashim_of_Sulu_Sultanate-24) The sultanate gained its independence from the [The Sultanate of Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Brunei_(1368%E2%80%931888) "Sultanate of Brunei (1368–1888)") in 1578.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-RingSalkin1996-25)

At its peak, it stretched over the islands that bordered the western peninsula of [Zamboanga Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula "Zamboanga Peninsula") in [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao") in the east to [Palawan/Palawanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan "Palawan") located in the Philippines in the north. It also covered areas in the northeast of [Borneo (Sarawak, Berau, Sabah and etc.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo"), stretching from [Marudu Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marudu_Bay "Marudu Bay"), [Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabah ## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Pre-establishment

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Pre-establishment")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Sulu_archipelago.png/250px-Sulu_archipelago.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sulu_archipelago.png) Map of the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago "Sulu Archipelago")

See also: [Hinduism in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_the_Philippines "Hinduism in the Philippines"), [Religion in pre-colonial Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_pre-colonial_Philippines "Religion in pre-colonial Philippines"), [Indosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indosphere "Indosphere"), and [Indianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianisation "Indianisation")

The present area of the Sultanate of Sulu was once under the influence of the [Bruneian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Empire "Bruneian Empire") before it gained its own independence in 1578.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-RingSalkin1996-25) During the 13th century the people of Sulu began migrating to present-day [Zamboanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City") and [Sulu archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_archipelago "Sulu archipelago") from their homelands in northeastern Mindanao. Scott (1994) writes that the Sulu are the descendants of ancient [Butuanons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuanon_people "Butuanon people") and [Surigaonons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_people "Surigaonon people") from the [Rajahnate of Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan_(historical_polity) "Butuan (historical polity)"), which was then Hindu, like pre-islamic Sulu. They moved south and established a spice trading port in Sulu. Sultan [Batarah Shah Tengah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batarah_Shah_Tengah "Batarah Shah Tengah"), who ruled as sultan in 1600, was said to be an actual native of Butuan.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-FOOTNOTEScott1994164-36) The Butuanon-Surigaonon origins of the Tausugs are suggested by the relationship of their languages, as the Butuanon, Surigaonon and Tausug languages are all members of the [Southern sub-family of Visayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisayan_languages "Bisayan languages"). Later, the earliest known settlement in this area soon to be occupied by the sultanate was in [Maimbung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimbung,_Sulu "Maimbung, Sulu"), [Jolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolo "Jolo"). During this time, Sulu was called [Lupah Sug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupah_Sug "Lupah Sug").[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Genealogy_of_Sultan_Sharif_Ul-Hashim_of_Sulu_Sultanate-24) The principality of [Maimbung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimbung "Maimbung"), populated by Buranun people (or _Budanon_, literally means "mountain-dwellers"), was first ruled by a certain rajah who assumed the title Rajah Sipad the Older. According to Majul, the origins of the title _rajah sipad_ originated from the Hindu [sri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri "Sri") pada, which symbolises authority.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-ibrahim_51-37) The principality was instituted and governed using the system of rajahs. Sipad the Older was succeeded by Sipad the Younger.

Some [Chams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chams "Chams") who migrated to Sulu were called Orang Dampuan.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-38)\[ _[unreliable source?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources "Wikipedia:Reliable sources")_\] The [Champa civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa "Champa") and the port-kingdom of Sulu engaged in commerce with each other which resulted in merchant Chams settling in Sulu, where they were known as Orang Dampuan in the 10th–13th centuries. In contrast to their cousins in the Butuan Rajahnate, who considered themselves diplomatic competitors of Champa for China trade,[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-39) (under Butuan's Rajah Kiling); instead, Sulu freely traded with the Champa civilization. The Orang Dampuans from Champa however were eventually slaughtered by envious native Sulu Buranuns due to the wealth of the Orang Dampuan.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-40) The Buranun were then subjected to retaliatory slaughter by the Orang Dampuan. Harmonious commerce between Sulu and the Orang Dampuan was later restored.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-41) The [Yakans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakan_people "Yakan people") were descendants of the Taguima-based Orang Dampuan who came to Sulu from Champa.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-42) Sulu received civilization in its Indic form from the Orang Dampuan.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-43)

During the reign of Sipad the Younger, a Sunni Sufi scholar and mystic[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-tan_85-44) named _Tuan_ Mashā′ikha[\[d\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-45) arrived in Jolo in 1280 CE.[\[e\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-47) Little is known to the origins and early biography of Tuan Mashā′ikha, except that he is a Muslim "who came from foreign lands" at the head of a fleet of Muslim traders,[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-decasa_321-48) or he was issued from a [stalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_stem "Plant stem") of [bamboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo "Bamboo") and was considered a [prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophet "Prophet"), thus well respected by the people.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-saleeby_155-49) Other reports, however, insisted that Tuan Mashā′ikha together with his parents, Jamiyun Kulisa and Indra Suga, were sent to [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu "Sulu") by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great "Alexander the Great") (who is known as _Iskandar Zulkarnain_ in [Malay Annals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Annals "Malay Annals")).[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-ibrahim_51-37) However, [Najeeb Mitry Saleeby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najeeb_Mitry_Saleeby "Najeeb Mitry Saleeby"), a Lebanese American doctor who wrote _A History of Sulu_ in 1908 and other studies of the Moros, dismisses this claim by concluding that Jamiyun Kulisa and Indra Suga were mythical names.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-saleeby_155-49) According to _tarsila_, during the coming of Tuan Mashā′ikha, the people of Maimbung worshipped tombs and stones of any kind. After he preached Islam in the area, he married Sipad the Younger's daughter, Idda Indira Suga, who bore three children:[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-tan_86-50) Tuan Hakim, Tuan Pam and 'Aisha. Tuan Hakim, in turn, begot five children.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-saleeby_149-51) From the genealogy of Tuan Mashā′ikha, another titular system of aristocracy called "tuanship" started in Sulu. Apart from the Idda Indira Suga, Tuan Mashā′ikha also married another "unidentified woman" and begot Moumin. Tuan Mashā′ikha died in 710 [A.H.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijri_year "Hijri year") (equivalent to 1310 AD), and was buried in Bud Dato near Jolo, with an inscription of _Tuan Maqbālū_.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-ibrahim_54-52)

A descendant of the Sunni Sufi Shaykh Tuan Mashā′ikha named Tuan May also begot a son named _Datu_ Tka. The descendants of Tuan May did not assume the title of _tuan_, but instead, used _[datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu "Datu")_. This was the first time _datu_ was used as a political institution.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-tan_86-50)[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-tan_88-53) During the coming of Tuan Mashā′ikha, the Tagimaha people (literally means "the party of the people") from [Basilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilan "Basilan") and several places in [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao"), also arrived and settled in Buansa. After the Tagimaha came the Baklaya people, (which means "seashore dwellers"), who are believed to have originated from [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi"), and settled in [Patikul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patikul,_Sulu "Patikul, Sulu"). After these came the [Bajau people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau_peoples "Sama-Bajau peoples") (or _Samal_) from [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor "Johor"). The Bajau were driven towards Sulu by a heavy [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon"), some of them to the shores of [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei") and others to [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao").[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-saleeby_41-42-54) The population of Buranun, Tagimaha, and Baklaya in Sulu created three parties with distinct systems of government and subjects. In the 1300s the Chinese annals, _Nanhai zhi_, reported that Brunei invaded or administered the Philippine kingdoms of [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Butuan "Rajahnate of Butuan"), Sulu and [Ma-i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma-i "Ma-i") (Mindoro), which did not regain their independence
until later date.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-55) According to the [Nagarakretagama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarakretagama "Nagarakretagama"), the [Majapahit Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit_Empire "Majapahit Empire") under Emperor [Hayam Wuruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayam_Wuruk "Hayam Wuruk") invaded Sulu in 1365. However in 1369, the Sulus rebelled and regained independence and in vengeance assaulted the Majapahit Empire and its province _Po-ni_ (Brunei), as well as the northeast coast of Borneo[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-56) and thereafter went to the capital, looting it of treasure and gold. In the sacking of Brunei, the Sulus stole two sacred pearls from the Bruneian king.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-57) A fleet from the Majapahit capital succeeded in driving away the Sulus, but _Po-ni_ was left weaker after the attack.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHistory_for_Brunei_Darussalam200944-58) Since Chinese historiographies later recorded there to be a Maharaja of Sulu, it is assumed that the Majapahit did not take it back, and it was a rival to it. By 1390 CE, [Rajah Baguinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Baguinda "Rajah Baguinda") Ali, a prince of the [Pagaruyung Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagaruyung_Kingdom "Pagaruyung Kingdom"), arrived at Sulu and married into the local nobility. At least in 1417, when Sulu rivaled Majapahit according to Chinese annals, three kings (or monarchs) ruled three civilized kingdoms in the island.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-59) Patuka Pahala (Paduka Batara) ruled the eastern kingdom (Sulu Archipelago) -- he was the most powerful; the western kingdom was ruled by Mahalachi (Maharajah Kamal ud-Din), ruler of [Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan") in Indonesia; and the kingdom near the cave (or Cave King) was Paduka Patulapok from Palawan Island.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-tan_2010_128-60) The Bajau settlers were distributed among the three kingdoms. During this time, Sulu avenged itself for Majapahit Imperialism by encroaching upon the Majapahit Empire as the alliance of the three Sulu kings had territory that reached [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Kalimantan "East Kalimantan") and [North Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kalimantan "North Kalimantan"), which were former Majapahit provinces.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-61)

Moumin's descendants the son of Tuan Mashā′ikha populated Sulu.\[ _[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\] After some time, a certain Timway Orangkaya Su'il was mentioned by the second page of tarsila; he received four Bisaya slaves (people from the Kedatuan of Madja-as) from Manila (presumably Kingdom of Maynila) as a sign of friendship between the two countries. The descendants of Su'il also inherited the title Timway, which means "chief". On tarsila's third page, it accounts the fact that the slaves were the ancestors of the inhabitants in the island to Parang, Lati, Gi'tung, and Lu'uk respectively.

The fourth page then narrates the coming of the Buranun (addressed in the _tarsila_ as "the Maimbung people"), Tagimaha, Baklaya, and finally the drifted Bajau immigrants from Johor.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-saleeby_152-153-62) The condition of Sulu before the arrival of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") can be summarized as such: The island was inhabited by several cultures, and was reigned over by three independent kingdoms ruled by the Buranun, Tagimaha, and Baklaya peoples. Likewise, the socio-political systems of these kingdoms were characterized by several distinct institutions: rajahship, datuship, tuanship and timwayship. The arrival of Tuan Mashā′ikha afterwards established a core Islamic community in the island.

### Islamization and establishment

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Islamization and establishment")\]

See also: [Islam in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines "Islam in the Philippines")

Main article: [List of Sultans of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sultans_of_Sulu "List of Sultans of Sulu")

The Sulu Archipelago was an [entrepôt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrep%C3%B4t "Entrepôt") that attracted merchants from south China and various parts of Southeast Asia beginning in the 14th century.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Donoso-63) The name "Sulu" is attested in Chinese historical records as early as 1349,[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Abinales-43-64) during the late [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") (1271–1368), suggesting trade relations around this time.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-gunn-93-65) Trade continued into the early [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") (1368–1644); envoys were sent in several missions to China to trade and [pay tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China "Tributary system of China") to [the emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_China "Emperor of China"). Sulu merchants often exchanged goods with [Chinese Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_China "Islam in China"), and also traded with Muslims of [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims "Arab Muslims"), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Iran "Islam in Iran"), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Southeast_Asia "Islam in Southeast Asia"), or [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India "Islam in India") descent.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Donoso-63) Islamic historian [Cesar Adib Majul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Adib_Majul "Cesar Adib Majul") argues that Islam was introduced to the Sulu Archipelago in the late 14th century by Chinese and Arab merchants and missionaries from [Ming China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_China "Ming China").[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Abinales-43-64)[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-gunn-93-65) The seven Arab missionaries were called "Lumpang Basih" by the Tausug, and were Sunni Sufi scholars from the [Ba 'Alawi sada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_'Alawi_sada "Ba 'Alawi sada") of Yemen.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-66) A yellow-colored flag was used in Sulu by the Chinese community.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-67)

Around this time, a notable Arab judge, [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni "Sunni") [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi "Sufi") and [religious scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_scholar "Religious scholar") [Karim ul-Makhdum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhdum_Karim "Makhdum Karim")[\[f\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-68) from [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca "Mecca") arrived in Malacca. He preached Islam, particularly the Ash'ari Aqeeda and Shafi'i Madh'hab as well as the [Qadiriyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadiriyya "Qadiriyya") Tariqa, and many citizens, including the ruler of [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate "Malacca Sultanate"), converted to Islam.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Saleeby%2520158-159-69) Sulu leader [Paduka Pahala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paduka_Pahala "Paduka Pahala") and his sons moved to China, where he died. Chinese Muslims brought up his sons in [Dezhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezhou#Sulu_Royal_Family "Dezhou"), where their descendants live and have the surnames An and Wen. In 1380 CE,[\[g\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-70) Karim ul-Makhdum arrived in [Simunul island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simunul,_Tawi-Tawi "Simunul, Tawi-Tawi") from Malacca, again with Arab traders. Apart from being a scholar, he operated as a trader; some see him as a Sufi missionary from Mecca.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Larousse%252040-71) He preached Islam, and was accepted by the core Muslim community. He was the second person to preach Islam in the area, after Tuan Mashā′ikha. To facilitate conversion of nonbelievers, he established a mosque in Tubig-Indagan, Simunul, the first Islamic temple to be constructed in the area, or in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"). This later became known as the [Sheik Karimal Makdum Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheik_Karimal_Makdum_Mosque "Sheik Karimal Makdum Mosque").[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-72) He died in Sulu, although the exact location of his grave is unknown. In Buansa, he was known as Tuan Sharif Awliyā.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-ibrahim_51-37) On his alleged grave in Bud Agad, Jolo, an inscription reads "Mohadum Aminullah Al-Nikad". In [Lugus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugus,_Sulu "Lugus, Sulu"), he is referred to as Abdurrahman. In [Sibutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibutu_Island "Sibutu Island"), he is known by his name.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-73) The differing beliefs about his grave's location came about because the [Qadiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadiri "Qadiri") [Shaykh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaykh "Shaykh") Karim ul-Makhdum travelled to several islands in the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea "Sulu Sea") to preach Islam. In many places in the archipelago, he was beloved. It is said that the people of [Tapul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapul,_Sulu "Tapul, Sulu") built a mosque honouring him and that they claim descent from Karim ul-Makhdum. The customs, beliefs and political laws of the people changed and adapted to adopt the Islamic tradition.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Saleeby%2520159-74)

Sulu abruptly stopped sending tributes to the Ming in 1424.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-gunn-93-65) [Antonio Pigafetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Pigafetta "Antonio Pigafetta") recorded in his journals that the sultan of Brunei invaded Sulu to retrieve the two sacred pearls Sulu had previously pillaged from Brunei.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-FOOTNOTENicholl198345-75) A sultan of Brunei, [Sultan Bolkiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bolkiah "Sultan Bolkiah") married a princess ( _dayang-dayang_) of Sulu, Puteri Laila Menchanai, and they became the grandparents of the Muslim prince of [Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Maynila "Rajahnate of Maynila"), [Rajah Matanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Matanda "Rajah Matanda"). Manila was a Muslim city-state and vassal to Brunei before the Spanish colonized it and converted it from Islam to Christianity.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Islamic Manila ended after the failed attack of [Tarik Sulayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarik_Sulayman "Tarik Sulayman"), a Muslim [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people "Kapampangan people") commander, in the failure of the [Conspiracy of the Maharlikas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_of_the_Maharlikas "Conspiracy of the Maharlikas"), when the formerly Muslim Manila nobility attempted a secret alliance with the Japanese shogunate and Bruneiean sultanate (together with her Manila and Sulu allies) to expel the Spaniards from the Philippines.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-:3-76) Many Tausugs and other native Muslims of Sulu Sultanate already interacted with Kapampangan and Tagalog Muslims called [Luzones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzones "Luzones") based in Brunei, and there were intermarriages between them. The Spanish had native allies against the former Muslims they conquered like Hindu Tondo which resisted Islam when Brunei invaded and established Manila as a Muslim city-state to supplant Hindu [Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_(historical_polity) "Tondo (historical polity)").

### Maritime power

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Maritime power")\]

Main article: [Piracy in the Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_the_Sulu_Sea "Piracy in the Sulu Sea")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Iranun_pirate.png/250px-Iranun_pirate.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Iranun_pirate.png) An [Iranun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_people "Iranun people") pirate.

The Sulu sultanate became notorious for its so-called "Moro Raids" or acts of piracy on Spanish settlements in the Visayan areas in Northern Philippines and from coastal and river villages in North Borneo (Sabah), with the aim of capturing natives to be sold at slave markets in the Sulu Island (Jolo Island) and Tawi Tawi Island.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-77) Tausug pirates used boats known collectively by Europeans as [_proas_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proa "Proa") (predominantly the _[lanong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanong "Lanong")_ and [_garay_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garay_(ship) "Garay (ship)") warships), which varied in design and were much lighter than the Spanish galleons and could easily out-sail these ships, and also often carried large swivel guns or _[lantaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantaka "Lantaka")_ and also carried a crew of pirates from different ethnic groups throughout Sulu, such as the [Iranun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_people "Iranun people"), [Bajaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau_peoples "Sama-Bajau peoples") and Tausugs alike. By the 18th century, Sulu pirates had become virtual masters of the Sulu seas and the surrounding areas, wreaking havoc and conducting raids to kidnap natives living in Spanish and Brunei North Borneo settlements for the slave trade.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-78) This prompted the Spaniards to build a number of fortifications[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-79) across the Visayan islands of Cebu and Bohol; churches were built on higher ground, and watchtowers were built along coastlines to warn of impending raids.

The maritime supremacy of Sulu was not directly controlled by the sultan; independent _datu_ s and warlords waged their own wars against the Spaniards and even with the [capture of Jolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93Moro_conflict "Spanish–Moro conflict") on numerous occasions by the Spaniards, other settlements like [Maimbung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimbung,_Sulu "Maimbung, Sulu"), [Banguingui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banguingui,_Sulu "Banguingui, Sulu") and [Tawi-Tawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawi-Tawi "Tawi-Tawi") were used as assembly areas and hideouts for pirates.

The sultanate's control over the Sulu seas was at its height around the late 17th to early 18th centuries when Moro raids became very common for the Borneo natives, Visayans and Spaniards.

In Sulu and in the Mindanao interior, the slave trade flourished and majority of the slaves that were being imported and exported were of Visayan ethnicity; the term _Bisaya_ eventually became synonymous to "slave" in these areas. Its maritime supremacy over the Spaniards, at the time, the Spaniards acquired steam-powered ships that began to curb Muslim piracy in the region, the Moro piratical raids began to decrease in number until Governor [Narciso Clavería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narciso_Claver%C3%ADa_y_Zald%C3%BAa,_1st_Count_of_Manila "Narciso Clavería y Zaldúa, 1st Count of Manila") launched the [Balanguingui expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expedition_to_Balanguingui "Spanish expedition to Balanguingui") in 1848 to crush the pirate settlements there, effectively ending the Moro pirate raids. By the last quarter of the 19th century, Moro pirates had virtually disappeared and the maritime influence of the sultanate became dependent on the [Chinese junk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_junk "Chinese junk") trade. The piracy and slave trade was brought to an end by the Spanish who destroyed the Sulu Sultanate in 1878 with a formal surrender and capitulation of the Sultanate once and for all. Once the Americans arrived, further attempts at resurrection to their old piracy ways were put down swiftly.

### Conquest of Northeastern Borneo

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Conquest of Northeastern Borneo")\]

See also: [Sultanate of Bulungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bulungan "Sultanate of Bulungan")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/18th_Century_Flag_of_Sulu.svg/250px-18th_Century_Flag_of_Sulu.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:18th_Century_Flag_of_Sulu.svg) 18th century flag of the Sultanate of Sulu according to [explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_explorers "List of explorers") [Pierre Sonnerat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Sonnerat "Pierre Sonnerat").[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-80)

In 1746, [Sultan Azim ud-Din I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azim_ud-Din_I_of_Sulu "Azim ud-Din I of Sulu"), with Spanish assistance, carried out punitive expeditions around the [Sebuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebuku_River "Sebuku River") region against the [Tidungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidung_people "Tidung people") to reconsolidate his power in Borneo. By April 1747, the expeditions were declared a success and the chief of [Tarakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarakan "Tarakan") declared his allegiance to Sulu. After the Sultan returned to Sulu, he brought back 50 captives who were previously under the hands of the Tidung and among them was a friar, while others were Tidung chiefs. Two of the Tidung chiefs were left in Jolo while the rest were brought to Zamboanga.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-:1-81) Tausug traders began settling in Tarakan. However, things began to complicate after the Tidungs made a political marriage with Bulungan, placing the Tidungs also under the [Sultanate of Bulungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bulungan "Sultanate of Bulungan").[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-auto3-82)

In 1789, amidst the civil war in [Berau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Berau?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sultanate of Berau (page does not exist)"), Sultan Azim ud-Din II raided Berau and Tarakan, which led to the de facto independence of Bulungan from Berau by the 18th-19th century. Sulu became a dominant power in the region, bringing Bulungan, in present-day North Kalimantan, Indonesia, within its sphere of influence. Tausug trading vessels subsequently arrived in increasing numbers, highlighting the historical importance of the maritime routes connecting Indonesia's northeastern Borneo coast with the wider Sulu Archipelago. The status of Bulungan as a subject of Sulu however would later change after they stopped paying tribute to Sulu in 1855. This would later become official after the British annexation of North Borneo and the Dutch annexation of Bulungan in 1878.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-:1-81)[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-:4-83)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-84)

In the 18th century, Sulu's dominion covered most of northeastern part of Borneo. However areas like Tempasuk and Abai had never really shown much allegiance to its earlier ruler, Brunei, subsequently similar treatment was given to Sulu. [Alexander Dalrymple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dalrymple "Alexander Dalrymple"), who made a treaty of allegiance in 1761 with Sulu, had to make a similar agreement with the rulers of Tempasuk and Abai on the north Borneo coast in 1762.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-85) The Sultanate of Sulu totally gave up its domain over Palawan to Spain in 1705 and Basilan to Spain in 1762. The territory ceded to Sulu by Brunei initially stretched south to Tapean Durian (now Tanjong Mangkalihat) (another source mentioned a southernmost boundary at Dumaring),[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-86) near the Straits of Macassar (now [Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan")). From 1726 to 1733, the Sulu sultanate restarted their tributary relationship with China, now the [Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"), about 300 years after it had ended.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-dV-87)

By 1849, the areas gained from Brunei had been effectively controlled by the [Sultanate of Bulungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bulungan "Sultanate of Bulungan") in Kalimantan, reducing the boundary of Sulu to a cape named Batu Tinagat and the [Tawau River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawau "Tawau").[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-icj638-88)

Concession of Sabah

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Concession_of_Sabah_%28Brunei%29_1877.jpg/250px-Concession_of_Sabah_%28Brunei%29_1877.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Concession_of_Sabah_(Brunei)_1877.jpg)

The first concession treaty was signed by Sultan [Abdul Momin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Momin "Abdul Momin") of Brunei on 29 December 1877, appointing [Baron de Overbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_von_Overbeck "Baron von Overbeck") as the Maharaja Sabah, Rajah Gaya and Sandakan[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-89)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Concession_of_Sabah_%28Sulu%29_1878.jpg/250px-Concession_of_Sabah_%28Sulu%29_1878.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Concession_of_Sabah_(Sulu)_1878.jpg)

The second concession treaty was signed by Sultan [Jamal ul-Azam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_ul-Azam "Jamal ul-Azam") of Sulu on 22 January 1878 also appointing Baron de Overbeck as Dato Bendahara and Raja Sandakan[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-90)

In 1848 and 1851, the Spanish launched [attacks on Balanguingui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expedition_to_Balanguingui "Spanish expedition to Balanguingui") and Jolo respectively. A peace treaty was signed on 30 April 1851[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-92) in which the sultan could only regain the capital if Sulu and its dependencies became a part of the Philippine Islands under the sovereignty of Spain. There were different understandings of this treaty; although the Spanish interpreted it as the sultan accepting Spanish sovereignty over Sulu and Tawi-Tawi, but the sultan took it as a friendly treaty amongst equals. These areas were only partially controlled by the Spanish, and their power was limited to military stations and garrisons and pockets of civilian settlements. This lasted until they had to abandon the region as a consequence of their defeat in the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War").

On 22 January 1878, an agreement was signed between Sulu and businessman [Gustav Overbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Overbeck "Gustav Overbeck"), who would later establish the [North Borneo Chartered Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_Chartered_Company "North Borneo Chartered Company"), granting Overbeck total control over the sultan's lands in northeastern Borneo, a region known as [Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabah "Sabah"). The ambiguity in the treaty of whether this was a cession or only a lease of the territory would later lead to the [North Borneo dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_dispute "North Borneo dispute").[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-93)[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Justice2003-94)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Major_Hugh_Scott%2C_Military_Governor_of_the_Sulu_Archipelago%2C_Philippines%2C_Sultan_Jamalul_Kiram_II%2C_interpreter_Charles_Schuck%2C_and_local_government_officials_and_hadjis%2C_dressed_to_call_on_LCCN2016650207.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Major_Hugh_Scott,_Military_Governor_of_the_Sulu_Archipelago,_Philippines,_Sultan_Jamalul_Kiram_II,_interpreter_Charles_Schuck,_and_local_government_officials_and_hadjis,_dressed_to_call_on_LCCN2016650207.jpg) American military governor [Hugh Lenox Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Lenox_Scott "Hugh Lenox Scott") meeting [Jamalul Kiram II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamalul_Kiram_II "Jamalul Kiram II") (c.1905)

Following the defeat of the [Moro Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Rebellion "Moro Rebellion"), the Sultanate of Sulu's existence effectively ceased on 22 March 1915, when American commanders demanded Sultan [Jamalul Kiram II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamalul_Kiram_II "Jamalul Kiram II") signed an agreement called the Carpenter Agreement. By this agreement, the sultan relinquished all sovereignty over territory then under control of the United States, retaining only his religious authority as head of Islam in Sulu.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-carpenter-34)

#### Aftermath

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Aftermath")\]

Main article: [North Borneo dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_dispute "North Borneo dispute")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/W.C.Cowie_and_A.Cook-SultanOfSulu.png/250px-W.C.Cowie_and_A.Cook-SultanOfSulu.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:W.C.Cowie_and_A.Cook-SultanOfSulu.png) [W. C. Cowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cowie_(merchant) "William Cowie (merchant)"), managing director of [BNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_Chartered_Company "North Borneo Chartered Company"), with the sultan of Sulu.

As a result of the language used in the treaty signed in 1878 to approve Sulu's cession of the eastern part of [Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabah "Sabah"), a territorial dispute emerged between the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") and [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), known as the [North Borneo dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_dispute "North Borneo dispute"). The English version states Sultan [Jamal ul-Azam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_ul-Azam "Jamal ul-Azam") _ceded_ the region in exchange for annual payments, whereas the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language "Malay language") version has the term _[pajakkan](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pajakan "wikt:pajakan")_, which some interpret as _lease_. Using the former interpretation, the [British protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_protectorate "British protectorate") of [North Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo "North Borneo") was formed in 1881, later becoming part of [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") in 1963 under the name of Sabah. The Philippines, acting as the [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_of_states "Succession of states") of Sulu, has claimed Sabah as its territory based on the idea that Sulu only rented the region.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Quezon2013-95)

Malaysia views the dispute as a "non-issue",[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-96) as it not only considers the agreement in 1878 as one of cession later confirmed in a 1903 agreement,[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-97)[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-98) but it also deems that the residents had exercised their act of self-determination when they joined to form the Malaysian federation in 1963.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-99)[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-100) It stood by the contract until 2013, issuing annual cheques in the amount of [RM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_ringgit "Malaysian ringgit") 5,300 (approx. [₱](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_pesos "Philippine pesos") 77,000 or US$1,710) to the legal counsel of the heirs of [Jamalul Kiram II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamalul_Kiram_II "Jamalul Kiram II"), the last sovereign sultan of Sulu.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-inq-timeline-101)

The heirs to the Sulu royals have also staked their claims to Sabah. In 2013, during the [Lahad Datu standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Lahad_Datu_standoff "2013 Lahad Datu standoff"), a group of armed individuals sent by one of the claimants to the Sulu throne, [Jamalul Kiram III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamalul_Kiram_III "Jamalul Kiram III"), arrived in [Lahad Datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahad_Datu "Lahad Datu"), Sabah, in an attempt to assert their territorial claim.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-102) A total of 72 people were killed in the incident, which led Malaysia to suspend the annuities. Seven of the invaders were later sentenced to death.[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-103)[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-104)

The suspension of the annual fees led the Sulu heirs to [sue Malaysia for breach of contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Sulu_case "Malaysia Sulu case"). Their use of [forum shopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_shopping "Forum shopping") led to an initial award of at least US$14.92 billion by a Paris arbitration court.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-105) After litigation in Spanish, French, and Dutch court systems,[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Fresh-106) Malaysia obtained consistent victories, culminating in a dismissal by the French [Court of Cassation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation_(France) "Court of Cassation (France)") on 6 November 2024.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-reuters.com-107)

## Economy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Economy")\]

### Weapons and slave trade

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Weapons and slave trade")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Moro_cannon_or_swivel_gun_%28lantaka%29_from_the_Sulu_Archipelago%2C_brass%2C_Honolulu_Museum_of_Art.jpg/250px-Moro_cannon_or_swivel_gun_%28lantaka%29_from_the_Sulu_Archipelago%2C_brass%2C_Honolulu_Museum_of_Art.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Moro_cannon_or_swivel_gun_(lantaka)_from_the_Sulu_Archipelago,_brass,_Honolulu_Museum_of_Art.jpg) A Moro brass _lantaka_ or swivel gun.

Chinese who lived in Sulu ran guns across a Spanish blockade to supply the Moro datus and sultanates with weapons to fight the Spanish, who were engaging in a campaign to subjugate the Moro sultanates on [Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao"). A trade involving the Moros selling slaves and other goods in exchange for guns developed. The Chinese had entered the economy of the sultanate, taking almost total control of the sultanate's economies in Mindanao and dominating the markets. Though the sultans did not like one group of people exercising exclusive control over the economy, they did business with them.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Iranun_lanong_warship_by_Rafael_Monle%C3%B3n_%281890%29.jpg/250px-Iranun_lanong_warship_by_Rafael_Monle%C3%B3n_%281890%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Iranun_lanong_warship_by_Rafael_Monle%C3%B3n_(1890).jpg) 19th century illustration of a _[lanong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanong "Lanong")_, the main warships used by the [Iranun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_people "Iranun people") and [Banguingui people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banguingui_people "Banguingui people") of the navies of the sultanates of Sulu and [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao") for piracy and slave raids

The Chinese set up a trading network between [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Singapore "British Singapore"), Zamboanga, Jolo and Sulu. The Chinese sold small arms like Enfield and Spencer Rifles to the Buayan Datu Uto. They were used to battle the Spanish invasion of [Buayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buayan "Buayan"). The datu paid for the weapons in slaves.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Warren2007-108) The population of Chinese in Mindanao in the 1880s was 1,000. The Chinese ran guns across a Spanish blockade to sell to Mindanao Moros. The purchases of these weapons were paid for by the Moros in slaves in addition to other goods. The main group of people selling guns were the Chinese in Sulu. The Chinese took control of the economy and used steamers to ship goods for exporting and importing. [Opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium "Opium"), [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory "Ivory"), textiles, and crockery were among the other goods which the Chinese sold.

The Chinese on [Maimbung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimbung "Maimbung") sent the weapons to the Sulu sultanate, who used them to battle the Spanish and resist their attacks. A [Chinese-Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese-Mestizo "Chinese-Mestizo") was one of the sultan's brothers-in-law, the sultan was married to his sister. He and the sultan both owned shares in the ship (named the Far East) which helped smuggle the weapons.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Warren2007-108) The Spanish launched a surprise offensive under Colonel [Juan Arolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Arolas "Juan Arolas") in April 1887 by attacking the sultanate's capital at Maimbung in an effort to crush resistance. Weapons were captured and the property of the Chinese were destroyed while the Chinese were deported to Jolo.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Warren2007-108)

### Pearling industry

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Pearling industry")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Sultan_of_Sulu_and_French_visitors.jpg/250px-Sultan_of_Sulu_and_French_visitors.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sultan_of_Sulu_and_French_visitors.jpg) A painting from 1880s depicting Sultan [Jamal ul-Azam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_ul-Azam "Jamal ul-Azam") having a conversation with French visitors.

After the destruction of the pirate haunts of [Balanguingui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanguingui "Balanguingui") effectively ending the centuries of slave raids, which the Sulu sultanate's economy had so depended on, along with the economy of mainland Mindanao, the sultanate's economy experienced a sharp decline as slaves became more inaccessible and the islands' agricultural produce wasn't enough, thus it became dependent on the Mindanao interior even for [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice "Rice") and produce.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-109) Although the Spaniards thought they had dealt the death blow for the sultanate when they captured Jolo in 1876, rather, the sultanate's capital and economic and trading hub was moved to Maimbung on the other side of the island. Up until the American occupation, this was the residence and economic center of Sulu. This is where the Sultan Jamalul Kiram II and his adviser Hadji Butu began the Sulu [pearling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl "Pearl") industry to increase the sultan's wealth,[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-110) they organized the Sulu pearling fleet, which remained active well into the early 20th century. In 1910, the sultan reportedly sold a giant pearl in London for $100,000.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Culture

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Culture")\]

### Social class system

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Social class system")\]

Among the people of the Sultanate of Sulu, nobility could be acquired only by lineage, a closed hereditary system.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Daru_Jambangan.jpg/250px-Daru_Jambangan.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Daru_Jambangan.jpg) [Darul Jambangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Jambangan "Darul Jambangan") (Palace of Flowers) in [Maimbung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimbung "Maimbung"), last residence to the sultans.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-111)

There were two royal classes:[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-112)

- **[Datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu "Datu")** ( _susultanun_), acquired purely by lineage. All male members of the [royal house of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_House_of_Sulu "Royal House of Sulu") held this title and style "His Royal Highness (HRH)". Their spouses automatically held the title of _Dayangdayang_ (princess of the first degree). Adopted members of the royal house of Sulu were styled "His Highness (HH)" and their spouses also held the title of Dayangdayang and the style: "Her Highness".
- **[Datu sadja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Sadja "Datu Sadja")**, which may be acquired through confirming the titles ( _gullal_) on the middleman of the sultan. The gullal is made if a commoner has achieved outstanding feats or services in line of duty through display of bravery, heroism, etc. Datu sadja is a life title of nobility and the title holders hold the style "His Excellency" and their spouses should hold the title of _dayang_ the style "Her Excellency".

**Maharlika**, or commoners, do not trace their descent from royalty. The upper subclasses held administrative roles:

- **Wakil Kesultanan** – regional representative outside the sultanate
- **Panglima** – regional representative inside the sultanate
- **Parkasa** – [aide-de-camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aide-de-camp "Aide-de-camp") of region representative inside the sultanate
- **Laksaman**– subregional representative inside the sultanate

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Sulu_Craft_off_Sandakau%2C_with_Mecca_Pilgrims.jpg/250px-Sulu_Craft_off_Sandakau%2C_with_Mecca_Pilgrims.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sulu_Craft_off_Sandakau,_with_Mecca_Pilgrims.jpg) Sulu vessel carrying pilgrims to Mecca, 1899.

The men who hold the offices above were addressed by the title of nobility _Tuan_ (the title is directly attached to the office), followed by the rank of the office they hold, their given name, surname and region. The women who held offices above shall be addressed by the title of nobility _Sitti_ (the title is directly attached to the office), followed by the same name order.

A very large part of the Sulu society, as well as in the [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao") were slaves captured from slave raids or bought from slave markets. They were known as the _bisaya_, reflecting their most common origin – Christianized [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans "Visayans") from Spanish territories in the Philippines – although they also included captured slaves from other ethnic groups throughout Southeast Asia. They were also known as _banyaga_, _ipun_, or _ammas_. It is estimated that as much as 50% of the population of Sulu in the 1850s were _bisaya_ slaves and dominated the Sulu economy. For the most part, they were treated like commoners, with their own houses and were responsible for cultivating farms and fisheries of Tausug nobility. But there were harsh punishments for attempts to escape, and a large number of the slaves were sold to European, Chinese, [Makassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassar_people "Makassar people"), and [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis_people "Bugis people") slavers in the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies").[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-warren2-113)[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-non-114)

### Visual arts

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sultanate_of_Sulu&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Visual arts")\]

Main article: [Okir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okir "Okir")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Lute_%28kutyapi%29%2C_Mindanao%2C_wood%2C_Honolulu_Museum_of_Art.jpg/250px-Lute_%28kutyapi%29%2C_Mindanao%2C_wood%2C_Honolulu_Museum_of_Art.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lute_(kutyapi),_Mindanao,_wood,_Honolulu_Museum_of_Art.jpg) A _[kutiyapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutiyapi "Kutiyapi")_ (lute) from Mindanao bearing _Ukkil_ motifs.

The Sultanate of Sulu, along with the rest of Mindanao, has a long tradition of decorative arts known as _ukkil_ or _okir_. Ukkil is the Tausug word for "wood carving" or "engraving". The Tausug and [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao "Maranao") peoples traditionally carved and decorated their boats, houses and even grave markers with ukkil carvings. Aside from wood carvings, ukkil motifs were found on various clothing in the Sulu archipelago. Ukkil motifs tend to emphasise geometric patterns and a flowing design, with floral and leaf patterns as well as folk elements. The Tausug also decorated their weapons with these motifs, and various _kris_ and [_barong_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barong_(knife) "Barong (knife)") blades have finely decorated handles as well as blades covered in floral patterns and the like.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-115) Bronze _[lantaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantaka "Lantaka")_ also bear some ukkil patterns.

The Philippines, as part of its claim to [Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabah "Sabah") in the [North Borneo dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_dispute "North Borneo dispute"), has occasionally revived the sultanate's institutions since 1962, when it accepted a "cession" of North Borneo by the heirs to Jamalul Kiram II, which had been under either _lease_ or _cession_ (depending on interpretation) to the [North Borneo Chartered Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_Chartered_Company "North Borneo Chartered Company") since 1878.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Ortiz1963-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Hernando1966-6) The royal family's descendants are still treated as royalty by the people in Sulu, constituting a form of [non-sovereign monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-sovereign_monarchy "Non-sovereign monarchy").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-McGeown2013-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Whaley2015-8) The Philippine government has not recognized any individual as [titular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_ruler "Titular ruler") "sultan" since 1986, leading to the rise of various [pretenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretender "Pretender").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-Golingai2024-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_note-VOA9Mar2013-10)

6. [↑](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_ref-68 "Jump up")Also _Karimul Makhdum_, _Karimal Makdum_ or _Makhdum Karim_ among others. Makhdum came from the Arabic word _makhdūmīn_, which means "master".
7. [↑](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu#cite_ref-70 "Jump up")Another uncertain date in Philippine Islamic history is the year of arrival of Karim ul-Makhdum. Though other Muslim scholars place the date as simply "the end of 14th century", Saleeby calculated the year as 1380 AD corresponding to the description of the _tarsilas_, in which Karim ul-Makhdum's coming is ten years before Rajah Baguinda's. The 1380 reference originated from the event in Islamic history when a huge number of _makhdūmīn_ started to travel to Southeast Asia from India. See Ibrahim's "Readings on Islam in Southeast Asia."
