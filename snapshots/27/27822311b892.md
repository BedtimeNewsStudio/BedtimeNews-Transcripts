# What is holding up the US-India trade deal?

Anuj SRIVAS

17 October 20264 min read

![](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/d651c73ac5df45092da4e0b58447375f4083a687e166e0d47054bfac90afb540/lightyear_networkapi/resizefit_w960%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Fafp.com.sg%2F6ed55a848a8f73c7712a9069bda9cfc1.jpg)

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio is set to visit India for the second time this year, where a long-awaited trade deal betwen the two countries is likely to be discussed (Mark Schiefelbein)

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio is set to visit India for the second time this year, as Washington seeks to bolster ties with a strategic partner despite months of friction over a trade deal.

Rubio will meet senior Indian officials, but will bypass the Indian capital during the trip in the coming week.

Instead he will focus on investment and energy during visits to financial hub Mumbai and India's oil hub of Jamnagar, which is home to billionaire Mukesh Ambani's refining complex, the world's largest.

The elephant in the room will be the inability by both countries to seal their long-awaited trade deal, experts say.

Several factors, including US President Donald Trump's tariff blitz, friction over energy imports and Washington's warmer ties with India's arch-rival Pakistan have made it harder for negotiators to clinch an agreement.

Rubio will start his six-day overseas trip on Saturday with a visit first to Uzbekistan before arriving in India, according to the US State Department.

Here are some of the challenges the trade pact has faced and the road ahead.

\- Why has the deal been delayed? -

India and the United States agreed in February on the framework for an interim deal under which Washington would lower reciprocal tariffs from 25 percent to 18 percent and India would reduce duties on a range of industrial and agricultural goods.

Despite months of negotiations, the agreement has not yet been finalised and signed.

One reason is the legal uncertainty surrounding Trump's tariff plans after court challenges undermined the reciprocal tariffs that formed their basis.

"The February framework had offered to reduce India's reciprocal tariff... in return for substantial Indian concessions. Once that tariff regime was struck down, the value of the US offer became unclear," trade expert Ajay Srivastava said in a note.

India is also wary of new trade measures by Washington that could impact it, including an investigation into alleged unfair practices in several countries and a new law that allows for up to 100 percent tariffs on nations that purchase Russian oil.

Trump on Friday signed an executive order that waived those threatened sanctions.

\- Where do negotiations stand? -

Progress appears to have hit several hurdles.

For the better part of this year, both governments spoke optimistically about the possibility of finalising the interim deal.

US ambassador to India Sergio Gor in June said negotiators were working on the "last one percent" of the pact.

More recently, the tone has turned markedly cautious.

Story continues

India's Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said last week that talks had hit a "plateau", with little room for further concessions.

Her remarks came days after US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said an announcement was not "imminent".

\- What have been the main sticking points? -

Agriculture remains a sensitive area for India, which is reluctant to expose farmers to greater foreign competition.

Washington, meanwhile, wants India to buy more US energy supplies.

New Delhi has also insisted that its final tariff rate should be competitive with those received by other regional manufacturing rivals such as Vietnam.

Indian trade minister Piyush Goyal said this week that the first tranche of the deal was in its final stages, but added that it would not be operational until India secured an edge over its competitors.

"It's going through the final contours, the legal drafting, and we hope to be able to finalise that soon and operationalise that once we get competitive advantage over our peer countries," he said.

\- How has trade been holding up? -

Goods exports to the US jumped 21 percent in August to $8.3 billion compared to the same month a year earlier, when India faced 50 percent tariffs.

The surge in exports has been a bright spot for the Indian economy, which has been roiled by high energy prices due to war in the Middle East.

If higher tariffs were to return, economists say it would cast a shadow on India's growth outlook, hurt market sentiment and heap pressure on an already-weak rupee.

Canada will seek to conclude a trade deal with India by the end of the year as the two countries look to strengthen economic ties and diversify partnerships amid growing global uncertainty.

(Oct 14): Canada will seek to conclude a trade deal with India by the end of the year as the two countries look to strengthen economic ties and diversify partnerships amid growing global uncertainty.

Negotiators from India and Canada began a fifth round of talks from Oct 5, building on discussions that concluded last month. India Prime Minister Narendra Modi is expected to visit Canada in December and sign a completed trade deal.

"The momentum is there, the energy is there to get it done," Maninder Sidhu, Canadian International Trade Minister, said in an interview with Haslinda Amin. "Hopefully we will get it done this year. That is the focus."

The push to deepen economic ties with India comes as Canada faces rising trade tensions with the US following the breakdown of negotiations between the two countries. India, meanwhile, is contending with fresh tariff threats from a new law that gives US President Donald Trump the power to impose levies of as much as 100% on countries that purchase Russian oil and gas.

Canada sees opportunities to deepen ties with India in mining and food security while also identifying infrastructure investment and nuclear energy as areas for deeper cooperation.

He signalled Ottawa's interest in participating in India's expansion plans.

That could create opportunities for Canada, which has some of the world's largest uranium resources as well as nuclear technology and expertise, to supply fuel and participate in new projects.

- # US, India officials signal trade deal talks at an impasse

Shruti Srivastava / Bloomberg

4 October 20263 min read

![](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/195c620dda39be16a79a56d9473f1ff89ffa3e2e0cefad9313f256ca5bb17d9c/lightyear_networkapi/resizefit_w960%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Fthe_edge_961%2F47a1c93591a12da51be007c082377432.jpg)

Top officials in the US and India signalled that negotiations over a trade deal have reached an impasse that is making it difficult to clinch an agreement.

(Oct 5): Top officials in the US and India signalled that negotiations over a trade deal have reached an impasse that is making it difficult to clinch an agreement.

Indian Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said Monday that talks have "reached a plateau" beyond which a compromise would be difficult. That followed comments from United States Trade Representative Jamieson Greer on Friday that a trade deal was in its final stages but not "imminent".

The two sides agreed to an interim deal earlier this year and have been locked in negotiations ever since. Indian officials have sought assurances from the US that the country would have a favourable tariff rate over its peers and that it would not face additional penalties once the trade deal is signed.

Speaking at the Munich Security Conference on Monday, Sitharaman said trade negotiations have been difficult and rigorous, and "we would like to believe that both sides have reached a plateau beyond which giving or taking might be very, very difficult".

"Maybe if there are rooms to operate from, both sides would do it," she said.

The talks have been complicated by new US legislation giving US President Donald Trump the ability to impose tariffs of as much as 100% on the top buyers of Russian oil and gas. India, a major importer of Russian energy, has warned that such measures could hurt bilateral ties with the US and disrupt global energy markets.

US-India ties have come under strain in the past 18 months, with Trump imposing punitive tariffs on India for buying Russian oil before removing them. The US leader has repeatedly claimed he'd helped mediate a ceasefire to end a brief armed conflict between India and Pakistan in May 2025. New Delhi has consistently rejected Trump's version of events.

Trump and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi held their first phone call since June last week, discussing trade and energy, as well as defence and critical minerals.

### 'Lonelier power'

India's External Affairs Minister Subrahmanyam Jaishankar on Sunday warned that the US was becoming increasingly isolated in the new global order.

"Nobody would disagree with the fact that the landscape today is primarily marked by a radical repositioning of the United States," he said at the Munich Security Conference. "A repositioning which is a posture of choice and where it has deliberately become a lonelier power."

Story continues

Rising competition between the US and China has also led to the "weaponisation of any leverage that any country has", he said.

Indian Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal held what he described as "productive talks" with Greer in Milwaukee on the sidelines of the Group of 20 trade ministers' meeting last week.

Greer said on Friday officials were working to iron out their disputes.

"I would characterise and say we are in the short strokes," Greer said. The two sides have identified the "universe of items that are sticking points and we are working diligently toward them", he said.

(Sept 23): India is accelerating trade talks with Canada and Mexico as the three economies seek new markets and partners in response to the disruption unleashed by US President Donald Trump's tariff policies.

Negotiators from India and Canada plan to hold a fifth round of talks from Oct 5, building on discussions that concluded last week, according to people familiar with the matter. A deal is likely to be inked in December when Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi is expected to visit Canada, the _Globe and Mail_ reported, citing New Delhi's envoy Dinesh Patnaik.

Separately, New Delhi is finalising a framework with Mexico to begin formal trade negotiations, the people said, asking not to be identified as the discussions are private. Officials are seeking to make faster progress in the trade talks as all three countries look to diversify their commercial ties, they said.

- # Modi courts investors with India's US$30 bil chipmaking bet

Sankalp Phartiyal / Bloomberg

16 September 20263 min read

![](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/884015caece73c126385c4778ee717059e9ebdca6374cfde40dd1d281e91ec38/lightyear_networkapi/resizefit_w960%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Fthe_edge_961%2F8315b410f5f7c44478136dda0fbd4d08.jpg)

Prime Minister Narendra Modi will launch a charm offensive for global investors on Thursday as he inaugurates a massive semiconductor conference in New Delhi.

(Sept 17): Prime Minister Narendra Modi will launch a charm offensive for global investors on Thursday as he inaugurates a massive semiconductor conference in New Delhi, marking India's audacious bid to win a seat at the chip-making table.

Modi will use the three-day Semicon event to display India's fledgling chip and electronics industry that barely existed a decade ago. He's courting global semiconductor producers, potential partners and customers for India's homegrown contenders, as well as equipment makers and parts suppliers all seeking to ride the artificial intelligence (AI) investment wave.

"Semicon in New Delhi is Modi's way of signalling that India has a long-term commitment to growing the chip industry," said Pranay Kotasthane, deputy director of Bangalore-based think tank Takshashila Institution. "That's his message to global investors."

The stakes are high for the planet's most populous country and an economy that's outpaced much of the world for years. Modi is trying to prove the nation has what it takes to become a hub in higher value-added industries such as chips, all part of a bid to further modernise the economy and keep it humming by bolstering exports. Expanding manufacturing is crucial for Modi's vision of moving more workers from farms into better-paying jobs.

[![](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/fb45d7724e7d9b264a35eb930f640cf2e913c230ed1cd6af544804b2af69d9a8/lightyear_networkapi/resizefit_w960%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Fthe_edge_961%2Ff0624464daf7747d7762b5d00b7a544b.jpg)](https://myassets.theedgemalaysia.com/pics/2026/Indian_semicon_market_20260917104326_sn7so8.jpg)

More than 600 exhibitors, over 150 speakers and 300 global companies will converge at 15,000 sq m of a state-of-the-art convention centre in New Delhi for the event. There'll be startup showcases as well as a student hackathon.

The event comes just two months after India pledged a further 1.9 trillion rupees in subsidies to boost local chip and electronics production. The first round of subsidies in 2020-21 helped Apple Inc expand iPhone manufacturing in the South Asian region - it now makes 25% of its marquee device here.

India sought to replicate that momentum in semiconductors with an initial US$10 billion fund that helped drive early initiatives. Some of those are starting to bear fruit: conglomerates Tata Group and Murugappa Group have begun packaging and assembling chips, while US memory maker Micron Technology Inc has also commenced production. Tata is also building a chip fabrication plant in western Gujarat state that's due to become operational in early 2028.

Countries around the world are committing massive budgets to making chips at home as the race for AI supremacy heats up. AI is already becoming ubiquitous in everything from self-driving cars and healthcare to finance and fraud detection. But as computing resources remain scarce, chips and chipmakers are fast becoming kingmakers in deciding who's winning.

Story continues

Though India's push is in early stages, it's accelerating efforts to build a domestic industry spanning the entire supply chain. It's offering seed funding to startups working on semiconductor products, support for chip-making gear as well as for new fabs and assembly plants.

Chipmaking is a complicated and long-drawn process that countries such as Taiwan, South Korea, the US and Malaysia have taken several decades to develop and perfect. India started its campaign by making less sophisticated legacy semiconductors that are used widely in electronics and power systems, but it will have to move swiftly on to producing cutting-edge chips and advanced packaging to catch up.

![](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/7f8c90f3f9aa92585cd5bcc0cc071ce7b5c81152a8bde93dfbe6722d37949ced/lightyear_networkapi/resizefit_w960%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Fthe_edge_961%2Fc81b0914f93d4ce985e23ff37943d39f.jpg)

(Sept 11): Russian President Vladimir Putin arrived in India for a BRICS summit with Prime Minister Narendra Modi and other leaders, as wars in the Middle East and Ukraine test the bloc's push for greater economic clout and a bigger voice in global affairs.

It is Putin's first in-person appearance at a BRICS summit outside Russia since the invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. The two-day gathering that kicks off Saturday in New Delhi brings together leaders from some of the world's biggest emerging economies at a time when geopolitical tensions are giving fresh urgency to efforts to boost trade in local currencies, strengthen cross-border payment links and deepen economic cooperation within the bloc.

BRICS - an acronym that stands for Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa - is a club of nations that accounts for around a quarter of the world's nominal gross domestic product and roughly half of its population. It now has 10full members, bringing together some of the globe's biggest energy producers and consumers.

Friday's agenda includes a BRICS business forum and a Russia-India trade fair. Putin and Modi are also expected to hold bilateral talks as Russia looks to broaden an economic relationship that has become increasingly dominated by energy. Russia has emerged as one of India's biggest oil suppliers since its invasion of Ukraine, pushing bilateral trade to record levels.

Modi is also scheduled to meet leaders from all 21 participating countries as well as representatives of the United Nations and five regional groupings - the Gulf Cooperation Council, the African Union, Asean, BIMSTEC and the Community of Latin American and Caribbean States. Asean is the 11-member Southeast Asian regional bloc while BIMSTEC is a seven-country grouping that promotes economic and technical cooperation around the Bay of Bengal.

The BRICS summit is expected to focus on strengthening food and energy security, health systems, disaster preparedness and critical supply chains, as host India seeks to position the bloc as a source of stability in an increasingly fragmented world.

The gathering comes as the global economy grapples with the fallout from Russia's war in Ukraine, the crisis in the Middle East and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, alongside the Trump administration's tariffs and trade policies.

Saturday's agenda is expected to include a bilateral meeting between Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping, who is making his first visit to India since 2019. Leaders are also due to take part in a joint tree-planting ceremony and attend a gala dinner hosted by Modi.

(Sept 8): Shares of Indian defence companies rose after the government gave an initial approval for about US$11 billion of proposed military purchases.

The BSE India Defence Index advanced as much as 2.1% Tuesday - the most in more than a month - with Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd and Bharat Electronics Ltd leading the gains. Bullish calls from Jefferies on some defence companies added to the optimism.

About 98% of the approved procurements, which include helicopters and recce vehicles, will be sourced from the local industry, the Ministry of Defence said in a statement released after market hours Monday.

The reaction in the stock market highlights growing investor expectations that India's push to modernise its armed forces and source more equipment locally will translate into a sustained order pipeline for defence firms.
