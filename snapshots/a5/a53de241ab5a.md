  [Cost of living](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/cost-living-isnt-killing-joy-100307935.html), jobs, and support for Singaporeans at every life stage remain key government priorities for the years ahead, Prime Minister [Lawrence Wong](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/pore-expects-parties-honour-commitments-024208090.html) said on 25 Oct. Speaking to around 3,000 party activists at the PAP's biennial conference at the Singapore Expo, he said the Government has a full agenda for the rest of this term, with "much more to come."

  Wong, who also serves as the PAP's secretary-general, acknowledged feedback from Singaporeans who asked about support for groups beyond families with children, following the major package he announced for parents at the National Day Rally in August. He addressed the concern directly, assuring the crowd that singles, seniors, and other groups had not been forgotten, even if he couldn't announce every measure in a single speech.

  He also reminded the party that the strong mandate it secured in the 2025 general election, where the PAP won 65.57 per cent of the vote, comes with responsibility rather than entitlement.

  Wong pointed to Singapore's continued appeal to global business as a sign of strength amid a more dangerous and uncertain world, citing recent meetings with semiconductor executives and tech start-up founders, as well as the arrival of AI firms like OpenAI and Anthropic, which have both set up offices here.

  Even so, he cautioned that strong headline growth doesn't capture the full picture, noting that some sectors and workers are thriving while others are struggling to keep up. He acknowledged growing concerns among lower-wage workers over rising costs and among [retrenched workers](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/structural-gaps-appearing-workforce-even-082000930.html) in their 40s and 50s over their job prospects, saying the Government's task isn't just to keep the economy growing, but to ensure that growth translates into better lives for everyone.

  Singapore's economy grew 5.4 per cent in the third quarter of 2026, beating initial [government forecasts](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/singapores-core-inflation-for-september-unexpectedly-cools-070649279.html), even as retrenchments climbed to 4,620 in the second quarter, the highest level since the pandemic-era final quarter of 2020.

  Wong said the Government understands these concerns and will continue working with the National Trades Union Congress to help workers adapt and recover from setbacks. He cited the case of Fajar Shah Salim, 45, who lost his restaurant management job in early 2025 after more than two decades in the industry and later switched careers to IT with support from the SkillsFuture Jobseeker Support Scheme. Wong called it an example of how government support and personal effort need to work together.

  Wong extended the same message to broader social policy, saying that even as exam pressure eases and education pathways widen, parents need to give children room to grow at their own pace. He added that support for families and seniors has also been strengthened, and that Singaporeans must look out for one another in turn.

  He closed by describing Singapore's future as something that has to be built together, across government, businesses, unions, workers, families, and the wider community, rather than simply delivered from the top down.

  # Retirement and re-employment ages to go up to 65 and 70 from 2029, a year ahead of plan

  ![Singapore- 5 Dec 2025: Group of retired men come to bond over the game of checkers or Chinese Chess in Chinatown, Singapore. Chess remains an important cultural pastime for the older generation](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/f90936f38d595e519dfab937ac1131e04fa2c3bfa31379390cf689398cc4441b/lightyear_networkapi/resizefill_w960%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fd29szjachogqwa.cloudfront.net%2Fimages%2F2026-10%2F8ba12f88-529c-4c09-b228-84266f82866d)

  Singapore will raise its retirement age to 65 and its re-employment age to 70 from 1 Jan 2029, a year ahead of the original target. (PHOTO: Getty Images)

  Singapore will raise the [retirement age](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/singapore-raise-retirement-age-64-102500189.html) to 65 and the re-employment age to 70 on 1 Jan 2029, one year ahead of the original target. The changes are part of efforts to build age-friendly jobs and age-diverse workplaces. The [Tripartite Workgroup](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/tripartite-council-help-workers-keep-090000461.html) on Senior Employment said on 26 Oct that the move gives senior workers greater assurance that they can keep working if they wish to. The retirement age was raised to 64 in July, while the re-employment age went up to 69.

  The workgroup was set up in July 2025 to review Singapore's senior employment strategy. It outlined six recommendations, which the Government has accepted. They aim to help seniors stay employed or return to work after a break, and to support those who want to work beyond the statutory ages. They also offer more targeted help to lower-wage seniors.

  A key focus is [flexi-load arrangements](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/govt-exploring-grants-incentives-firms-133000155.html), which let employees take on different workloads with matching adjustments to their pay. The workgroup found a significant gap in such options for seniors, even though flexi-time and flexi-place arrangements are fairly common. [Ministry of Manpower (MOM)](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/no-worker-face-alone-close-010000458.html) data from 2024 showed that among full-time resident employees aged 50 to 64, 85.5 per cent were offered flexi-time work and 78.7 per cent flexi-place arrangements. Only 74.5 per cent were offered flexi-load arrangements.

  To close the gap, the workgroup proposed a new flexi-load programme and a dedicated programme office. This replaces the part-time employment grant, which has supported about 68,500 seniors and 7,800 employers. The programme drew on a workgroup study trip to Japan, which gives its senior workers tailored support to help them navigate later-life career transitions.

  The new office, led by the Skills and Workforce Development Agency, will work with the National Trades Union Congress (NTUC) and the Singapore National Employers Federation. It will push for more ways to help employers defray the costs of setting up such arrangements. Breaking jobs down into smaller roles, known as job fractionalisation, would also make it easier to match senior job seekers with employers' needs.

  Minister of State for Manpower Dinesh Vasu Dash said at a press conference on 26 Oct that the aim is to help seniors find meaningful and sustainable work in more flexible ways. That could mean staying in the same job for longer, moving into a different role, or working at a different pace. He added that prototypes developed by the Alliance for Action on Empowering Multi-Stage Careers for Mature Workers have benefited more than 1,600 seniors. NTUC deputy secretary-general Desmond Tan said the priority is to give seniors choice, assurance, and dignity. That choice must come with "personal readiness and responsibility", he said.

  The workgroup also recommended more financial support. Workers aged 55 to 59 currently get $6,000 in earned income relief, and those aged 60 and above get $8,000. The workgroup wants this enhanced for seniors who work beyond the statutory ages, to raise their take-home pay. It also proposed updating the minimum and maximum amounts of the Employment Assistance Payment, which employers must pay as a last resort if they cannot find a suitable position for a worker at the re-employment age.

  For employers, the Senior Employment Credit, which expires at the end of 2027, will be replaced by a new Re-Employment Credit from 2028. The current credit gives up to 7 per cent in wage offsets for hiring Singaporeans aged 60 and above who earn below $4,000 a month. The workgroup also recommended enhancing the Workfare Income Supplement and updating the Progressive Wage Credit Scheme. This is to improve the salaries and retirement adequacy of lower-wage seniors, while giving employers more support to raise wages sustainably.

  More details will be shared in the first half of 2027, when MOM presents its budget plans at the Committee of Supply.

  Riyadh is in talks with foreign governments to give Saudi Aramco access to storage space. (PHOTO: Bloomberg)

  Singapore will store Saudi Arabian oil in underground caverns from the second half of 2027, Bloomberg reported. The arrangement is part of a Saudi plan to cope with disruptions from the [US-Iran war](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/signs-point-renewed-major-us-175157051.html). The crude will be held at the Jurong Rock Caverns, in a petrochemicals and refining complex in southern Singapore, said Minister for Energy, Trade and Industry [Tan See Leng](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/gas-supply-may-pursue-remedies-054011721.html). He announced the move in a speech at the Singapore International Energy Week conference on Monday (26 Oct). He said it strengthens the resilience of Saudi crude supply in Southeast Asia and adds to Singapore's own energy security.

  Saudi Aramco is set to sign a memorandum of understanding with the Energy Market Authority and JTC Corp on Monday, Tan said, with talks aimed to conclude in a few months. Aramco chief executive Amin Nasser said earlier this month that Riyadh is in talks with foreign governments to give the state-owned oil company access to storage space.

  Bloomberg reported that the move follows disruptions in both the [Strait of Hormuz](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/no-injuries-reported-2-singapore-130331672.html) and the Red Sea, the main routes for Saudi crude exports. It also follows attacks on a major Saudi pipeline and other energy infrastructure.

  JTC, the government agency that owns the caverns, says they can hold up to 1.47 million cubic metres of liquid hydrocarbons, including crude and condensates. That is equivalent to about 9 million barrels of oil. In a pre-recorded video shown at the conference, Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said bringing supply closer to the key Asian market would make the supply network more flexible. He added that the two sides' cooperation has the potential to grow.

* # Former Japanese PM Yasuo Fukuda dies at 90, Nikkei reports

  Former Japanese prime minister [Yasuo Fukuda](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/japan-ex-pm-fukuda-meets-chinas-xi-amid-191032699.html), who sought closer ties with Asian neighbours, particularly [China](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/chinas-communist-party-elite-hold-042836254.html), during his brief time in office, has died at the age of 90. Japanese business daily Nikkei reported the news on Monday (26 Oct).

  The Nikkei said Fukuda died on Thursday from heart failure. He was the first son of a prime minister to become premier himself. His father, Takeo, took the post in 1976 and served for two years.

  The younger Fukuda became prime minister in 2007, following the sudden resignation of [Shinzo Abe](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/shinzo-abe-shanmugam-shocked-death-despicable-murder-122131250.html). He left to become a political secretary when his father became premier. He was elected to the lower house of parliament in 1990 and served as chief cabinet secretary from 2000 to 2004. He resigned that post along with several other cabinet ministers who admitted not paying into the pension system.

  Known for his testy manner, Fukuda drew criticism in 2003 for off-the-record comments suggesting that women who dressed provocatively were asking to be raped.

  Regarded as a diplomatic dove, Fukuda made several visits to China after leaving office to try to improve often fraught relations. He leaves a son, Tatsuo, who is also a lawmaker and has been viewed as a potential future prime minister.

He added that political parties should be open to policy shifts when they are justified.nLim, who represents Sengkang GRC, was responding to remarks by Prime Minister [Lawrence Wong](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/govt-tackle-living-costs-secure-025000713.html) at the PAP's biennial conference earlier that day.

  PM Wong had said the WP has not always taken consistent or coherent positions. He cited its past and present stances on housing and foreign manpower, which he said seemed to shift with changing political sentiment.

  PM Wong said the PAP's approach is different because, as the governing party, it must take a wider and longer-term view. Unlike the opposition, he said, it has to reconcile competing demands and take responsibility for the consequences of its decisions, rather than argue for positions that may be popular at a particular point in time.

  Within this term of Parliament, Lim added, the PAP had also changed its positions on smaller class sizes and COE categorisation by engine size. In those cases, the ruling party resisted alternatives until the last moment. Lim also said PM Wong's criticism lacked nuance and took the WP's positions out of context.

  On housing, PM Wong had said the WP called for fewer new HDB flats to be built in 2019, when resale prices were falling. He noted that the party later criticised the Government for not building enough flats in 2023, following COVID-19 construction disruptions. Lim said the earlier position was raised in the context of lease decay, a longer-term policy challenge. It was not about the short-term construction disruptions caused by the pandemic.

  On foreign manpower, PM Wong cited the WP's 2012 call for greater flexibility in hiring foreign workers, as well as its call for zero growth in the foreign workforce during the population debate. He contrasted these with the party's recent calls to ease foreign worker quotas amid manpower shortages faced by small and medium-sized enterprises. Lim said the WP has always maintained that foreign workers are not fundamentally an issue for a nation of immigrants. Its concern, he explained, is the pace of inflows and the pressure they place on Singapore's physical and institutional infrastructure.

  Beyond the specific disagreements, Lim argued that parties should be willing to change their positions when circumstances or evidence warrant it. Back-and-forth debate refines policy and keeps it relevant, he said. He added that parties should not sacrifice key principles, but neither should they stubbornly cling to outdated policy ideas.

  PAP party newsletter Petir responded to Lim in a separate Facebook post on 25 Oct. It said the issue was not whether policies should change, but whether a party's different positions add up to a coherent approach that accounts for trade-offs and consequences. On foreign manpower, it asked whether the WP's calls formed part of a carefully considered approach, or were simply shifting positions to suit whichever concern was most pressing at the time.

  Policy changes can reflect new evidence, changing circumstances, or lessons learnt, Petir added. However, they must rest on a clear rationale, with the wider consequences properly considered and explained.

  Sunday's vote kept the CEC's slate unchanged, with all 12 elected and two co-opted members returning, including veterans like Senior Ministers Lee Hsien Loong and [K Shanmugam](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/pore-every-legal-regulatory-lever-090000306.html).   Analysts mostly read the result as a preference for stability over sweeping change. Singapore Management University's Eugene Tan said cadres appear to be weighing renewal against the stability and experience veteran leaders bring, while the Institute of Policy Studies' Tan Ern Ser and independent analyst Felix Tan suggested newer ministers like Siow and Lau simply need more time to earn cadres' trust, having only joined the party shortly before the May 2025 general election. Felix Tan, however, noted some tension between the unchanged lineup and Wong's stated push for fresher voices, questioning how much renewal it can really deliver. He also flagged that the outcome also hints at limits on how much influence Wong can exercise over what remains a democratic internal process.

  Analysts linked the preference for continuity to ongoing pressures, including the current energy crisis with Indonesia, the upcoming US midterm elections, and the unresolved conflict in the Middle East. The CEC line-up isn't final yet, with up to four more co-opted members and internal appointments still to be announced, a process that has historically taken between 10 and 20 days.

  Singh suggested Siow and Lau remain likely candidates for co-option, while Minister in the Prime Minister's Office Ng Chee Meng is tipped to join given his position as labour chief.
