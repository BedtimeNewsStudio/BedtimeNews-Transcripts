Oracle spent $55.7 billion on AI data centers in fiscal 2026. Then warned investors the bet may not pay off. In its own SEC filing. The 10-K filed June 22 states: "To grow our OCI business, which requires increased computing capacity, we must incur significant capital and operating expenditures." What the filing does not say — but the numbers do: Capex jumped 162% in one year, from $21.2 billion to $55.7 billion. Free cash flow went negative $23.7 billion — a figure Oracle has essentially never produced before. Severance costs hit $1.84 billion — nearly five times the prior year — as 21,000 employees were eliminated to partially fund the infrastructure spend. The other side of that ledger: Oracle's contracted backlog ballooned from $138 billion to $638 billion in twelve months. More than half of that backlog is reportedly tied to a single name: OpenAI, under a five-year $300 billion Project Stargate data center supply agreement. Here is the deal architecture lesson: Oracle is not making a diversified infrastructure bet. It is making a single-customer concentration bet — at the scale of an entire company's balance sheet. The $638 billion backlog is only valuable if OpenAI's model revenue scales fast enough to consume that compute capacity on schedule. If OpenAI's revenue ramp slows, Oracle's capex is stranded. If OpenAI's IPO reprices the valuation down, Oracle's debt picture changes. Oracle has disclosed exactly this risk in writing — which is precisely why the filing matters more than the earnings call. Fiscal 2027 guidance: $70 billion more in capex. The bet is not smaller. It is accelerating. Raj Brar | Global Deal Architect & Mentor
