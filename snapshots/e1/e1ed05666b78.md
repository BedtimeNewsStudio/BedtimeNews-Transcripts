# Automotive industry in Australia

Automotive industry in Australia

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fa/NAA_A1200_L84254_Ben_Chifley_at_the_launching_of_the_first_mass-produced_Australian_car_1948.jpg/500px-NAA_A1200_L84254_Ben_Chifley_at_the_launching_of_the_first_mass-produced_Australian_car_1948.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NAA_A1200_L84254_Ben_Chifley_at_the_launching_of_the_first_mass-produced_Australian_car_1948.jpg) Labor PM [Ben Chifley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Chifley "Ben Chifley") at the launching of the Holden 48-215 on 29 November 1948[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a9/Australian_large_car_sales_1991_onwards.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Australian_large_car_sales_1991_onwards.png) Graph of large car sales in Australia since 1991

A substantial car industry was created in Australia in the 20th century through the opening of Australian plants by international manufacturers. The first major carmaker was [Ford Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Australia "Ford Australia") and the first Australian-designed mass production car was manufactured by [Holden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden "Holden") in 1948. Australian manufacture of cars rose to a maximum of almost half a million in the 1970s (10th [place in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_motor_vehicle_production "List of countries by motor vehicle production")) and still exceeded 400,000 in 2004.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) Australia was best known for the design and production of 'large' sized passenger vehicles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) By 2009 total production had fallen to around 175,000 and the Australian market was dominated by cars imported from Asia and Europe.

As of 2015, Australian-designed cars were manufactured by [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors "General Motors") subsidiary Holden, and Ford Australia, while [Toyota Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Australia "Toyota Australia") manufactured local variants of its international models, particularly the [Camry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry "Toyota Camry"). However, the Ford Australia engine and vehicle plants closed in October 2016 and the Holden and Toyota Australia factories closed in late 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) Only Ford's design and development facilities remain in operation, leaving Australia as one of 13 countries with the capabilities to design and develop mass market cars from scratch.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) As of 2020, Holden has been shuttered and will no longer be sold as a GM brand.

The [Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Chamber_of_Automotive_Industries?action=edit&redlink=1 "Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries") (FCAI) is the peak representative organisation for car manufacturing companies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) It is Australia's "biggest car manufacturer lobby group" according to [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_(Australia) "ABC News (Australia)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

Australian constructors were very active at the very beginning of both car and aircraft development and in some areas, ahead of their overseas counterparts. Due to the isolation of Australia, it was more practical for Australia to make its own cars.[&#8202;](#ref-back-austehc.unimelb.edu.au-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-austehc.unimelb.edu.au) The first imported car in Australia is believed to be the [Pender-Hertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pender-Hertel "Pender-Hertel") of 1897.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

The [Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_(automobile) "Highland (automobile)") was one of the first types of [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles "Automobiles") to be offered for sale in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), firstly in 1894 as a primitive motorised [tricycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycle "Tricycle"), then two years later as a four-wheeled, two-seater vehicle using bicycle components.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

The first true cars made in Australia were [steam cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_car "Steam car"). The first of these steam cars, the _Phaeton_, was made in 1896 by Herbert Thomson and Edward Holmes of [Armadale, Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadale,_Victoria "Armadale, Victoria"). It was exhibited in 1900 using the first [pneumatic tyres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tire "Tire") made in Australia by [Dunlop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunlop_Tyres "Dunlop Tyres"). The 5 [horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower "Horsepower") single cylinder steam carriage which is now in the Institute of Applied Sciences, [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne"), was reliable and durable enough to take Thomson and a friend 493 miles from [Bathurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathurst,_New_South_Wales "Bathurst, New South Wales") to [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne") at an average speed of 8.7 mp/h.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)

In 1900 Bruno Hammer built [a one off automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammer_(Australian_automobile) "Hammer (Australian automobile)") in [Mount Torrens, South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Torrens,_South_Australia "Mount Torrens, South Australia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

In 1901 Harley Tarrant produced the first [Tarrant automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarrant_automobile "Tarrant automobile"), which was the first petrol-driven car built entirely in Australia in a small workshop in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne"). Before that, Tarrant had been using the shop to build engines. Tarrant was joined in this endeavour by bicycle maker, Howard Lewis. The car was powered by a rear-mounted 6hp Benz engine. This car was followed by many improved designs, including the first fully enclosed car body made in Australia. Later models included locally produced components including: engines, gearboxes and rear axles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-austehc.unimelb.edu.au-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-austehc.unimelb.edu.au) The sole surviving Tarrant is on display at the [RACV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Automobile_Club_of_Victoria "Royal Automobile Club of Victoria") City Club, on the chancery level.

In 1903, the [Australian Motoring Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Motoring_Association?action=edit&redlink=1 "Australian Motoring Association") was formed in [New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales "New South Wales"), [South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Australia "South Australia") and [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_(Australia) "Victoria (Australia)") to protect the interests of motorists. In 1924, this was followed by the [Australian Automobile Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Automobile_Association "Australian Automobile Association").[&#8202;](#ref-back-austehc.unimelb.edu.au-2)[\[12\]](#refnote-austehc.unimelb.edu.au)

In 1917 the [vehicle bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_body "Vehicle body") were required to be made in Australia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)

On 31 March 1925, Ford announced that its Australian headquarters were to be at [Geelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong "Geelong"), [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Australia "Victoria, Australia"). The first Australian-built Ford was a [Model T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Model_T "Ford Model T") launched in June 1925.

Tyre manufacturing also existed in Australia. However, the last tyre factory closed in April 2010 when [Bridgestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgestone "Bridgestone") ceased production in their facility in [Salisbury, South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury,_South_Australia "Salisbury, South Australia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

## Large scale manufacturers

### Current

The current mainstream automotive manufacturers in Australia all build heavy vehicles. Large scale production of light vehicles ended with the departure of Holden.

#### Holden

Main article: [Holden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden "Holden")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/2016_Holden_Commodore_%28VF_II%29_SV6_sedan_%282016-08-13%29_01.jpg/500px-2016_Holden_Commodore_%28VF_II%29_SV6_sedan_%282016-08-13%29_01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2016_Holden_Commodore_(VF_II)_SV6_sedan_(2016-08-13)_01.jpg) The [Holden Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Commodore "Holden Commodore") (prior to 2018) was a locally produced vehicle by [Holden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden "Holden").

In December 2013, Holden announced they would end their local manufacturing operations in Australia on 20 October 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) After the closure of its production plant in [Elizabeth, South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth,_South_Australia "Elizabeth, South Australia"), Holden changed its business focus to car styling and importing.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The Holden brand name was retired in 2020 and dealers compensated as they were shut down.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

#### Nissan Australia

Main article: [Nissan Motor Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Motor_Australia "Nissan Motor Australia")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/1989-1992_Nissan_Pintara_%28U12%29_Ti_sedan_01.jpg/500px-1989-1992_Nissan_Pintara_%28U12%29_Ti_sedan_01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1989-1992_Nissan_Pintara_(U12)_Ti_sedan_01.jpg) The [Nissan Pintara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Pintara "Nissan Pintara") was a product of Nissan Australia.

[Nissan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan "Nissan") began assembling cars in 1966, when [Pressed Metal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressed_Metal_Corporation "Pressed Metal Corporation") began assembly of the [Datsun Bluebird 1300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_Bluebird_1300 "Datsun Bluebird 1300"). By 1971 locally assembled cars were to include the [1200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_1200 "Datsun 1200") and [1600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun_1600_(510) "Datsun 1600 (510)") saloons, with at least 60% local parts content. A deal lasting until 1976 was signed with Motor Producers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)

Nissan used the Clayton factory to build cars in the wake of the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis "1973 oil crisis"). Models produced in Australia included the [Pulsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Pulsar "Nissan Pulsar"), [Pintara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Pintara "Nissan Pintara"), and [Skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_Skyline "Nissan Skyline"). By the end of the 1980s, however, Nissan was facing financial difficulties, while Nissan's local car assembly lines closed in 1992.

#### Volkswagen Australia

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Volkswagen_Country_Buggy.jpg/500px-Volkswagen_Country_Buggy.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Volkswagen_Country_Buggy.jpg) The [Volkswagen Country Buggy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Country_Buggy "Volkswagen Country Buggy") was a product of Volkswagen Australia.

[Volkswagen Australia Pty Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Australia "Volkswagen Australia") was formed in 1954 by [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen "Volkswagen") of Germany and various Australian state Volkswagen distributors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Davis-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-Davis) The company acquired a suitable site from [Martin & King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_&_King "Martin & King") situated at [Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton,_Victoria "Clayton, Victoria") having facilities for CKD vehicle assembly in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Australia "Victoria, Australia"), that site having been used for local assembly of the [Volkswagen Beetle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Beetle "Volkswagen Beetle") since 1954.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Davis-1)[\[39\]](#refnote-Davis) By 1960, sheet metal panels were being pressed at Clayton, and by 1967 the engine and most components were being produced there.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Davis-2)[\[39\]](#refnote-Davis)

In 1967 Volkswagen Australia developed a unique model, the [Country Buggy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Country_Buggy "Volkswagen Country Buggy"), which used components from the [Beetle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Beetle "Volkswagen Beetle") and the [Kombi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Kombi "Volkswagen Kombi").[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40) This local design was later also manufactured in the Philippines.

Due to falling sales, the operation reverted to assembly only in 1968.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Davis-3)[\[39\]](#refnote-Davis) A new company, Motor Producers Limited, was formed and operations were expanded to include [Datsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datsun "Datsun") and [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo "Volvo") models as well as Volkswagens.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Davis-4)[\[39\]](#refnote-Davis) The factory was sold to [Nissan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan "Nissan") in 1976 and Australian assembly of Volkswagens ended shortly after.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Davis-5)[\[39\]](#refnote-Davis)

#### Western Star

Western Star began assembly of kit trucks in 1983 at the former White Trucks factory in Wacol, Queensland. The kits were imported from Kelowna, BC Canada. This continued until 1992, when fully built trucks were imported from Canada.

## Tuning companies

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Holden_Commodore_VK_SS_HDT_%2815322155954%29.jpg/500px-Holden_Commodore_VK_SS_HDT_%2815322155954%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Holden_Commodore_VK_SS_HDT_(15322155954).jpg) A [HDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Dealer_Team "Holden Dealer Team") [VK Commodore SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Commodore_VK "Holden Commodore VK")

Current tuning or customisation companies in Australia include:

- [General Motors Specialty Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_Specialty_Vehicles "General Motors Specialty Vehicles") (GMSV) – official performance vehicle partner of [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors "General Motors") in Australia

- [Walkinshaw Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkinshaw_Performance "Walkinshaw Performance") – authorised Holden Special Vehicles and [Hummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hummer "Hummer") tuning company
- [Corsa Specialised Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsa_Specialised_Vehicles "Corsa Specialised Vehicles") (CSV) is a small-scale automaker established in 1994 that is based in [Mildura, Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildura "Mildura"). Its range consists of V8 engine high performance cars based on those produced by [Holden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden "Holden").
