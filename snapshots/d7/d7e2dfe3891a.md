2026 Southeast Asian haze

The **2026 Southeast Asian haze crisis** is an [air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution "Air pollution") event and the current continuous [transboundary haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze "Southeast Asian haze") season that is affecting several countries in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") since August 2026, including [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), [Timor Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-3) and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"). As wildfires are also happening in [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea "Papua New Guinea"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-4) the [haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haze "Haze") has affected the country as well as causing transboundary haze to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") and several islands of [Eastern Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Indonesia "Eastern Indonesia") including [Lesser Sunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Sunda_Islands "Lesser Sunda Islands"), [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands "Maluku Islands"), [Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea") and [Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulawesi "Sulawesi"). The haze is also suspected to have reached [southern Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Vietnam "Southern Vietnam")[&#8202;](#ref-back-Viet-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-Viet) and [Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicobar_Islands "Nicobar Islands"), India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nicobar-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-nicobar)

This particular haze season was severely exacerbated by a record-strength [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o%E2%80%93Southern_Oscillation "El Niño–Southern Oscillation") climate cycle. Triggering the highest-level environmental alerts across the region, the dense particulate smog has blanketed neighbouring nations, resulting in widespread school closures, flight cancellations, and hundreds of thousands of respiratory illness cases.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-7)

The environmental disaster has significantly strained Indonesia's bilateral relations with [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Malaysia_relations "Indonesia–Malaysia relations") and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Singapore_relations "Indonesia–Singapore relations").[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-8) Tensions rapidly escalated due to the Indonesian government's defensive public reactions, including dismissing the toxic smoke as a natural phenomenon, its ambiguity to formally accept external firefighting aid from its neighbours,[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-9) and highly criticised statements from state communications officials claiming that neighbouring countries should be grateful for the oxygen Indonesian forests provide when there are no fires.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-10)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-11)

## Background and causes

See also: [2026–2027 El Niño event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%932027_El_Ni%C3%B1o_event "2026–2027 El Niño event") and [2026 Indonesia wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Indonesia_wildfires "2026 Indonesia wildfires")

The haze is attributed to the ongoing [2026 Indonesia wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Indonesia_wildfires "2026 Indonesia wildfires"), particularly fires in parts of [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), [Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan") and [Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea "Western New Guinea"). The forecasted [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%932027_El_Ni%C3%B1o_event "2026–2027 El Niño event") has been the strongest ever measured, which intensifies drought and wildfire haze spread, challenging wildfire reduction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-14) Moreover, positive [Indian Ocean Dipole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_Dipole "Indian Ocean Dipole") (IOD) amplifies the risk of wildfire and haze.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fu-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-Fu) The combination of both El Niño and positive IOD causes drier ground cover and adds the risk for severe fires in September–October.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-16) During periods of dry weather, vegetation and peatlands become highly susceptible to fire. Smoke and fine particulate matter generated by these fires can be transported by prevailing winds across national borders, affecting air quality in neighbouring countries such as Malaysia and Singapore.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fu-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-Fu)

In [West Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Kalimantan "West Kalimantan"), the worsening conditions are being closely watched from [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak "Sarawak"), given the proximity of the two regions and the potential for haze to be carried across the border by prevailing winds.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-17)

### Malaysia

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/Persiaran_Perdana_shrouded_in_thick_haze_in_Putrajaya_%28261007%29.jpg/500px-Persiaran_Perdana_shrouded_in_thick_haze_in_Putrajaya_%28261007%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Persiaran_Perdana_shrouded_in_thick_haze_in_Putrajaya_(261007).jpg) Government ministry buildings and boulevard along Persiaran Perdana in [Putrajaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putrajaya "Putrajaya") shrouded in dense smog and haze in October.

The [government of Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sarawak "Government of Sarawak"), in response to the rising API levels, activated its Sarawak State Disaster Management Committee (SDMC or JPBNS) to monitor air quality and issue protective measures for its citizens, including face mask mandates and the cancellation or closure of events and public buildings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-malaymail-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-malaymail)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-20)\[ _[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\]

On 10 August, a forest fire broke out in Sri Aman, [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak "Sarawak"), that it could worsen the haze.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-21) It affected 80 hectares and continued at least for five days.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-23)

There were several forest fires happened in Sarawak. Another forest fire affected 15 hectares land in Daro, [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak "Sarawak") for eight days since 10 August.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-24)[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-25) On 11 August, another forest fire happens in Lundu, Sarawak.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-26) On 14 August, another forest fire happens in Lundu, Sarawak.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-27)

On 21 August, Sarawak continued to experience the second wave of transboundary haze season, with [Serian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serian_Division "Serian Division") surging the highest [Air Pollutant Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Pollution_Index "Air Pollution Index") (API) reading in Sarawak at 242, placing the area in the "Very Unhealthy" category. The SDMC data showed eight other areas in Sarawak recording the unhealthy air quality, underscoring the widening impact of the transboundary haze. These affected areas were Serian at 191, [Lubok Antu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubok_Antu "Lubok Antu") (189), [Kuching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching "Kuching") (188), [Sri Aman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simanggang "Simanggang") and [Lundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lundu,_Sarawak "Lundu, Sarawak") (178), [Samarahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Samarahan "Kota Samarahan") (171), [Tebedu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tebedu "Tebedu") (164), [Sarikei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarikei "Sarikei") (153), and [Sibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibu "Sibu") (125).[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-28)

The SDMC said that they were closely monitoring weather conditions, air quality, hotspots, the risk of forest and peatland fires, and the status of the state's water supply was continuing in coordination with relevant agencies. The intensified monitoring reflects growing concern over the wider environmental and public health risks posed by the worsening haze, particularly in areas close to [Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan "Kalimantan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-sarawak-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-sarawak)

On 24 August, Pengerang, [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor "Johor"), recorded a peatland fire for 51.4 hectares of peatland and dry grass. The peatland and dry grass fire concluded at noon on August 28.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-31)

On 29 August, Serian recorded a hazardous air quality (302). The condition worsens, and on 4 September, _[Yang di-Pertuan Agong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong "Yang di-Pertuan Agong")_, [Sultan Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Iskandar_of_Johor "Ibrahim Iskandar of Johor"), with advice from Prime Minister [Anwar Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Ibrahim "Anwar Ibrahim") and Sarawak Premier [Abang Johari Openg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abang_Johari_Openg "Abang Johari Openg"), declared an emergency after the AQI reached over 500.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-32) [Cloud seeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_seeding "Cloud seeding") operations have been approved in affected areas to mitigate the air quality issues and supplement the area's available water resources.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-34)

On 31 August, one of Sawarak's monitoring stations recorded an API level of 475. [Douglas Uggah Embas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Uggah_Embas "Douglas Uggah Embas") said that the National Disaster Management Agency (NDMA) may declare an emergency should the API reach 500. Sawarak has also cancelled their [National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_(Malaysia) "Independence Day (Malaysia)") activities due to the haze.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-35)

On 6 September, another 10 hectare peatland fire happened on Pengerang, [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor "Johor"). It controlled on 8 September.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-36)[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-37)

On 18 September, according to Environment Department's Air Pollutant Index Management System (APIMS), 34 of 68 stations nationwide recording unsafe levels, with several regions edging dangerously close to the "Very Unhealthy" level. Johan Setia in [Selangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selangor "Selangor") recorded the nation's worst air quality at 198, followed closely by [Kuching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching "Kuching") (192) and [Samarahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Samarahan "Kota Samarahan") (184) in Sarawak. Heavy haze also gripped [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia"), with Nilai logging a reading of 181, Seri Manjung at 176, [Putrajaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putrajaya "Putrajaya") at 173, and both [Shah Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Alam "Shah Alam") and [Seremban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seremban "Seremban") at 171. In Sarawak, [Serian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serian_Division "Serian Division") also recorded an unhealthy reading of 180. Out of 68 stations nationwide, only three logged "Good" air quality, all located in [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia "East Malaysia"): [Limbang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbang "Limbang"), Sarawak (49; [Sandakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan "Sandakan"), Sabah (46); and [Kota Kinabalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Kinabalu "Kota Kinabalu"), Sabah (44).[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-38)

On 6 October, [Batu Pahat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Pahat_(town) "Batu Pahat (town)") recorded an API of 203 at the "very unhealthy" range, which was the highest reading in Malaysia at the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-johor-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-johor)

### Brunei

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Ong_Sum_Ping%2C_1_September_2026_by_Storymalting.jpg/500px-Ong_Sum_Ping%2C_1_September_2026_by_Storymalting.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ong_Sum_Ping,_1_September_2026_by_Storymalting.jpg) Haze covering over Ong Sum Ping Armada Properties buildings, Brunei

The [Department of Environment, Parks and Recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Environment,_Parks_and_Recreation "Department of Environment, Parks and Recreation") under the [Ministry of Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Development_(Brunei) "Ministry of Development (Brunei)") on 31 August at 11 am recorded 94 [pollutant standard index (PSI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollutant_Standards_Index "Pollutant Standards Index") for [Brunei-Muara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei%E2%80%93Muara_District "Brunei–Muara District"), 79 at [Tutong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutong_District "Tutong District"), 95 at [Belait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belait_District "Belait District") and 69 at [Temburong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temburong_District "Temburong District") which was considered in the 'moderate' category (51 PSI - 100 PSI).[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-40)

On 1 September at 4 pm, after taking account of both [particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter "Particulate matter") (PM10) and particulate matter 2.5 (PM2.5), readings from two districts were categorized in the 'Very unhealthy' category (201 PSI - 300 PSI). Brunei-Muara recorded 215 PSI, 168 at Tutong, 211 at Belait and 164 at Temburong.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-41)

The [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_(Brunei) "Ministry of Education (Brunei)") and [Ministry of Religious Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Religious_Affairs_(Brunei) "Ministry of Religious Affairs (Brunei)") advised all government and private institutions to implement a Home-Based Learning (HBL) from 2 to 3 September.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-42)\[ _[non-primary source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources "Wikipedia:No original research")_\]

In May 2026, the [National Environment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Environment_Agency "National Environment Agency") (NEA) and [Meteorological Service Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorological_Service_Singapore "Meteorological Service Singapore") (MSS) warned that there is an increased likelihood that Singapore could experience haze from June to October due to the effects of [El Niño–Southern Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o%E2%80%93Southern_Oscillation "El Niño–Southern Oscillation") and the [Indian Ocean Dipole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_Dipole "Indian Ocean Dipole").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fu-2)[\[14\]](#refnote-Fu)[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-43) The NEA stated that smoke haze from southern Sumatra and West Kalimantan was observed to drift towards Singapore.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-44)

On 4 September, the Central Region[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[b\]](#refnote-45) entered the Unhealthy range (100 - 200 PSI) with a [PSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollutant_Standards_Index "Pollutant Standards Index") of 101 at 7 am. This was the first time since October 2023 that the PSI hit the 'Unhealthy' category.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-46) The Haze Task Force announced that should the PSI enter the 'Very Unhealthy' category (201 - 300 PSI), air-conditioned rooms in all [community centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_centre#Urban_planning "Community centre") (CCs) and selected [residents’ committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residents'_committee "Residents' committee") (RCs) will be on standby. The NEA also announced that it would begin providing daily haze advisories, including the additional 24-hour PSI forecast.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-47)[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-48)

On 7 October, with a 1-hour [PM2.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM2.5 "PM2.5") reading of 152 at 4 pm, the Western region entered the high range (151–250) for the first time in this haze season,[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-49)[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-50) and on 9 October, the Central Region recorded a PSI of 181 at 12 pm,[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-51) rising to 192 from 5 pm to 8 pm, the highest recorded in 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-52) The Haze Task Force announced that N95 masks have been set aside and will be distributed to those who are more affected by the haze should the PSI reach the very unhealthy range (201–300). School closures were considered if the 24-hour PSI forecast is above 300, at the hazardous range.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-53)[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-54)[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-55)

On 14 October, the West Region recorded a PM2.5 of 288 at 3 pm, entering the very high band, the highest reading since 2015. At that time, PM2.5 readings were also at the high range for the South, Central, and Eastern regions, with readings of 177, 218, and 208, respectively. The 24-hour PSI was also in the unhealthy band across all regions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hourly_PM2.5_reading-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-Hourly_PM2.5_reading)[&#8202;](#ref-back-outdoor_sports-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-outdoor_sports) Many outdoor facilities managed by [ActiveSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Singapore "Sport Singapore") in the west, as well as outdoor activities in some schools, were closed or suspended due to the worsening haze,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hourly_PM2.5_reading-1)[\[54\]](#refnote-Hourly_PM2.5_reading)[&#8202;](#ref-back-outdoor_sports-1)[\[55\]](#refnote-outdoor_sports)[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-58) while N95 masks and [air-conditioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning "Air conditioning") haze shelters were made available from that day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-59) The haze negatively affected businesses and food delivery riders, with a drop of up to 60% for businesses since the haze began.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-60)

The 24-hour PSI readings of three regions in the country reached the very unhealthy range on 16 October, the 12th consecutive day where the air quality was poor since [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Southeast_Asian_haze "2015 Southeast Asian haze").[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-61) The readings in central Singapore entered the very unhealthy range at midnight, and reached 229 at 3 pm, making the new highest of the year in Singapore.[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-62)[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-63)

The haze improved throughout the country on 19 October, with the one-hour PM2.5 readings across all five regions decreasing back into the normal range,[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-64) albeit momentarily, as the 24-hour PSI and 1-hour PM2.5 readings increased the following day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-65) The air quality improved throughout the country on 22 October, with the 24-hour PSI of all regions being in the moderate range by 3 pm. Hourly PM2.5 readings were also in the normal range for all regions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-66)[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-67)

Due to improving haze conditions, the Singapore government announced on 5 November they will close all air-conditioned rooms serving as haze shelters and cease all haze mitigation procedures from 7 November, including ending the distribution of N95 masks to vulnerable Singaporeans and daily advisories. The Ministry of Health will also deactivate both the Public Health Preparedness Clinic (PHPC) Scheme and the Haze Subsidy Scheme from 12 November.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-68)[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-69)

### Philippines

In early September 2026, the haze had reached and affected air quality across several parts of the Philippines after smoke from fires in Kalimantan, Indonesia was carried by prevailing monsoon winds. [Metro Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila "Metro Manila"), [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon "Calabarzon") and [Mimaropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimaropa "Mimaropa") were among the affected areas, with several cities recording _very unhealthy_ to _acutely unhealthy_ air quality levels. In [Quezon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon_City "Quezon City"), face-to-face classes in public schools were suspended due to hazardous air pollution, while similar suspensions were implemented in parts of [Metro Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Cebu "Metro Cebu") as the haze persisted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-70)[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-71)

Authorities advised residents to limit outdoor exposure and wear properly fitted particulate masks, such as [N95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N95_respirator "N95 respirator") or [KN95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_filter_(respirator) "Mechanical filter (respirator)") masks, particularly in areas with unhealthy air quality. The haze also affected parts of the Visayas and Mindanao, including Iloilo, Cebu and [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City"), highlighting the wide geographical reach of the transboundary pollution.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-72)

### Vietnam

However, there was not yet enough evidence to draw that conclusion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Viet-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-Viet)

At the same time, the haze was confirmed to have reached the city of [Sabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabang,_Aceh "Sabang, Aceh") in Weh Island.[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-79) However, as of 18 October, no authority had confirmed that the haze in Great Nicobar was caused by Indonesia's forest fires.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nicobar-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-nicobar)

On 3 September, the [Sarawak government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sarawak "Government of Sarawak") approved a three-day [cloud seeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_seeding "Cloud seeding") operation targeting key [hydroelectric dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectricity "Hydroelectricity") in an effort to increase the potential of heavy rainfall in the targeted areas that recorded hazy and unhealthy air quality levels in Sarawak, and also to subsequently help maintain the availability of the region's water resources. This involves the use of [the Royal Malaysian Air Force's C-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Royal_Malaysian_Air_Force "List of equipment of the Royal Malaysian Air Force"), with steel tanks carrying salt solutions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-82)[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-83)

The problem with the cloud seeding operation in Indonesia is there are no clouds and low visibility.[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-84)

### Mitigation of health effects

On 16 October 2026, as haze smokes entered [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta"), Governor [Pramono Anung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pramono_Anung "Pramono Anung") states that he has mobilize 31 hospitals and 44 clinics to anticipate for any health risk may be inflicted after receiving reports from [BMKG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMKG "BMKG") that haze from Kalimantan may spread south towards Jakarta.[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-85)

## Impacts

### Cancellations of outdoor activities and events

Several outdoor activities and events in [Kuching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching "Kuching"), [Samarahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Samarahan "Kota Samarahan"), and [Serian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serian,_Sarawak "Serian, Sarawak") have been postponed or delayed due to the ongoing haze season in [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak "Sarawak"). This including 53rd Sarawak Schools Sports Council (MSS Sarawak) Athletics Championship 2026 which is actually scheduled and take place on August 10 until August 14, but postponed due to unhealthy air quality in Samarahan. Sarawak Education Department ( _Jabatan Pendidikan Sarawak_) also reminded to all district education offices ( _Pejabat Pendidikan Daerah_) and all educational institutions under its purview to take seriously preventive measures to safeguard students and school staff during [hot weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_wave "Heat wave") and [haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haze "Haze") conditions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-86)

On 19 August, Deputy Minister in the Sarawak Premier's Department, Datuk Dr. Abdul Rahman Junaidi, said the 2026 Sarawak-level Maulidur Rasul celebration, will be held at Dewan Sentral Sarikei on August 25, which is will feature only a gathering, without a procession due to the [unhealthy haze situation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze "Southeast Asian haze") and [hot weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_wave "Heat wave"). This Maulidur Rasul celebration was originally to be scheduled and take place at the Sarikei Sports Complex, but the venue and programme were changed as a precautionary measure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-87)

[The Purple Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Purple_Parade "The Purple Parade"), a [disability rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability_rights "Disability rights") movement based in Singapore, postponed its annual march, set for October 24, to the first half of 2027 due to the haze.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-88)

### Closure of schools and education institutions

On 12 August, [Sarawak government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sarawak "Government of Sarawak") announced a total of 108 schools in [Serian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serian,_Sarawak "Serian, Sarawak") area have been closed after the [air pollutant index (API)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Pollution_Index "Air Pollution Index") exceeded over 200. [Deputy Premier of Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Sarawak "Cabinet of Sarawak"), Datuk Amar Douglas Uggah Embas said this solution was in line with the National Haze Action Plan ( _Pelan Tindakan Jerebu Kebangsaan_), which allowed the immediate closure of all schools, kindergartens and childcare centres once the API breaches 200 and above. At the same time, it is also as the precautionary solutions to protect the safety, health and well-being of all students and school staff in Serian. To ensure the continuous learning for the students, home-based teaching and learning (PdPR) will be implemented during the closure period.[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-89)[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-90)

On 20 August, despite the second wave of transboundary haze situation, the deteriorating air quality in [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak "Sarawak") has prompted the closure of all 591 schools under 10 district education offices across Sarawak, amid growing concerns over the impact of the transboundary haze on pupils and students. Sarawak Education Department said in a statement yesterday that the closures involved 509 primary schools with a combined enrolment of 107,590 pupils, as well as 82 secondary schools involving 89,733 students. The affected schools fall under the [Kuching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching "Kuching"), [Padawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padawan,_Sarawak "Padawan, Sarawak"), [Bau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bau,_Sarawak "Bau, Sarawak"), [Lundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lundu,_Sarawak "Lundu, Sarawak"), [Samarahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Samarahan "Kota Samarahan"), [Simunjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simunjan_District "Simunjan District"), [Serian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serian_Division "Serian Division"), [Sri Aman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simanggang "Simanggang"), [Lubok Antu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubok_Antu "Lubok Antu") and [Betong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betong,_Sarawak "Betong, Sarawak") district education offices.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sarawak-1)[\[28\]](#refnote-sarawak)

In [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), dozens of schools in [Pontianak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak "Pontianak") were closed on 11 August, due to the spreading haze caused from the wildfires and open burning activities in [West Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Kalimantan "West Kalimantan") and [Central Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Kalimantan "Central Kalimantan"). The mayor of Pontianak, Edi Rusdi Kamtono, ordered all schools should be closed due to the smoke wafting over the city from fires elsewhere.[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-91)

On 7 October, the [government of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Johor "Government of Johor") announced that all schools in the state will be closed for the next two days due to the worsening air quality.[&#8202;](#ref-back-johor-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-johor) The school closures were extended to 16 October as projected wind movements from 13 to 15 October would bring poor air quality to Johor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-92)

On October 14, the government of [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste") shut down schools nationwide between 15 and 17 October.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-93)

### Impact on sports

On 1 September 2026, a [2026–27 Malaysia FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_Malaysia_FA_Cup "2026–27 Malaysia FA Cup") home fixture between [DPMM FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPMM_FC "DPMM FC") and [Terengganu FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu_FC "Terengganu FC") on this date was postponed due to the worsening conditions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-HazeSept1-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-HazeSept1) The annual cycling event, Lekas Highway Ride, originally scheduled for 12 September 2026 was postponed due to the haze.[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-95)[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-96)

Due to the haze, the [half-marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_marathon "Half marathon") of [Decathlon's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decathlon_(retailer) "Decathlon (retailer)") inaugural Kiprun race on 27 September in Singapore was shortened from the standard 21.1 kilometres (13.1mi) to 10 kilometres (6.2mi), though distances for the other formats stayed the same, with the awards ceremony being cancelled and all categories becoming non-competitive.[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-97)

The [Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One "Formula One") (F1) races in [Sepang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Malaysian_Grand_Prix "2026 Malaysian Grand Prix") and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Singapore_Grand_Prix "2026 Singapore Grand Prix") held in October 2026 were affected by the haze.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-99) Despite the haze, most fans and drivers were largely unbothered by the haze and continued to attend the races anyway,[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-100)[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-101) with no significant impact on business operations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-102) On 8 October, the [Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Internationale_de_l'Automobile "Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile") (FIA) announced that outdoor activity at the Singapore Grand Prix such as entertainment may be restricted should the haze worsen, though the race itself would remain unaffected as the haze was not deemed severe enough to affect outdoor events such as the race. Contingency plans, including the availability of [N95 masks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N95_respirator "N95 respirator"), were set up by the [Singapore Tourism Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Tourism_Board "Singapore Tourism Board").[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-103)[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-104)[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-105) [Williams Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Racing "Williams Racing") driver [Carlos Sainz Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Sainz_Jr. "Carlos Sainz Jr.") noted that there was dilemma for race drivers to continue with the racing event despite the worsening air conditions but will go on based on information given by local authorities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sainz-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-Sainz) He further commented that the haze won't be affecting much towards drivers' performance since they got used to inhale exhaust fumes from cars ahead of them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sainz-1)[\[104\]](#refnote-Sainz) The [MotoGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_prix_motorcycle_racing "Grand prix motorcycle racing") race held in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_motorcycle_Grand_Prix "Indonesian motorcycle Grand Prix") was also similarly affected by the haze, with local track operations at [Mandalika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalika_International_Street_Circuit "Mandalika International Street Circuit") working quickly to ensure track operations were undisrupted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-107)[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-108)

Local sports in Singapore have been impacted by the haze, with matches being cancelled or rescheduled.[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-109) The [Singapore FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_FA_Cup "Singapore FA Cup") final held at the [Jurong East Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_East_Stadium "Jurong East Stadium") was postponed as the PSI in the Western region, where the stadium was located in, was more than 200. Other events were cancelled or postponed as well.[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-110)

#### Rising tensions

### Government censorship and intimidation attempts

Several Malaysian social media influencers got harassed by pro-government Indonesians who send death threats for criticizing Prabowo's handling over the haze.[&#8202;](#ref-back-173-0)[\[171\]](#refnote-173) Similarly, Indonesian streamer [Windah Basudara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windah_Basudara "Windah Basudara") were also harassed by the same group of Indonesians who accused him of being paid by [Anies Baswedan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anies_Baswedan "Anies Baswedan") or having a political interest to smear the government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Website-0)[\[172\]](#refnote-Website) Windah states that he has his own "buzzers" who can harass pro-government influencers in response and he doesn't have to pay them unlike the government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Website-1)[\[172\]](#refnote-Website) Another Indonesian influencer reveals that he was contacted by the government to engage in a social campaign where he was not allowed to discuss about the haze and offered Rp.80 million for doing so, leading to debates on transparency and attack of free speech in Indonesia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-175-0)[\[173\]](#refnote-175)

Several internet users in Indonesia has reported a shadow ban attempt by the government against their social media account. Social media influencer Tasya Farasya and Ustadz Adi Hidayat were among the influential people who spoke about the shadow banning of their Instagram accounts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-176-0)[\[174\]](#refnote-176) The [Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Communication_and_Digital_Affairs "Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs") denied their role in censorship of wildfire related issues and stated that different social media have different community standards and assure the government is open to criticism and suggestions from the people.[&#8202;](#ref-back-177-0)[\[175\]](#refnote-177)

### Sanctions against forest fire perpetrators

[Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Indonesia "Government of Indonesia") President [Prabowo Subianto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabowo_Subianto "Prabowo Subianto") has called for very strict regulations and sanctions against slash-and-burn land clearances, branding the practice as "ecological terrorism", as the country struggles to contain wildfires that have been raging for weeks. [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") is battling its most intense wildfires in 11 years, with " [Super El Nino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%932027_El_Ni%C3%B1o_event "2026–2027 El Niño event")" weather conditions parching the country's forests and fields.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

The islands of [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo") and [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra") have been the worst hit, with the hazardous haze causing a spike in respiratory illnesses. Transboundary pollution has also caused unhealthy air conditions in neighbouring countries including [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") and [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"). During a meeting with his government cabinet, Prabowo said there will be zero tolerance for people or corporations found responsible for starting wildfires. He also instructed more regional governments to immediately abolish exemptions that allow smallholders to clear small plots of land using fire.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Prabowo also called for the police to launch criminal investigations against corporations found to be violating fire safeguards. Following Prabowo's remarks, Indonesian police official Mohammad Irhamni told reporters on September 17 that police have launched 219 criminal investigations linked to the fires, with 162 people named as suspects so far. He added that the police were also investigating 95 companies, but he did not disclose how many of them are suspected of criminal offences.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

On 14 October, the [Indonesian Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Police "Indonesian National Police") named 223 people and nine companies as those responsible for causing forest and land fires, with over 5,150 hectares (12,700 acres) of land area being burnt.[&#8202;](#ref-back-178-0)[\[176\]](#refnote-178)

On 15 October, Singapore's Prime Minister [Lawrence Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Wong "Lawrence Wong") said that they are checking if some companies were responsible for haze-causing fires under the Transboundary Haze Pollution Act.[&#8202;](#ref-back-channelnewsasia.com-1)[\[118\]](#refnote-channelnewsasia.com)[&#8202;](#ref-back-179-0)[\[177\]](#refnote-179) On 16 October, Singapore would send legal notices containing preventive notices to seven companies, which were responsible for starting fires that caused the haze under the Act.[&#8202;](#ref-back-THPA-0)[\[178\]](#refnote-THPA)[&#8202;](#ref-back-181-0)[\[179\]](#refnote-181) Some lawyers noted that the legal action may implicate diplomatic relationships.[&#8202;](#ref-back-182-0)[\[180\]](#refnote-182) On the same day, Minister [Grace Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Fu "Grace Fu") announced that Singapore has issued warnings to 7 Indonesian companies involved in the haze under the [Transboundary Haze Pollution Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transboundary_Haze_Pollution_Act_of_2014 "Transboundary Haze Pollution Act of 2014"). The seven companies are as follows:[&#8202;](#ref-back-THPA-1)[\[178\]](#refnote-THPA)

- PT. Bumi Andalas Permai
- PT. Bumi Mekar Hijau
- PT. Paramitra Mulia Langgeng
- PT. Sumatera Alam Anugerah
- PT. SBA Wood Industries
- PT. Hutan Lestari Raya
- PT. Bangkanesia

On 19 October, Shanmugam announced that the [Singapore Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Exchange "Singapore Exchange") has launched investigations on seven locally listed agriculture companies with possible links to the fires in Indonesia for questioning. The seven companies are as follows:[&#8202;](#ref-back-Shanmugam-0)[\[181\]](#refnote-Shanmugam)

- [Golden Agri-Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Agri-Resources "Golden Agri-Resources")
- [Bumitama Agri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumitama_Agri "Bumitama Agri")
- First Resources
- [Wilmar International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmar_International "Wilmar International")
- Kencana Agri
- [Indofood Agri Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indofood_Agri_Resources "Indofood Agri Resources")
- TSH Resources

In addition, the government of Singapore would also look into [Asia Pulp & Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Pulp_&_Paper "Asia Pulp & Paper") which was incorporated in Singapore and First Borneo Plantations which has ownership links to Singaporean-incorporated companies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Shanmugam-1)[\[181\]](#refnote-Shanmugam)
