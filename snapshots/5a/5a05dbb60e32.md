Summary

This report presents a profile of the membership of the 119th Congress (2025-2026) as of August 4, 2025, or, where noted, at the beginning of the 119th Congress (January 3, 2025). Statistical information is included on selected characteristics of Members, including data on party affiliation, average age, occupation, education, length of congressional service, religious affiliation, gender, ethnicity, foreign birth, and military service.

In the House of Representatives, there are 219 Republicans (plus 3 Delegates), 212 Democrats (plus 2 Delegates and the Resident Commissioner from Puerto Rico), and 4 vacant seats. The Senate has 53 Republicans, 45 Democrats, and 2 Independents, who both caucus with the Democrats. Additionally,

- The average age of Members of the House at the beginning of the 119th Congress was 57.9 years; of Senators, 63.9 years.
- The overwhelming majority, 96%, of Members of Congress are four-year college graduates.
- The dominant professions of Members are public service/politics, business, and law.
- Most Members identify as Christians, and the collective majority of these affiliate with a Protestant denomination. Roman Catholics account for the largest single religious denomination. Numerous other affiliations are represented, including Jewish, Latter-day Saints, Buddhist, Muslim, Hindu, Greek and Russian Orthodox, and Unitarian Universalist.
- The average length of service for Representatives at the beginning of the 119th Congress was 8.6 years (4.3 House terms); for Senators, 11.2 years (1.9 Senate terms).
- One hundred fifty-five women serve in the 119th Congress: 129 in the House, including 4 Delegates, and 26 in the Senate.
- There are 61 African American Members of the House and 5 in the Senate. This House number includes 2 Delegates.
- There are 56 Hispanic or Latino Members serving: 50 in the House, including 2 Delegates and the Resident Commissioner, and 6 in the Senate.
- There are 24 Members (18 Representatives, 3 Delegates, and 3 Senators) who are Asian Americans or Pacific Islander Americans.
- Four Native Americans (American Indians) serve in the 119th Congress (3 in the House, 1 in the Senate).

The portions of this report covering political party affiliation, gender, ethnicity, and vacant seats may be updated as events warrant. The remainder of the report will not be updated.

Education

As has been true in recent Congresses, the vast majority of Members (96% of House Members and 99% of Senators) at the beginning of the 119th Congress have earned at least a bachelor's degree. Sixty-six percent of House Members and 78% of Senators hold advanced educational degrees beyond a bachelor's.[17](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48535#fn17 "CQ, \"11 9 th Congress: Education,\" https://plus.cq.com/members/factfile/education .") The _CQ Member Profiles_ at the beginning of the 119th Congress indicate the following:

- 19 Members of the House have no educational degree beyond a high school diploma or GED;
- 1 Member of the House, and 1 Senator, has an associate's degree as their highest degree;
- 109 Members of the House and 22 Senators earned a master's degree as their highest attained degrees;
- 140 Members of the House (31.7% of the House) and 47 Senators (47% of the Senate) hold law degrees;
- 23 Representatives and 4 Senators have doctoral (Ph.D., D.Phil., Ed.D., or D.Min.) degrees; and
- 21 Members of the House and 5 Senators have medical degrees.[18](#fn18 "Four Senators and 16 Representatives have M.D. degrees, 1 Senator has an O.D. (doctor of optometry) degree, and 4 Representatives have D.D.S. (doctor of dental surgery) degrees.")

By comparison, approximately 40 years ago in the 99th Congress (1985-1986), 84.8% of House Members and 88% of Senators held bachelor's degrees. Fifty years ago, in the 94th Congress (1975-1976), 82.5% of House Members and 88% of Senators held bachelor's degrees. Sixty years ago, in the 89th Congress (1965-1966), 74% of House Members and 57% of Senators held bachelor's degrees.[19](#fn19 "See CRS Report R42365, Representatives and Senators: Trends in Member Characteristics Since 1945 , coordinated by R. Eric Petersen .")

Seven Representatives and two Senators are graduates of the U.S. Military Academy (West Point),[20](#fn20 "Another House graduate of the U.S. Military Academy resigned in July 2025.") five Representatives and two Senators graduated from the U.S. Naval Academy, two Representatives graduated from the U.S. Air Force Academy, and one Senator graduated from the U.S. Merchant Marine Academy. One Representative and two Senators were Rhodes Scholars, one Representative was a Fulbright Scholar, two Representatives were Marshall Scholars, and one Representative and two Senators were Truman Scholars.[21](#fn21 "Rhodes and Marshall scholarships fund study at British universities; Fulbright scholarships fund international exchange programs; Truman scholarships fund graduate study toward public service.")

Religion

Asian/Pacific Islander American Members

Twenty-four Members of the 119th Congress (4.4% of the total membership) are of Asian, South Asian, or Pacific Islander ancestry. This is 0.74% of the total congressional membership.

Foreign Birth

Twenty-six Representatives and six Senators (6.0% of the 119th Congress) were born outside the United States. Their places of birth include Cuba, Germany, Guatemala, India, Japan, South Korea, Peru, and Ukraine. Military Service

At the beginning of the 119th Congress, there were 98 individuals (18.1% of the total membership) who had served or were serving in the military, the same number as at the beginning of the 118th Congress.
