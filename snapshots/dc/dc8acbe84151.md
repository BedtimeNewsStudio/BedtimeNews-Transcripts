Get a Free sample for the Chronic Hepatitis B Market Forecast, Size & Share Analysis Report @ Chronic Hepatitis B Therapeutics Market Chronic Hepatitis B Overview Hepatitis B is the most common severe liver infection in the world. It is caused by the hepatitis B virus (HBV), which attacks and injures the liver and is transmitted through blood and infected bodily fluids, including direct contact with blood, illegal drugs, and unsterilized or contaminated needles. Most hepatitis B infections clear up within 1–2 months without treatment. When the infection lasts more than 6 months, it can develop into chronic hepatitis B, leading to chronic inflammation of the liver, cirrhosis, liver cancer, and/or liver failure. The risk of chronic infection is directly related to the age at first exposure: nearly 90% of infected newborns and babies, up to 50% of infected children (1–5 years), and about 5–10% of infected adults develop chronic hepatitis B infection. Symptoms include fatigue, poor appetite, stomach pain, fever, nausea, vomiting, darker urine, and jaundice. Screening involves a simple, low-cost blood test to detect HBsAg and anti-HBs in serum. A patient who tests positive for HBsAg has an active HBV infection, and further testing, including serum ALT and HBV DNA levels, HBeAg, and anti-HBe, is needed to determine the phase of the disease and course of action. For chronic cases, a liver biopsy may be needed. Antivirals and interferon injections can suppress the virus, but there is no permanent solution. Drug therapy is usually used only in patients with active liver disease. The US FDA has approved seven drugs to treat hepatitis B. Two are injectable interferons, while the other five antivirals are tablets that must be taken every day to suppress viral growth and reduce liver swelling and damage. Immunomodulators, such as conventional IFN-a2 and PEG-IFN-a2, aim to enhance the host immune response but are limited by significant side effects and variable efficacy.
