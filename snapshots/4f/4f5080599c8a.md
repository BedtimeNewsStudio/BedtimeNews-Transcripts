The **XM1216 small unmanned ground vehicle** (SUGV) is a [Future Combat Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Combat_Systems "Future Combat Systems") specific, man packable (< 30 pounds (14kg)) version of the [iRobot's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRobot "IRobot") [PackBot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PackBot "PackBot"). The SUGV is used in Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance (ISR).[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-:0)

## Description

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=XM1216_small_unmanned_ground_vehicle&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Description")\]

The XM1216 small unmanned ground vehicle (SUGV) is a lightweight, man portable [unmanned ground vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_ground_vehicle "Unmanned ground vehicle") (UGV) capable of conducting military operations in urban terrain, tunnels, sewers, and caves. The SUGV aids in the performance of manpower-intensive or high-risk functions (i.e. urban intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) missions, chemical/toxic industrial chemicals (TIC), toxic industrial materials (TIM), reconnaissance, etc.).

Working to minimize soldiers' exposure directly to hazards, the SUGV's modular design allows multiple payloads to be integrated in a [plug and play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug_and_play "Plug and play") fashion. Weighing less than 29 pounds (13kg), it is capable of carrying up to 6 pounds (2.7kg) of payload weight.

The XM1216 can either be remotely manned, or manipulated through use of a [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360 "Xbox 360") [gamepad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamepad "Gamepad") fitted with speciality drivers. Alternatively a ruggedized controller known as Small HaWC (HArm's Way Controller), more suited to combat environments may be used in place of the Xbox 360 controller.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)

The SUGV is part of Spin Out 1 and has entered evaluation at the Army Evaluation Task Force (AETF). It will be fielded to IBCTs starting in 2011.

In February 2012, the Army announced their intention to issue a sole-source contract to iRobot for the XM1216 SUGV robotic system. The contract is for developing, supporting, and testing hardware and software related to the XM1216.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-:0)

In August 2015, the [U.S. Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marine_Corps "U.S. Marine Corps") ordered 75 SUGVs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)
