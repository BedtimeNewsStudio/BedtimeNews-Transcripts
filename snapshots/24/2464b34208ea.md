**Pegasus** is [spyware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spyware "Spyware") created by the Israeli [cyber-arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber-arms_industry "Cyber-arms industry") company [NSO Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSO_Group "NSO Group") that is designed to be covertly and remotely installed on [mobile phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone "Mobile phone") running [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS") and [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_(operating_system) "Android (operating system)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-WP-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-WP) While NSO Group markets Pegasus as a product for fighting crime and terrorism, governments around the world have routinely used the spyware to surveil journalists, lawyers, political dissidents, and human rights activists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-haaretz_9093465-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-haaretz_9093465) The sale of Pegasus licenses to foreign governments must be approved by the [Israeli Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_(Israel) "Ministry of Defense (Israel)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)

As of September 2023, Pegasus operators were able to remotely install the spyware on iOS versions through [16.6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_16#Release_history "IOS 16") using a [zero-click](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploit_(computer_security)#Zero-click "Exploit (computer security)") exploit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-blastpass-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-blastpass) While the capabilities of Pegasus may vary over time due to software updates, Pegasus is generally capable of [reading text messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_surveillance "Phone surveillance"), [call snooping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call-tracking_software "Call-tracking software"), [collecting passwords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystroke_logging "Keystroke logging"), [location tracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_tracking "Mobile phone tracking"), accessing the target device's microphone and camera, and harvesting information from apps.[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:17-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-:17) The spyware is named after [Pegasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus "Pegasus"), the winged horse of [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology").[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)

Cyber watchdog [Citizen Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Lab "Citizen Lab") and Lookout Security published the first public technical analyses of Pegasus in August 2016 after they captured the spyware in a failed attempt to spy on the [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone") of [human rights activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_activist "Human rights activist") [Ahmed Mansoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mansoor "Ahmed Mansoor").[&#8202;](#ref-back-citizen-lab-reoport-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-citizen-lab-reoport)[&#8202;](#ref-back-lookout-report-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-lookout-report) Subsequent investigations into Pegasus by [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International"), Citizen Lab, and others have garnered significant media attention, including in July 2021 with the release of the [Pegasus Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus_Project_(investigation) "Pegasus Project (investigation)") investigation, which centered on a leaked list of 50,000 phone numbers reportedly selected for targeting by Pegasus customers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-pegasusproject-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-pegasusproject)[&#8202;](#ref-back-amnesty-pegasus-project-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-amnesty-pegasus-project)

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

NSO Group developed its first iteration of Pegasus spyware in 2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:17-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-:17) The company states that it provides "authorized governments with technology that helps them combat terror and crime."[&#8202;](#ref-back-VICE-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-VICE)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13) NSO Group has published sections of contracts which require customers to use its products only for criminal and national security investigations and has stated that it has an industry-leading approach to human rights.[&#8202;](#ref-back-project-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-project)

### Discovery

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Discovery")\]

Pegasus' iOS exploitation was identified in August 2016. [Emirati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") human rights defender [Ahmed Mansoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mansoor "Ahmed Mansoor") received a text message promising "secrets" about torture happening in prisons in the United Arab Emirates by following a link. Mansoor sent the link to [Citizen Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Lab "Citizen Lab") of the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto"), which investigated, with the collaboration of Lookout, finding that if Mansoor had followed the link it would have jailbroken his phone and implanted the spyware into it, in a form of [social engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_engineering_(security) "Social engineering (security)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc_37192670-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-bbc_37192670)

Citizen Lab and Lookout discovered that the link downloaded software to exploit three previously unknown and unpatched [zero-day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-day_(computing) "Zero-day (computing)") vulnerabilities in [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS").[&#8202;](#ref-back-citizen-lab-reoport-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-citizen-lab-reoport)[&#8202;](#ref-back-lookout-report-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-lookout-report) According to their analysis, the software can [jailbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jailbreak_(iOS) "Jailbreak (iOS)") an [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone") when a malicious [URL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/URL "URL") is opened. The software installs itself and collects all communications and locations of targeted iPhones. The software can also collect [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi "Wi-Fi") passwords.[&#8202;](#ref-back-forbes-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-forbes) The researchers noticed that the software's code referenced an NSO Group product called "Pegasus" in leaked marketing materials.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc_37192670-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-bbc_37192670) Pegasus had previously come to light in a leak of records from [Hacking Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacking_Team "Hacking Team"), which indicated the software had been supplied to the government of Panama in 2015.[&#8202;](#ref-back-laprensa-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-laprensa) Citizen Lab and Lookout notified [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.")'s security team, which patched the flaws within ten days and released an update for iOS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-apple-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-apple) A patch for [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS "MacOS") was released six days later.[&#8202;](#ref-back-apple2-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-apple2)

Regarding how widespread the issue was, Lookout explained in a blog post: "We believe that this spyware has been in the wild for a significant amount of time based on some of the indicators within the code" and pointed out that the code shows signs of a "kernel mapping table that has values all the way back to [iOS 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_7 "IOS 7")" (released 2013).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Lookout_blog_post-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-Lookout_blog_post) _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ and _[The Times of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_Israel "The Times of Israel")_ both reported that it appeared that the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") was using this spyware as early as 2013.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20180831NYT-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-20180831NYT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20160902NYT-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-20160902NYT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20180831TOI-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-20180831TOI) It was used in Panama by former president [Ricardo Martinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Martinelli "Ricardo Martinelli") from 2012 to 2014, who established the _Consejo de Seguridad Pública y Defensa Nacional_ (National Security Council) for its use.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

### Chronology

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Chronology")\]

Several lawsuits outstanding in 2018 claimed that NSO Group helped clients operate the software and therefore participated in numerous violations of human rights initiated by its clients.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20180831TOI-1)[\[23\]](#refnote-20180831TOI) Two months after the murder and dismemberment of _[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")_ journalist [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi "Jamal Khashoggi"), a Saudi human rights activist, in the Saudi Arabian Consulate in [Istanbul, Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul,_Turkey "Istanbul, Turkey"), Saudi dissident [Omar Abdulaziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Abdulaziz_(vlogger) "Omar Abdulaziz (vlogger)"), a Canadian resident, filed suit in Israel against NSO Group, accusing the firm of providing the Saudi government with the surveillance software to spy on him and his friends, including Khashoggi.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Boot5Dec-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-Boot5Dec)

In February 2024, a US court ordered NSO Group to hand over the code for Pegasus to [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") as part of an ongoing lawsuit filed by the [Meta Platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms")-owned communication app related to the alleged use of Pegasus against WhatsApp users.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)

In September 2024, Apple announced its intention to drop its court appeal against NSO. The company argued that the most critical files about the spyware tool might never be disclosed, while Apple's own disclosures could aid NSO and similar companies. Another reason cited was a US ban on doing business with NSO, and a perceived boost to Apple's threat detection technologies in the three years since the claim was first filed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)
In December 2024, a US court ruled that NSO Group was liable for hacking 1,400 WhatsApp users' devices through Pegasus. The judge determined the NSO Group violated the [Computer Fraud and Abuse Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Fraud_and_Abuse_Act "Computer Fraud and Abuse Act"), [California Comprehensive Computer Data Access and Fraud Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Comprehensive_Computer_Data_Access_and_Fraud_Act "California Comprehensive Computer Data Access and Fraud Act"), and WhatsApp's terms of service.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)

## Technical details

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Technical details")\]

The spyware can be installed on devices running certain versions of [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS")—Apple's mobile operating system—as well as some Android devices.[&#8202;](#ref-back-amnesty-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-amnesty) Rather than being a specific exploit, Pegasus is a suite of exploits that uses many vulnerabilities in the system. Infection vectors include clicking links, the [Photos app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photos_(Apple) "Photos (Apple)"), the [Apple Music app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_(app) "Music (app)"), and [iMessage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMessage "IMessage"). Some of the exploits Pegasus uses are [zero-click](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-click "Zero-click") — that is, they can run without any interaction from the victim. Once installed, Pegasus has been reported to be able to run arbitrary code, extract contacts, call logs, messages, photos, web browsing history and settings,[&#8202;](#ref-back-The_New_York_Times-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-The_New_York_Times) as well as gather information from apps including but not limited to communications apps [iMessage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMessage "IMessage"), [Gmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmail "Gmail"), [Viber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viber "Viber"), [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp"), [Telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegram_(software) "Telegram (software)"), and [Skype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype "Skype").[&#8202;](#ref-back-forbes_20160825_everything-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-forbes_20160825_everything)

In April 2017, after a Lookout report, Google researchers discovered [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_(operating_system) "Android (operating system)") malware "believed to be created by NSO Group Technologies" and named it [Chrysaor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysaor "Chrysaor") (Pegasus' brother in [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology "Greek mythology")). According to Google, "Chrysaor is believed to be related to the Pegasus spyware".[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35) At the 2017 [Security Analyst Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Analyst_Summit "Security Analyst Summit") held by [Kaspersky Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspersky_Lab "Kaspersky Lab"), researchers revealed that Pegasus was available for Android in addition to iOS. Its functionality is similar to the iOS version, but the mode of attack is different. The Android version tries to gain [root access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooting_(Android) "Rooting (Android)") (similar to jailbreaking in iOS); if it fails, it asks the user for permissions that enable it to harvest at least some data. At the time Google said that only a few Android devices had been infected.[&#8202;](#ref-back-KasperskyOnPagasusOnAndroid-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-KasperskyOnPagasusOnAndroid)

Pegasus hides itself as far as possible and self-destructs in an attempt to eliminate evidence if unable to communicate with its command-and-control server for more than 60 days, or if on the wrong device. Pegasus can also self-destruct on command.[&#8202;](#ref-back-KasperskyOnPagasusOnAndroid-1)[\[36\]](#refnote-KasperskyOnPagasusOnAndroid) If it is not possible to compromise a target device by simpler means, Pegasus can be installed by setting up a wireless transceiver near a target device, or by gaining physical access to the device.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-:8)

### Development of capabilities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Development of capabilities")\]

The earliest version of Pegasus – which was identified in 2016 – relied on a [spear-phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spear-phishing "Spear-phishing") attack which required the target to click a malicious link in a text message or email.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-1)[\[37\]](#refnote-:8)

As of August 2016, according to a former NSO employee, the U.S. version of Pegasus had 1-click capabilities for all phones apart from old Blackberry models which could be infiltrated with a 0-click attack.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-:9)

In 2019, WhatsApp revealed Pegasus had employed a vulnerability in its app to launch zero-click attacks (the spyware would be installed onto a target's phone by calling the target phone; the spyware would be installed even if the call was not answered).[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-2)[\[37\]](#refnote-:8)

Since 2019, Pegasus has come to rely on iPhone iMessage vulnerabilities to deploy spyware.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-3)[\[37\]](#refnote-:8)

By 2020, Pegasus shifted towards [zero-click exploits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-click_attack "Zero-click attack") and network-based attacks. These methods allowed clients to break into target phones without requiring user interaction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-:5)

### Vulnerabilities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Vulnerabilities")\]

Lookout provided details of the three iOS vulnerabilities:[&#8202;](#ref-back-Lookout_blog_post-1)[\[20\]](#refnote-Lookout_blog_post)

- CVE-2016-4655: Information leak in kernel – A kernel base mapping vulnerability that leaks information to the attacker allowing them to calculate the kernel's location in memory.
- CVE-2016-4656: Kernel memory corruption leads to jailbreak – 32 and 64 bit iOS kernel-level vulnerabilities that allow the attacker to secretly jailbreak the device and install surveillance software – details in reference.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41)
- CVE-2016-4657: Memory corruption in the webkit – A vulnerability in the Safari WebKit that allows the attacker to compromise the device when the user clicks on a link.

[Google's Project Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Zero "Project Zero") documented another exploit, dubbed [FORCEDENTRY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FORCEDENTRY "FORCEDENTRY"), in December 2021. According to Google's researchers, Pegasus sent an [iMessage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMessage "IMessage") to its targets that contained what appeared to be [GIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIF "GIF") images, but which in fact contained a [JBIG2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JBIG2 "JBIG2") image. A vulnerability in the [Xpdf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xpdf "Xpdf") implementation of JBIG2, re-used in Apple's iOS phone operating software, allowed Pegasus to construct an emulated [computer architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_architecture "Computer architecture") inside the JBIG2 stream which was then used to implement the zero-click attack. Apple fixed the vulnerability in iOS 14.8 in September 2021 as CVE-2021-30860.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42)

As of July 2021, Pegasus likely uses many exploits, some not listed in the above [CVEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Vulnerabilities_and_Exposures "Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures").[&#8202;](#ref-back-amnesty-1)[\[32\]](#refnote-amnesty)

### Pegasus Anonymizing Transmission Network

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Pegasus Anonymizing Transmission Network")\]

Human rights group Amnesty International reported in the 2021 [investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus_Project_(investigation) "Pegasus Project (investigation)") that Pegasus employs a sophisticated [command-and-control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_control_(malware) "Command and control (malware)") (C&C) infrastructure to deliver exploit payloads and send commands to Pegasus targets. There are at least four known iterations of the C&C infrastructure, dubbed the _Pegasus Anonymizing Transmission Network_ (PATN) by NSO group, each encompassing up to 500 [domain names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_name "Domain name"), [DNS servers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_Name_System "Domain Name System"), and other network infrastructure. The PATN reportedly utilizes techniques such as registering high [port numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_(computer_networking) "Port (computer networking)") for their online infrastructure as to avoid conventional [internet scanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_scanner "Port scanner"). PATN also uses up to three randomised [subdomains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdomain "Subdomain") unique per exploit attempt as well as randomised [URL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/URL "URL") paths.[&#8202;](#ref-back-amnesty-2)[\[32\]](#refnote-amnesty)

### Detecting Pegasus

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Detecting Pegasus")\]

Due to the covert nature of its installation, Pegasus was previously only able to be detected via digital forensics. On January 16, 2024, Kaspersky Labs announced in a press release a new method of detecting the spyware for iOS devices that involved inspecting the shutdown.log file, which logs reboot events, for indicators of compromise.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43) Kaspersky developed a tool that extracts, analyzes, and parses the shutdown.log file to ease the process of locating the malicious signature. This method is only effective if the device is rebooted on the same day that it is infected with Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)

Amnesty International released an open-source utility called Mobile Verification Toolkit that's designed to detect traces of Pegasus. The software runs on a personal computer and analyzes data including backup files exported from an iPhone or Android phone.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45)[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46)

Although Pegasus is stated as intended to be used against criminals and terrorists,[&#8202;](#ref-back-project-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-project) it has also been used by both [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism "Authoritarianism") and democratic governments to spy on critics and opponents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47) A UN special rapporteur on freedom of opinion found that the use of the spyware by abusive governments could "facilitate extrajudicial, summary or arbitrary executions and killings, or enforced disappearance of persons."[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)

### Armenia

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Armenia")\]

About twenty Armenian citizens were spied on via Pegasus spyware. Media expert Arthur Papyan said it targeted the key figures of the opposition and the government: current and past government employees who knew valuable state secrets and have political influence, including the former director of the National Security Service and current chairman of the center-right Homeland Party. The local experts suspected that they were targeted either by the government of Armenia or Azerbaijan, or perhaps both. Papyan said that NSO group appears to be jailbreaking a phone and provides interface for viewing the obtained data. Minister of high-tech industry [Vahagn Khachaturyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahagn_Khachaturyan "Vahagn Khachaturyan") also received a warning letter from Apple, he rejected the theory that the spying party could be the current Armenian government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49)

### Azerbaijan

The list\[ _[needs context](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Writing_better_articles#Provide_context_for_the_reader "Wikipedia:Writing better articles")_\] of spied-upon citizens included dozens of journalists and activists from Azerbaijan. It was alleged that their mobile phones were tapped.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50) The head of Azerbaijani service of [Radio Liberty/Radio Free Europe (Azadliq)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty "Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty") Jamie Fly expressed his anger when it was revealed that the phones of his five current and former employees were tapped with Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51)

Reports and investigations indicate Bangladesh is among 45 countries where Pegasus spyware infections were detected between 2016 and 2018, according to research by Citizen Lab.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DailyStar1-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-DailyStar1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-TBS-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-TBS) The spyware, developed by Israel's NSO Group, enables undetected access to smartphones' messages, cameras, and microphones.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DailyStar2-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-DailyStar2)

Citizen Lab identified an operator codenamed "GANGES" using politically themed domains to target devices in Bangladesh, India, and Pakistan.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TBS-1)[\[53\]](#refnote-TBS) During the 2018 elections, opposition figures reported phone intrusions consistent with Pegasus infections, although no direct government link was proven.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FinancialExpress-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-FinancialExpress) The government denies purchasing or using Pegasus, calling allegations "baseless".[&#8202;](#ref-back-DailyStar1-1)[\[52\]](#refnote-DailyStar1)

Bangladesh lacks comprehensive cybersecurity laws, relying on the ICT Act (2006) and Digital Security Act (2018), which prohibit unauthorized surveillance but remain inadequate against advanced spyware.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BusinessHR-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-BusinessHR) Constitutional Article 43(B) and ICT Act Section 63 protect communication privacy, but enforcement gaps persist.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BusinessHR-1)[\[56\]](#refnote-BusinessHR)

Israeli surveillance equipment reached Bangladeshi authorities through intermediaries despite no formal diplomatic ties.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JPost-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-JPost)[&#8202;](#ref-back-AlJazeera-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-AlJazeera) While Pegasus specifically remains unconfirmed in these transactions, the sales underscore Bangladesh's access to advanced monitoring tools.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JPost-1)[\[57\]](#refnote-JPost)

### Bahrain

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Bahrain")\]

Citizen Lab revealed the government of Bahrain used the [NSO Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSO_Group "NSO Group")'s Pegasus to hack activists, bloggers, members of Waad (a secular Bahraini political society), a member of [Al Wefaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Wefaq "Al Wefaq") (a Shiite Bahraini political society), and members of the [Bahrain Center for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_Center_for_Human_Rights "Bahrain Center for Human Rights"). Bahrain reportedly acquired access to spyware in 2017. As per the report, the mobile phones of a total of nine rights activists were "successfully hacked" between June 2020 and February 2021. Those hacked included three members of Waad, three of the BCHR, one of Al Wefaq, and two of the exiled dissidents who reside in London. The Citizen Lab attributed "with high confidence" that a Pegasus operator, LULU, was used by the Bahraini government to breach the phones of at least four of the nine activists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-59)[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-60)

In January 2022, Bahrain was accused of using the Pegasus spyware to hack a human rights defender, [Ebtisam al-Saegh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebtisam_al_Saegh "Ebtisam al Saegh"). The prominent activist's phone was hacked at least eight times between August and November 2019. As per the Citizen Lab, following the hacking attempt, al-Saegh faced incidents where she was harassed by the Bahrain authorities. It included being summoned to a police station, interrogation, rape threats, and physical and sexual assault. The attack left the rights defender in a state of "daily fear and terror".[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-61)

In February 2022, an investigation by [Citizen Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Lab "Citizen Lab") and [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International") revealed that the Pegasus spyware was used to infect the devices of a lawyer, an online journalist, and a mental health counsellor in Bahrain. All of the three activists were critical of the Bahraini authorities and were targeted with Pegasus between June and September 2021. One of the three activists remained anonymous, while the other two were [Mohammed al-Tajer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_al-Tajer "Mohammed al-Tajer") and Sharifa Swar (mental health counselor).[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-62)

In December 2022, an exiled Bahraini activist, Yusuf al-Jamri filed a lawsuit against the Bahraini government and the NSO Group, alleging his phone was hacked using the Pegasus spyware in August 2019. The hacking was confirmed by the Citizen Lab researchers, who claimed that the servers that targeted al-Jamri were connected to Bahrain.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-Guardian)[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64) Yusuf al-Jamri fled to the UK with his family in 2017, after facing multiple detentions, and episodes of interrogation, torture, sexual assault, and rape threats. Experts claimed that he was hacked days after posting tweets about Moosa Mohammed, the Bahraini activist who protested the executions in Bahrain and climbed to the roof of the country's embassy in London.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian-1)[\[63\]](#refnote-Guardian)

### El Salvador

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: El Salvador")\]

In January 2022, [El Faro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Faro_(digital_newspaper) "El Faro (digital newspaper)"), a prominent Salvadoran news outlet, revealed that a majority of its staff had their phones infiltrated using Pegasus. The targeting was uncovered in an investigation conducted by Citizen Lab, and Access Now; the investigation revealed that the journalists of another 13 Salvadoran news organisations were targeted as well. Between July 2020 and November 2021, Pegasus was deployed on the phones of 22 employees of El Faro, including reporters, editors, and other staff. At the time of the targeting, El Faro was looking into governmental corruption scandals, and the government's clandestine dealings with the country's gangs. The Salvadoran government denied responsibility for the espionage, and NSO Group declined to reveal whether the Salvadoran government was a client.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-68)

### Estonia

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Estonia")\]

Estonia entered negotiations to procure Pegasus in 2018, and had made a $30 million down payment for the tool. Estonia hoped to use the tool against Russian phones (presumably for gathering intelligence). Israel initially approved the export of Pegasus to Estonia, but after a senior Russian defense official approached the Israeli defense agencies and revealed that Russia had learned of Estonia's intentions to obtain Pegasus, Israel decided to disallow Estonia from using Pegasus against any Russian phone number (following a heated debate among Israeli officials) so as to avoid damaging Israeli relations with Russia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bergman_&_Mazzetti_23-Mar-2022_NYT-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-Bergman_&_Mazzetti_23-Mar-2022_NYT)

Estonia gained access to Pegasus spyware in 2019. Citizen Lab has uncovered that Estonia has deployed Pegasus outside its borders, against targets in "many" EU countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-:15)

### Hungary

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Hungary")\]

The government of [Viktor Orbán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Orb%C3%A1n "Viktor Orbán") authorized the use of Pegasus by Hungarian intelligence and law enforcement services to target the government's political opponents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bergman_&_Mazzetti_23-Mar-2022_NYT-1)[\[69\]](#refnote-Bergman_&_Mazzetti_23-Mar-2022_NYT) The Orbán government has been accused of using it to spy on members of media as well as on Hungarian opposition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-:6) According to the findings released in July 2021, journalists and managers of media holdings appear to have been spied on by the Hungarian government with Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-80)[&#8202;](#ref-back-81-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-81) Phone numbers of at least 10 lawyers, at least 5 journalists, and an opposition politician were included on a leaked list of potential Pegasus surveillance targets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-82)

### India

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: India")\]

Main articles: [Pegasus Project revelations in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus_Project_revelations_in_India "Pegasus Project revelations in India") and [WhatsApp snooping scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp_snooping_scandal "WhatsApp snooping scandal")

See also: [Pegasus\_(spyware) §United\_States\_(US)](#United_States_(US))

In late 2019, Facebook [initiated a suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp_snooping_scandal "WhatsApp snooping scandal") against NSO, claiming that Pegasus had been used to intercept the WhatsApp communications of a number of activists, journalists, and bureaucrats in India, leading to accusations that the Indian government was involved.[&#8202;](#ref-back-qz_1739097-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-qz_1739097)[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-85)[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-86) 17 individuals including human rights activists, scholars, and journalists confirmed to an Indian publication they had been targeted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-:1)

Phone numbers of Indian ministers, opposition leaders, ex-election commissioners and journalists were allegedly found on a database of NSO hacking targets by Pegasus Project in 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-88)[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-89)[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-90) Phone numbers of [Koregaon Bhima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koregaon_Bhima "Koregaon Bhima") activists who had compromising data implanted on their computers through a hack were found on a Pegasus surveillance phone number list.[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-91)

Independent digital forensic analysis conducted on 10 Indian phones whose numbers were present in the data showed signs of either an attempted or successful Pegasus hack. The results of the forensic analysis threw up shows sequential correlations between the time and date a phone number is entered in the list and the beginning of surveillance. The gap usually ranges between a few minutes and a couple of hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-92)

Eleven phone numbers associated with a female employee of the [Supreme Court of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India "Supreme Court of India") and her immediate family, who accused the former Chief Justice of India, [Ranjan Gogoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjan_Gogoi "Ranjan Gogoi"), of sexual harassment, were also allegedly found on a database indicating possibility of their phones being snooped.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-93)[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-94)

Records also indicate that phone numbers of some of the key political players in [Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka "Karnataka") appear to have been selected around the time when an intense power struggle was taking place between the Bharatiya Janata Party and the Janata Dal (Secular)-Congress-led state government in 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-95)[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-96)

In October 2023, [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") warned Indian journalists and opposition politicians that they may have been targets of state-sponsored attacks using Pegasus spyware. A [Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post") investigation found that the Indian government officials pressured Apple to rescind the warnings. Apple instead sent out emails saying the warnings could have been a false alarm and asked media to mention the same in their articles, while government officials told media that it could have been an "algorithmic malfunction". [Minister of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_and_Industry_(India) "Ministry of Commerce and Industry (India)") [Piyush Goyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piyush_Goyal "Piyush Goyal") said that the warnings were a "prank" by Apple. An advisor to the government, [Sanjeev Sanyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjeev_Sanyal "Sanjeev Sanyal"), alleged that the warnings were part of a conspiracy involving [Access Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access_Now "Access Now"), [George Soros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Soros "George Soros"), Apple and opposition politicians to falsely accuse the government of hacking.[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-97)

### Israel

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Israel")\]

**Israeli police use**

In January 2022, it was reported that Pegasus was unlawfully used by the [Israeli Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Police "Israel Police") to monitor citizens as well as foreign nationals who were accidentally or intentionally infected by the software.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ctech-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-ctech) The surveillance was ordered by high-ranking police officers, and was carried out without warrants or judicial supervision.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:10-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-:10) The legal basis for use of spyware against citizens is disputed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:11-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-:11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-102) The police had allegedly targeted civilians not suspected of any crime, including organisers of antigovernmental protesters, mayors, anti-LGBT parade activists, employees of government-owned companies, an associate of a senior politician,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:11-1)[\[101\]](#refnote-:11) and former government employees.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:10-1)[\[100\]](#refnote-:10) In one case, it was alleged that police targeted an activist who was not suspected of a crime, allegedly to gather information about the activist's extra-marital affairs and use it as leverage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:11-2)[\[101\]](#refnote-:11)

In some cases, Pegasus was used to obtain information unrelated to an ongoing investigation to be used later to pressure the subject of an investigation. In some cases, police used Pegasus to obtain incriminating information from suspects' devices, and then concealed the source of the incriminating information claiming it would expose intelligence assets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-103) While the Israeli Police formally denied the allegations in the report, some senior police officials have hinted that the claims were true.[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-104) The report led to the announcement of a number of parallel investigations into the police's conduct,[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-105) with some officials demanding a [Commission of inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_inquiry "Public inquiry").[&#8202;](#ref-back-ynet.co.il-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-ynet.co.il) Although the [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Israel "Attorney General of Israel") launched an internal probe into the allegations,[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-107) the Privacy Protection Council (which advises the Minister of Justice),[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-108) demanded that a state commission of inquiry be created.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ynet.co.il-1)[\[106\]](#refnote-ynet.co.il)

On 1 February, the police admitted that there was, in fact, misuse of the software.[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-109) On February 7, the widespread extent of the warrantless surveillance was further revealed to have included politicians and government officials, heads of corporations, journalists, activists, and even Avner Netanyahu, the son of then-Prime Minister, [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu"). This has led to renewed calls for a public inquiry, including from the current police commissioner [Kobi Shabtai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobi_Shabtai "Kobi Shabtai") himself (appointed January 2021), as well as from the [Minister of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_(Israel) "Ministry of Interior (Israel)"), [Ayelet Shaked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayelet_Shaked "Ayelet Shaked") and others.[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-110)

Later in the day, the [Minister of Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Security_(Israel) "Ministry of Public Security (Israel)") (the minister responsible for the police), [Omer Bar-Lev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omer_Bar-Lev "Omer Bar-Lev"), announced that he would be forming a commission of inquiry, to be chaired by a retired judge. Bar-Lev stressed that this commission will essentially be granted all the powers of a state commission (whose formation requires full cabinet support), including having the authority to subpoena witnesses, "regardless of seniority," whose testimony may be used in future prosecutions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-111) Despite this, calls for a state commission persisted from several ex-ministry heads who were targeted. The next day, the [State Comptroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Comptroller_of_Israel "State Comptroller of Israel") [Matanyahu Englman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matanyahu_Englman "Matanyahu Englman"), calling the crisis a "trampling on the values of democracy and privacy," said that the investigation launched by his office will also be extensive, adding that it will not only include the police, but also the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_(Israel) "Ministry of Justice (Israel)") and the [State Attorney's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Attorney_(Israel) "State Attorney (Israel)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-112)

In September 2023, [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") issued an emergency software patch after it had been warned that Israel's NSO Group had injected its Pegasus spyware remotely and surreptitiously on to iPhones and iPads.[&#8202;](#ref-back-113-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-113)

### Latvia

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Latvia")\]

Citizen Lab first noted the use of Pegasus in Latvia in 2018; Citizen Lab believes Pegasus is still being used by Latvia as of 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-3)[\[70\]](#refnote-:15)

In February 2023, the iPhone of [Galina Timchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galina_Timchenko "Galina Timchenko"), Russian journalist and co-founder of the Latvia-based Russian news publication _[Meduza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meduza_(website) "Meduza (website)")_, was infected with Pegasus. Timchenko received a notification about a state-sponsored attack against her device from Apple, with experts from Citizen Lab and Access Now subsequently confirming that the device had indeed been compromised with Pegasus, with the attacker having gained full access to the device. The attack occurred the day before a conference of exiled independent Russian media in Berlin which Timchenko attended; her phone could have been used to evesdrop on the journalists' conversations during the conference. This attack is the first confirmed instance of Pegasus being used against a Russian journalist. It is unclear which state carried out the attack: a European intelligence agency have also sought to surveil prominent Russian expatriates, and while NSO Group does not export Pegasus to Russia, a third country could also have carried out the attack on Russia's behest. Timchenko was in Germany during the attack but had a phone with a Latvian [sim card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM_card "SIM card").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-4)[\[70\]](#refnote-:15)

_[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ subsequently ascertained that three other Russian expatriate journalists with Latvian phone numbers also received notifications about state-sponsor attacks against their devices from Apple (as well as one additional Russian journalist with a non-Latvian phone number).[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-122)

### Mexico

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Mexico")\]

According to the _New York Times_, Mexico has been "the first and most prolific user of Pegasus".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-:16) Mexico in 2011 became the first country to purchase Pegasus,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-:7) seeing it as a novel tool in the country's struggle against drug cartels. Mexican authorities also sought to cultivate autonomous intelligence capabilities, having hitherto been highly reliant on the United States for intelligence gathering capabilities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-1)[\[123\]](#refnote-:16)

Early versions of Pegasus were used to surveil the phone of Joaquín Guzmán, known as [El Chapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Chapo "El Chapo"). In 2011, Mexican President [Felipe Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Calder%C3%B3n "Felipe Calderón") reportedly called NSO to thank the company for its role in Guzmán's capture.[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-125)[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-126)

Within years, authorities began to use Pegasus to target civil society (including human rights advocates, anti-corruption activists, and journalists).[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-2)[\[123\]](#refnote-:16) When a list of 50,000 phone numbers of potential Pegasus surveillance targets (selected by individual client governments) was leaked in 2021, a third of them were Mexican.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[124\]](#refnote-:7)

President [Andrés Manuel López Obrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Manuel_L%C3%B3pez_Obrador "Andrés Manuel López Obrador") (who took office in 2018) had pledged to halt the use of the spyware by Mexican authorities, nonetheless, reports of use and abuse of Pegasus have continued throughout his presidency (including an opposition politician). After federal police and intelligence agency reforms by the Obrador government, the Mexican military became the sole Pegasus user in 2019. The Mexican armed forces have been a prolific user of Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-3)[\[123\]](#refnote-:16) The Mexican armed forces have taken on an ever more prominent role during Obrador's presidency, and may have grown into an independent power center capable of autonomously spying on civilian detractors and critics, with the government powerless to reign in its abuses.[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-127)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:18-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-:18) The military went so far as to target [Alejandro Encinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Encinas_Rodr%C3%ADguez "Alejandro Encinas Rodríguez"), the country's under-secretary for human rights (and a close ally of President Obrador), a prominent critic of the military who was investigating human rights abuses committed by the Mexican military at the time of the targeting, as well as other government officials involved in this inquiry.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:18-1)[\[128\]](#refnote-:18)

In 2023, an investigation concluded that journalists at the newspaper [El Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Universal_(Mexico_City) "El Universal (Mexico City)") and human rights defender Raymundo Ramos was illegally surveillanced by the Mexican Armed Forces using Pegasus. The analysis by Citizen Lab found that Ramos’ phone was infected multiple times in connection to his reporting in July 2020. This incident was a manhunt where soldiers chasing several pickup trucks resulted in killing passengers who the military claimed were involved in a local criminal group.[&#8202;](#ref-back-129-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-129) Ramos interviewed family members of the victims of this incident who said they were innocent and were actually kidnapped by the cartel, which led to Ramos publishing allegations that the government targeted innocent people in the manhunt.[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-130)

As of 2023, Mexico's spending on Pegasus had totaled over $60 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-4)[\[123\]](#refnote-:16)

**Targeting of scientists and health campaigners**

In 2017, Citizen Lab researchers revealed that NSO exploit links may have been sent to Mexican scientists and public health campaigners.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bitter-sweet-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-bitter-sweet) The targets supported measures to reduce childhood obesity, including [Mexico's "Soda Tax."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugary_drink_tax#Mexico "Sugary drink tax")[&#8202;](#ref-back-bitter-sweet-1)[\[131\]](#refnote-bitter-sweet)

**2014 Iguala mass kidnapping**

In July 2017, the international team assembled to investigate the [2014 Iguala mass kidnapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Iguala_mass_kidnapping "2014 Iguala mass kidnapping") publicly complained they thought they were being surveilled by the Mexican government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ahmed_10-Jul-2017_NYT-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-Ahmed_10-Jul-2017_NYT) They stated that the Mexican government used Pegasus to send them messages about funeral homes containing links which, when clicked, allowed the government to surreptitiously listen to the investigators.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ahmed_10-Jul-2017_NYT-1)[\[132\]](#refnote-Ahmed_10-Jul-2017_NYT) The Mexican government has repeatedly denied any unauthorized hacking.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ahmed_10-Jul-2017_NYT-2)[\[132\]](#refnote-Ahmed_10-Jul-2017_NYT)

In 2023, it was revealed that Mexican Army intelligence was using Pegasus to monitor [Guerreros Unidos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerreros_Unidos "Guerreros Unidos") cartel members and police officials in the area at the time of the kidnapping, capturing a cartel boss and the police commander discussing where to take the students that night. The Army had soldiers on the streets and a local battalion had an informant embedded with the students. Intercepted communications days later revealed two suspects talking about releasing students, indicating they may still have been alive. Despite this, the military never shared any of this information with officials searching for the students, and there is no evidence to suggest they attempted a rescue.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYTimesIgualaStudents-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-NYTimesIgualaStudents)

**Assassination of journalist Cecilio Pineda Birto**

Cecilio Pineda Birto, a Mexican freelance journalist was assassinated by hitmen while resting in a hammock by a carwash. Brito had been reporting on the ties between local politicians and criminal organizations, and had received anonymous death threats during the weeks preceding the assassination; at about the same time, his phone number was selected as a possible target for Pegasus surveillance by a Mexican Pegasus client. Pegasus spyware may have been used to ascertain Brito's location to carry out the hit by geolocating his phone; the deployment of Pegasus on his phone, however, could not be confirmed as his phone disappeared from the scene of the murder.[&#8202;](#ref-back-134-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-134)

**Targeting of presidential candidate Obrador**

In the run-up to the 2018 Mexican presidential election, dozens of close associates of the presidential candidate Andrés Manuel López Obrador (who was subsequently elected) were selected as potential targets. Potential targets included close family members, his cardiologist, and members of his personal and political inner circle. Recordings of Obrador's conversations with family and party colleagues were subsequently leaked to the public in an attempt to disrupt his electoral campaign.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:14-2)[\[67\]](#refnote-:14)

**Use by Mexican drug cartels**

Pegasus has been used by drug cartels and cartel-entwined government actors to target and intimidate Mexican journalists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-135)

**Other**

A widow of slain renowned Mexican journalist was a target of an attempted Pegasus attack 10 days after her husband was assassinated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-136-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-136)

### Morocco

### Netherlands

### Panama

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: Panama")\]

President of Panama [Ricardo Martinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Martinelli "Ricardo Martinelli") personally sought to obtain cyberespionage tools after his election in 2009. After a rebuff by the U.S. in 2009, Martinelli successfully sought such tools from Israeli vendors, expressing an interest in acquiring a tool capable of hacking into mobile phones in a 2010 private meeting with Israeli PM Netanyahu. In 2012, NSO systems were installed in Panama City. The equipment was subsequently widely used for illicit domestic and foreign spying, including for spying on political opponents, magistrates, union leaders, and business competitors, with Martinelli allegedly going so far as to order the surveillance of his mistress using Pegasus. After Mexico, it was the second country to use Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:17-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-:17)

### Poland

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=34 "Edit section: Poland")\]

Pegasus licenses were agreed on between [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu") and [Beata Szydło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beata_Szyd%C5%82o "Beata Szydło") in July 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-144) Citizen Lab revealed that several members of political opposition groups in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland") were hacked by Pegasus spyware, raising alarming questions about the Polish government's use of the software. A lawyer representing Polish opposition groups and a prosecutor involved in a case against the ruling Law and Justice party were also compromised.[&#8202;](#ref-back-145-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-145) A subsequent investigation by the prosecutor general's office revealed Pegasus was used against 578 people from 2017 to 2022, by three separate government agencies: the Central Anticorruption Bureau, the Military Counterintelligence Service and the Internal Security Agency.[&#8202;](#ref-back-146-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-146)

In December 2021, Citizen Lab announced that Pegasus was used against lawyer [Roman Giertych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Giertych "Roman Giertych") and prosecutor Ewa Wrzosek, both critical of the ruling [Law and Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_Justice_(Poland) "Law and Justice (Poland)") (PiS) government, with Giertych's phone suffering 18 intrusions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-147) 33 hacks to the phone of [Krzysztof Brejza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Brejza "Krzysztof Brejza"), a [senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Poland "Senate of Poland") from the opposition [Civic Platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Platform "Civic Platform") (PO) were uncovered,[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-148) and confirmed by [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-:2) Leading to the [2019 European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election "2019 European Parliament election") and [Polish parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Polish_parliamentary_election "2019 Polish parliamentary election") elections, Brejza's text messages were stolen as he was leading the opposition parties' campaign. The texts were doctored by state-run media, notably [TVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telewizja_Polska "Telewizja Polska"), and used in a [smear campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smear_campaign "Smear campaign") against the opposition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[149\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-150)[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-151) This prompted the Polish Senate to begin an inquiry into the deployment of the spyware.[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-152)

On January 25, 2022, more victims were confirmed by Citizen Lab, including [Michał Kołodziejczak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C5%82_Ko%C5%82odziejczak "Michał Kołodziejczak") of the agrarian movement [Agrounia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGROunia "AGROunia"), and Tomasz Szwejgiert, a journalist and alleged former associate of the CBA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-153-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-153)[&#8202;](#ref-back-154-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-154)

According to the [Supreme Audit Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Audit_Office_(Poland) "Supreme Audit Office (Poland)") (NIK), 544 of its employees' devices were under surveillance over 7,300 times, some could be infected with Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-155-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-155)

In January 2024, Poland's [Sejm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm "Sejm"), the lower house of its parliament, established a special commission to investigate the use of Pegasus by the PiS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-156-0)[\[156\]](#refnote-156) Appearing in front of the commission in March 2024, former prime minister [Jarosław Kaczyński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaros%C5%82aw_Kaczy%C5%84ski "Jarosław Kaczyński") testified that “use of Pegasus was in accordance with the law, there were no shortcomings, and in 99% it was used against criminals.”[&#8202;](#ref-back-157-0)[\[157\]](#refnote-157)

On January 31, 2025, former justice minister [Zbigniew Ziobro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zbigniew_Ziobro "Zbigniew Ziobro") was arrested over allegations of the misuse of Pegasus spyware.[&#8202;](#ref-back-158-0)[\[158\]](#refnote-158)[&#8202;](#ref-back-159-0)[\[159\]](#refnote-159)

### Saudi Arabia

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=36 "Edit section: Saudi Arabia")\]

In December 2020, it was reported that [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia") and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") deployed a zero-click iMessage Pegasus exploit against two [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London")-based reporters and 36 journalists at the _[Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Media_Network "Al Jazeera Media Network")_ television network in [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar "Qatar").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[39\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-:5)

**Jamal Khashoggi**

Pegasus was used by Saudi Arabia to spy on [Jamal Kashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi "Jamal Khashoggi"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-nytimes_20181202_world-0)[\[163\]](#refnote-nytimes_20181202_world) who was later [assassinated in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Jamal_Khashoggi "Assassination of Jamal Khashoggi"). In October 2018, Citizen Lab reported on the use of NSO software to spy on the inner circle of [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi "Jamal Khashoggi") just before his murder. Citizen Lab's October report[&#8202;](#ref-back-164-0)[\[164\]](#refnote-164) stated with high confidence that NSO's Pegasus had been placed on the iPhone of Saudi dissident Omar Abdulaziz, one of Khashoggi's confidantes, months before. Abdulaziz stated that the software revealed Khashoggi's "private criticisms of the Saudi royal family," which according to Abdulaziz "played a major role" in Khashoggi's death.[&#8202;](#ref-back-seattletimes_Satter_20190124-0)[\[165\]](#refnote-seattletimes_Satter_20190124)

In December 2018, a _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times")_ investigation concluded that Pegasus software played a role in the Khashoggi's murder, with a friend of Khashoggi stating in a filing that Saudi authorities had used the Israeli-made software to spy on the dissident.[&#8202;](#ref-back-166-0)[\[166\]](#refnote-166) NSO CEO Shalev Hulio stated that the company had not been involved in the "terrible murder", but declined to comment on reports that he had personally traveled to the Saudi capital [Riyadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh "Riyadh") for a $55 million Pegasus sale.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[167\]](#refnote-:3)

In 2021, allegations arose that the software may also have been used to spy on members of [Kashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi "Jamal Khashoggi")'s family.[&#8202;](#ref-back-168-0)[\[168\]](#refnote-168) The wife of Jamal Khashoggi, Hanan Elatr, intended to sue the NSO Group, alleging that she was targeted with Pegasus spyware. She also prepared a lawsuit in the US against the governments of Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates for their involvement in the attempts to install the software on her mobile phone. Elatr was arrested in [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai") in April 2018. Activity on Etatr's confiscated phone, while she was in the custody of UAE intelligence services, further suggested that an attempt was made to install the software at that time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-169-0)[\[169\]](#refnote-169)

**Targeting of Jeff Bezos**

Main article: [Jeff Bezos phone hacking incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos_phone_hacking_incident "Jeff Bezos phone hacking incident")

Pegasus was also used to spy on [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos "Jeff Bezos") after [Mohammed bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Salman "Mohammed bin Salman"), the crown-prince of Saudi Arabia, exchanged messages with him that exploited [then-unknown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-day_(computing) "Zero-day (computing)") vulnerabilities in WhatsApp.[&#8202;](#ref-back-170-0)[\[170\]](#refnote-170)[&#8202;](#ref-back-171-0)[\[171\]](#refnote-171)

**Targeting of journalist Ben Hubbard**

Ben Hubbard, a [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East") correspondent for the _New York Times_, revealed in October 2021 that Saudi Arabia used the NSO Group's Pegasus software to hack into his phone. Hubbard was targeted repeatedly over a three-year period between June 2018 to June 2021 while he was reporting on [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia"), and writing a book about the Saudi Crown Prince [Mohammed bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Bin_Salman_Al_Saud "Mohammad Bin Salman Al Saud"). Hubbard was possibly targeted for writing the book about the Crown Prince, and for his involvement in revealing the UAE's hacking and surveillance attempt of Project Raven. Saudis attempted to peek into Hubbard's personal information twice in 2018, one through a suspicious text message and the other through an Arabic [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") message inviting him to a protest at a Saudi embassy in Washington.

Two other attacks were launched against him in 2020 and 2021 using the zero-click hacking capabilities. Lastly, on June 13, 2021, an iPhone belonging to Hubbard was successfully hacked using the FORCEDENTRY exploit. Citizen Lab said in "high confidence" that the four attacks were attempted using Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-172-0)[\[172\]](#refnote-172)[&#8202;](#ref-back-173-0)[\[173\]](#refnote-173)

**Other targets**

Another Saudi exile [Omar Abdulaziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Abdulaziz_(vlogger) "Omar Abdulaziz (vlogger)") in Canada was identified by [McKinsey & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_&_Company#Role_in_Saudi_clampdown_on_dissidents "McKinsey & Company") as being an influential dissident, and hence had two brothers imprisoned by the Saudi authorities, and his cell phone hacked by Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nytimes_20181202_world-1)[\[163\]](#refnote-nytimes_20181202_world)[&#8202;](#ref-back-174-0)[\[174\]](#refnote-174)

In June 2018, a Saudi satirist, [Ghanem Almasarir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanem_al-Dosari "Ghanem al-Dosari"), was targeted by Saudi Arabia with Pegasus software. The targeting and hacking of Almasarir's phone by a network linked to Saudi Arabia was confirmed by researchers at the [Citizen Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Lab "Citizen Lab"). On May 28, 2019, the letter of claim was delivered to the [Saudi embassy in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Saudi_Arabia,_London "Embassy of Saudi Arabia, London") on behalf of Ghanem Almasarir. In August 2022, a British judge ruled that the prominent dissident in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") can sue Saudi Arabia for Pegasus hacking.[&#8202;](#ref-back-175-0)[\[175\]](#refnote-175)

### Spain

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=39 "Edit section: Spain")\]

#### Use against Catalan and Basque officials and independence proponents

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=40 "Edit section: Use against Catalan and Basque officials and independence proponents")\]

See also: [CatalanGate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CatalanGate "CatalanGate")

According to an investigation by _The Guardian_ and _El País_, Pegasus software was used by the government of [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") to compromise the phones of several politicians active in the [Catalan independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_independence_movement "Catalan independence movement"), including [President of the Parliament of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Parliament_of_Catalonia "List of presidents of the Parliament of Catalonia") [Roger Torrent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Torrent "Roger Torrent"), and former member of the [Parliament of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Catalonia "Parliament of Catalonia") [Anna Gabriel i Sabaté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Gabriel_i_Sabat%C3%A9 "Anna Gabriel i Sabaté").[&#8202;](#ref-back-179-0)[\[179\]](#refnote-179)

[The scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CatalanGate "CatalanGate") resurfaced in April 2022 following the publication of a report of a CitizenLab investigation that revealed widespread use of Pegasus against Catalan politicians and citizens, as well as Basque politician [Arnaldo Otegi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaldo_Otegi "Arnaldo Otegi") and MP [Jon Iñarritu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_I%C3%B1arritu "Jon Iñarritu").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[180\]](#refnote-:12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-181-0)[\[181\]](#refnote-181) A total of 63 victims was identified,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-0)[\[182\]](#refnote-:13) with targets including elected officials (including high-ranking ones) and civil society members (including activists, journalists, lawyers, and computer scientists).[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-1)[\[180\]](#refnote-:12) The true extent of the targeting was potentially far larger as Android devices are far more common in Spain while CitizenLab tools are specialised to uncover infiltration of Apple devices. Citizen Lab did not attribute the responsibility for the attacks to any perpetrators, but did note that circumstantial evidence strongly suggests the attacks were perpetrated by the Spanish Government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-1)[\[182\]](#refnote-:13) On May 5, 2022, the Spanish Defense Minister admitted to surveillance of 20 people involved in the Catalan independence movement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-183-0)[\[183\]](#refnote-183)

#### Use against Spanish government officials

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=41 "Edit section: Use against Spanish government officials")\]

In May 2022, the Spanish Government revealed that the smartphones of Prime Minister [Pedro Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_S%C3%A1nchez "Pedro Sánchez") and Defense Minister [Margarita Robles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarita_Robles "Margarita Robles") had been targeted by Pegasus in May 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-184-0)[\[184\]](#refnote-184) Prime Minister Sanchez's device was infected twice, and Robles' device was infected once. A total of over 2.7GB of data was exfiltrated from the PM device, while only 9MB of data was extracted from the Defense Minister's device.[&#8202;](#ref-back-185-0)[\[185\]](#refnote-185) [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco") was believed to be behind the attack given the diplomatic tensions between the two at the time of the target.[&#8202;](#ref-back-186-0)[\[186\]](#refnote-186)

In 2025, the Spanish high criminal court dismissed its investigation from 2023 (which was a re-opened from the incident in 2022 due to new evidence showing that Pegasus targeted French politicians, lawyers and journalists) due to lack of co-operation from Israeli authorities in responding to requests for information.[&#8202;](#ref-back-187-0)[\[187\]](#refnote-187)

### Ukraine

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=46 "Edit section: Ukraine")\]

At least since 2019, Ukraine had sought to obtain Pegasus in its effort to counter what it saw as an increasing threat of Russian aggression and espionage. However, Israel had imposed a near-total ban on weapons sales to Ukraine (which also encompassed cyberespionage tools), wary of selling Pegasus to states that would use the tool against Russia so as not to damage relations with Russia. In August 2021, at a time when Russian troops were amassing on the Ukrainian border, Israel again rebuffed a request from a Ukrainian delegation asking to obtain Pegasus; according to a Ukrainian official familiar with the matter, Pegasus could have provided critical support in Ukraine's effort to monitor Russian military activity. In the wake of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine"), Ukrainian officials rebuked Israel's tepid support of Ukraine and Israeli efforts to maintain amicable relations with Russia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bergman_&_Mazzetti_23-Mar-2022_NYT-2)[\[69\]](#refnote-Bergman_&_Mazzetti_23-Mar-2022_NYT)

### United Arab Emirates

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=47 "Edit section: United Arab Emirates")\]

In December 2020, it was reported that [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia") and the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") deployed a zero-click iMessage Pegasus exploit against two [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London")-based reporters and 36 journalists at the _[Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Media_Network "Al Jazeera Media Network")_ television network in [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar "Qatar").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-2)[\[39\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-2)[\[40\]](#refnote-:5)

Qatar reportedly attempted to hire Israeli-based cybersecurity firm Sdema Group for a "several dozen million-euro contract to provide physical and cyber-security services to Qatar's World Cup facilities.".[&#8202;](#ref-back-191-0)[\[191\]](#refnote-191)

The United Arab Emirates used Pegasus to spy on the members of Saudi-backed Yemeni government according to an investigation published in July 2021. The UAE used the spyware to monitor and spy on the ministers of the internationally recognized government of President [Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdrabbuh_Mansur_Hadi "Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi"), including Yemeni president and his family members, former Prime Minister [Ahmed Obaid Bin Dagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Obaid_Bin_Dagher "Ahmed Obaid Bin Dagher"), former Foreign Minister [Abdulmalik Al-Mekhlafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulmalik_Al-Mekhlafi "Abdulmalik Al-Mekhlafi"), and current Minister of Youth and Sports, [Nayef al-Bakri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayef_al-Bakri "Nayef al-Bakri").[&#8202;](#ref-back-192-0)[\[192\]](#refnote-192)

In August 2021, Amnesty International confirmed that [David Haigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Haigh "David Haigh"), a prominent British Human Rights lawyer and founder of Human Rights NGO Detained International, was the first British person to have evidence on his mobile phone that it had been hacked by NSO spyware.[&#8202;](#ref-back-193-0)[\[193\]](#refnote-193) It is believed the illegal hacking was carried out in August 2020 by the government of [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai"). At the time of the infection, [David Haigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Haigh "David Haigh") was the lawyer representing Dubai Princess [Latifa bint Mohammed Al Maktoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latifa_bint_Mohammed_Al_Maktoum_(born_1985) "Latifa bint Mohammed Al Maktoum (born 1985)") who was being held hostage, and he was assisting [Princess Haya bint Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Haya_bint_Hussein "Princess Haya bint Hussein") and her legal team as well.[&#8202;](#ref-back-194-0)[\[194\]](#refnote-194) Haigh had been exchanging videos and text messages in secret for more than a year and a half with Princess Latifa through a phone that had been smuggled into the Dubai villa where she was being held. She stopped responding on July 21, 2020, according to a screenshot of the messages Haigh shared. The analysis shows that Haigh's phone was hacked two weeks later.[&#8202;](#ref-back-195-0)[\[195\]](#refnote-195)

On September 24, 2021, _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ reported that the telephone of [Alaa al-Siddiq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaa_al-Siddiq "Alaa al-Siddiq"), executive director of [ALQST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALQST "ALQST"), who died in a car accident in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") on June 20, 2021, was infected with the Pegasus spyware for 5 years until 2020. Citizen Lab confirmed that the Emirati activist was hacked by a government client of Israel's NSO Group. The case represented a worrying trend for activists and dissidents, who escaped the UAE to live in the relative safety, but were never out of the reach of Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-196-0)[\[196\]](#refnote-196)

In October 2021, the British [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice "High Court of Justice") ruled that agents of [Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Rashid_Al_Maktoum "Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum") used Pegasus to [hack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking "Phone hacking") the phones of his (ex)-wife, [Princess Haya bint Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Haya_bint_Hussein "Princess Haya bint Hussein"), her solicitors (including baroness [Fiona Shackleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Shackleton "Fiona Shackleton")), a personal assistant and two members of her security team in the summer of 2020. The court ruled that the agents acted "with the express or implied authority" of the sheikh; he denied knowledge of the hacking. The judgment referred to the hacking as "serial breaches of (UK) domestic criminal law", "in violation of fundamental common law and [ECHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights "European Convention on Human Rights") rights", "interference with the process of this court and the mother's access to justice" and "abuse of power" by a head of state. NSO had contacted an intermediary in August 2020 to inform Princess Haya of the hack and is believed to have terminated its contract with the UAE.[&#8202;](#ref-back-197-0)[\[197\]](#refnote-197)

On October 7, 2021, the [NSO Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSO_Group "NSO Group") stated that it had terminated its contract with the UAE to use its Pegasus spyware tool after the ruling by [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom")'s High Court that [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai")'s ruler misused the firm's Pegasus software to spy on his ex-wife and her legal advisers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-198-0)[\[198\]](#refnote-198)

In 2022, sources revealed that a unit of Abu Dhabi's [Mubadala Investment Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubadala_Investment_Company "Mubadala Investment Company"), Mubadala Capital was one of the largest investors in €1 billion Novalpina Capital [private equity fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_equity_fund "Private equity fund"), which bought the NSO Group in 2019. Since then, Mubadala has been an investor in the firm with its commitment of €50 million, acquiring a seat on the committee of largest investors of the equity fund. Journalists, human rights defenders and the [women of Dubai's royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Haya_bint_Hussein "Princess Haya bint Hussein") were traced to have been hacked using the Pegasus spyware during the same time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-199-0)[\[199\]](#refnote-199)

A report by the [Citizen Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Lab "Citizen Lab") revealed that Pegasus spyware linked to an Emirati operative was used to hack into the phones at the [Downing Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downing_Street "Downing Street") and the Foreign Office. One of the spyware attack on No 10 was on July 7, 2020, which was asserted to have infected the phone of British Prime Minister [Boris Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Johnson "Boris Johnson"). Besides, at least five attacks were identified on Foreign Office phones by UK allies, including the UAE, between July 2020 and June 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-200-0)[\[200\]](#refnote-200) The UAE was also accused of hiring a firm to "monitor" [Jeremy Corbyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Corbyn "Jeremy Corbyn").[&#8202;](#ref-back-201-0)[\[201\]](#refnote-201)

In April 2022, [Citizen Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Lab "Citizen Lab") released a report stating that [10 Downing Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_Downing_Street "10 Downing Street") staff had been targeted by Pegasus, and that the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") was suspected of originating the attacks in 2020 and 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-guardian-0)[\[202\]](#refnote-guardian)

### United States (US)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=49 "Edit section: United States (US)")\]

Main article: [WhatsApp snooping scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp_snooping_scandal "WhatsApp snooping scandal")

NSO Group pitched its spyware to the [Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_Enforcement_Administration "Drug Enforcement Administration"), which declined to purchase it due to its high cost.[&#8202;](#ref-back-203-0)[\[203\]](#refnote-203)

In August 2016, NSO Group (through its U.S. subsidiary Westbridge) pitched its U.S. version of Pegasus to the [San Diego Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Police_Department "San Diego Police Department") (SDPD). In the marketing material, Westbridge emphasized that the company is U.S.-based and majority-owned by a U.S. parent company. An SDPD Sergeant responded to the sales pitch with "sounds awesome". The SDPD declined to purchase the spyware as it was too expensive.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-:9)

In July 2021, it was revealed that the phone numbers of about a dozen U.S. citizens – including diplomats, journalists, aid workers, and dissident expatriates – were on a list of prospective targets for Pegasus infiltration, but it was not known whether an attack was ever attempted or completed against any of their devices. Among the phone numbers discovered on the list were those of the Obama administration's chief negotiator of the [Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Comprehensive_Plan_of_Action "Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action") as well as those of several United Nations diplomats residing in the U.S. NSO Group has said that Pegasus is not deployed against any device located within the territory of the U.S., but it has been suggested that U.S. citizens can become targets when abroad.[&#8202;](#ref-back-204-0)[\[204\]](#refnote-204)

In November 2021, the [Biden administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") placed NSO Group on a [Commerce Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce_Department "Commerce Department") blacklist from government contracts, after determining NSO Group action was "contrary to the foreign policy and national security interests of the US."[&#8202;](#ref-back-NSO_group_2021_11_03_guardian-0)[\[205\]](#refnote-NSO_group_2021_11_03_guardian)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ice_2025_09_02_guardian-0)[\[206\]](#refnote-ice_2025_09_02_guardian)

In December 2021, it was reported that Pegasus spyware was found in the preceding months on the [iPhones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhones "IPhones") of at least nine [U.S. State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_State "United States Department of State") employees, all of whom were either stationed in Uganda or worked on matters related to Uganda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-207-0)[\[207\]](#refnote-207) Later the same month, AP reported that a total of 11 U.S. State Department employees stationed in Uganda had their iPhones hacked with Pegasus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-208-0)[\[208\]](#refnote-208) The US government blacklisted the NSO Group to stop what it called " [transnational repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnational_repression "Transnational repression")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-209-0)[\[209\]](#refnote-209)

In January 2022, it was reported that the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") had secretly bought the Pegasus spyware in 2019 and had seen a demonstration of Phantom, a newer tool capable of targeting American phone numbers. The FBI went on to test both tools, and considered their use for domestic surveillance in the U.S., which reportedly led to discussions between the FBI and [United States Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice "United States Department of Justice"); ultimately the FBI decided against using NSO spyware. However, despite the 2021 decision rejecting use of NSO software, Pegasus equipment is still in the FBI's possession at a New Jersey facility.[&#8202;](#ref-back-210-0)[\[210\]](#refnote-210)[&#8202;](#ref-back-211-0)[\[211\]](#refnote-211) Responding to the reports, FBI officials played down the domestic surveillance aspect of the Pegasus testing, instead stressing [counter-intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterintelligence "Counterintelligence") as their purported main goal. A document later obtained by _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ clearly showed that the agency weighed using Pegasus and Phantom in domestic law enforcement cases.[&#8202;](#ref-back-212-0)[\[212\]](#refnote-212)[&#8202;](#ref-back-213-0)[\[213\]](#refnote-213)

[L3Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L3Harris "L3Harris"), a U.S. defense contractor, was in talks to acquire NSO Group, the maker of Pegasus. L3Harris reportedly had the backing of U.S. intelligence in undertaking the acquisition negotiations. After months of negotiations, the talks were scuttled after they were made known to the public by the news media in June 2022, with the U.S. government publicly rebuking the acquisition attempt.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mazzetti_&_Bergman_10-Jul-2022_NYT-1)[\[65\]](#refnote-Mazzetti_&_Bergman_10-Jul-2022_NYT) In October 2025, NSO Group confirmed that a US investment group had acquired a controlling stake in the company after investing tens of millions of dollars.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TechCrunch-0)[\[214\]](#refnote-TechCrunch)

In March 2023, President [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") signed an [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order "Executive order") that prohibited "operational use by the United States Government of commercial spyware that poses risks to national security or has been misused by foreign actors to enable human rights abuses around the world."[&#8202;](#ref-back-215-0)[\[215\]](#refnote-215)[&#8202;](#ref-back-exec_order_14093_fed_reg-0)[\[216\]](#refnote-exec_order_14093_fed_reg) This helped trigger a hold on a [Department of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Homeland_Security "Department of Homeland Security") contract with NSO competitor [Paragon Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragon_Solutions "Paragon Solutions") in late 2024,[&#8202;](#ref-back-ice_2025_09_02_guardian-1)[\[206\]](#refnote-ice_2025_09_02_guardian) but the incoming [Trump Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Presidency_of_Donald_Trump "Second Presidency of Donald Trump") reinstated the contract a year later.[&#8202;](#ref-back-217-0)[\[217\]](#refnote-217)

With the transition from the Biden to Trump administrations in January 2025, NSO Group began heavily investing in [lobbying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying_in_the_United_States "Lobbying in the United States") efforts to overturn the Commerce Department ban on their products. NSO hired Vogel Group lobbyists with close ties to the Trump administration, and spent over $1.8 million on [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") political campaigns in the [2024 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_elections "2024 United States elections").[&#8202;](#ref-back-218-0)[\[218\]](#refnote-218)

In May 2025, NSO Group was ordered by a [US district court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_district_court "United States district court") to pay $167 million in damages to [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") for exploiting their messaging systems for Pegasus spyware injections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-219-0)[\[219\]](#refnote-219)[&#8202;](#ref-back-220-0)[\[220\]](#refnote-220) The order also included deletion of data collected by targeted activists and also cease targeting of the affected Whatsapp users and barred them of doing said activities again in the future. This amount was later adjusted to proper standard of 4 million, due to a cap on punitive damages.[&#8202;](#ref-back-221-0)[\[221\]](#refnote-221)

## Pegasus Project

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=53 "Edit section: Pegasus Project")\]

Main article: [Pegasus Project (investigation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus_Project_(investigation) "Pegasus Project (investigation)")

In 2020, a leak of a list of more than 50,000 telephone numbers believed to have been identified as those of people of interest by clients of NSO since 2016 became available to Paris-based media nonprofit organisation [Forbidden Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_Stories "Forbidden Stories") and [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International"). They shared the information with 17 news media organisations in what has been called the _Pegasus Project_, and a months-long investigation was carried out, which included [forensic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_forensics "Computer forensics") analysis of mobile phones of potential targets. The Pegasus Project involved 80 journalists from the media partners including _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ (UK), [Radio France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_France "Radio France") and _[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde "Le Monde")_ (France), _[Die Zeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Zeit "Die Zeit")_ and _[Süddeutsche Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCddeutsche_Zeitung "Süddeutsche Zeitung")_ (Germany), _[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")_ (United States), _[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz "Haaretz")_ (Israel), _Aristegui Noticias_, [_Proceso_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceso_(magazine) "Proceso (magazine)") (Mexico), the [Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_Crime_and_Corruption_Reporting_Project "Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project"), [_Knack_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knack_(magazine) "Knack (magazine)"), _[Le Soir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Soir "Le Soir")_, [_The Wire_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wire_(India) "The Wire (India)"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-224-0)[\[224\]](#refnote-224) _Daraj_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-225-0)[\[225\]](#refnote-225) Direkt36 (Hungary),[&#8202;](#ref-back-226-0)[\[226\]](#refnote-226) and [_Frontline_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS_Frontline "PBS Frontline").[&#8202;](#ref-back-PBS/Pegasus-0)[\[227\]](#refnote-PBS/Pegasus)[&#8202;](#ref-back-pegasusproject-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-pegasusproject) Evidence was found that many phones with numbers in the list had been targets of Pegasus spyware.[&#8202;](#ref-back-project-2)[\[14\]](#refnote-project)[&#8202;](#ref-back-228-0)[\[228\]](#refnote-228)

The investigation report, published on July 18, 2021, revealed that Pegasus spyware was used to target and spy on heads of state, activists, journalists, and dissidents, enabling "human rights violations around the world on a massive scale". They identified 11 countries as NSO clients: Azerbaijan, Bahrain, Hungary, India, Kazakhstan, Mexico, Morocco, Rwanda, Saudi Arabia, Togo, and the United Arab Emirates. The investigation also revealed that journalists from multiple media organizations including _[Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Media_Network "Al Jazeera Media Network")_, [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN"), the _[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")_, the [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press"), _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_, _[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_, _[Bloomberg News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News")_ and _[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde "Le Monde")_ were targeted, and identified at least 180 journalists from 20 countries who were selected for targeting with NSO spyware between 2016 and June 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-229-0)[\[229\]](#refnote-229)[&#8202;](#ref-back-230-0)[\[230\]](#refnote-230)

However Shalev Hulio, co-founder and CEO of NSO Group, denied that the list was in any way related to the company, and questioned the reliability and legitimacy of its source. In a July 20, 2021, interview with [CTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcalist "Calcalist"), he asserted, "This is an attempt to build something on a crazy lack of information", and "There is something fundamentally wrong with this investigation".[&#8202;](#ref-back-231-0)[\[231\]](#refnote-231)

On August 2, 2021, French intelligence ( [ANSSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSSI "ANSSI")) confirmed that Pegasus spyware had been found on the phones of three journalists, including a journalist of [France 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_24 "France 24"), in what was the first time an independent and official authority corroborated the findings of the investigation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-232-0)[\[232\]](#refnote-232)

On January 26, 2022, the reports revealed that mobile phones of Lama Fakih, a US-Lebanese citizen and director of crisis and conflict at [Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch "Human Rights Watch"), were repeatedly hacked by a client of NSO Group at a time when she was investigating the [2020 Beirut explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Beirut_explosion "2020 Beirut explosion") that killed more than 200 people.[&#8202;](#ref-back-233-0)[\[233\]](#refnote-233)

## Other incidents

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=54 "Edit section: Other incidents")\]

### Ahmed Mansoor

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=55 "Edit section: Ahmed Mansoor")\]

In August 2016, it was reported that Emirati human rights activist [Ahmed Mansoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mansoor "Ahmed Mansoor") had been targeted in an attempted spyware attack, which an investigation by Citizen Lab traced back to NSO Group.[&#8202;](#ref-back-234-0)[\[234\]](#refnote-234)

In response, NSO Group released a statement "that stopped short of acknowledging that the spyware was its own" which said that they provide "authorized governments with technology that helps them combat terror and crime", and denied knowledge of any particular incidents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-theguardian_20160825_apple-0)[\[235\]](#refnote-theguardian_20160825_apple)

### Bug-bounty program skepticism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=56 "Edit section: Bug-bounty program skepticism")\]

In the aftermath of this, critics asserted that Apple's [bug-bounty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_bounty_program "Bug bounty program") program, which rewards people for finding flaws in its software, might not have offered sufficient rewards to prevent exploits being sold on the [black market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market "Black market"), rather than being reported back to Apple. Russell Brandom of _[The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verge "The Verge")_ commented that the reward offered in Apple's bug-bounty program maxes out at $200,000, "just a fraction of the millions that are regularly spent for iOS exploits on the black market". He goes on to ask why Apple doesn't "spend its way out of security vulnerabilities?", but also writes that "as soon as \[&#8202;](#ref-back-theverge_12660800-0)[the Pegasus\] vulnerabilities were reported, Apple patched them—but there are plenty of other bugs left. While spyware companies see an exploit purchase as a one-time payout for years of access, Apple's bounty has to be paid out every time a new vulnerability pops up."

Brandom also wrote; "The same researchers participating in Apple's bug bounty could make more money selling the same finds to an exploit broker." He concluded the article by writing: "It's hard to say how much damage might have been caused if Mansoor had clicked on the spyware link... The hope is that, when the next researcher finds the next bug, that thought matters more than the money."[\[236\]](#refnote-theverge_12660800)

Since these developments, in 2019, Apple increased the payout of its bug bounty program to $1m,[&#8202;](#ref-back-237-0)[\[237\]](#refnote-237) and maintains that it is the fastest-growing bug bounty program in the world.[&#8202;](#ref-back-238-0)[\[238\]](#refnote-238)

## Legal challenges

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=57 "Edit section: Legal challenges")\]

See also: [NSO Group §Criticism and controversies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSO_Group#Criticism_and_controversies "NSO Group")

### WhatsApp

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=58 "Edit section: WhatsApp")\]

On October 20, 2019, [Meta Platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms") Inc.’s [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") filed a lawsuit against NSO Group for exploiting a bug in its WhatsApp messaging app to install spyware (Pegasus) that allowed the surveillance of 1400 people in 20 countries, including journalists, human rights activists, political dissidents and diplomats. WhatsApp said it was seeking a permanent demand to block NSO from using its service, and asked lawmakers to bar the use of [cyberweapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberweapon "Cyberweapon") like those NSO Group has sold to governments.[&#8202;](#ref-back-239-0)[\[239\]](#refnote-239)

In 2020, NSO Group appealed a trial judge’s refusal to award it “conduct-based immunity”, a common law doctrine which protects foreign officials acting in their official capacity. The company's argument was that it was immune from being sued because it was acting as an agent for unidentified foreign governments when it installed the Pegasus spyware, and that Pegasus "helps law enforcement and intelligence agencies fight crime and protect national security".[&#8202;](#ref-back-USC-0)[\[240\]](#refnote-USC)

On January 9, 2023, the [Supreme Court of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") denied the appeal and decided against reversing the decision of the lower court, allowing WhatsApp's lawsuit to continue.[&#8202;](#ref-back-USC-1)[\[240\]](#refnote-USC)

In December 2024, the court found NSO Group liable for attacks on approximately 1,400 [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp") users, including activists and journalists, and ruled that NSO Group violated the [Computer Fraud and Abuse Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Fraud_and_Abuse_Act "Computer Fraud and Abuse Act") and the [California Comprehensive Computer Data Access and Fraud Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Comprehensive_Computer_Data_Access_and_Fraud_Act "California Comprehensive Computer Data Access and Fraud Act"), dismissing the company's defense that it was not liable because Pegasus was operated by their clients.[&#8202;](#ref-back-241-0)[\[241\]](#refnote-241)

On May 6, 2025, [Meta Platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms") Inc. announced that a jury ruled that NSO Group owed Meta $167.3 million in punitive damages and $444,719 in compensatory damages.[&#8202;](#ref-back-242-0)[\[242\]](#refnote-242)

On October 18, 2025, U.S. District Court Judge [Phyllis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_J._Hamilton "Phyllis J. Hamilton"), U.S. issued a permanent injunction prohibiting NSO Group from targeting WhatsApp and its users. In the same decision, the judge reduced the jury-awarded punitive damages down to about $4 million, citing limits on punitive‐to‐compensatory damage ratios.[&#8202;](#ref-back-243-0)[\[243\]](#refnote-243)[&#8202;](#ref-back-244-0)[\[244\]](#refnote-244)

### Apple

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=59 "Edit section: Apple")\]

On November 23, 2021, [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") announced that it had filed a lawsuit against NSO Group and its parent company OSY Technologies for allegedly surveilling and targeting iPhone users with its Pegasus spyware. Apple stated that contrary to NSO's claim of targeting terrorists and criminals, their spyware has also been used against activists, politicians, and journalists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-245-0)[\[245\]](#refnote-245) Apple's statement said the company is seeking a permanent injunction to bar NSO Group from using Apple's software, services, or devices to prevent further abuse and harm to users.[&#8202;](#ref-back-246-0)[\[246\]](#refnote-246)

## Reuses

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pegasus_(spyware)&action=edit&section=60 "Edit section: Reuses")\]
