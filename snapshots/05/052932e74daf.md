(a) Background

According to [Map 3Somalia – Approximate Territorial Control as of 31 March 2025,](https://euaa.europa.eu/coi/somalia/2025/security-situation/12-armed-actors-and-relevant-developments/121-territorial-control-maps) [1230](#footnote_230_Z46RrFW27n4Dpq7zwYK7k-rJA1awzyAttXwXQRamlU_zuIave2dqCgo "PolGeoNow, Somalia Approximate Territorial Control, as of 31 March 2025, n.a.") and [Map 2 Somalia – Approximate Territorial Control as of 1 April 2023](https://euaa.europa.eu/coi/somalia/2025/security-situation/12-armed-actors-and-relevant-developments/121-territorial-control-maps),[1231](#footnote_231_vb-bfw5QCZnaqfo-CFeewUN1sgwmg0UlrfvwCVy-7A_q1IgzJfpCQxY "PolGeoNow, Somalia Approximate Territorial Control, as of 1 April 2023, n.a.") Sool region is part of the areas disputed between Somaliland and Puntland. Sool shares an international border with Ethiopia to the south and internal borders with Sanaag region to the north, Togdheer region to the west, and Nugal and Bari regions to the east. It is divided into four districts, Taleex (also Taleh), Xudun (also Hudun), Caynabo (also Aynabo or Ainado) and Laas Caanood (also Laas Anood). The capital of Sool is Laas Caanood. According to the reference map, Togdheer region’s Buhodle (or Buuhoodle) district and parts of Burco district are part of the disputed areas.[1232](#footnote_232_7SOdbt-dDSgOKFwt1OXDkZmKjxPM3YPZm0sqlXFXdI_hvLUWGplMklI "UNOCHA, Somalia Administrative Reference Map, as of 20 July 2023, url")

As of September 2024, sources variously estimated the population of Sool region at 548 975 (IPC),[1233](#footnote_233_hb71R9EAkSDABvYPWviN9lphFZ2fhwj4Rwp06EEisbc_xEIL3wOiTUTQ "IPC, Somalia Acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition analysis July – December 2024, 23 September 2024, url, p. 3") and 84 024 (IOM).[1234](#footnote_234_YmffBSmV4klz2flecK9cAeloV3zR02KfA0HTj9QhKo_sZHLAAYPTxAs "IOM, DTM Somalia – Baseline Assessment Dataset – Round 3 (February 2024 – September 2024), 3 December 2024, url. IOM notes that ‘Regarding the regions in Somaliland state, DTM teams reached a low coverage of the settlements to be assessed. Therefore, the population figures displayed in this report are underestimated and should be interpreted with caution’") Most of Sool’s population belongs to the Dhulbahante clan,[1235](#footnote_235_yrgxxMfkV8-B2hrfNHn2H177VAOiNnoaPRWSrvYM48_kdTn6xEmg6Fp "International Crisis Group, Somaliland’s Peaceful Handover Withstands Neighbourhood Strains, 11 December 2024, url") which is part of the Darod family. The Dhulbahante are not affiliated with Somaliland’s dominant Isaaq clan.[1236](#footnote_236_qVGCxtNzccerjnEpf7MCDXuVE88D6PkhQV1lYXTktn0_uPPwlrBWGKBw "International Crisis Group, The Stakes in the Ethiopia-Somaliland Deal, 6 March 2024, url") A map produced by the Max Planck Institute and published by Somalia expert Markus Hoehne in a February 2023 article on crisis in Sool region shows Dhulbahante territories in eastern Sool and also in Togdheer’s Buhodle district.[1237](#footnote_237_AmxbhdqNItTZPhmdCKrvQH9PwYs7x-Zsq68d68BuA0Q_rltwDsE8GFiP "Hoehne, M.V., Towards a proper understanding of the conflict in Somaliland [Map], ROAPE, 31 July 2023, url") Members of the Dhulbahante clan refer to Togdheer region as Cayn.[1238](#footnote_238_u21KsVOggBvARQ4dwN3RKunP2Zoj2xFMN9tNhoQzyQs_bHDcu8Z52RX3 "Hoehne, M.V., Towards a proper understanding of the conflict in Somaliland, ROAPE, 31 July 2023, url") The map also shows a smaller Warsangeli (Darod) territory in the northeastern area bordering Puntland’s Bari region and Sanaag region.[1239](#footnote_239_E6WHQTFjrhk1LJbbmLERiTkJW-kowwdSjbpwbWaAIz0_f2HGg7EVDsqm "Hoehne, M.V., Crisis in Lasanod: Border Disputes, Escalating Insecurity and the Future of Somaliland [Map], African Arguments, 6 February 2023, url")

On 6 February 2023,[1240](#footnote_240_3BZNYWiqUpAM-34i5uAjgHZ9wNfsIw5MuuyhBhpQCgo_auFygzmNM4np "Horseed Media, Somali Federal Government Recognizes New SSC-Khatumo Administration, 19 October 2023, url") elders and leaders established the Sool, Sanaag, and Cayn-Khatumo (SSC-Khaatumo, SSC-K) administration,[1241](#footnote_241_oaO55NdT7wamXjGqy9bQ9ajYK2K3ygi3X3F3tAN65QM_dI1OexEctQzQ "SD, Breaking News: SSC Clan Leaders’ Summit release a 13 point declaration, 6 February 2023, url") representing the Dhulbahante clan along with the Fiqishine and Madiban clans.[1242](#footnote_242_yrgxxMfkV8-B2hrfNHn2H177VAOiNnoaPRWSrvYM48_gqn8bgzkEiDc "International Crisis Group, Somaliland’s Peaceful Handover Withstands Neighbourhood Strains, 11 December 2024, url") SSC-K led efforts to remove Somaliland forces in August 2023 and has since declared its allegiance to Somalia rather than Somaliland.[1243](#footnote_243_yrgxxMfkV8-B2hrfNHn2H177VAOiNnoaPRWSrvYM48_b8aIUP9iZIoO "International Crisis Group, Somaliland’s Peaceful Handover Withstands Neighbourhood Strains, 11 December 2024, url") In October 2023, the Federal Government of Somalia formally recognised the SSC-K administration.[1244](#footnote_244_eTbOYybM1Hyvmq626xTCab2gUNLCcQAH1cua6LveqSY_yHuqEiA4zFaG "Hiiraan Online, Somali government expands influence in Las Anod with new immigration office, 13 August 2024, url; Hiiraan Online, Federal government recognizes SCC-Khatumo administration, pledges cooperation, 19 October 2023, url; Horseed Media, Somali Federal Government Recognizes New SSC-Khatumo Administration, 19 October 2023, url") The leader of SSC-K is Abdikhkhadir Ahmed Aw Ali Firdhiye.[1245](#footnote_245_z9qSYuCkSaWJtvcUQK9DB2N7TsoBchDwxlqSiDy1eYc_cfQQqo6hHzt5 "Hiiraan Online, SSC Khaatumo leader vows to reclaim Erigavo from Somaliland control, 13 January 2025, url; Garowe Online, Somalia: There will be no new administration in SSC, Bihi says, 15 October 2023, url")

Events and incidents taking place in Buhodle district are covered in this section and not in in the section [2.8.3 Togdheer (Somaliland)](https://euaa.europa.eu/coi/somalia/2025/security-situation/28-somaliland/283-togdheer).

For additional details on clans in Sool region, please see section 2.6.3.1 of the [EASO COI Report Somalia: Security Situation](https://euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2021_09_EASO_COI_Report_Somalia_Security_situation_new_AC.pdf) (September 2021) and section 2.7.1 of the [EUAA COI Report Somalia: Security Situation (February 2023](https://euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2023-02/2023_02_COI_Report_Somalia_Security_Situation_EN.pdf)).

For background information and additional details see the [EUAA COI Report Somalia: Security Situation](https://coi.euaa.europa.eu/administration/easo/PLib/2023_02_EUAA_COI_Report_Somalia_Security_situation.pdf "(opens in a new window)") (February 2023),[1250](#footnote_250_Zg7xdKRn4nUwxR7VqxgxIO0oUTEJXK1zVqLHeWvGx0_anw8kyUipiwt "EUAA, Country of Origin information Report on Somalia: Security Situation, February 2023, url, p. 22") and the [EASO COI report Somalia: Actors](https://coi.easo.europa.eu/administration/easo/PLib/2021_07_EASO_COI_Report_Somalia_Actors.pdf) (July 2021).[1251](#footnote_251_4jB2rauqjin9iRSlmPCENKFU7LPtSns2fKVX4Hovs_n2Jidbm5j0R8 "EASO, Country of Origin Information Report on Somalia: Actors, July 2021, url, pp. 101-116")

Between February and August 2023, there was intense fighting between Somaliland forces and Dhulbahante clan militias, supported by allied clans.[1252](#footnote_252_f6ZJZ637RF4mF2rcTLY-PJJHXVHFOc3s7XpBurFREtI_jc8JlLqr8uKZ "International Crisis Group, Somaliland’s Peaceful Handover Withstands Neighbourhood Strains, 11 December 2024, url; UNSG, Situation in Somalia, S/2023/758, 13 October 2023, url, para. 9") According to a June 2023 article by Markus Hoehne, from March 2023 onward, the conflict around Laas Caanood involved fighters from Dhulbahante, Warsangeli, Majeerteen and other clans (the Harti clan coalition) on one side and clans like Ise and Gadabursi besides Isaaq clans on the Somaliland army side. The majority of the Harti clan coalition, however, belonged to the Dhulbahante clan.[1253](#footnote_253_xszrICbmn-m-oBRumHWwcQYhW2HVI697KVA28lTF7s_i0XpnPJFOa5A "Hoehne, M., The Dynamics of Conflict over Lasanod: Ongoing Fighting, Administration Building, Failed Mediation and Forecast, African Arguments, 12 June 2023, url") In May 2023, for example, fighting took place around the villages of Dhabansaar and Samakab.[1254](#footnote_254_VZeizYbtzusvweACgj7VkXkcx1VVrv2N3VvGz7GncA_co5WTYRRpyqW "International Crisis Group, CrisisWatch – Somaliland: April 2023 – March 2025, n.d., url; Mustaqbal Media, Somaliland, SSC forces clash in Samakaab, 16 May 2023, url") Multiple clashes and mortar exchanges were reported from 24 to 27 June 2023 in and around Laas Caanood.[1255](#footnote_255_Og0WuiwJvYjbUxtEUXhZuvBNWQWySMnf7eVNqYPkfI_deUElvqoH7mq "International Crisis Group, CrisisWatch – Somaliland: April 2023 – March 2025, n.d., url; Horn Observer, Renewed clashes and shelling erupt in Laascaanood, 26 June 2023, url") In August 2023, SSC-K launched a counteroffensive against Somaliland forces, driving them out of Laas Caanood.[1256](#footnote_256_8lYvMVUCXbX48seZKWfD9Yl5CDD4KHs2PqNYR3QkAE_pXlTMVGpE8VJ "HIPS, State of Somalia 2023 Report, May 2024, url, p. 27; Halqabsi, SSC-Khaatumo Forces Capture Somaliland’s Biggest and Last Military Base in Sool, 25 August 2023, url") A new frontline emerged between Oog and Guumays in western Sool, aligning roughly with the boundary between the Isaaq and Dhulbahante clans.[1257](#footnote_257_OJCIJUIQd9fKYiHm5uLkrzfH3r3lZ6TU-hj7x2uaU_zpkvfP0NwNx4 "International Crisis Group, CrisisWatch – Somaliland: April 2023 – March 2025, n.d., url") Troops remained deployed along the frontlines and both sides sent reinforcements to the area in September 2023.[1258](#footnote_258_WDdJ5wSeQys171zUwZCnLhbZGcACl5JlmfaiwQEEIwU_iIXItoy3zdAp "International Crisis Group, CrisisWatch – Somaliland: April 2023 – March 2025, n.d., url; Horn Observer, Somaliland deploys more forces to Oog, announces military action against SSC-Khaatumo, 6 September 2023, url") In December 2023, Somaliland forces and Dhulbahante clan militias continued to strengthen their positions near the frontline in Sool region, raising concerns about renewed fighting.[1259](#footnote_259_OJCIJUIQd9fKYiHm5uLkrzfH3r3lZ6TU-hj7x2uaU_w4C26YIegLS5 "International Crisis Group, CrisisWatch – Somaliland: April 2023 – March 2025, n.d., url") Reportedly, in the first half of 2024, the situation in Sool remained calm, despite troop movements of Somaliland forces and Dhulbahante clan militias along the frontline.[1260](#footnote_260_OJCIJUIQd9fKYiHm5uLkrzfH3r3lZ6TU-hj7x2uaU_y7GCCD4t0Feg "International Crisis Group, CrisisWatch – Somaliland: April 2023 – March 2025, n.d., url")

In January 2025, fighting between Somaliland and SSC-Khaatumo forces resumed in Sool, with clashes erupting in Guumays, in Oog area. The clashes resulted in casualties, though exact numbers were unclear.[1261](#footnote_261_7t9Y2zz08iSN3JJ1bbzVcEN5YcyJRPPMGipzR9Wxo48_ylQ6V4LEN4G0 "Kaab TV, Somaliland Forces Clash SSC-Khaatumo in Guumeys, 20 January 2025, url; Sahan, The Somali Wire, Issue no. 776, 20 January 2025, source requiring registration, url, accessed 28 March 2025") Ongoing military movements had heightened tensions, sparking fears of the conflict spreading further.[1262](#footnote_262_5mu09kRtD68rig4QMLoM1vo2IhVmodhBVDYvQ0tgVPY_jheRHppRHW7n "Sahan, The Somali Wire, Issue no. 776, 20 January 2025, source requiring registration, url") Early March 2025, members of Habar Je’lo (sub-clan of the Isaaq) and Dhulbahante clans clashed in the disputed village of Buq Dharkayn. The confrontation involved both government forces and troops from SSC-Khatumo. The violence led to numerous casualties, with dozens reported killed or injured.[1263](#footnote_263_OJCIJUIQd9fKYiHm5uLkrzfH3r3lZ6TU-hj7x2uaU_aWoOUr4eevJx "International Crisis Group, CrisisWatch – Somaliland: April 2023 – March 2025, n.d., url")

Other incidents involving violence between clan militias were also reported in Sool region. **Conflict-related displacement**

According to the UNHCR PRMN, 54 077 individuals were newly displaced from Sool region (including Buhoodle district) due to conflict or insecurity in the period from April 2023 to 16 March 2025. Among them, 48 294 IDPs were displaced within Sool region (including Buhoodle), while 5 783 IDPs were displaced to other regions, including Bari, Nugal, Togdheer, and Woqooyi Galbeed. 36 118 were displaced within the region to Buhoodle district. In the same period, 4 633 individuals arrived from other regions, including Nugal, and Sanaag regions. Xudun was the district most affected by arrivals from other regions (2 392 individuals, all arriving from Ceerigaabo) during the reference period, while Laas Caanood was the district most affected by individuals leaving for other regions (3 072), including Bari, Nugal, Togdheer, and Woqooyi Galbeed.[1274](#footnote_274_yiRPmP87kEu0UYojbhD86knrMXsbSHDgqhfq-MWlFw_l2JjsQArqB6m "UNHCR, PRMN Datafile – Somalia, as of 21 March 2025, url") For information on conflict-related displacement prior to April 2023, please see section 2.4.1 of the [EUAA COI Report Somalia: Security Situation (February 2023](https://euaa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/2023-02/2023_02_COI_Report_Somalia_Security_Situation_EN.pdf)).

According to UNHCR’s Somalia Protection and Solutions Monitoring Network (PSMN) fighting in Buhodle district in November 2024 resulted in the displacement of 26 550 individuals. The conflict caused widespread displacement, especially in areas such as ‘Qorilugud, Shangeed, Gocondhaale, Maygaagle, Galgal, Harufadhi, Sooljoogto, Xadhadhan, and Shimbiraale’.[1275](#footnote_275_DQPBZhfzZJECmD3YhrYXTl9Z5YziCPYSoouzHf28Uvo_e5w9EB82p6E9 "UNHCR, UNHCR Somalia Protection and Solutions Monitoring Network (PSMN) Flash Alert #18 | November 2024, 5 November 2024, url, p. 1")
