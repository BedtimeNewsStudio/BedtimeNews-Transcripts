STS-4

**STS-4** was the fourth [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") mission, and also the fourth for [Space Shuttle _Columbia_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia "Space Shuttle Columbia"). Crewed by [Ken Mattingly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Mattingly "Ken Mattingly") and [Henry Hartsfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hartsfield "Henry Hartsfield"), the mission launched on June 27, 1982,[&#8202;](#ref-back-oomops-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-oomops) and landed a week later on July 4, 1982.[&#8202;](#ref-back-owastwrd-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-owastwrd) Due to parachute malfunctions, the [SRBs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Solid_Rocket_Booster "Space Shuttle Solid Rocket Booster") were not recovered.

STS-4 was the final test flight for the Space Shuttle; it was thereafter officially declared to be operational. _Columbia_ carried numerous scientific payloads during the mission, as well as military missile detection systems.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-:0)

## Crew

STS-4, being the last test flight of the Space Shuttle, was also the last to carry a crew of two astronauts. Commander Ken Mattingly had previously flown as [Command Module Pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_ranks_and_positions "Astronaut ranks and positions") on [Apollo 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_16 "Apollo 16"), and was also the original Command Module Pilot for [Apollo 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_13 "Apollo 13") before being replaced by his backup, [Jack Swigert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Swigert "Jack Swigert"), after being exposed to [German measles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_measles "German measles").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) Pilot Henry Hartsfield was a rookie astronaut who had transferred to NASA in 1969 after the cancellation of the [Air Force's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force") [Manned Orbiting Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned_Orbiting_Laboratory "Manned Orbiting Laboratory") (MOL) program. He had previously served as a [capsule communicator (CAPCOM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_controller "Flight controller") on [Apollo 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_16 "Apollo 16"), all three [Skylab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab "Skylab") missions, and [STS-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-1 "STS-1"). Both men had graduated from [Auburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University "Auburn University"), the only time an entire Space Shuttle flight crew were graduates of the same university.

### Backup crew

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=STS-4&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Backup crew")\]

From STS-4 onwards, NASA halted the appointment and training of complete backup flight crews. Instead, individual flight crew members were assigned backups who could take their place within the prime crew. The decision on whether to appoint a reserve crew member was made on a per-flight basis by flight management teams at [Johnson Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Space_Center "Johnson Space Center"). Consequently, the last NASA flight to have a full-time backup crew was [STS-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-3 "STS-3").

## Mission summary

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=STS-4&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Mission summary")\]

STS-4 launched from [Kennedy Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center "Kennedy Space Center") (KSC) on June 27, 1982, at 15:00:00 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time "Coordinated Universal Time") (10:00 a.m. local time), with [Ken Mattingly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Mattingly "Ken Mattingly") as commander and [Henry Hartsfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hartsfield "Henry Hartsfield") as pilot.[&#8202;](#ref-back-oomops-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-oomops) This mission marked the first time the Space Shuttle launched precisely at its scheduled launch time. It was also the last research and development flight in the program, after which NASA considered the shuttle operational. After this flight, _Columbia's_ ejection seats were deactivated, and shuttle crews did not wear pressure suits again until [STS-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-26 "STS-26") in 1988.

STS-4's cargo consisted of the first [Getaway Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getaway_Special "Getaway Special") (GAS) payloads, including nine scientific experiments provided by students from [Utah State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_State_University "Utah State University"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-hopesp-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-hopesp)[&#8202;](#ref-back-chrftj-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-chrftj) and a classified [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force") payload.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wilford19841218-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-wilford19841218) A secret [mission control center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Control_Center "Mission Control Center") in [Sunnyvale, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyvale,_California "Sunnyvale, California") participated in monitoring the flight. Mattingly, an active-duty naval officer, later described the classified payload – two sensors for detecting missile launches – as a "rinky-dink collection of minor stuff they wanted to fly". The payload failed to operate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cassutt200908-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-cassutt200908) The [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office "National Reconnaissance Office") intended to fly DAMON, a secret payload intended to replace [KH-9 HEXAGON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KH-9_HEXAGON "KH-9 HEXAGON"), but it was canceled in December 1980.[&#8202;](#ref-back-day20190701-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-day20190701)

In the shuttle's mid-deck, a Continuous Flow [Electrophoresis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrophoresis "Electrophoresis") System and the Mono-disperse Latex Reactor flew for the second time. The crew conducted a [lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning "Lightning") survey with hand-held cameras, and performed medical experiments on themselves for two student projects. They also operated the [Remote Manipulator System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadarm "Canadarm") (Canadarm) with an instrument called the Induced Environment Contamination Monitor mounted on its end, designed to obtain information on gases or particles being released by the orbiter in flight.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-:0)

_Columbia_ landed on July 4, 1982, at 16:09:31UTC, on the 15,000ft (4.6km) concrete runway 22 at [Edwards Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards_Air_Force_Base "Edwards Air Force Base"), the first orbital Shuttle landing on a concrete runway. This time the lead escorting [T-38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_T-38_Talon "Northrop T-38 Talon") "Chase 1" was piloted by [Guy Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Gardner_(astronaut) "Guy Gardner (astronaut)") with crewmate [Jerry L. Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_L._Ross "Jerry L. Ross"). [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") and [his wife Nancy Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Reagan "Nancy Reagan") greeted the crew upon arrival. Following the landing, President Reagan gave a speech to the crowd gathered at Edwards, during which he declared the Space Shuttle operational.[&#8202;](#ref-back-owastwrd-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-owastwrd) He was followed by remarks from Mattingly and Hartsfield and a flyover of the new shuttle _[Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger "Space Shuttle Challenger")_ atop the [Shuttle Carrier Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttle_Carrier_Aircraft "Shuttle Carrier Aircraft") (SCA), headed for KSC.

The flight lasted 7days, 1hour, 9minutes, and 31seconds, and covered a total distance of 4,700,000km (2,900,000mi) in 112 complete orbits. The mission achieved all objectives except for the Air Force payload, but the [SRBs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Solid_Rocket_Booster "Space Shuttle Solid Rocket Booster") were lost when their main parachutes failed, causing the empty casings to impact the ocean at high velocity and sink.[&#8202;](#ref-back-oomops-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-oomops) This and [STS-51-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster") were the only missions where the SRBs were not recovered. _Columbia_ returned to KSC on July 15, 1982.

## Mission insignia

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=STS-4&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Mission insignia")\]

The path of the red, white, and blue streak on the mission patch forms the numeral "4", indicating the flight's numerical designation in the Space Transportation System's mission sequence.
