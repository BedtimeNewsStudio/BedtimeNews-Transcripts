# Memphis Meats' Lab-Grown Meat Raises $17M With Help From Bill Gates And Richard Branson

Memphis Meats

In another recent leap for lab grown meat, [clean meat company Memphis Meats announced that it completed a $17 million Series A fundraising round](https://twitter.com/intent/tweet?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.forbes.com%2Fsites%2Fchristinatroitino%2F2017%2F08%2F24%2Fmemphis-meats-lab-grown-meat-raises-17m-with-help-from-bill-gates-and-richard-branson%2F&text=clean%20meat%20company%20Memphis%20Meats%20announced%20that%20it%20completed%20a%20%2417%20million%20Series%20A%20fundraising%20round)  that was led by [Draper Fisher Jurvetson](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5674c0c22399a3a13cbc3af2/t/599d77dfa803bbe20ff02424/1503492063640/Memphis+Meats+-+Press+Release+23+Aug+2017+vFF.pdf) (DFJ), a leading venture capital firm that has also [invested](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/draper-fisher-jurvetson/investments) in SpaceX, Twitter and Tesla. The round included a number of notable investors including Richard Branson, Bill Gates and Suzy and Jack Welch. The round was also supported by food industry leaders including farm-to-table [founding father](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/220392) Kimbal Musk and agriculture company Cargill Inc., the [largest privately held](https://www.forbes.com/companies/cargill/) corporation in the United States whose last [declared](https://www.cargill.com/about/financial/five-year-financial-summary) revenues ($136.7 billion) would rank it number 15 on the Fortune 500.

[Memphis Meats has been developing real meat grown directly from animal cells](https://twitter.com/intent/tweet?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.forbes.com%2Fsites%2Fchristinatroitino%2F2017%2F08%2F24%2Fmemphis-meats-lab-grown-meat-raises-17m-with-help-from-bill-gates-and-richard-branson%2F&text=Memphis%20Meats%20has%20been%20developing%20real%20meat%20grown%20directly%20from%20animal%20cells) as a way to bypass the [various complications](http://www.memphismeats.com/about-us/) associated with traditional animal agriculture. With their lab grown clean meat, they aim to minimize meat's environmental impact, preserve animal welfare and produce meat that is safer to eat than traditionally farmed meat.

The company plans to use the funding to continue to develop products as well as enhance its economies of scale. The company has been making progress in scaling production and reducing costs over the last year, but still has a ways to go. As of March, the company [estimated](http://www.businessinsider.com/memphis-meats-chicken-lab-grown-2017-3) that it could produce 1 pound of chicken meat for under $9,000, which is roughly half of what it cost to produce 1 pound of its [beef meatballs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y027yLT2QY0) in 2016. While currently still expensive, this is a tremendous reduction from the [$300,000](http://www.gfi.org/mosa-meat-all-beef-no-butcher) production cost of the first ever clean burger produced in 2013. Memphis Meats aims to ultimately produce their products at a market competitive price point that is comparable or cheaper to traditional meat.

Memphis Meats

With approximately [30 percent](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2935116/) of the planet's ice-free surface already dedicated to animal production alongside a simultaneous meat consumption increase of [73 percent](http://www.fao.org/news/story/en/item/116937/icode/) by 2050, a number of clean meat and plant-based meat companies join Memphis Meats in the mission of reducing consumption of traditional meat. Both [Mosa Meats](http://www.gfi.org/mosa-meat-all-beef-no-butcher) and [Hampton Creek](http://www.businessinsider.com/hampton-creek-lab-grown-meat-2017-6) have been developing their own lab grown meat products, with the latter mired in more [recent controversy](http://www.latimes.com/business/la-fi-just-mayo-target-20170822-story.html) over alleged food safety violations. Moreover, [Beyond Meat](http://beyondmeat.com/) and [Impossible Foods](https://www.impossiblefoods.com/) have been making advances with their plant-based meat products. In addition to investing in Memphis Meats' recently announced Series A round, earlier this month Bill Gates also invested in Impossible Foods' recent [$75 million](https://www.crunchbase.com/funding-round/6699204907b4592e50bded978d053b67) fundraising round.

![Memphis Meats' lab grown chicken, produced by growing animal cells. Debuted in 2017, this is the world's first ever poultry product grown from animal cells.](https://blogs-images.forbes.com/christinatroitino/files/2017/08/Southernfriedchicken2-1200x800.jpg)

Memphis Meats' lab grown chicken, produced by growing animal cells. Debuted in 2017, this is the world's first ever poultry product grown from animal cells.

Memphis Meats

As of 2016, there were still [concerns](http://www.wsj.com/articles/sizzling-steaks-may-soon-be-lab-grown-1454302862) with Memphis Meats' use of fetal bovine serum in the production of its lab-grown meat, however the company claimed that it would be replacing the serum with a plant-based alternative by year's end. Regardless of progression on this resolution, the company feels optimistic that it will solve both the ecological as well as economic issues associated with traditional meat.

"We believe this to be one of the biggest technological leaps for humanity," says [Uma Valeti](https://youtu.be/b5ezRx23EMg?t=1m6s), CEO of Memphis Meats.
