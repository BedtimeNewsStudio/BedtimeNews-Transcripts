# Turkey plans to send cluster munitions to Ukraine, US congress records show

Ankara plans to send a military aid package containing 70 M39 ATACMS missiles to Ukraine as part of its efforts its legacy equipment.

US congressional records and transfer documents show that Turkey wants to transfer 70 M39 ATACMS missiles, alongside rockets and artillery shells containing millions of explosive submunitions, to [Ukraine](https://bianet.org/tag/ukraine-36625). Turkey had long-standing declarations that it would not use or transfer cluster munitions.

In the face of this revelation, [Russia](https://bianet.org/tag/russia-3851) warned that the potential transfer of US-made weapons and ammunition from Turkey's inventory to Ukraine would cause "serious damage" to Moscow's relations with both Ankara and Washington.

Russian Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Maria Zakharova said on Aug 15 that they requested explanations from Washington and Ankara regarding the transfer plan, TASS reported.

Zakharova asserted that the shipment would not significantly alter the course of the war, but would lead to more casualties and destruction.

### Congressional records

An official notification submitted by the US State Department to Congress on Aug 3 and published in the Congressional Record on Aug 6 states that Turkey proposed to permanently transfer 70 M39 ATACMS missiles to Ukraine. The US administration informed Congress that it is ready to authorize the transfer, taking into account political, military, economic, human rights, and arms control considerations.

The document lists Turkey as the proposing country and Ukraine as the recipient. The transfer is projected to be carried out through Turkey's state-owned weapons and munitions manufacturer Makine ve Kimya Endüstrisi (MKE), along with US-based companies Pansophico and Patriot Defense Group. Turkey's justification is to reduce its inventory of legacy defense materials and direct its financial resources toward the modernization and maintenance of existing systems. Ukraine intends to use these systems to strengthen its offensive and defensive fire support against the Russian invasion.

The document does not mean the transfer has occurred. Congress has the opportunity to review the process and decide against the transfer. HRW also called on Congress on Aug 11 to block the transfer.

![](https://static.bianet.org/2026/08/ekran-alintisi.PNG)

### What is ATACMS?

ATACMS is a US-made surface-to-surface tactical ballistic missile system. It can be launched from M270 and HIMARS systems.

The M39 model in Turkey's transfer list has a range of approximately 165 kilometers, and each missile disperses 950 M74 explosive submunitions over the target area. For this reason, the M39 is classified as a cluster munition.

### The package is not limited to ATACMS

According to an analysis [published](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/08/11/turkiye-seeks-to-transfer-us-cluster-munitions-to-ukraine) by HRW on Aug 11, the 256 million US dollar package that Turkey wants to transfer includes 12 M270 multiple launch rocket systems, as well as three types of cluster munitions:

- **70 M39 ATACMS missiles**: A total of 66,500 submunitions, with 950 M74 submunitions in each.
- **2,524 M26 rockets**: A total of 1,625,456 submunitions, with 644 M77 submunitions in each.
- **47,000 M509A1 203 mm artillery shells:** A total of 8,460,000 submunitions, with 180 M42 submunitions in each.

This brings the total number of explosive submunitions carried by the cluster munitions in the package to 10,151,956. HRW points out that the munitions are decades-old legacy systems, and submunitions that fail to explode on the ground could pose a long-term danger to civilians.

### What is a cluster bomb?

Cluster munitions are based on the principle of dispersing a large number of small explosive submunitions over a wide area via missiles, rockets, artillery shells, or air-dropped bombs. Some submunitions may fail to explode upon impact, creating a landmine-like hazard years after a war ends.

The 2008 Convention on Cluster Munitions prohibits signatory states from using, producing, stockpiling, and transferring these weapons. Turkey, the US, Russia, and Ukraine are not parties to the convention. Therefore, the prohibition in the convention does not create a direct treaty obligation for these four countries.

### Allegations against Turkey

A report [published](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1994/turkey2/) by HRW in Dec 1994 noted that there were allegations in the Turkish media indicating the Turkish Air Force used [cluster bombs](https://bianet.org/tag/cluster-bombs-15407) in some operations against the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK) militant group.

The report specifically highlights an air operation in Jan 1994 against the PKK's Zaleh camp in northern Iraq. According to a television news report cited by HRW, F-16 and F-4 fighter jets participated in the operation, and cluster bombs were among the total of 132 bombs dropped. HRW based this information on media reports.

The Landmine and Cluster Munition Monitor, which tracks use and policy developments regarding landmines and cluster munitions, reviews records indicating Turkey may have used cluster munitions in the past, but Turkey states it has not used these munitions since 2005.

### What did Turkey say before?

Turkey's Permanent Mission to the UN in Geneva reported at the 2023 meeting of the Convention on Cluster Munitions that Turkey has not used, produced, imported, or transferred cluster munitions since 2005, and has no intention of doing so in the future. Turkey declared the same stance again in Sep 2024.

Since Turkey is not a party to the convention, these statements do not constitute a legal obligation arising from the treaty. However, they reflect the official policy that Ankara has declared to the public and to the states parties to the convention for years.

### How does it differ from the 2023 allegations?

The allegation that Turkey sent cluster munitions to Ukraine did not surface for the first time.

_Foreign Policy_ wrote in Jan 2023, based on unnamed current and former US and European officials, that Turkey sent US-designed DPICM-type cluster munitions to Ukraine in late 2022. Turkey denied the allegation. Ukraine's Ambassador to Ankara, Vasyl Bodnar, also said the news was not accurate.

However, the issue continued to be discussed afterward. According to the Cluster Munition Monitor 2024, photographs showing Turkish-made M483A1 cluster munitions in the hands of Ukrainian forces were published in Aug 2023. Import records announced the same year contained data indicating Ukraine acquired Turkish-made ammunition, but the records listed Romtehnica, a trading company under the Romanian Defense Ministry, as the shipper instead of Turkey.

The fundamental difference between the current situation and the 2023 discussion is that the intent to transfer is no longer just the subject of a report based on anonymous sources. Turkey's plan to re-transfer US-origin weapons to Ukraine is now reflected in official US Congress records.

### US also sent cluster munitions to Ukraine

According to the Cluster Munition Monitor, the Joe Biden administration announced at least seven cluster munition transfers to Ukraine between July 2023 and October 2024. The US did not share the total amount of munitions sent with the public.

According to HRW, US defense budgets have contained provisions limiting the export of certain cluster munitions since 2009. The Biden administration utilized a legal exception mechanism for the transfers made to Ukraine. HRW notes that it is not publicly known whether the Trump administration made a similar exception decision for the current Turkey-Ukraine transfer. (NÖ/VK)

# Turkey navigates regional tensions as Yemen conflict tests Mecca defense pact

Ankara faces a delicate balancing act as it seeks to curb Iranian influence in Yemen while avoiding offensive military operations under the Mecca pact amid escalating conflict.

The war in [Yemen](https://bianet.org/tag/yemen-4116) between the Iran-backed Ansarallah group, also known as the Houthis, and the Saudi-backed, internationally recognized government continues to escalate. The Houthis have also been regularly striking targets in Saudi Arabia, including airports and oil infrastructure.

The 12-year war had remained largely dormant since 2021, until Saudi Arabia on Jul 13 bombed what it described as an unauthorized landing by a plane arriving from Tehran. The aircraft was carrying a delegation from the Houthi-led government in Sanaa that had attended the funeral of Iran's late supreme leader, Ali Khamenei, who was killed alongside family members and other senior Iranian officials in Israeli airstrikes on Feb 28.

In response to the bombing, the Houthis resumed strikes against Saudi Arabia as part of a campaign they call "blockade against blockade." The strikes were followed by a renewed conflict between the Saudi-backed government and the Houthis, who seized large swaths of territory in early October, effectively gaining control of Bab al-Mandeb, a strategic waterway leading to the Red Sea.

With Saudi air support, the Aden-based government reportedly recaptured some of the lost territory, though the situation on the ground remains fluid.

The escalation has raised questions about whether the trilateral collective defense pact between Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan will be invoked against the Houthis.

The Mecca pact follows a principle similar to Article 5 of NATO's founding treaty, which considers an attack on one member an attack on all. This has fueled speculation about a possible deployment of Turkish troops.

While Pakistan has reportedly joined the campaign against the Houthis, Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan addressed the speculation at an Oct 8 press conference. He said Turkey had no intention of deploying troops for offensive operations against another country. However, Turkish military officials would consider providing air defense, electronic warfare capabilities and technical support.

“But this is a defensive agreement. When a country party ​to the pact comes under attack, you stand by it in its defence. This is not an offensive ‌agreement. ⁠Turkey will not send troops to another country to carry out attacks on its territory," Fidan said.

![](https://static.bianet.org/2026/10/suudi-arabistandaki-husi-saldirilari-haritasi.png)The situation of territorial control in Yemen as of Oct 18 according to the Institute for the Study of War

### Balancing Iran

Political analyst Mehmet Akif Koç said that this is to be expected. Turkish support will likely be in an intelligence, and deterrence-based capacity, according to Koç.

Even as a NATO member, Turkey has often remained reluctant to engage in direct confrontations, he noted. Principles of collective security do not necessarily require Turkey to engage in direct warfare.

The US also had requested Turkey send troops during the US invasion of Iraq, which Ankara refused. During the US invasion of Afghanistan, Turkey did send troops, but only as military police to secure cities and regions, rather than to fight with the Taliban.

“Turkish [foreign policy](https://bianet.org/tag/foreign-policy-76633) is not operating, is not necessitating, directly sending any troops to hot regions, but of course there are some proxy actors that Turkey may mobilize for the interests of Saudi Arabia … so that would be an option for solving this collective security article,” Koç said.

Ultimately, the pact does not indicate a significant shift in Turkey’s geopolitical maneuvering in the region, often centered on counterbalancing the influence of other parties.

Koç said that among what he calls the four major actors in the Middle East — Turkey, Israel, Iran and the Gulf Arab states, such as Saudi Arabia — Saudi Arabia is in a particularly vulnerable position right now due to its war with the Houthis. By bolstering Riyadh, Ankara will balance Israel and Iran.

“Turkey is trying to balance the power in the Middle East by leveraging, by strengthening, the Gulf Arabs against Israel and Iran in Middle Eastern geopolitics,” Koç said.

### Conflicting interests

Writer and international relations analyst Aydın Sezer also noted the pact’s impact on Tehran.

“With the Mecca Agreement, Turkey has effectively positioned itself in the Gulf in opposition to Iran,” said Sezer. Although Erdoğan and Fidan frequently state that the Mecca pact is not directed against Iran, this is the reality.”

According to Koç, Iran is not a direct threat to Turkey, but does threaten Turkish geopolitical interests in the Middle East, which is why balancing Iran is important for Ankara’s foreign policy agenda. Turkish and Iranian interests often go head-to-head across the region, such as during the Syrian civil war, where Turkey supported armed opposition groups and Iran supported the Bashar al-Assad regime; in Lebanon, where Turkey supports the Lebanese state and Iran supports Hezbollah; and in Iraq, where Turkey is supporting Turkmen and Sunni Arabs, while Iran is supporting the Baghdad government and Shias.

Through strengthening Sunni-Arab and Turkish cooperation in the region, the pact will also counteract the influence of militant non-state actors, which threaten the status quo of traditional regional powers, like Turkey, Israel and Saudi Arabia. Many of these actors receive support from Iran, such as Hezbollah, Hashd al-Shaabi, Ansar Allah (the Houthi rebels) and Hamas.

### Outmaneuvering Iran without total destruction

Iran expert Reza Talebi said that although a weakened Iran amplifies Turkey’s regional leverage, a total collapse would pose an issue for Ankara due to spillover across their shared border. Ankara hopes to outmaneuver Iran without direct conflict or complete destruction.

“While both Ankara and Tehran are wary of Israeli influence, the simultaneous weakening of both Israel and Iran could position Turkey as the primary alternative anchor for regional security pacts in the absence of a cohesive US strategy,” Talebi said.

Sezer, Koç and Talebi all agreed that a direct military intervention by Turkey or Pakistan is unlikely. Talebi said that it might only take place in the case of a Yemeni Houthi operation that would expand to threaten Mecca or Medina. No other event could create the necessary justification for such a large-scale intervention.

Talebi also said that Ankara’s geopolitical calculus is calibrated entirely on this delicate balance. Consequently, he does not anticipate any fundamental shifts or strategic overhaul in Iran-Turkey relations any time soon.

“While we may witness occasional tactical alignments or localized rapprochements, these will remain strictly temporary and transactional,” he said.

Koç said that historically as two regional powers with imperial legacies, Turkey and Iran have always taken great care not to get into direct conflicts with one another.

“Iran and Turkey are accepting this rivalry, but of course they are behaving wisely to find a way not to war — and Turkish and Iranian leadership are attaching importance to this,” Koç said.

Although not brokered by the United States, all three countries in the pact also maintain close ties with the US, and the alliance furthers US interests amidst its and Israel’s war on Iran. When speaking to US reporters at the White House on Oct 7 about the pact US President Donald Trump said, "I think they want to help Saudi Arabia, and I think that’s great."

Separately, Turkey has also acknowledged US sanctions on Tehran. Major Turkish carriers have halted flights to Iran, however, natural gas imports continue.

Koç does not believe that either the Mecca pact or Turkey’s application of US sanctions present a serious threat to Turkey-Iran relations as they stand. He noted that Turkey was also obligated to follow US sanctions on Iran imposed during the first Trump administration and this did not significantly strain the Iran-Turkey relationship.

The alliance may also provide much needed economic opportunities for Ankara. Talebi views the pact also through a commercial lens, rather than a strictly security-oriented one. He believes the framework will be mutually beneficial for Islamabad and Ankara. As relations grow, so will opportunities for new commerce from the Gulf Arab states.

“These three countries are aimed at expanding the framework of this pact with new commerce, especially Arab countries in the region — for their willingness also the power of this pact is important,” Koç said.

Sezer said that Turkey needs Saudi Arabian capital to attempt to solve its economic crisis. Turkey will also heavily focus on selling weapons at this stage.

“The fundamental problem in Turkey today is the need to take steps to avert the economic crisis in light of the upcoming elections,” said Sezer. “At this point, Erdoğan believes he can secure both the financial resources he needs and his legitimacy through his relations with the US." (İK/VK)

# Europe questioning its official history

The national narratives held by dominant cultural groups regarding "others" or minorities are no longer welcomed as legitimate and democratically acceptable political commentary in a multicultural Europe.

[**Nazım Tural**](https://bianet.org/author/nazim-tural-8319)

\\
**Nazım Tural**](https://bianet.org/author/nazim-tural-8319)

[![](https://static.bianet.org/icons/icon-large-facebook.svg)](https://www.facebook.com/sharer.php?u=https://bianet.org/yazi/europe-questioning-its-official-history-324079)[![](https://static.bianet.org/icons/icon-large-twitter.svg)](https://twitter.com/intent/tweet?url=https://bianet.org/yazi/europe-questioning-its-official-history-324079&text=Europe%20questioning%20its%20official%20history)[![](https://static.bianet.org/icons/icon-large-whatsapp.svg)](https://wa.me/?text=https://bianet.org/yazi/europe-questioning-its-official-history-324079)[![](https://static.bianet.org/icons/icon-large-bluesky.svg)](https://bsky.app/intent/compose?text=https://bianet.org/yazi/europe-questioning-its-official-history-324079)![](https://static.bianet.org/icons/icon-large-copylink.svg)[![](https://static.bianet.org/icons/icon-large-email.svg)](mailto:?subject=Europe%20questioning%20its%20official%20history&body=https://bianet.org/yazi/europe-questioning-its-official-history-324079)![](https://static.bianet.org/icons/icon-large-print.svg)

The European approach to facing the past is closely linked to the development of democracy and the rule of law; consequently, "History"—as the reflection of the past in the present—is addressed and debated as a distinct sub-topic due to its comprehensive nature. It is evident that Europe has entered a process of remembering, commemorating, and questioning past events, a process in which "History" is being opened to debate not only within educational institutions and academic circles but also across various segments of society.

In short, Europe has recently begun to discuss and question traditional nationalist historiography, which focuses on the formation processes of nation-states and national identities. This questioning entails re-examining history through a more critical, transnational, and multidimensional perspective, stripped of single-center narratives or national myths. Briefly, by adopting a critical historical approach today, traditional historiography is being [challenged](https://journals.uclpress.co.uk/herj/article/pubid/Hist_Educ_Res_J-23-10), and it is proposed that history be revisited, subjected to self-criticism, and rewritten.

The questioning of [nationalist historiography](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331912160_State_of_Mind_History_and_the_Narrative_of_Nationalism) in Europe represents one of the most profound intellectual and political transformations that began in the aftermath of World War II and continues to this day. National narratives centered on "heroism, victimhood, and superiority," constructed in the 19th century to legitimize nation-states and create homogeneous identities, have given way to more critical, pluralistic, and transnational approaches. Furthermore, critiques in this context argue that official historiography, which constructs a homogeneous and unbroken past based on the assumption that nations are "primordial and eternal," is far from realistic. In this regard, the intellectual foundations of nationalist ideologies are being [questioned](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4462456) by drawing attention to political scientists Benedict Anderson and Eric Hobsbawm, who conceptualize nations as "imagined communities."

It is observed that nationalist official historical narratives in Europe have come under intense scrutiny, particularly following the Second World War and alongside the process of European integration. This academic and political questioning criticizes not only the weak intellectual bases of [national discourses](https://europedebate.hypotheses.org/866) but also the instrumentalization of history for political conflicts. Furthermore, it is noted that this approach [hinders](https://bianet.org/https:/www.researchgate.net/publication/327468358_The_concept_of_nationalism_in_discussions_on_a_European_society) efforts toward supranational integration, an initiative intended as a project for regional peace in Europe.

The questioning of the nationalist historiographical perspective has [shattered](https://polsci.institute/political-theory/rise-growth-nationalism-historical-perspectives/) the myth of the nation-state as a monolithic entity and led to a reinterpretation and reshaping of the concepts of state and nation within modern European society through a broader lens. European states have long maintained multicultural structures, inherited from the past, in which diverse groups of people live together. Today, modern historiography and political science are critically analyzing these national discourses.

### Key reasons for questioning official history

Examining the questioning of official history in Europe calls for a broader approach to consider the issue from the perspective of human rights, democratic culture, and civil society. In short, "Official History" is on the agenda in Europe as part of a broader re-examination of its past and present.

**1\. The impact of World War II and the Holocaust**

The suffering caused by World War II, grave human rights violations, and the collective trauma and sense of guilt resulting from the Holocaust across Europe are viewed as the primary motivations behind growing calls for re-examination. The fact that World War II and the Holocaust demonstrated the destructive consequences of nationalism has prompted the questioning of nationalist "Official History." Germany, in particular, initiated a process of confronting its past and criticizing the dark periods of its national history—an issue that now occupies the agendas of all societal groups, including the education system. These processes of confronting the past and questioning official history, which began in Germany, served as a model and an inspiration for other European countries.

**2\. Colonial era grave human rights violations**

The recent questioning of human rights and official historical narratives noted above has compelled former colonial powers such as France, the UK, Belgium, and the Netherlands to confront the realities of exploitation, slavery, and massacres that contributed to their wealth and global position. In recent years, African nations have begun raising the issue of colonialism not only in bilateral relations but also on international platforms; these initiatives place European leaders who seek to avoid such accountability in a difficult position, compelling them to reckon with their pasts.

**3\. European Integration**

Processes of European integration and the establishment of the Council of Europe and the European Union are forcing a shift away from historical perspectives that cast nation-states as mutual "enemies." The process of forging a common European culture, cultivating shared European values rooted in democracy and multiculturalism, and striving for a transnational European identity necessitates a revision of official historical narratives.

In other words, European integration mandates a move away from narrow, nation-state-centric histories toward an appreciation of a multicultural European structure characterized by coexistence and peace. Within this context, policies implemented by dominant cultural groups regarding minorities—as well as assimilation, exclusion, and ethnic nationalism-driven conflicts escalating to violence—are being critically examined.

**4\. Official narratives not fitting with a multicultural Europe**

The multicultural structure of today's Europe highlights its incompatibility with official historical theses. This means the official historical narrative, developed around unitary and monolithic state structures, is incompatible with the contemporary structure of Europe and is criticized for this reason.

Historically, European nations built their cultures and identities upon the idea of a shared origin and ancient history. However, [research](https://polsci.institute/political-theory/rise-growth-nationalism-historical-perspectives/) by reformist historians demonstrates that these concepts were, in fact, modern, socially constructed political notions that served as intellectual foundations to justify nationalist, unitary states.

Primarily, dominant state narratives of cultural groups holding power within the system insistently emphasize past victories and the superior qualities of their group while ignoring past failures. Critical historians questioning these narratives (for instance, by exposing colonial atrocities, collaboration with wartime regimes, or the oppression of minorities) trigger reactions from nationalist groups who perceive such self-criticism as a threat to their identity.

Today, questioning official history is also necessitated by the fact that Europe is multicultural and the political role of regions is rising. In short, the growing prominence of existing regions and autonomous public structures in Europe demonstrates that traditional narratives of the unitary or monolithic state are incompatible with the changed structure of Europe.

Briefly, it can be concluded that traditional nationalist historiography that excludes minorities, colonial pasts, migration, and periods of violence in the process of constructing national identities is no longer acceptable. The national narratives held by dominant cultural groups regarding "others" or minorities are no longer welcomed as legitimate and democratically acceptable political commentary in a multicultural Europe.
