The **Antonov An-24** ( [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language")/ [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language "Ukrainian language"): **Антонов Ан-24**; [NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name "NATO reporting name"): **Coke**) is a 50-seat twin [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop "Turboprop") [regional airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_airliner "Regional airliner") designed in 1957 in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") by the [Antonov Design Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov "Antonov").[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-An) Later variants saw other uses, such as [military transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft "Military transport aircraft") and [aerial cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_survey "Aerial survey"). The aircraft was manufactured by the Kyiv, Irkutsk and Ulan-Ude Aviation Factories. It was license-produced in China as the early versions of the [Xi'an Y-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an_Y-7 "Xi'an Y-7").

The An-24 was the first of a future family of turboprops by Antonov. The first variant was the [An-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-30 "Antonov An-30"), which came equipped with a cartographic configuration. It was then followed by the [An-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-26 "Antonov An-26"), which was a more militarized version of the airliner. The final variant was the [An-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-32 "Antonov An-32"), which was given more modernized equipment and powerful engines. The [An-132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov/Taqnia_An-132 "Antonov/Taqnia An-132") was the result of a joint venture between Antonov and Saudi Arabia's Taqnia to develop a modernized version of the An-24 family, but Taqnia left the project and the only prototype [was destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Antonov_Airport "Battle of Antonov Airport"), which led to the project being cancelled.

First flown in 1959, the An-24 is powered by two [Ivchenko AI-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivchenko_AI-24 "Ivchenko AI-24") turboprop engines, and was produced in some 1,000 units of various versions; in 2023, 93 were still in service worldwide, mostly in the [Commonwealth of Independent States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Independent_States "Commonwealth of Independent States") and Africa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)

It was designed to replace the veteran piston [Ilyushin Il-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-14 "Ilyushin Il-14") transport on short- to medium-haul trips, optimised for operating from rough strips and unprepared airports in remote locations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-stroud_p78-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-stroud_p78) The high-wing layout protects engines and blades from debris, the [power-to-weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-to-weight_ratio "Power-to-weight ratio") is higher than that of many comparable aircraft, and the machine is rugged, requiring minimal ground-support equipment.

Due to its rugged airframe and good performance, the An-24 was adapted to perform many secondary missions, such as ice reconnaissance and engine/propeller test bed, as well as further development to produce the An-26 tactical transport, An-30 photo-mapping/survey aircraft and An-32 tactical transport with more powerful engines. Various projects were envisaged such as a four-jet, short- and medium-haul airliner and various iterations of powerplant.

The main production line was at the [Kyiv-Svyatoshino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoshyn_Airfield "Sviatoshyn Airfield") (later renamed " [Aviant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviant "Aviant")") aircraft production plant, which built 985, with 180 built at [Ulan Ude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulan-Ude_Aviation_Plant "Ulan-Ude Aviation Plant"), and a further 197 An-24T tactical transport/freighters at [Irkutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irkut_Corporation "Irkut Corporation"). Production in the USSR was shut down by 1978.

Production continued at China's [Xi'an Aircraft Industrial Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an_Aircraft_Industrial_Corporation "Xi'an Aircraft Industrial Corporation"), which made licensed, reverse-engineered, and redesigned aircraft as the [Xian Y-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xian_Y-7 "Xian Y-7") and its derivatives. Manufacture of the Y-7, in civil form, has now been supplanted by the [MA60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MA60 "MA60") derivative with western engines and avionics, to improve performance and economy, and widen the export appeal.

## Specifications (An-24V)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Antonov_An-24_3-view_line_drawing.svg/960px-Antonov_An-24_3-view_line_drawing.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Antonov_An-24_3-view_line_drawing.svg)

_Data from_ Jane's All the World's Aircraft 1976–77[&#8202;](#ref-back-jawa76p406-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-jawa76p406)

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** 3
- **Capacity:** 50 passengers
- **Length:** 23.53m (77ft 2in)
- **Wingspan:** 29.20m (95ft 10in)
- **Height:** 8.32m (27ft 4in)
- **Wing area:** 74.98m2 (807.1sqft)
- **[Aspect ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio_(aeronautics) "Aspect ratio (aeronautics)"):** 11.7:1
- **Empty weight:** 13,300kg (29,321lb)
- **Max takeoff weight:** 21,000kg (46,297lb)
- **Fuel capacity:** 5,550L (1,470USgal; 1,220impgal)
- **Powerplant:** 2 × [Ivchenko AI-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivchenko_AI-24 "Ivchenko AI-24") A [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop "Turboprop") engines, 1,900kW (2,550shp) each (eshp)
- **Propellers:** 4-bladed AV-72 [constant-speed propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant-speed_propeller "Constant-speed propeller"), 3.90m (12ft 10in) diameter

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 485km/h (301mph, 262kn) at 6,000m (20,000ft)
- **Cruise speed:** 450km/h (280mph, 240kn) at 6,000m (20,000ft)
- **Range:** 2,400km (1,500mi, 1,300nmi) with maximum fuel; 550km (340mi; 300nmi) with maximum payload
- **Service ceiling:** 8,400m (27,600ft)
- **Time to altitude:**[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-24p65-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-An-24p65)

  - 17.5 min to 6,000m (20,000ft)
  - 45.2 min to 8,100m (26,600ft)
