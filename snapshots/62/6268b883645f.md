The **Antonov An-2** ( [USAF/DoD reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF/DoD_reporting_name "USAF/DoD reporting name") **Type 22**,[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) [NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name "NATO reporting name") **Colt**[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)) is a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") mass-produced single-engine [biplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biplane "Biplane") utility/agricultural aircraft designed and manufactured by the [Antonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov "Antonov") [Design Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_Bureau "Design Bureau") beginning in 1947.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-An) Its durability, lifting power, and ability to take off and land from poor runways have given it a long service life. The An-2 was produced up to 2001 and remains in service with military and civilian operators around the world.

The An-2 was designed as a utility aircraft for forestry and agriculture, but the basic [airframe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airframe "Airframe") is adaptable and numerous variants have been developed. These include hopper-equipped crop-dusters, scientific versions for atmospheric sampling, water-bombers for fighting [forest fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_fire "Forest fire"), [air ambulances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_ambulance "Air ambulance"), seaplanes, and versions for dropping [paratroopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratrooper "Paratrooper").[&#8202;](#ref-back-harp-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-harp)

The most common version is the **An-2T** 12-seater passenger aircraft. All versions (other than the [An-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-3 "Antonov An-3") and the An-2-100) are powered by a 750kW (1,010hp) nine-cylinder [Shvetsov ASh-62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shvetsov_ASh-62 "Shvetsov ASh-62") [radial engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_engine "Radial engine").[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

## Design and development

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Design and development")\]

### Origins

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Origins")\]

The Antonov An-2 was designed to meet a 1940s Soviet Ministry of Forestry requirement to replace the smaller [Polikarpov Po-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polikarpov_Po-2 "Polikarpov Po-2"), which was used in large numbers in both agricultural and utility roles. Antonov designed a large single bay [biplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biplane "Biplane") of all-metal construction, with an enclosed cockpit and a cabin with seats for twelve passengers. The first prototype, designated **SKh-1**, powered by a [Shvetsov ASh-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shvetsov_ASh-21 "Shvetsov ASh-21") radial engine, flew on 31 August 1947 from [Yeltsovka Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeltsovka_Airport "Yeltsovka Airport") in [Novosibirsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk_Oblast "Novosibirsk Oblast"), Russia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6) The second prototype was fitted with a more powerful [Shvetsov ASh-62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shvetsov_ASh-62 "Shvetsov ASh-62") engine, which allowed the aircraft's payload to be increased from 1,300 to 2,140kg (2,870 to 4,720lb), and in this form saw production.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guns-ton_Russian_p20-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-Guns-ton_Russian_p20)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/An-2_OK-HFL_EDST_02.jpg/250px-An-2_OK-HFL_EDST_02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An-2_OK-HFL_EDST_02.jpg) On the static display of "Oldtimer Fliegertreffen" Hahnweide 2011

Initial production was at State Factory 473 in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv "Kyiv"), [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR "Ukrainian SSR"), where the majority of up to 5,000 units had been produced by 1960. Later Soviet production (after 1965, of model An-2M especially) was at State Factory 464 at Dolgoprudniy, [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR "Russian SFSR"). After 1960, most An-2s were constructed at Poland's [WSK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZL_Mielec "PZL Mielec") factory in [Mielec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mielec "Mielec"). It is believed that over 13,000 were built in Poland before manufacturing ended in 1991.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-auto1)

Until 2001, limited production was undertaken using remaining stocks of spares, including a small batch of four aircraft that were produced for Vietnam.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-auto1) [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") also builds the An-2 under licence as the **Shijiazhuang Y-5**.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
The An-2 is commonly used as a light utility transport, parachute drop aircraft, [agricultural work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_application "Aerial application") and other tasks suited to a large slow biplane. Its slow flight and good short field performance make it suited for short, unimproved fields, and some specialized variants have also been built for cold weather and other extreme environments. The _[Guinness Book of World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records")_ states that the 45-year production run for the An-2 was for a time the longest for any aircraft and challenged the over two decade-long run of the 1920s [Polikarpov Po-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polikarpov_Po-2 "Polikarpov Po-2") it replaced. The An-2's record has since been exceeded.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-4)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

### Further development

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Further development")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Antonov.an-2.ha-mkf.arp.jpg/250px-Antonov.an-2.ha-mkf.arp.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Antonov.an-2.ha-mkf.arp.jpg) Closeup of a private An-2TP

During the early 1980s, Antonov experimented with an An-2 powered by a 1,080kW (1,450hp) Glushenkov [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop "Turboprop") engine. Aircraft with this engine had a longer, streamlined nose. It received the designation [An-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-3 "Antonov An-3").[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-5)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

During 2013, Antonov announced that it had flown a new An-2 version, the _An-2-100_, with a three-blade reversible propeller and a 1,100kW (1,500shp) [Motor Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Sich "Motor Sich") MS-14 turboprop running on [kerosene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerosene "Kerosene") rather than Avgas, which is no longer produced in CIS countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-:0) That same year, the company stated that it had received orders for upgrading hundreds in [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan "Azerbaijan"), [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba") and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") to the An-2-100 version.[&#8202;](#ref-back-усовершенствованные_кукурузники-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-усовершенствованные_кукурузники)

The Siberian Research Institute of Aviation (SIBNIA) test flew a modified An-2 with winglets and a carbon fibre wing structure. It was equipped with a turboprop engine with a five bladed propeller. According to Russian aviation company [Sukhoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi "Sukhoi"), this aircraft was a demonstrator for an An-2 replacement announced on 10 June 2015. The carbonfibre composite materials, including wing panels, spars and ribs were produced by the Novosibirsk Aviation Plant. Sukhoi says the design change increased its speed by 50%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

## Design

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Design")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/An-2_on_skis.jpg/250px-An-2_on_skis.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An-2_on_skis.jpg) An-2 on skis at Volosovo air field,[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) [Chekhovsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chekhovsky_District "Chekhovsky District"), [Moscow region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Oblast "Moscow Oblast")[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/An-2_GJT.jpg/250px-An-2_GJT.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An-2_GJT.jpg) An-2 at [Grand Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Junction_Regional_Airport "Grand Junction Regional Airport") aviation show

The An-2 is a mass-produced single-engine [biplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biplane "Biplane") that was deliberately furnished with a minimum of complex systems. The wing leading edge slats that give the aircraft its slow flight ability are fully automatic, being held shut by the airflow over the wings. When airspeed drops below 64km/h (40mph), elastic rubber springs extend the slats.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-6)[\[1\]](#refnote-An) Under typical conditions, take-off can be made within 170m (560ft) while the landing run requires 215m (705ft). These figures vary dependent on weight, air temperature, runway surface, and wind direction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-7)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

The An-2 is equipped with features which make it suitable [for operation in remote areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_plane "Bush plane") with unimproved airstrips. It is fitted with a [pneumatic brake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_brake_(road_vehicle) "Air brake (road vehicle)") system similar to those used on heavy trucks to stop on short runways, along with an air line attached to the [compressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressor "Compressor"), so the pressure in the [tires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tire "Tire") and [shock absorbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_absorber "Shock absorber") can be adjusted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-8)[\[1\]](#refnote-An) The batteries, while sizable, are easy to remove, and it does not need a [ground power unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_power_unit "Ground power unit") for starting the engine. Likewise, there is no need for an external fuel pump to refuel the aircraft as it is provided with its own.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-9)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/An2TP-Rusalka.jpg/250px-An2TP-Rusalka.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An2TP-Rusalka.jpg) An An2-TP

The operating handbook does not explicitly specify a [stall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stall_(flight) "Stall (flight)") speed, stating instead: "If the engine quits in instrument conditions or at night, the pilot should pull the control column full aft and keep the wings level. The leading-edge slats will snap out at about 64km/h (40mph) and when the airplane slows to a forward speed of about 40km/h (25mph), the airplane will sink at about a parachute descent rate until the aircraft hits the ground."[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-10)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

The low stall speed makes it possible for the aircraft to fly backwards relative to the ground under high wind conditions while under control.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-11)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

Many western countries prohibit the use of the An-2 commercially because the aircraft has not been certified by the relevant national aviation authorities. These restrictions vary by country, but all prevent the An-2 being used for any commercial purpose, with the exception of the United States, where PZL-built An-2s are exempt from this restriction due to a [bilateral agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateral_Air_Transport_Agreement "Bilateral Air Transport Agreement") with Poland.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-12)[\[1\]](#refnote-An) Other An-2s are operated non-commercially under an experimental certification.[&#8202;](#ref-back-harp-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-harp)

## Operational history

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Operational history")\]

### Military service

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Military service")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d4/AN2_R.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:AN2_R.jpeg) [Ukrainian hryvnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_hryvnia "Ukrainian hryvnia") depicting the An-2 airplane

The An-2 was operated in large numbers by the [Soviet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Force "Soviet Air Force") and [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc") forces. Its first military use was during the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War") of the early 1950s.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

The [Vietnam People's Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Air_Force "Vietnam People's Air Force") (VPAF) operated the An-2 during the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") and occasionally used the type as an attack aircraft. During the 1960s, a single An-2 that was engaging [South Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnamese "South Vietnamese") naval units was shot down by a [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force") [McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_F-4_Phantom_II "McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II") fighter.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-13)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

On 12 January 1968, a clandestine [TACAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TACAN "TACAN") site ( [call sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_sign "Call sign"): _[Lima Site 85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Site_85 "Lima Site 85")_/Phou Pha Ti) installed by the United States Air Force in Northern [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos") for directing [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF "USAF") warplanes flying from [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand") to [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam") was [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lima_Site_85 "Battle of Lima Site 85") by three [North Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnamese "North Vietnamese") An-2s. A pair of An-2s fired on the outpost using a mixture of machine guns and rockets while a third An-2 orbited overhead.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto2-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-auto2) An [Air America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_America_(airline) "Air America (airline)") [Bell UH-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_UH-1 "Bell UH-1") B resupplying the site gave chase to the attacking aircraft. Using an [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47 "AK-47"), the American crew (Ted Moore Captain, Glen Wood Kicker) succeeded in shooting down one of the An-2s while the second aircraft was forced down by combined ground and air fire, eventually crashing into a mountain. The surviving Antonov returned to its home base, Gia Lam, near Hanoi.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto2-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-auto2)

During the [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence "Croatian War of Independence") in 1991, a number of elderly An-2s previously used for crop-spraying were converted by the [Croatian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Air_Force_and_Defense "Croatian Air Force and Defense") to drop makeshift [barrel bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_bombs "Barrel bombs"). They were also used to conduct supply missions to besieged parts of Croatia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WAPJ_24_p145-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-WAPJ_24_p145)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Antonov_An-2_Vientiane.jpg/250px-Antonov_An-2_Vientiane.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Antonov_An-2_Vientiane.jpg) An AN-2 of the Laos air force

The chief advantage of the An-2 was its ability to operate from small improvised airstrips. They frequently dropped supplies by parachute to isolated garrisons. At least one An-2 was shot down on 2 December 1991 over [Vinkovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinkovci "Vinkovci"), eastern [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia "Slavonia"), by a Serbian salvo of [SA-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2K12_Kub "2K12 Kub") surface to air missiles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

[North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") has also operated An-2s. The [Korean People's Army Special Operation Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_People's_Army_Special_Operation_Force "Korean People's Army Special Operation Force") has used the An-2 to infiltrate paratroopers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NKSF-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-NKSF)\[ _[pageneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources "Wikipedia:Citing sources")_\]

During the [2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_war "2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war"), Azerbaijan operated unmanned An-2s for surveillance and bombing of Armenian defenses,[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) however the type of the drone was unknown as of October 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) Armenian forces revealed footage of the alleged shootdown of an Azerbaijani An-2, according to video evidence at least 11 An-2 have been destroyed, with 10 confirmed as shot down and one crashing after takeoff.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

On March 2, 2022, Russian An-2s were observed at [Seshcha Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seshcha_(air_base) "Seshcha (air base)"), [Bryansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryansk_Oblast "Bryansk Oblast"). As the base is close to the [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine") border, it was speculated that the aircraft were to be used in the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine").[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

### Civil aviation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Civil aviation")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/An-2_plane_spraying_wheat_crops.jpg/250px-An-2_plane_spraying_wheat_crops.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An-2_plane_spraying_wheat_crops.jpg) An-2 sprays pesticide on wheat crops during [Operation Barnstormer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_coalition_military_operations_of_the_Iraq_War#2006 "List of coalition military operations of the Iraq War") (May 2006).

Over the years, dozens of nations and companies have employed the An-2 in civil roles. The type was heavily used throughout the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") and the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc") nations. In particular, the Russian airline [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot "Aeroflot") operated a large number of them. The An-2 was used as a short-range airliner, and in [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia "Estonia"), made regular flights between the towns of [Kuressaare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuressaare "Kuressaare") and [Kärdla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A4rdla "Kärdla"), which are on separate islands, [Saaremaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saaremaa "Saaremaa") and [Hiiumaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiiumaa "Hiiumaa").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Since the collapse of the Soviet Union, most airlines in these regions have been retiring their An-2s, as some were over 40 years old, as well as a result of the decline in the production of [avgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avgas "Avgas") to fuel the type.[&#8202;](#ref-back-harp-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-harp) Private operators are still using An-2s, as they remain popular for some functions, such as for [skydiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydiving "Skydiving").[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-14)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)[&#8202;](#ref-back-harp-3)[\[5\]](#refnote-harp)

High noise levels, maintenance costs and fuel consumption[&#8202;](#ref-back-harp-4)[\[5\]](#refnote-harp) has rendered them obsolete for the majority of commercial routes in Europe, but the large number available mean that unit prices are low in comparison to alternatives (as little as US$30,000 for a serviceable example). Price has made them attractive in the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world "Developing world"), where their abilities makes them an asset to airlines on a budget. Many ex-Aeroflot An-2s have found work with regional operators across [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America "Central America") and [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_America "South America"), [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba") and southeast [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-15)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

As of 2015, there were thousands of An-2s in operation around the world, including over 1,500 in Russia, 294 in [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan") and 54 in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine").[&#8202;](#ref-back-усовершенствованные_кукурузники-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-усовершенствованные_кукурузники)

In September 2024, [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") ordered the [UZGA LMS-901 Baikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UZGA_LMS-901_Baikal "UZGA LMS-901 Baikal") aircraft into production as a replacement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Baikal-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-Baikal) However, certification of that aircraft's [Klimov VK-800SM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_VK-800 "Klimov VK-800") engine is not expected until 2025, with engine deliveries not starting until 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Baikal-1)[\[25\]](#refnote-Baikal)

#### Prototype

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Prototype")\]

- _**Izdeliye F**_ – The in-house designation for the An-2NAK observation aircraft which was later re-designated An-2K and/or An-2F.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-16)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- _**Izdeliye K**_ – A prototype weather reconnaissance/research aircraft with an observers cockpit immediately forward of the fin above the rear [fuselage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage "Fuselage"). First flown on 21 March 1948 'Izdeliye K' was found to be under-powered and unable to carry out its intended mission at high altitude, the aircraft was abandoned after a landing accident in October 1948. The concept was resurrected in 1954 as the 'An-2ZA' / 'An-6 Meteo'.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-17)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- _**Izdeliye T**_ – The original design concept originated at [OKB-153](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OKB-153 "OKB-153") in Novosibirsk.
- _**Izdeliye Sh**_ – The in-house designation for the 'Izdeliye T' (presumably to confuse spies or infiltrators) design project at Novosibirsk.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-18)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **SKh-1** – the original designation of the An-2 used during the design phase of the project before the OKB had been named after Antonov.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-19)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **An-2A** – Developed from the An-2T for use as a Balloon interceptor to intercept reconnaissance balloons from, US intelligence assets, over-flying the USSR. The prototype was converted from a Kyiv built An-2 (c/n 110347315) using the Turbo-charged Ash-62IR/TK of the 'An-6 Meteo' and a combined searchlight/gun turret mounted on top of the rear fuselage, which mounted either a single [Gryazev-Shipunov GSh-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gryazev-Shipunov_GSh-23 "Gryazev-Shipunov GSh-23") 23mm calibre twin-barrelled cannon or a single [Afanas'yev A-12.7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afanasev_A-12.7 "Afanasev A-12.7") 12.7mm calibre heavy machine-gun.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-20)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **An-2E** ( _Ekranoplan_ – wing in ground effect)[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/An-2_converted_into_WIG_%28Rybachy%2C_Kaliningrad_Oblast_-_2006_08_15%29.jpg/250px-An-2_converted_into_WIG_%28Rybachy%2C_Kaliningrad_Oblast_-_2006_08_15%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An-2_converted_into_WIG_(Rybachy,_Kaliningrad_Oblast_-_2006_08_15).jpg) The An-2E prototype ekranoplan (first use of the designation) – One of a series of projects from the early 1970s for WIG ( [Wing In Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_effect_vehicle "Ground effect vehicle") effect) derivatives of the An-2, designed at the TsLST ( _Tsentral'naya Laboratoriya Spasatel'noy Techniki_ – central laboratory for new types of rescue equipment).
- **An-2E** (second use of the designation) – An [Ekranoplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekranoplan "Ekranoplan") conversion ( [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Ан-2Э) of an An-2P (RA-84692). The fuselage was retained and a large trapezoidal monoplane wing having extended wingtips with approx 45° dihedral, attached to the lower wing attachment points. The prototype conversion was intended to be a floatplane; however, its first public appearance was as a landplane with the standard An-2 undercarriage. Jointly developed by the Moscow Aviation Institute, MARZ ( _Moskovskiy Aviaremontnyy Zavod_ – Moscow Aircraft Overhaul Plant) and The TSZP-Saturn research institute the An-2E was also known as EA-00078. Power was supplied by the standard ASh-62IR radial engine with AV-2 propeller, but production versions were intended to be powered by automotive diesel engines of around 430kW (580hp).[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-21)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-2e-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-An-2e)
- **An-2F** – Developed concurrently with the An-2 prototype, 'Izdeliye F' was an experimental artillery-observation aircraft with a twin-tail, ventral observer's position and dorsal defensive machine-gun position. The initial service designation of An-2NAK was changed to An-2F once post test flight modifications had been carried out. Two prototypes were built, with the first completed in the summer of 1948, but flight testing was delayed to April 1949 due to the need to incorporate modifications called up from the standard An-2 flight test programme. The flight test of the An-2F showed that the aircraft met all the requirements of the specification, but helicopters were demonstrating that they could perform the artillery correction role without the need to provide fixed base airstrip for them to operate from, thus the An-2F was not proceeded with and the two prototypes were retained by the Antonov bureau for hack duties with one later serving as a flying wind tunnel to investigate para-dropping from the [Antonov An-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-8 "Antonov An-8") twin-turboprop transport.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-22)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **An-2F** (second use of designation) – A VTOL project with vertical thrust to be provided by a Mikulin AM-9 turbojet in the rear fuselage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-23)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **An-2F** (third use of the designation) – A proposed photo-mapping aircraft project, from the early 1990s, similar to the An-2PF equipped with one of five alternate cameras. Overall control was to have been by IBM 286 computer and the camera could be augmented or replaced by a thermal imaging system, Infra-Red imaging system, AP-6E autopilot, LDI-3 laser rangefinder and/or a GPS global positioning system.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-24)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **An-2G** (a.k.a. **An-2Geo**) – During 1974 WSK-Mielec developed a geo-physical survey aircraft for the Polish State Geophysical Survey Agency ( _Państwowe Przedsiębiorstwo Poszukiwań Geofizycznych_), with equipment for measuring magnetic fields, radiation and other instruments for prospecting. Adapted to fly in tropical latitudes at low altitude the 'An-2G' could fly for six hours and two were used for survey work in [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria") in 1974.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-25)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **An-2LP** ( _Lesopozharnyy_ – forest firefighter) – A single prototype converted from an An-2V floatplane, with 630 litre(139 Imp.Gal.) water tanks in each float for dropping on fires. The water tanks could be re-filled in flight, as the aircraft skimmed the surface of suitable bodies of water. Prior to dropping an alkaline wetting agent (NP-1 Sulphanol) was added to the water which was released through the scoops which also picked up the water to refill the tanks. Ten production aircraft were built seeing service in the Siberian and Far East regions of the USSR.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-26)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

The An-2M featured an all-duralumin welded and bonded fuselage, lockable tailwheel, enlarged fibre-glass hopper, Shvetsov ASh-62 radial engine with power take off to an auxiliary gearbox to drive the dusting/spraying equipment, an angular fin of increased area and a hermetically sealed cockpit with no access to the cabin/hopper area. - **An-2S** ( _Sanitarnyy_ – medical) – The An-2S was an adaptation of the standard An-2T cargo plane with up to six stretcher patients or three stretchers and six walking wounded along with attendants and support equipment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-43)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)
- **An-2SKh** ( _Sel'skoKhozyaistvennyy_ – agricultural) – The agricultural version optimised for crop-spraying and crop-dusting. A metal construction chemical hopper was installed in the cabin near the centre of gravity, feeding a spreader through a 300mm orifice. The original spreader was replaced in 1975 by a standardised RTSh-1 three channel spreader which increased distribution width from 18–22m (59–72ft) to 34–36m (112–118ft). Liquid chemicals could be sprayed using an interchangeable system which delivered chemical to spraybars via a ram air turbine driven regulator pump to give coverage 30m (98ft) wide. Powdered or granulated chemicals were loaded through a hatch in the roof of the cabin and liquid chemical through a special connector on the port side of the fuselage. Early An-2SKh's were typically limited to a 6,000-hour life, as opposed to a normal life of 12,000 hours, due to the corrosive effects of the chemicals. The introduction of epoxy based corrosion protection systems partly alleviated the situation, allowing the agricultural An-2s to continue flying for much longer. Five An-2SKh aircraft were converted to firefighters, from 1954, by pressurising the hopper filled with water and/or retardant and ejecting it from a nozzle in place of the dusting/spraying equipment, single aircraft were allocated to Civil Air Directorates across the country but proved relatively ineffectual.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-44)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

#### An-6

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: An-6")\]

- **An-6** – A transport aircraft fitted with the Shvetsov Ash-62IR/TK turbo-charged engine was produced for use in mountainous areas at high altitudes. An unknown number were delivered to the Tajik Civil Aviation Directorate and the Polar directorate for use in the far north of the USSR and to support the USSR's Antarctic research stations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-63)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/An6.webp/250px-An6.webp?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:An6.webp) An-6 Meteo
- **An-6 Meteo** – After the failure of 'Izdeliye K', the Antonov bureau was tasked with developing the 'An-2ZA' high-altitude weather reconnaissance aircraft based on the An-2. Featuring the same observers cockpit, forward of the vertical stabiliser above the rear fuselage, used on 'Izdeliye K', power was supplied by a turbo-charged Shvetsov Ash-62IR/TK which enabled the 'An-2ZA' to carry out its intended high-altitude research role. On 9 June 1954 OKB-153 test pilot V.A. Kalinin and Flight Engineer V.I. Baklaykin set a World altitude record for Class C-1e-1 (3,000–6,000kg (6,600–13,200lb)) at 11,248m (36,903ft) which had not been broken by 2004. The 'An-2ZA' was re-designated 'An-6 Meteo' early in its career and continued to fly until a take-off accident in September 1958 caused extensive damage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-64)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)

### Chinese variants

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Chinese variants")\]

- **Feng Shou-2** (Harvester-2) – The name given to the first Nanchang built agricultural 'Y-5II'.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-68)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Yunshuji_Y-5.jpg/250px-Yunshuji_Y-5.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Yunshuji_Y-5.jpg) Nanchang Y-5 at China Aviation Museum, Beijing
- **Feihong-98** (FH-98) – Unmanned transport aircraft with a payload of 1.5 tonnes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)
- **Nanchang Y-5** – ( _Yunshuji_ – transport) Chinese version of An-2, initially built from Soviet blueprints and with supervision from Soviet advisors, 727 had been built when production was transferred to Harbin in 1968.[&#8202;](#ref-back-An-69)[\[1\]](#refnote-An)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sinodefence1-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-sinodefence1)
- **Nanchang Y-5II** – Crop-sprayer/duster with a chemical hopper/tank in the cabin and interchangeable spraying/dusting equipment. Cooling of the cockpit was increased to improve comfort in the sub-tropical regions of China. 229 built.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-1)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)
- **Shijiazhuang Y-5A** – First mass-produced, Shijiazhuang built, version, light passenger transport, equivalent to the An-2T, 114 built.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-2)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sinodefence1-1)[\[33\]](#refnote-sinodefence1)
- **Shijiazhuang Y-5B** – Shijiazhuang built agricultural aircraft, equivalent to the An-2 SKh.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-3)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sinodefence1-2)[\[33\]](#refnote-sinodefence1)
- **Shijiazhuang Y-5B-100** – Y-5B aircraft fitted with triple tipsails on the upper wing tips, which reputedly gave 20% higher climb rate and improved L/D ratio by 15%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-4)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)
- **Shijiazhuang Y-5B(T)** – A para-dropping version developed for the PLAAF, with up-dated avionics including a GPS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-5)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)
- **Shijiazhuang Y-5B(K)** – Variously reported as a tourist variant or Agricultural variant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-6)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)
- **Shijiazhuang Y-5B(D)** – Variously reported as an Agricultural variant or Tourist variant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-7)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)
- **Nanchang Y-5C** – Amphibian version of Y-5A fitted with two floats.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-8)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sinodefence1-3)[\[33\]](#refnote-sinodefence1)
- **Nanchang Y-5D** – Bomber crew trainer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-9)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/Antonov-2_cockpit.jpg/250px-Antonov-2_cockpit.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Antonov-2_cockpit.jpg) Cockpit of a 1971 ex- [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot "Aeroflot") An-2 at the [Historic Aircraft Restoration Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Aircraft_Restoration_Museum "Historic Aircraft Restoration Museum"). See also [&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34) for a very high resolution image of this cockpit.

- **Nanchang Y-5K** – A VIP passenger variant with five seats. Eleven were delivered to the PLAAF from 1958. A seven seater was delivered to [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh "Ho Chi Minh") in [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam") and two were given to the Nepal Royal Flight for use by [King Birendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Birendra "King Birendra").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-10)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)
- **Shijiazhuang Y-5B (turboprop)** – A projected turboprop upgrade conversion programme replacing the 1,000hp Huosai-5 radial engine with a more powerful turboprop.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chinese-11)[\[32\]](#refnote-Chinese)
- **HY100 Large UAV** \- An [unmanned aerial vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aerial_vehicle "Unmanned aerial vehicle") based on either the Shijiazhuang or Nanchang models. According to the manufacturer, the HY100 "is the first and only "large-scale" UAV approved by the [Civil Aviation Administration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Administration_of_China "Civil Aviation Administration of China")," has a listed payload of 1900 kilograms, and can be equipped for roles including crop spraying, forestry monitoring, and aerial firefighting.

## Accidents and incidents

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Accidents and incidents")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Niger%2C_Agadez%2C_airport_%2806%29%2C_Antonov_An-2_from_Bask_Air.jpg/250px-Niger%2C_Agadez%2C_airport_%2806%29%2C_Antonov_An-2_from_Bask_Air.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Niger,_Agadez,_airport_(06),_Antonov_An-2_from_Bask_Air.jpg) Two wrecked An-2R aircraft, both from Bask Air, at Agadez Airport in Niger

- On March 27, 1972, Timofei Shovkunov stole an An-2 and flew it directly into his apartment building in [Voroshilovgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voroshilovgrad "Voroshilovgrad") (now [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk "Luhansk")) apparently despondent after his wife having left along with his son the day before. He was the lone casualty.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-60)

- On 26 September 1976, Russian national Vladimir Serkov made an unauthorized takeoff with an An-2 (Reg # USSR-79868) from [Novosibirsk-Severny Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severny_Airport "Severny Airport"). He crashed it into the stairwell of an apartment complex at Stepnayast., house43 / 1, where his ex-wife's parents lived, in an attempt to kill his ex-wife. After completing two laps around the scene, Serkov attempted to pilot the plane to the parents' apartment where his wife and two-year-old son were visiting. The aircraft pierced the stairwell between the 3rd and 4th floors, and being fueled with 800 liters of gasoline, ignited a large fire inside the stairwell that ultimately spread to damage 30 total apartments. Firefighters were on scene in five minutes, taking 57 minutes to extinguish the blaze. A four-year-old and two six-year-old children were killed at the scene from burns. Another four-year-old child died eight days later as a result of burns. In total, 11 residents were injured as a result of fire burns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto3-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-auto3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-auto) Serkov's ex-wife (and her parents) and his toddler son were not injured in the incident.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto3-1)[\[61\]](#refnote-auto3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[62\]](#refnote-auto)

- On March 6, 2006, an Antonov An-2 was destroyed in Lubaimanga[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[9\]](#refnote-:0)

- On 11 February 2009, an Antonov An-2 aircraft suffered a loss in engine power while climbing through an altitude of about 50–70 metres. The pilot had to carry out a forced landing in a wooded area because he could not reach the airport at [Pskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov "Pskov"). All three crew members and ten paratroopers on board survived.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-63)

- On 22 February, 2009, a [Golden Rule Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Rule_Airlines "Golden Rule Airlines") Antonov An-2, carrying cargo and crew, was damaged beyond repair after it crashed in a field due to loss of engine power. There were no fatalities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64)

- On May 6, 2016, after an unsuccessful attempt to restart their engines and approaching [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_Airport "San Bernardino Airport"), an Antonov An-2R en route from Cable Airport to San Bernardino made a forced landing in a residential area which left none injured. According to the subsequent investigation, the lower gascolator and the fine fuel filter were filled partially with water, which led to engine failure.

- On 27 July 2018, an Antonov An-2 crashed on take-off from an airstrip near [Kamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamako?action=edit&redlink=1 "Kamako (page does not exist)"), killing five of seven occupants.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-65)

- On January 2021, a North Korean Air Force Antonov An-2 airplane lost control and crashed, killing the pilot and four students from the Chaehyun Military Academy.

- On 14 November 2022, in the [Everglades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everglades "Everglades"), an An-2, which had been seized by [U.S. Customs and Border Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Customs_and_Border_Protection "U.S. Customs and Border Protection") was being transported to [Fort Lauderdale Executive Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale_Executive_Airport "Fort Lauderdale Executive Airport") and overturned during a forced landing caused by an engine failure. Both pilots survived.

- On October 25, 2024, an An-2 was landing at a local airstrip after an agricultural spraying flight when the plane hit a tuk tuk with 15 people on board. Of the occupants of the tuk tuk 4 died and 8 other were injured. The 3 crew members, composed of Ukrainian and Sudanese nationals, were uninjured. The aircraft sustained substantial damage to its right wing and propeller.

- On August 16, 2025, an Antonov An-2 operated by [Air Kasaï](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Kasa%C3%AF "Air Kasaï") crashed in a wooded area while on a ferry flight near Kisangani, Democratic Republic of the Congo, killing all seven occupants.[&#8202;](#ref-back-kis24-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-kis24)[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67)

- On 21 June 2026 a privately operated Antonov An-2 crashed while landing at Luxembourg Findel Airport, after a wing clipped the runway, leading to the airport being closed for several hours with all flights diverted or cancelled.

## Specifications (An-2)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antonov_An-2&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Specifications (An-2)")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Antonov_An-2_3view.svg/330px-Antonov_An-2_3view.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Antonov_An-2_3view.svg)

_Data from_ Biplanes, Triplanes, and Seaplanes[&#8202;](#ref-back-BTS-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-BTS)

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** 1–2
- **Capacity:** 12 passengers / 2,140kg (4,718lb)
- **Length:** 12.4m (40ft 8in)
- **Upper wingspan:** 18.2m (59ft 9in)
- **Lower wingspan:** 14.2m (46ft 7in)
- **Height:** 4.1m (13ft 5in)
- **Wing area:** 71.52m2 (769.8sqft)
- **[Airfoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airfoil "Airfoil"):** TsAGI R-11 (14%)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Selig-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-Selig)
- **Empty weight:** 3,300kg (7,275lb)
- **Gross weight:** 5,440kg (11,993lb)
- **Fuel capacity:** 1,200L (320USgal; 260impgal)
- **Powerplant:** 1 × [Shvetsov ASh-62IR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shvetsov_ASh-62IR "Shvetsov ASh-62IR") 9-cylinder air-cooled supercharged radial piston engine, 750kW (1,010hp)
- **Propellers:** 4-bladed [constant-speed propeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable-pitch_propeller_(aeronautics) "Variable-pitch propeller (aeronautics)")

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 258km/h (160mph, 139kn)
- **Cruise speed:** 190km/h (120mph, 100kn)
- **Stall speed:** 50km/h (31mph, 27kn) circa
- **Range:** 845km (525mi, 456nmi)
- **Service ceiling:** 4,500m (14,800ft)
- **Rate of climb:** 3.5m/s (690ft/min)
- **[Power/mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-to-weight_ratio "Power-to-weight ratio"):** 0.136kW/kg (0.083hp/lb)
- **Fuel consumption:** 185–200L/h (49–53gal/h; 41–44impgal/h)
