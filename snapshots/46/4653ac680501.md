In some ways, William Trump is a typical teenager. He likes to swim, snack on peanut butter sandwiches and roughhouse with his older brother. He has wide, blue eyes, an untamed smile and a love for reggae music inspired by a Jamaican caretaker. Ice cream is one of his favorite things.

But unlike most of his classmates at Central Middle School, William, who uses a wheelchair, cannot walk, talk or care for himself. He has cerebral palsy, a physical disability diagnosed 13 years ago, shortly after his birth.

"When that young woman would come to the house, we were like, `Wow. This is great,'" Fred Trump said. "For the next hour she is going to care for our child.' "

William's in-home therapy visits were provided by Abilis, a Greenwich nonprofit that services clients with developmental disabilities. The organization matches clients with home therapists, extracurricular programming and jobs caring for animals at pet stores or setting tables at Sundown Saloon on Greenwich Avenue. It also manages group housing for about 100 adults.

On Monday, Trump will host his second Golf for Abilis fundraiser, a sold-out event that has drawn 112 golfers for 18 holes at Trump National in Briarcliff Manor, N.Y.

The outing drew 74 golfers last year. The money they raised helped fund 9,000 home therapy visits, weekly toddler play groups and a gift shop that provides adults with retail skill training. With the proceeds from this year's event, Abilis plans to open an online gift shop and a new group residence for three local youths who spent their childhoods in facilities outside the state.

"This is my opportunity to give back to Abilis, which has helped us so much," Trump said.

Just as they undermine mental and muscle maturation, developmental disabilities can also bankrupt families.

A 2004 study by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention determined the average lifetime cost of having cerebral palsy just shy of a million dollars. This figure accounts not only for medical care but also lost income.

William, who receives a range of private care including music and aquatic therapy, comes from a family of means. Trump knows his son might not be functioning at the level he is today if he didn't have the opportunity to receive high caliber services from agencies such as Abilis.

"For those who aren't fortunate enough to have the resources, Abilis can help," Trump said. "I'll just say it: I'm sure the therapy they are offering at some state institutions are not quality like you'll get here."

Golfing slots for Monday's event are no longer available, but there are sponsorships and tickets available for the awards dinner, which includes a cocktail hour and live and silent auctions. Auction items include an all-inclusive vacation for four to Casa de Campo in the Dominican Republic, an eight-person golf trip to Old Chatham Golf Club in North Carolina and a weekend getaway to the Kimberly Hotel in New York City.
