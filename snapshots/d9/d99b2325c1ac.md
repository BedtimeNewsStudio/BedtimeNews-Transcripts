**Ken Orsatti** (January 31, 1932 – August 31, 2010) was an American [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_director "Film director"). Orsatti was the national [executive director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_director "Executive director") of the [Screen Actors Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild "Screen Actors Guild") from 1981 to 2000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes)

Orsatti was born Alfred Kendall Orsatti in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") in 1932.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes) His father and two uncles were [talent agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talent_agent "Talent agent"), who owned and operated a leading talent agency, the Orsatti Agency.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes) He received a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in [business administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_administration "Business administration") from the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California") in 1956.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes) Following graduation, Orsatti became a [production assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_assistant "Production assistant") at [Rorvic Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorvic_Productions "Rorvic Productions"), which was owned by his uncle, [Victor Orsatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Orsatti "Victor Orsatti"), and the actor [Rory Calhoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_Calhoun "Rory Calhoun").[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-4)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes)

Orsatti became a business representative at the [American Federation of Television and Radio Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Television_and_Radio_Artists "American Federation of Television and Radio Artists") (AFTRA) in 1960.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-5)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes) He left AFTRA in 1961 and joined the Screen Actors Guild (SAG).[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-6)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes) He became the SAG's Western [regional director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_director?action=edit&redlink=1 "Regional director") in 1966.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-7)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes) He was promoted to SAG's Hollywood executive secretary in 1971, a position he held until 1981.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-8)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes)

Orsatti, became the [executive director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_director "Executive director") of the Screen Actors Guild in 1981. He negotiated and brokered more than 20 union contracts as SAG's chief [negotiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation "Negotiation") during his nineteen-year tenure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-9)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes)

In 1994, Orsatti told the _[Los Angeles Business Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Business_Journal "Los Angeles Business Journal")_, "Someone once said that the perfect negotiation is one where both sides are unhappy. There's some truth to that."[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-10)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes) He retired from the Screen Actors Guild in 2000.

## Death

Orsatti died on August 31, 2010, of [pulmonary disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_disease "Pulmonary disease") at West Hills Hospital in [West Hills, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hills,_Los_Angeles "West Hills, Los Angeles"), aged 78.[&#8202;](#ref-back-latimes-11)[\[1\]](#refnote-latimes)
