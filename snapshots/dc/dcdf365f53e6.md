# El Salvador's textile and apparel sector will drive 26% of exports in the first quarter of 2026.

by [CAMTEX](https://en.camtex.com.sv/author/ecastanedaargosoftgroup-com/ "CAMTEX messages") \| Apr 30, 2026 \| [2026](https://en.camtex.com.sv/category/2026/) \| [Comments](https://en.camtex.com.sv/The-textile-and-clothing-sector-of-El-Salvador-will-drive-26%25-of-exports-in-the-first-quarter-of-2026./#respond)

![Textile and clothing industry of El Salvador](https://www.camtex.com.sv/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/C4AMRQJEVVHOLC3YPNNRGQCDAI.jpg)

_Exports from the Salvadoran textile and clothing industry totaled $438,68 million, consolidating its leadership as a key driver of economic growth during the beginning of the year._

El Salvador's textile and apparel sector is consolidating its position as one of the main drivers of the national economy in 2026. According to information released this Thursday by Camtex (Chamber of the Textile, Apparel, and Free Trade Zone Industry of El Salvador), exports from this sector reached US$438,68 million in the first quarter of the year, representing 26% of the country's total exports. This performance reflects a trend of resilience and competitiveness in strategic markets, with favorable prospects for the coming months.

According to a statement from Camtex, the United States remains the primary destination market for Salvadoran textiles and apparel. Exports to the US reached US$306,6 million between January and March, surpassing the US$304,7 million reported during the same period in 2025. This growth occurred within a challenging international context, influenced by adjustments in global demand.

In addition to the U.S. market, the industry has shown progress in Guatemala and Honduras, with exports increasing by 8% and 4%, respectively, during the first quarter of the year. These figures, emphasized by the trade association, demonstrate the sector's ability to diversify markets and capitalize on regional opportunities despite global challenges.

The full-package model has solidified its position as the primary export format, representing 76% of the sector's shipments and accounting for 85% of total exports. Camtex attributes this result to the specialization and added value that characterize the Salvadoran textile industry. Apparel manufacturing, as a sub-segment, has continued to increase its presence and market share since 2022, according to the organization's data.

![Textile and clothing industry of El Salvador](https://www.camtex.com.sv/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/UE6DG3ZEH5E6TCUH4AUQ7U5QQQ.jpg)

Regarding production linkages, the industry association emphasizes that it has begun to receive new investments and strategic expansions aimed at strengthening export capacity and job creation. The reciprocal trade agreement with the United States is identified as a way to attract international orders and strengthen regional value chains.

Patricia Figueroa, executive director of Camtex, explained that the sector has faced challenges similar to those of the region, although it maintains its competitiveness and relevance to the Salvadoran economy. “The most important thing is that today we are already seeing concrete signs of stability and opportunities in our main markets. The textile and apparel sector is better positioned to take advantage of a second half of the year more focused on added value,” the executive stated.

International benchmarks such as the National Retail Federation (NRF) maintain a positive outlook on demand in the United States during 2026. The NRF report states that low unemployment, improved wages, and higher tax refunds could benefit regional textile manufacturing.

Camtex states that El Salvador's textile and apparel industry will continue to play a central role in the national economy, with expectations of sustained growth and new business opportunities in key markets for the country.

It is worth mentioning that the collapse in exports of t-shirts and knitted shirts set the tone for foreign trade in the first quarter of 2026. This product, historically among the main drivers of foreign exchange for the country, reduced its sales abroad from USD 495,8 million in 2025 to just USD 112,9 million in the same period of 2026, which implies a decrease of USD 382,9 million.

The magnitude of the drop impacted the overall export balance and reflected a widespread trend in the textile sector. Sweaters, pullovers, and similar garments suffered a similar decline, falling from USD 393,2 million to USD 98,8 million, a decrease of USD 294,4 million. The sector, which in previous years led exports, is thus facing one of its worst recent contractions.
