Thirty-eighth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland

The **Thirty-eighth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland** is an [amendment to the Constitution of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Amendments to the Constitution of Ireland") which altered the provisions regulating [divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce "Divorce"). It removed the constitutional requirement for a defined period of separation before a Court may grant a dissolution of marriage, and eased restrictions on the recognition of foreign divorces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-doje29jan-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-doje29jan) The amendment was effected by an [act of the Oireachtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_the_Oireachtas "Act of the Oireachtas"), the **Thirty-eighth Amendment of the Constitution (Dissolution of Marriage) Act 2019** (introduced as [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_(law) "Bill (law)") no. 57 of 2016).

The bill as introduced did not propose the total deletion of a waiting period from the Constitution, merely a reduction in the required term. After amendments by the Oireachtas, the bill was put to a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendum "Referendum") on 24 May 2019, the same date as the [local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Irish_local_elections "2019 Irish local elections") and [European elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election_in_Ireland "2019 European Parliament election in Ireland").[&#8202;](#ref-back-doje26mar-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-doje26mar) The proposal was approved by 82% of voters.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) The bill was [signed into law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_into_law "Signed into law") on 11 June 2019 by [Michael D. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Higgins "Michael D. Higgins"), the [President of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ireland "President of Ireland").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thirty-eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

When the [Constitution of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ireland "Constitution of Ireland") was [adopted in 1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Adoption of the Constitution of Ireland"), divorce was prohibited by Article 41.3.2°. A [referendum held in 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_Bill_1986 "Tenth Amendment of the Constitution Bill 1986") to remove this prohibition was defeated. The prohibition was removed after a second [referendum held in 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifteenth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Fifteenth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland"), which was narrowly approved by 50.28% to 49.72%. The recognition of foreign divorce is regulated by Article 41.3.3°, which was unaffected by the 1995 referendum. The text of these two subsections prior to the 2019 amendment is set out below:[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)

> 2° A Court designated by law may grant a dissolution of marriage where, but only where, it is satisfied that –
> i. at the date of the institution of the proceedings, the spouses have lived apart from one another for a period of, or periods amounting to, at least four years during the previous five years,ii. there is no reasonable prospect of a reconciliation between the spouses,iii. such provision as the Court considers proper having regard to the circumstances exists or will be made for the spouses, any children of either or both of them and any other person prescribed by law, andiv. any further conditions prescribed by law are complied with.
>
> 3° No person whose marriage has been dissolved under the civil law of any other State but is a subsisting valid marriage under the law for the time being in force within the jurisdiction of the Government and Parliament established by this Constitution shall be capable of contracting a valid marriage within that jurisdiction during the lifetime of the other party to the marriage so dissolved.

Divorce is regulated in statute law by the Family Law (Divorce) Act 1996, passed after the 1995 amendment, which repeats conditions i, ii and iii of Article 41.3.2° verbatim.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6) Limited recognition of foreign divorce is provided by the Domicile and Recognition of Foreign Divorces Act 1986.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) In 2015, the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Ireland "Supreme Court of Ireland") called for reform of the 1986 Act.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

## Legislative history

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thirty-eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Legislative history")\]

The bill was [introduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_reading "First reading") in the [32nd Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_D%C3%A1il "32nd Dáil") as the _Thirty-fifth Amendment of the Constitution (Divorce) Bill 2016_, a [private member's bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_member's_bill "Private member's bill") by a [backbench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backbencher "Backbencher") [TD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_D%C3%A1la "Teachta Dála") from the [Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael "Fine Gael") party, [Josepha Madigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josepha_Madigan "Josepha Madigan"). It proposed to reduce the period of separation from four years to two years by the substitution of the following text for clause 41.3.2°(i) above:[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

> i. at the date of the institution of the proceedings, the spouses have lived separate and apart from one another for a period of, or periods amounting to, at least two years in the previous three years,

The [Fine Gael-led government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Government_of_Ireland "30th Government of Ireland") agreed to support the bill, thereby permitting it to go [second stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_(legislature) "Reading (legislature)") in the [Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann "Dáil Éireann") on 6 April 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dail20170406-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-dail20170406) The [committee stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_stage "Committee stage") was referred to the Select Committee On Justice And Equality, which considered it on 12 July 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) The [Independents 4 Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independents_4_Change "Independents 4 Change") TD, [Clare Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Daly "Clare Daly"), wanted the separation period removed from the Constitution and dealt with instead in statute law, by amending the Family Law (Divorce) Act 1996, as allowed under Article Article 41.3.2°(iv). Josepha Madigan said she was "not sure that the Irish people are completely ready for this provision to be taken out of the Constitution". The [Solidarity–People Before Profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity%E2%80%93People_Before_Profit "Solidarity–People Before Profit") TD, [Ruth Coppinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Coppinger "Ruth Coppinger"), proposed the specification of a maximum separation period that would be set by statute law, or a shorter one, or none. The Fine Gael TD, [David Stanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Stanton_(politician) "David Stanton (politician)"), the [Minister of State for Equality, Immigration and Integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_for_Equality,_Immigration_and_Integration "Minister of State for Equality, Immigration and Integration"), said the government would propose substantive amendments to the bill at [report stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Report_stage "Report stage"), which other deputies criticised as rendering the committee stage redundant. The bill passed committee stage unamended.

In November 2017 Madigan was appointed to the government as [Minister for Culture, Heritage and the Gaeltacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Culture,_Heritage_and_the_Gaeltacht "Minister for Culture, Heritage and the Gaeltacht"). Nevertheless, on the Dáil order paper the bill formally remained as a private member's bill rather than a government bill; bills of the former class of proceed more slowly than those of the latter, because less Dáil time is reserved for private members' business.

### Government amendments

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thirty-eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Government amendments")\]

In January 2019, the government announced that it would propose amendments to the bill at report stage to eliminate the waiting period and to regulate foreign divorce.[&#8202;](#ref-back-doje29jan-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-doje29jan) In March 2019, the government announced the wording for these amendments. It would propose to delete paragraph (i) of Article 41.3.2° and renumber subsequent paragraphs; and to substitute the following for subsection 3°:[&#8202;](#ref-back-doje26mar-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-doje26mar)

> 3° Provision may be made by law for the recognition under the law of the State of a dissolution of marriage granted under the civil law of another state.

The government also published the Draft of a General Scheme of the Family Law (Divorce) (Amendment) Bill 2019, which it said that it would enact if the referendum passed. The promised bill would amend Section 5(1) of Family Law (Divorce) Act 1996 by the substitution of “two years during the previous three years” for “four years during the previous five years”.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

Minister [Charles Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Flanagan "Charles Flanagan") proposed the amendments to the constitutional bill in the Dáil at report stage on 3 April 2019; they passed unopposed and the bill completed remaining Dáil stages.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailreportstage-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-dailreportstage) Flanagan also said that the issue of changing the law on foreign divorces within the terms of the revised Article 41.3.3° would be referred to the [Law Reform Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Reform_Commission_(Ireland) "Law Reform Commission (Ireland)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailreportstage-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-dailreportstage) The number of the amendment was also changed from 35 to 38, as amendment numbers [36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-sixth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland") and [37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-seventh_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-seventh Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland") had been enacted since the divorce bill was introduced.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailreportstage-2)[\[14\]](#refnote-dailreportstage)

The bill passed all stages in the [Seanad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seanad_%C3%89ireann "Seanad Éireann") on 11 April 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-seanad20190411-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-seanad20190411) The following day, [Eoghan Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eoghan_Murphy "Eoghan Murphy"), the [Minister for Housing, Local Government and Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Housing,_Planning_and_Local_Government "Minister for Housing, Planning and Local Government") set 24 May 2019 as the date for the referendum.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

## Referendum

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thirty-eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Referendum")\]

The bill was submitted to [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendum "Referendum") on 24 May 2019, the same day as the [European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election_in_Ireland "2019 European Parliament election in Ireland") and [local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Irish_local_elections "2019 Irish local elections"). It was the first time that a private member's bill was scheduled for a referendum. The "statement for the information of voters" that appeared on the ballot paper was approved by identical [resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_(law) "Resolution (law)") of the Dáil and Seanad passed immediately after the bill.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)[&#8202;](#ref-back-seanad20190411-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-seanad20190411)

The [Referendum Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendum_Commission "Referendum Commission") for the amendment referendum was established on 26 February 2019 with [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_(Ireland) "High Court (Ireland)") judge Tara Burns appointed as chair.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) Registration as an " [approved body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approved_body_(Irish_referendums) "Approved body (Irish referendums)")" entitled to campaign for or against the bill was open from 10 April to 25 April.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)

### Campaign

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thirty-eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Campaign")\]

The referendum was overshadowed by campaigning for the simultaneous local and European elections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) The lacklustre campaign was attributed to "campaign fatigue" by a campaigner on the "No" side, Seamas de Barra, who is treasurer for the Alliance for the Defence of the Family and Marriage:

> I think it's largely campaign fatigue from the abortion referendum.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

One argument in favour was that couples under the current law often obtain a [judicial separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_separation "Judicial separation") after two years apart and a divorce after four years, thus incurring two sets of legal costs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Shannon2019-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-Shannon2019)

Some commentators criticised the bundling into a single amendment bill of two distinct changes (removal of the four-year waiting period and recognition of foreign divorces) thus preventing voters from approving one while rejecting the other. An opinion poll taken on 7–9 May for _The Irish Times_ found 77% of respondents would vote Yes, 8% No, 11% unsure, and 4% would not vote. Among [likely voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likely_voters "Likely voters"), 91% favoured Yes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Leahy3888125-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-Leahy3888125)

The amendment was supported by [Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael "Fine Gael"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-dail20170406-1)[\[11\]](#refnote-dail20170406) [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_F%C3%A1il "Fianna Fáil"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-dail20170406-2)[\[11\]](#refnote-dail20170406) [Sinn Féin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinn_F%C3%A9in "Sinn (Ireland)"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26) [Solidarity–People Before Profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity%E2%80%93People_Before_Profit "Solidarity–People Before The stakes aren’t remotely as high as [the abortion debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Thirty-sixth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bray3893407-1)[\[34\]](#refnote-Bray3893407) [Denis Nulty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Nulty "Denis Nulty"), Catholic [Bishop of Kildare and Leighlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Kildare_and_Leighlin "Roman Catholic Diocese of Kildare and Leighlin") and chair of the Council for Marriage & Family of the [Irish Catholic Bishops' Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Bishops'_Conference "Irish Catholic Bishops' Conference"), called on voters to "reflect deeply" and said the amendment's objective was "liberalising divorce rather than supporting marriage".[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)

## Legislation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thirty-eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Legislation")\]

The amendment [came into force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Came_into_force "Came into force") when the amending act was [signed into law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_into_law "Signed into law") on 11 June 2019 by [Michael D. Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Higgins "Michael D. Higgins"), the [President of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ireland "President of Ireland").[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)

The concomitant Family Law Bill 2019 was [published and introduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_reading "First reading") in the Dáil on 9 October 2019; among the changes to divorce laws which it proposed were those newly permitted by the constitutional amendment, namely a reduction in the separation period from four to two years, and recognition of divorces granted in the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") and, post [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit"), [in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce_in_the_United_Kingdom_(disambiguation) "Divorce in the United Kingdom (disambiguation)") and [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar "Gibraltar").[&#8202;](#ref-back-bill78of2019-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-bill78of2019) The bill's [second reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_reading "Second reading") in the Dáil was moved by [David Stanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Stanton_(politician) "David Stanton (politician)") on 16 October,[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39) and it passed remaining Dáil stages that day without opposition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40) On 23 October the Seanad passed the bill unamended,[&#8202;](#ref-back-bill78of2019-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-bill78of2019) and then passed a motion for its early signature by the President,[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41) who accordingly signed it into law on 25 October as the **Family Law Act 2019**.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42) The sections reducing the waiting time came into force on 1 December 2019;[&#8202;](#ref-back-isbc2019_37-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-isbc2019_37)as of 10July2020[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thirty-eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland&action=edit) the sections recognising foreign divorce have not been commenced.[&#8202;](#ref-back-isbc2019_37-1)[\[43\]](#refnote-isbc2019_37)
