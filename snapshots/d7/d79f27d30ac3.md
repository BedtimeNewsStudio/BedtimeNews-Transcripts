## General Information

**At Stake in this Election:**

- Candidacy in the Argentinian general election of 22 October 2023

Argentina holds mandatory primary elections for all directly elected national positions. These elections are known as [PASO](https://www.electoral.gob.ar/nuevo/paginas/btn/pf.php) s (primary, open, simultaneous, and obligatory). Party lists and pre-candidates must receive at least [1.5%](https://www.electoral.gob.ar/nuevo_legislacion/pdf/26571.pdf) of the vote (at the constituency or national level depending on the office) to advance to the general election. Elections for the governor of Santa Cruz and the head of government of Buenos Aires will occur on the [same day](https://www.electoral.gob.ar/nuevo/paginas/pdf/MAPA%20PROV%2027%20de%20junio.pdf) as the primary election.

**[Government Structure:](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/argentina/#government)**

- Chief of State: President Alberto Ángel FERNÁNDEZ (since 10 December 2019)

- Head of Government: President Alberto Ángel FERNÁNDEZ (since 10 December 2019)

- Assembly: bicameral National Congress ( _Congreso Nacional_) with a 72-seat Senate ( _Senado_) and a 257-seat Chamber of Deputies ( _Cámara de Diputados_)

**[Electoral System:](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/argentina/#government)**

- The President is directly elected by qualified plurality vote in up to two rounds. Candidates must receive either 45% of the overall vote or 40% of the vote and a 10-point lead to be elected in the first round. If those conditions are not met, the top two candidates advance to the second round, which is decided by absolute majority vote. The President may serve up to two 4-year terms.

- In the Senate, all 72 members are directly elected from multi-seat constituencies. Each constituency (province) awards 2 seats to the highest-polling party and 1 seat to the second-place party. All members serve 6-year terms, and one-third of seats are up for election every 2 years.

- In the Chamber of Deputies, all 257 members are directly elected by party-list proportional representation vote from multi-seat constituencies. Members serve 4-year terms, and half the seats are up for election every 2 years.

**[Last Election:](https://electionguide.org/elections/id/3164/)**

In 2019, Alberto FERNÁNDEZ (Everyone’s Front) won the presidency in one round with 48.10% of the vote, defeating Mauricio MACRI (Together for Change) and Roberto LAVAGNA (Federal Consensus). Voter turnout was 79%. The most recent national primary elections occurred in [2021](https://old.pjn.gov.ar/cne/secelec/secciones/resultados/info.php?eID=503&dID=2&GP=1), when only Senate and Chamber of Deputy seats were up for election.
