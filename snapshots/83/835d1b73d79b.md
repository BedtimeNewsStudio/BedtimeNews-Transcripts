The **Sahel region** ([/səˈhɛl/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language")ساحل __sāḥil__[\[ˈsaːħil\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Arabic "Help:IPA/Arabic"), "coast, shore"), or **Sahelian acacia savanna**, is a biogeographical region in Africa. It is the [transition zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotone "Ecotone") between the more humid [Sudanian savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanian_savanna "Sudanian savanna") to its south and the drier [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara "Sahara") to the north. The Sahel has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Hot_semi-arid_climates "Semi-arid climate") and stretches across the southernmost latitudes of [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa") between the Atlantic Ocean and the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea"). Although geographically located in the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics "Tropics"), the Sahel does not have a [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate "Tropical climate").

The Sahel has one of the world's fastest-growing populations due to its high [human birthrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_birthrate "List of countries by birthrate"), with approximately 80 million people in 2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) The western Sahel in particular has experienced frequent food and water shortages due to [droughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts_in_the_Sahel "Droughts in the Sahel") caused by its [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate "Semi-arid climate"), as well as high levels of [government corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index "Corruption Perceptions Index"). Since the 2010s, various coups, [insurgencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_the_Sahel "War in the Sahel"), terrorist incidents, and foreign interventions have taken place across the Sahel and [Françafrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7afrique "Françafrique"), leading to the region being called the [Coup Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_Belt "Coup Belt").[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) As a result, the Sahel is viewed as both an ecoregion and a geopolitical space shaped by internal instability and external strategic competition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)

## Etymology

The term is borrowed from the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language") name for the region, الساحل __al-sāḥil__. __Sāḥil__ literally means "coast, shore",[&#8202;](#ref-back-OxfordDict-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-OxfordDict) which has been explained as a figurative reference to the southern edge of the vast [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara "Sahara").[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)

## Geography

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Sahel_forest_near_Kayes_Mali.jpg/500px-Sahel_forest_near_Kayes_Mali.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sahel_forest_near_Kayes_Mali.jpg) The lush green of the Sahelian acacia savanna during the [rainy summer season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Hot_semi-arid_climates "Semi-arid climate") in [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali"). Note the large [baobab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adansonia_digitata "Adansonia digitata") amongst the acacia.[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Azawakh_52_jd.jpg/500px-Azawakh_52_jd.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Azawakh_52_jd.jpg) Herders with livestock and [azawakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawakh "Azawakh") dogs in the Sahel

The Sahel spans 5,900km (3,670mi) from the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") in the west to the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea") in the east, in a belt several hundred to a thousand kilometers (c. 600 miles) wide. It covers an area of 3,053,200 square kilometers (1,178,850sqmi).

Representing a climatic and ecological [transition zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotone "Ecotone") with [hot semi-desert and steppe conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Hot_semi-arid_climates "Semi-arid climate"), the region borders the [more humid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate "Tropical savanna climate") [Sudanian savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanian_savanna "Sudanian savanna") to its south and the [dry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate "Desert climate") Sahara desert to the north. This [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion#Terrestrial "Ecoregion") is also called the Sahelian [acacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia "Acacia") savanna in honour of its most prominent and very drought tolerant genus of tree.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wwf-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-wwf)

The topography is mainly flat; most of the region lies between 200 and 400 meters (660 and 1,310ft) in elevation. Isolated plateaus and mountain ranges include the [Marrah Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrah_Mountains "Marrah Mountains"), [Aïr Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFr_Mountains "Aïr Mountains"), and [Ennedi Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennedi_Plateau "Ennedi Plateau"), which are designated as separate ecoregions because their flora and fauna are distinct from the surrounding lowlands (e.g. [East Saharan woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Saharan_montane_xeric_woodlands "East Saharan montane xeric woodlands")). Annual rainfall varies from around 100–200mm (4–8in) in the north to around 700–1,000mm (28–39in) in the south.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wwf-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-wwf)

### Flora and fauna

The Sahel is mostly covered in [grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland "Grassland") and [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna "Savanna"), with areas of [woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland "Woodland") and [shrubland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrubland "Shrubland"). Grass cover is fairly continuous across the region, dominated by annual grass species such as _[Cenchrus biflorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenchrus_biflorus "Cenchrus biflorus"), [Schoenefeldia gracilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoenefeldia_gracilis "Schoenefeldia gracilis")_ and _Aristida stipoides_. Species of acacia are the dominant trees, with _[Acacia tortilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia_tortilis "Acacia tortilis")_ as the most common one. In the north, areas of desert shrub, including _[Panicum turgidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panicum_turgidum "Panicum turgidum")_ and _Aristida sieberiana_, alternate with areas of grassland and savanna. During the long dry season, many trees lose their leaves and the predominantly annual grasses die.

The Sahel was formerly home to large populations of grazing mammals, before the larger species were greatly reduced due to over-hunting and competition with livestock. Several species are [vulnerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerable_species "Vulnerable species") (Dorcas gazelle, cheetah, lion and red-fronted gazelle), [endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered_species "Endangered species") ( [Dama gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dama_gazelle "Dama gazelle") and [African wild dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_wild_dog "African wild dog")), or [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction "Extinction") (the Scimitar-horned oryx is probably [extinct in the wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct_in_the_wild "Extinct in the wild"), and both _Pelorovis_ and the Bubal hartebeest are extinct).

The seasonal wetlands are important for migratory birds moving within Africa and on the African-Eurasian [flyways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyway "Flyway").[&#8202;](#ref-back-wwf-2)[\[9\]](#refnote-wwf)

### Climate

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Acacia_Trees_%2824227057806%29.jpg/500px-Acacia_Trees_%2824227057806%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Acacia_Trees_(24227057806).jpg) [Ennedi Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennedi_Plateau "Ennedi Plateau") is located at the border of the Sahara and the Sahel[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Seasonal_westafrica_rainfall_mean_20070501_20070930.gif/500px-Seasonal_westafrica_rainfall_mean_20070501_20070930.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Seasonal_westafrica_rainfall_mean_20070501_20070930.gif) 10 year average precipitation during the summer rainy season (May–September) in the Sahel and adjacent regions

The Sahel has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate "Hot semi-arid climate") ( [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification") _BSh_). The climate is typically hot, sunny, dry and somewhat windy all year long. The climate is similar to, but less extreme than, the climate of the Sahara desert located just to the north.

The Sahel receives a low to very low amount of precipitation annually. The [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe "Steppe") has a very long, prevailing dry season and a short rainy season. The precipitation is also extremely irregular and varies considerably from season to season. Most of the rain usually falls during four to six months in the middle of the year, while the other months may remain absolutely dry. The interior generally receives between 200mm and 700mm of rain yearly.

A system of subdivisions often adopted for the climate based on annual rainfall is as follows: the Saharan-Sahelian climate, with mean annual precipitation between around 100 and 200mm (such as [Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum "Khartoum"), Sudan); the strict Sahelian climate, with mean annual precipitation between around 200 and 700mm (such as [Niamey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niamey "Niamey"), Niger); and the Sahelian-Sudanese climate, with mean annual precipitation between around 700 and 900mm (such as [Ouagadougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouagadougou "Ouagadougou"), Burkina Faso).

The relative humidity is low to very low, often between 10% and 25% during the dry season and between 25% and 75% during the rainy season. The least humid places have a relative humidity under 35%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) Annual rain levels were measured to fall between 20 and 40% in a span of two decades from 1931 to 1960 and 1968–1990; the levels recovered slightly in the mid-1990s in some areas, especially in central and eastern areas as far as Senegal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKhatim_KherrazNabil_Ben_Khatra20198-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKhatim_KherrazNabil_Ben_Khatra20198)

The Sahel is characterized by constant, intense heat, with an unvarying temperature. The Sahel rarely experiences cold temperatures. During the hottest period, the average high temperatures are generally between 36 and 42°C (97 and 108°F) (and even more in the hottest regions), often for more than three months, while the average low temperatures are around 25 to 31°C (77 to 88°F). During the cooler period, the average high temperatures are between 27 and 33°C (81 and 91°F) and the average low temperatures are between 15 and 21°C (59 and 70°F).[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) Everywhere in the Sahel, the average mean temperature is over 18°C (64°F).

The Sahel has very high [sunshine duration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_duration "Sunshine duration") year-round, approaching desert levels of between 2,400 hours (about 55% of the daylight hours) and 3,600 hours (more than 80% of the daylight hours). The [cloud cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_cover "Cloud cover") is low. [Niamey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niamey "Niamey") has 3,082 hours of sunshine; [Gao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao "Gao") has 3,385 hours; [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu "Timbuktu") 3,409 hours; and [N'Djamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Djamena "N'Djamena") 3,205 hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

### Recent droughts

Further information: [Droughts in the Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts_in_the_Sahel "Droughts in the Sahel")

For hundreds of years, the Sahel region has experienced frequent droughts and [megadroughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadrought "Megadrought"). One megadrought lasted 250 years from 1450 to 1700.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) There was a major drought in 1914, leading to large-scale famine. From 1951 to 2004, the Sahel experienced some of the most consistent and severe droughts in Africa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18) The 1960s saw a large increase in rainfall in the region, making the northern drier region more accessible. There was a push, supported by governments, for people to move northwards. When the long drought period from 1968 through 1974 began, grazing quickly became unsustainable and large-scale denuding of the terrain followed. Like the drought in 1914, this led to a large-scale famine, but this time somewhat tempered by international visibility and an outpouring of aid. This catastrophe led to the founding of the [International Fund for Agricultural Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fund_for_Agricultural_Development "International Fund for Agricultural Development").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

#### 2010 drought

Main article: [2010 Sahel famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Sahel_famine "2010 Sahel famine")

Between June and August 2010, famine struck the Sahel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) Niger's crops failed to mature in the heat, 350,000 faced starvation, and 1,200,000 were at risk of famine.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) In Chad the temperature reached 47.6°C (117.7°F) on 22 June in [Faya-Largeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faya-Largeau "Faya-Largeau"), breaking a record set in 1961. Niger tied its highest temperature record set in 1998, also on 22 June, at 47.1°C in [Bilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilma "Bilma"). That record was broken the next day, when Bilma hit 48.2°C (118.8°F). The hottest temperature recorded in Sudan was reached on 25 June, at 49.6°C (121.3°F) in [Dongola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongola "Dongola"), breaking a record set in 1987.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MastersNOAA-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-MastersNOAA) Niger reported on 14 July that diarrhoea, starvation, gastroenteritis, malnutrition and respiratory diseases had sickened or killed many children. The military junta appealed for international food aid and took serious steps to call on overseas help.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) On 26 July, the heat reached near-record levels over Chad and Niger,[&#8202;](#ref-back-underground.com-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-underground.com) and in northern Niger about 20 people reportedly died of dehydration by 27 July.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Desertification and soil loss

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c1/Camels_in_Chad.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Camels_in_Chad.png) [Camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel "Camel") at a [watering hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watering_hole "Watering hole") in the [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Hot_semi-arid_climates "Semi-arid climate") Sahel in [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad "Chad")

The Sahel region faces environmental issues that are contributing to global warming. If the change in climate is not slowed down and [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification_in_Africa "Desertification in Africa") possibly reversed through sustainable practices and any form of [reforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reforestation "Reforestation"), it is only a matter of time before countries like Niger lose their entire landmass to desert due to unchecked unsustainable human practices.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ReferenceA-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-ReferenceA):9[Over-farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overexploitation "Overexploitation"), over-grazing, [over-population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-population "Over-population") of marginal lands, and natural [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion "Soil erosion") have caused serious desertification of the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26) This has affected shelter construction, making it necessary to change the used materials. The Woodless Construction project was introduced in Sahel in 1980 by the Development Workshop, achieving since then a high social impact in the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27) A major initiative to combat desertification in the Sahel region via reforestation and other interventions is the [Great Green Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Green_Wall_(Africa) "Great Green Wall (Africa)").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Village_Telly_in_Mali.jpg/500px-Village_Telly_in_Mali.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Village_Telly_in_Mali.jpg) Sahel region of [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali")

Major [dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm "Dust storm") are a frequent occurrence. During November 2004 several such storms hit Chad, originating in the [Bodélé Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bod%C3%A9l%C3%A9_Depression "Bodélé Depression").[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) This is a common area for dust storms, occurring on average on 100 days every year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29) On 23 March 2010, a major sandstorm hit Mauritania, Senegal, [The Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gambia "The Gambia"), [Guinea-Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau "Guinea-Bissau"), [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea "Guinea"), and inland [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone"). Another struck in southern [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria "Algeria"), inland Mauritania, Mali and northern [Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Coast "Ivory Coast")[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30) at the same time.

Following the drought period of the 1970s and 1980, however, the Sahel began to experience increased rainfall.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31) This may be due to global warming, which can cause stronger monsoons, in turn caused by a warmer Atlantic Ocean.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32) Warming of the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea") may also be a factor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33)
Groundwater levels are also rising, even more than can be accounted for just from the increased rainfall, according to some hydrologists. Other possible contributing factors include the [Great Green Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Green_Wall_(Africa) "Great Green Wall (Africa)"), the revival of traditional water harvesting methods and the abandonment of irrigation projects.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34)

### Protected areas

Protected areas include [Ferlo Nord Wildlife Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferlo_Nord_Wildlife_Reserve "Ferlo Nord Wildlife Reserve") in Senegal, [Sylvo-Pastoral and Partial Faunal Reserve of the Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvo-Pastoral_and_Partial_Faunal_Reserve_of_the_Sahel "Sylvo-Pastoral and Partial Faunal Reserve of the Sahel") in Burkina Faso, Ansonga-Ménake Faunal Reserve in Mali, [Tadres Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadres_Reserve "Tadres Reserve") in Niger, and [Waza National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waza_National_Park "Waza National Park") in Cameroon.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)

Traditionally, most of the people have been semi- [nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad "Nomad"), farming and raising livestock in a system of [transhumance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transhumance "Transhumance"). The difference between the dry north with higher levels of soil nutrients and the wetter south with more vegetation is exploited by having the herds graze on high-quality feed in the north during the wet season, and trek several hundred kilometers to the south to graze on more abundant but less nutritious feed during the dry period.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] In the western Sahel, polygamy and child marriage are common.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36) Female genital mutilation is also practiced across the Sahel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bixby.berkeley.edu-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-bixby.berkeley.edu)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)

### Music

[Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") historians and historians of [African-American music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_music "African-American music"), such as [Paul Oliver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Oliver "Paul Oliver") and [Samuel Charters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Charters "Samuel Charters"), have suggested that the essential elements of the blues originated in the Sahel, brought over by Africans via the slave trade.[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39) The slaves brought to South America and the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") were largely from percussion-based musical cultures in southern, coastal West Africa (like southern Nigeria), as well as [Central African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Africa "Central Africa") and [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages "Bantu languages")-speaking parts of Africa, whose musical cultures lacked many elements that would become characteristic of the blues.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40) In contrast, many of the slaves brought to North America were from the Sahel region and much more familiar with stringed instruments; the [banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo "Banjo") evolved from Sahelian string instruments such as the [akonting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akonting "Akonting"). Charters found that many Sahelian slaves were from Muslim cultures, favouring stringed, melodic, and solo [melismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melisma "Melisma") singing, which differed from the drum-based music of other African regions, who generally favoured drumming and group chants. These traditions—which were sometimes permitted by plantation owners who feared drums—became tools of rebellion, evolving into blues.

Historian [Sylviane Diouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylviane_Diouf "Sylviane Diouf") and [ethnomusicologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnomusicology "Ethnomusicology") [Gerhard Kubik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Kubik "Gerhard Kubik") identify [Islamic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_music "Islamic music") as an influence on blues music.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Curiel-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-Curiel)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tottoli-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-Tottoli) Diouf notes a striking resemblance between the [Islamic call to prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhan "Adhan") (originating from [Bilal ibn Rabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilal_ibn_Rabah "Bilal ibn Rabah"), a famous [Abyssinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian_people "Abyssinian people") [African Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Africa "Islam in Africa") in the early 7th century) and 19th-century [field holler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_holler "Field holler") music, noting that both have similar lyrics praising God, melody, note changes, "words that seem to quiver and shake" in the vocal chords, dramatic changes in [musical scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_scale "Musical scale"), and nasal [intonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intonation_(music) "Intonation (music)"). She attributes the origins of field holler music to [African Muslim slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_Muslims "African-American Muslims") who accounted for an estimated 30% of African slaves in America. According to Kubik, "the vocal style of many blues singers using melisma, wavy intonation, and so forth is a heritage of that large region of The Western Sahel that had been in contact with the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world "Islamic world") via the Maghreb since the seventh and eighth centuries."[&#8202;](#ref-back-Curiel-1)[\[41\]](#refnote-Curiel)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tottoli-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-Tottoli) There was particularly a significant trans-Saharan cross-fertilization between the musical traditions of the Maghreb and the Sahel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tottoli-2)[\[42\]](#refnote-Tottoli)

The [blue note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_note "Blue note")—a hallmark of blues music and [rhythm and blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues") characterised by flattened thirds, fifths, or sevenths—has roots in the musical traditions of the Sahel,[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43) and so genres descending from blues have a strong Sahelian influence. In contrast, the more percussion-based [Afro-Brazilian music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Brazilian_music "Afro-Brazilian music") and [Afro-Cuban music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Cuban_music "Afro-Cuban music") have more of a southern, coastal West African, Central African, and Bantu influence, where the blue note is absent; this originated from non-Muslim slaves, who generally favoured drums and group chants. The [griot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griot "Griot") tradition of the Sahel also may have influenced [talking blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_blues "Talking blues"), and by extension, [hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop "Hip hop").[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)

### Ethnic groups

Sahel is home to diverse ethnic groups like the [Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_people "Fula people"), [Tuareg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_people "Tuareg people"), [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_people "Hausa people"), [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_people "Dogon people"), [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people "Songhai people"), [Mande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mand%C3%A9_peoples "Mandé peoples"), [Kanuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people "Kanuri people"), and [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs "Arabs"). Arabs are dominant eastern and western Sahel, notably in [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan") and [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania "Mauritania"). Many Sahelian tribes have strong historical connections with Arab communities, fostered by trans-Saharan trade routes and the spread of Islam. These ties have influenced language, religion and social customs, creating a unique blend of cultures in the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45)

## Language

French is spoken widely, as many of its nations are former French colonies, with two adopting French as an official language and many more using it colloquially.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46)[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47) French is a remnant of colonial history and a foreign import to a region characterized by linguistic diversity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48) After establishing French culture in northern Senegal in the mid-19th century, colonial governors like General [Louis Faidherbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Faidherbe "Louis Faidherbe") pushed deeper into the Sahel's interior, facing opposition from regional leaders. Despite resistance, territory was accrued and placed under the control of lieutenant governors who extracted resources and labor from local populations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49) The greater Sahel was subsequently organized into the massive territory of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa "French West Africa") in 1895, noted for its linguistic diversity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50)

French has been the official language of most Sahel countries at various points in their history, but the trend of removing its official status has gained momentum since the end of [French military intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barkhane "Operation Barkhane") in the region in 2022. Since then, the governments of Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger have stripped French from being an official language, punctuating a broader theme of the Sahel's self-isolation from Paris.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51) Although there is evidence of its use in nearly every Sahel country, research suggests that French is more often a _[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca")_ of business among the elite and educated classes than a conduit of practical everyday dialogue.[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-52)

West African and Sahel loanwords have entered the lexicon of modern standard French, usually in the context of vernacular or slang elocution. This trend is mostly understood by the wave of African migrants to France since the end of the colonial era, but has intensified since the explosion of the youth population of Africa. Sahel loanwords are challenging the historic rigidity of the French language and its corresponding cultural norms.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-53) [Code-switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-switching "Code-switching") and linguistic blending is common among French speakers in the Sahel, like other regions known for their linguistic diversity. Loanwords and phonetic irregularities derived from local languages have permeated Sahel French:

- Overt influence of English and even Chinese words in Burkinabè French, such as _enjoy_ "take pleasure/enjoy" and _chao_/ _mao_ "old."
- _Ligidi_ is a [Mooré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moor%C3%A9 "Mooré") loanword meaning "money."[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-:2)
- _C' nekh_ comes from the French expression _c'est bon_ but replaces _bon_ with the [Wolof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolof_language "Wolof language") word _nekh_, meaning "good" or "pleasant."
- The phoneme /y/ is pronounced more closely to /i/ by most French locutors in the Western Sahel.
- The apical trill pronunciation of /r/, once common in metropolitan France, continues in West Africa despite the more common use of the uvular trill pronunciation by standard French speakers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-55)
- Less phonetic variation exists between the vowels /ø/, /ə/, and /e/ among French speakers in Burkina Faso and Niger; /e/ characterizes the majority of these pronunciations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[54\]](#refnote-:2)

## History

### Early agriculture

Around 4000 BC, the climate of the Sahara and the Sahel started to become drier at an exceedingly fast pace. This climate change caused lakes and rivers to shrink significantly and caused increasing [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification "Desertification"). This in turn decreased the amount of land conducive to settlements and caused migrations of farming communities to the more humid climate of West Africa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-56)

### Sahelian kingdoms

Further information: [Trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade "Trans-Saharan trade") and [Islamization of the Sudan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization_of_the_Sudan_region "Islamization of the Sudan region")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Geschichte_des_Kost%C3%BCms_%281905%29_%2814580574910%29.jpg/500px-Geschichte_des_Kost%C3%BCms_%281905%29_%2814580574910%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Geschichte_des_Kost%C3%BCms_(1905)_(14580574910).jpg) 1905 depiction of ethnic groups in the Sahel

The larger [Sahelian kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelian_kingdoms "Sahelian kingdoms") emerged from 750 AD and founded several large cities in the [Niger valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_River "Niger River") region, including [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu "Timbuktu"), [Gao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao "Gao") and [Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9 "Djenné").[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-57)

The kingdom of [Alodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alodia "Alodia") was a Christian Nubian Kingdom in the eastern Sahel that reached its zenith from the 9th to 12th century. It was first mentioned in 569, its capital [Soba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soba_(city) "Soba (city)") located near modern-day [Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum "Khartoum"). Soba was described as a city of "extensive dwellings and churches full of gold and gardens".[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEZarroug199120-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEZarroug199120) With the [forest-savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest-savanna_mosaic "Forest-savanna mosaic") to their south, the Sahelian states were hindered from expanding into the north Akan state of the [Bono state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono_state "Bono state") and [Yoruba peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people "Yoruba people"), as mounted warriors were ineffective in the forests. In addition, the horses and camels were susceptible to the humidity and diseases of the tropics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-59)

### Colonial period

The western Sahel fell to France in the late 19th century as part of [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa "French West Africa").[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-60) Chad was added in 1900 as part of [French Equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Equatorial_Africa "French Equatorial Africa"). The French territories in the Sahel were [decolonised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa "Decolonisation of Africa") in 1960.[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-61) Eastern Sahel did not fall to the European powers but to the [Khedivate of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khedivate_of_Egypt "Khedivate of Egypt") when it was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turco-Egyptian_conquest_of_Sudan_(1820%E2%80%931824) "Turco-Egyptian conquest of Sudan (1820–1824)") by [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt "Muhammad Ali of Egypt") in the 1820s. By 1899 it came under [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan "Anglo-Egyptian Sudan") until [granted independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Sudan_(1956%E2%80%931969) "Republic of Sudan (1956–1969)") at [Egypt's behest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Egyptian_revolution#Post-War_period "1952 Egyptian revolution") in 1956.[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-62)[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-63)[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64)

## Instability and violence

### Terrorism

See also: [List of Islamist terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Islamist_terrorist_attacks "List of Islamist terrorist attacks")

According to _[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")_, in recent years the Sahel has become the epicenter of terrorist violence, contributing to 35% of all global deaths from terrorism by 2021, with [Jama'at Nasr al-Islam wal Muslimin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jama'at_Nasr_al-Islam_wal_Muslimin "Jama'at Nasr al-Islam wal Muslimin"), an al-Qaeda-affiliated group, identified as the world's fastest-growing terrorist organization.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-:0) In 2023, fatalities from conflict in the central Sahel rose by 38%, according to data from the research organization Armed Conflict Location and Event Data Project.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-:1)

In the wake of the [Libyan Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Crisis_(2011%E2%80%93present) "Libyan Crisis (2011–present)") beginning in 2011,[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67) terrorist organizations operating in the Sahel—including [Boko Haram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boko_Haram "Boko Haram"), [Islamic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State "Islamic State") and [al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb "Al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb") (AQIM)—have greatly exacerbated the violence, extremism and instability of the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-68)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Csis-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-Csis) In March 2020, the United States sent a special envoy to combat the rising violence from terrorist groups.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-70) The Catholic charity [Aid to the Church in Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_to_the_Church_in_Need "Aid to the Church in Need") has highlighted the fact that the Sahel has become one of the most dangerous regions in the world for Christians.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-71) As of 2024, a wave of military juntas, favoring Russian mercenaries over Western forces and UN peacekeepers, has intensified violence. This led Mauritania and Chad to disband the [G5 Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G5_Sahel "G5 Sahel"), an anti-terrorism alliance, after the military regimes in Burkina Faso, Niger, and Mali withdrew.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[66\]](#refnote-:1)

According to the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC"), the Sahel region has become the global epicenter of terrorism, accounting for over half of all terrorism-related deaths, according to the [Global Terrorism Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Terrorism_Index "Global Terrorism Index"). In 2023, the region recorded 3,885 fatalities out of a global total of 7,555, marking a nearly tenfold increase since 2019. The surge in extremist violence is attributed to the expansion of groups like the Islamic State's affiliate in the Sahel and Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal Muslimeen (JNIM), who compete for land and influence while imposing strict sharia-based governance. Political instability, weak governance, and the rise of military juntas following coups in Mali, Burkina Faso, Guinea, and Niger have further fueled the insurgency. These groups sustain their operations through ransom kidnappings, illicit gold mining, and drug trafficking, with the Sahel a major route for cocaine smuggling from South America to Europe. Meanwhile, governments in the region have shifted their alliances from Western nations to Russia and China, relying on paramilitary groups like the [Africa Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Corps_(Russia) "Africa Corps (Russia)") for security assistance, though with limited success. The violence is increasingly spilling into neighboring countries such as Togo and Benin, raising concerns about the broader destabilization of West Africa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-72)

### Human rights issues and political instability

In 2020 the United States raised concerns over growing number of allegations of human rights violations and abuses by state security forces in Sahel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-73) The US response came after [Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch "Human Rights Watch") released documents regarding the same.[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-74) Reports in March 2022 show militants are expanding and spreading out south of the Sahel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-75)

### Other challenges

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Caravane_des_nomades_10.jpg/500px-Caravane_des_nomades_10.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Caravane_des_nomades_10.jpg) Movement of nomads in Chad

The violent [herder–farmer conflicts in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herder%E2%80%93farmer_conflicts_in_Nigeria "Herder–farmer conflicts in Nigeria"), [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogossagou_massacre "Ogossagou massacre"), [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_nomadic_conflicts "Sudanese nomadic conflicts") and other countries in the Sahel have been exacerbated by climate change, land degradation, and rapid population growth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-76)[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-77)[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-78) Droughts and food shortages have been linked to the [Mali War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_War "Mali War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-79)[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-80)

The Sahel is experiencing more severe weather due to climate change, exemplified by the extreme heatwave of March–April 2024 in Burkina Faso and Mali.[&#8202;](#ref-back-81-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-81) This event was intensified by a 1.2°C global temperature increase from human activities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-82)
