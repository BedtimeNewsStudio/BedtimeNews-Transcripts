Help? Here’s where I’m struggling with this: 1) Consumption Paradox: If 50% of white-collar roles vanish in five years, we create a deflationary economic collapse where nobody has the capital to buy the very products AI is making. 2) The Resource Issue: Your "exponential" growth curve ignores realities like thermodynamics. Humans will not prioritize cooling a data center over irrigating crops or cooling a city as climate pressures make water and power sticky resources. Not to mention growing “safe” land scarcity. 3) The Societal Ceiling: The moment AI-driven instability threatens the tax base or social order, we’ll see governments roll out anti-AI stabilization measures (robot taxes, resource nationalism...) 4) The Myth of Agency: What you’re calling "taste" is actually just statistical consensus. AI can guess the best choice based on what’s been done, but it lacks the wealth of factors needed create something net new. 5) Infrastructure Bottleneck: AI needs more than code to evolve; it needs hardware and electrical grids. We are already facing the physical inability to pull the amount of gigawatts needed from the grid — and the final ceiling is visible — forcing plateau in model scaling.

So many comments and yet most are limited to talking about how good/bad AI is for jobs/economy/industry/… let’s take a step back, shall we? How sustainable is it to keep up this breakneck development of AI? And what’s an acceptable cost: How many ecosystems and what percentage of finite resources, and thus, how many lives—human and otherwise—are we willing to pay for A.I. to get better? This isn’t doom and gloom. It’s fact. Climate change is here. There is ample evidence and then some. It’s already reshaping our planet and poses an existential threat to humans, and A.I. development is only making things worse by consuming resources equivalent to entire countries. AI may be able to do a lot of things quicker and better, but at the end of the day, all of us are still going to need clean air, water and healthy ecosystems to survive as a species. And when it comes to efficiency vs survival, what would a human choose? What would A.I.? Does an intelligent species adapt to its own shiny inventions or for its long-term survival? Sometimes, the question isn’t: what’s possible? The question is: what is necessary?
