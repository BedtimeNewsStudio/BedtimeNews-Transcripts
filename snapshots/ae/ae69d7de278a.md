Belarusian Premier League

The **Belarusian Premier League** ( [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language "Belarusian language"): Вышэйшая ліга, _Vyšejšaja Liha_ or _Vysheyshaya Liga_; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Высшая лига; "Top League"), also called the **BETERA Premier League** for sponsorship reasons, is a professional [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football "Association football") league in [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus "Belarus") and the highest level of the [Belarusian football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_football_league_system "Belarusian football league system"). It is organized by the [Belarusian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Football_Federation "Belarusian Football Federation"). The number of teams in the competition has varied over the years from as high as 17 (1992–93 season) to as low as 11 (2012). As of 2025,[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Belarusian_Premier_League&action=edit) the league included 16 teams. Each team plays every other team twice during the course of the season. At the end of the season, the two teams with the fewest points are automatically relegated to the [Belarusian First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_First_League "Belarusian First League"), while the third worst team plays a promotion-relegation playoff against the third best team from the second tier. The top two teams from the Belarusian First League automatically win promotion to the Premier League. [Maxline Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Maxline_Vitebsk "FC Maxline Vitebsk") are the current champions, after winning their first championship title in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Belarusian_Premier_League "2025 Belarusian Premier League").

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Belarusian_Premier_League&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

The Belarusian Premier League was organized in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_in_association_football "1992 in association football"). The first participants were: [Dinamo Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dinamo_Minsk "FC Dinamo Minsk"), the only Belarusian side in the former [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League "Soviet Top League"), five teams from the lower tiers of the Soviet league system and represented other five regional centers of Belarus, and ten teams who were previous competitors in the [Belarusian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic "Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic") First League.

After the league creation, it was decided to change its schedule from a Soviet-style summer season to a European-style winter season. In 1995, the winter season experiment was proven unsuccessful due to poor weather and field conditions in Belarus in the late autumn and early spring. The season was changed back to summer. Every season since 1996 has been played in the summer. Throughout the 2000s, the number of competing teams has changed several times. 2012 season was played with only 11 teams due to last minute withdrawal of [Partizan Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Partizan_Minsk "FC Partizan Minsk").

In its earliest years, the league was dominated by [Dinamo Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dinamo_Minsk "FC Dinamo Minsk"), who won the league five times in a row between 1992 and 1995. During the next ten seasons, seven different teams finished as champions: [Slavia Mozyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Slavia_Mozyr "FC Slavia Mozyr") (1996 as MPKC Mozyr, 2000), [Dinamo Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dinamo_Minsk "FC Dinamo Minsk") (1997, 2004), [Dnepr-Transmash Mogilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnepr_Mogilev "FC Dnepr Mogilev") (1998), [BATE Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_BATE_Borisov "FC BATE Borisov") (1999, 2002), [Belshina Bobruisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Belshina_Bobruisk "FC Belshina Bobruisk") (2001), [Gomel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Gomel "FC Gomel") (2003), [Shakhtyor Soligorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtyor_Soligorsk "FC Shakhtyor Soligorsk") (2005). Since 2006, BATE Borisov has dominated the league, winning 13 championships in a row (2006–2018).

In March 2020, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), all the other football leagues in Europe were postponed, and by the end of the month, the Belarusian Premier League was the only top-flight league in the continent that was still playing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) Due to this, the league gained substantially increased viewership from abroad, with fans from all over the world watching the games online, due to the league being the only significant professional football available; the league signed new television rights deals with networks from countries including Russia and India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) Matches were also streamed on the [Belarusian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Football_Federation "Belarusian Football Federation")'s [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") channel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) British betting companies also offered odds for the various matches,\[ _[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\] as the league's profile, previously relatively unknown outside of the country, grew a larger audience due to sporting inactivity elsewhere.[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

## Reserves League

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Belarusian_Premier_League&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Reserves League")\]

Main article: [Belarusian Premier League Reserves Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Premier_League_Reserves_Championship "Belarusian Premier League Reserves Championship")

An annual league competition is organized for the reserve teams of Premier League clubs since 2001. This tournament was won by the reserves of [Dinamo Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dinamo_Minsk "FC Dinamo Minsk") (9 titles), [Gomel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Gomel "FC Gomel") (2 titles), [Shakhtyor Soligorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtyor_Soligorsk "FC Shakhtyor Soligorsk") (2 titles), [BATE Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_BATE_Borisov "FC BATE Borisov") (1 title), [Torpedo-BelAZ Zhodino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Torpedo-BelAZ_Zhodino "FC Torpedo-BelAZ Zhodino") (1 title) and [Dnepr Mogilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnepr_Mogilev "FC Dnepr Mogilev") (1 title).
