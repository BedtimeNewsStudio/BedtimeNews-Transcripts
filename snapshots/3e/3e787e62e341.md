NEW DELHI: In the last few days, the Tablighi Jama'at headquarters in Delhi's [Nizamuddin](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/topic/nizamuddin) has been making headlines after several of its members were tested positive for novel coronavirus (Covid-19).

Here is a look at the primary role of the organisation and its history —

- Tablighi Jamaat is an Islamic missionary movement that focuses on urging Muslims to return to practising their religion during the lifetime of the Islamic prophet Muhammad.
- The organisation is estimated to have between 150 million to 250 million adherents. Majority of them living in South Asia.
- It has presence in somewhere between 180 to 200 countries.
- The movement was founded in 1927 by Muhammad Ilyas al-Kandhlawi in Mewat region of India.
- In 2018, the Jama'at split into two factions after differences arose over running of the organisation.
- While the bigger faction is headquartered at Banglewali Masjid in Nizamuddin, the breakaway faction runs from a mosque in Nerul near Mumbai.
- Experts describe Tablighi Jama'at as an a-political, quietest movement of internal grassroots missionary renewal. It is one of the most important grassroots Islamic movements in the contemporary Muslim world.
- An integral part of Tablighi Jama'at is the establishments of madarsaas, which sought to relink the Muslim masses with Islamic institutions.
- The emergence of the Tablighi Jama῾at came at a time when Hindu proselytizing movements as the Shuddhi (Purification) and Sangathan (Consolidation) was taking shape. The Hindu movements launched massive efforts in the early twentieth century to "reclaim" those "fallen‐away" Hindus who had converted to Islam in the past, according to Oxford Institute of Islamic Studies.
