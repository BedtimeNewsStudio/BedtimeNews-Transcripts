**Hon Hai Precision Industry Co., Ltd.** (鴻海精密工業股份有限公司), [doing business as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_name "Trade name") **Hon Hai Technology Group** (鴻海科技集團) in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"), **Foxconn Technology Group** (富士康科技集团) in China, and **Foxconn** (富士康) internationally, is a Taiwanese [multinational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation "Multinational corporation") [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics "Electronics") [contract manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_manufacturer "Contract manufacturer") established in 1974 with headquarters in [Tucheng District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucheng_District "Tucheng District"), New Taipei City, Taiwan. In 2023, the company's annual revenue reached 6.16trillion [New Taiwan dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Taiwan_dollar "New Taiwan dollar") (equivalent to [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") 198.533 billion in 2024) and was ranked 20th in the 2023 [_Fortune_ Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500 "Fortune Global 500"). It is the world's largest contract manufacturer of electronics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) While headquartered in Taiwan, the company earns the majority of its revenue from assets in China and is one of the [largest employers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_employers "List of largest employers") worldwide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) [Terry Gou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gou "Terry Gou") is the company founder and former chairman.

Foxconn manufactures electronic products for major American, Canadian, Chinese, Finnish, and Japanese companies. Notable products manufactured by Foxconn include the [BlackBerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackBerry "BlackBerry"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) [iPad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad "IPad"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6) [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone"), [iPod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod "IPod"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-wsj) [Kindle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Kindle "Amazon Kindle"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-computerworld-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-computerworld) all [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo") gaming systems since the [GameCube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameCube "GameCube"), [Nintendo DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_DS "Nintendo DS") models, [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega "Sega") models, [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMD_Global "HMD Global") devices, [Cisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco "Cisco") products, [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony "Sony") devices (including most [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation "PlayStation") gaming consoles), [Google Pixel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Pixel "Google Pixel") devices, [Xiaomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaomi "Xiaomi") devices, every successor to [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft")'s [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_(console) "Xbox (console)") console,[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) and several CPU sockets, including the [TR4 CPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socket_TR4 "Socket TR4") socket on some motherboards. As of 2012, Foxconn factories manufactured an estimated 40% of all consumer electronics sold worldwide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-:0)

Foxconn named Young Liu its new chairman after the retirement of founder Terry Gou, effective on 1 July 2019. Young Liu was the special assistant to former chairman Terry Gou and the head of business group S (semiconductor). Analysts said the handover signaled the company's future direction and underscored the importance of [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor"), together with technologies like [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics "Robotics"), and [autonomous driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-driving_car "Self-driving car"). That was in the context of the maturation of Foxconn's traditional major business of smartphone assembly.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

Foxconn's Q2 '24 revenue was NT$1.551 trillion (US$31.17 billion).[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) _Circuits Assembly_ magazine named Foxconn the largest electronics manufacturing services company in the world for the 14th straight year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

## History

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Foxconn_2EG04927-D2D-DF_20140403_tag.jpg/500px-Foxconn_2EG04927-D2D-DF_20140403_tag.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Foxconn_2EG04927-D2D-DF_20140403_tag.jpg) Foxconn connector box tag in 2014

Terry Gou established Hon Hai Precision Industry Co., Ltd. as an electrical components manufacturer in 1974 in Taipei, Taiwan. Foxconn's first manufacturing plant in China opened in [Longhua Town, Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhua_Subdistrict,_Shenzhen "Longhua Subdistrict, Shenzhen"), in 1988.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-wsj)

One of the important milestones for Foxconn occurred in 2001 when [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel "Intel") selected Foxconn to manufacture its Intel-branded [motherboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motherboard "Motherboard") instead of [Asus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asus "Asus").[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) By November 2007, Foxconn further expanded with an announced plan to build a new US$500 million plant in [Huizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huizhou "Huizhou"), Southern China.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)

In January 2012, Foxconn named Tien Chong (Terry) Cheng chief executive of its subsidiary FIH Mobile Limited.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) At this time, Foxconn made up approximately 40% of worldwide consumer electronics production.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-:0)

Expansion was further pursued after a March 2012 acquisition of a 10-percent stake in the Japanese electronics company [Sharp Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation "Sharp Corporation") for US$806 million and to purchase up to 50 percent of the [LCDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCD "LCD") produced at Sharp's plant in [Sakai, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakai,_Japan "Sakai, Japan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) However, the agreed deal was broken as Sharp's shares continued to plunge in the following months.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18) In September 2012, Foxconn announced plans to invest US$494 million in the construction of five new factories in [Itu, Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itu,_Brazil "Itu, Brazil"), creating 10,000 jobs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)

In 2014, the company purchased Asia Pacific Telecom and won some spectrum licenses at an auction, which allowed it to operate [4G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4G "4G") telecommunications equipment in Taiwan.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)

On 25 February 2016, Sharp accepted a ¥700 billion (US$6.24 billion) takeover bid from Foxconn to acquire over 66 percent of Sharp's voting stock.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) However, Sharp had undisclosed liabilities which were later informed by Sharp's legal representative to Foxconn. The deal was halted by Foxconn's board of directors. Foxconn asked to call off the deal, but it was proceeded by the former Sharp president. Terry Gou, in the meeting, then wrote the word "義", which means "righteousness", on the whiteboard, saying that Foxconn should honor the deal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) A month later, on 30 March 2016, the deal was announced as finalized in a joint press statement, but at a lower price.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

In 2016, Foxconn, together with [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent "Tencent") and luxury-car dealer Harmony New Energy Auto, founded [Future Mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Mobility "Future Mobility"), a car start up that aimed to sell all-electric fully autonomous premium cars by 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24) A Foxconn unit, Foxconn Interconnect Technology, acquired [Belkin International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belkin_International "Belkin International") for $866m on 26 March 2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)

In July 2019, Foxconn appointed Liu, Young-Way as the new chairman of the Group.

On 5 February 2020, Foxconn started producing medical masks and clothing at its Shenzhen factory in China during the Chinese New Year and the height of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"). The company initially said the masks it makes would be for internal employee use. The outbreak of [coronavirus disease 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019 "Coronavirus disease 2019") led to a worldwide spike in demand for masks, resulting in global shortages.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

Following almost a year of public controversy regarding its [COVID-19 vaccine shortage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccination_in_Taiwan#Timeline "COVID-19 vaccination in Taiwan");[&#8202;](#ref-back-FinTimes-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-FinTimes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-Reuters)[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30) in June 2021, Taiwan agreed to allow founder Terry Gou, through his Yongling Foundation charity,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FinTimes-1)[\[27\]](#refnote-FinTimes) to join with contract chip maker [TSMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSMC "TSMC"), and negotiate purchasing [COVID-19 vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine "COVID-19 vaccine") on its behalf.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters-1)[\[28\]](#refnote-Reuters)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FinTimes-2)[\[27\]](#refnote-FinTimes) In July 2021, [BioNTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioNTech "BioNTech")'s Chinese sales agent [Fosun Pharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fosun_Pharma "Fosun Pharma") announced that Foxconn and TSMC had reached an agreement to purchase 10 million BioNTech COVID-19 vaccines from Germany for Taiwan.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FinTimes-3)[\[27\]](#refnote-FinTimes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters-2)[\[28\]](#refnote-Reuters) The two technology manufacturers pledged to each buy five million doses for up to $175 million,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FinTimes-4)[\[27\]](#refnote-FinTimes) for donation to Taiwan's vaccination program.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters-3)[\[28\]](#refnote-Reuters)

In 2024, Foxconn surpassed expectations with record fourth-quarter revenue.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)

## International operations

Foxconn has 137 campuses and offices in 24 countries and areas around the globe. The majority of Foxconn's factories are located in East Asia, with others in Brazil, India, Europe, and Mexico.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Duhigg-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-Duhigg)

### China

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Electronics_factory_in_Shenzhen.jpg/500px-Electronics_factory_in_Shenzhen.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Electronics_factory_in_Shenzhen.jpg) One of the production floors in Foxconn factory at Shenzhen

As of 2012, Foxconn had 12 factories in nine Chinese cities—more than in any other country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-probase-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-probase)

The largest Foxconn factory is located in [Longhua Subdistrict, Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhua_Subdistrict,_Shenzhen "Longhua Subdistrict, Shenzhen"), where hundreds of thousands of workers (varying counts include 230,000,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Duhigg-1)[\[32\]](#refnote-Duhigg) 300,000,[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34) and 450,000)[&#8202;](#ref-back-focustw-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-focustw) are employed at the [Longhua Science & Technology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhua_Science_&_Technology_Park "Longhua Science & Technology Park"), a walled campus[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj-2)[\[7\]](#refnote-wsj) sometimes referred to as "Foxconn City".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Economist-Foxconn-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-Economist-Foxconn)

Covering about 3km2 (1.2sqmi),[&#8202;](#ref-back-bloom-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-bloom) the park includes 15 factories,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Economist-Foxconn-1)[\[36\]](#refnote-Economist-Foxconn) worker dormitories, four swimming pools,[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38) fire brigade,[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj-3)[\[7\]](#refnote-wsj) own television network (Foxconn TV),[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj-4)[\[7\]](#refnote-wsj) city centre with grocery store, bank, restaurants, book store, hospital.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj-5)[\[7\]](#refnote-wsj) While some workers live in surrounding towns and villages, others live and work inside the complex;[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39) a quarter of the employees live in the dormitories.

Another Foxconn factory "city" is located at [Zhengzhou Technology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengzhou_Technology_Park "Zhengzhou Technology Park") in [Zhengzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengzhou "Zhengzhou"), [Henan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan "Henan"), where a reported 120,000 workers were employed as of 2012,[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40) later, 200,000 workers were employed as of November 2022.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41) The park produces the bulk of Apple's [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone") line and is sometimes referred to as "iPhone City".[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42) Foxconn manufactures iPhones through a division named the integrated Digital Product Business Group (iDPBG).[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43)[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)

#### Events

Foxconn's planned future expansion includes sites at [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan "Wuhan") in [Hubei province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubei "Hubei"), Kunshan in Jiangsu province, Tianjin, Beijing, Huizhou and Guangzhou in Guangdong province, China.[&#8202;](#ref-back-probase-1)[\[33\]](#refnote-probase) A Foxconn branch that primarily manufactures Apple products is [Hongfujin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongfujin "Hongfujin").

On 25 May 2016, the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") reported that Foxconn replaced 60,000 employees because it had [automated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation "Automation") "many of the manufacturing tasks associated with their operations". The organization later confirmed those claims.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45)

In September 2017, Foxconn agreed with the Nanjing government to invest US$5.7 billion for the development of intelligent terminal devices, LCD development, and other research.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1222-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-:1222):74

On 21 October 2022, and in response to a [Covid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") outbreak at Zhengzhou Technology Park, Foxconn imposed restrictions on its iPhone assembly plant, with dine-in meal facilities closed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47)[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48) On 31 October 2022, after policies intended to control a Covid outbreak prevented workers from leaving the complex, many workers jumped the fence in order to escape.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49) On 2 November 2022, the government imposed the lockdown to the [Zhengzhou Airport Economy Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengzhou_Airport_Economy_Zone "Zhengzhou Airport Economy Zone"), where the Foxconn factory is situated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50) On 23 November, workers clashed with law enforcement over the harsh COVID restrictions and claims that Foxconn failed to provide the salary packages that were promised to new hires. Videos circulated on Chinese social media depicting law enforcement beating protesting workers as well as large crowds of workers fighting back law enforcement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51)[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-52)

### Brazil

### Europe

Foxconn has manufacturing facilities in the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic "Czech Republic"), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary "Hungary") and [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia "Slovakia").

In the Czech Republic, its factories in [Pardubice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardubice "Pardubice") and [Kutná Hora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutn%C3%A1_Hora "Kutná Hora") employ around 4,500 people. They produce computers, networking equipment, servers and other electronics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-56) The Kutná Hora plant also manufactures liquid-cooled supercomputers for [Hewlett Packard Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett_Packard_Enterprise "Hewlett Packard Enterprise").[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-57) Foxconn was the country's second-largest exporter in 2024, with exports worth CZK 166 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-58) In 2026, the company announced plans to manufacture AI computing systems in Pardubice in partnership with [Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_(company) "Bull (company)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-59)

In Hungary, the [Komárom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kom%C3%A1rom "Komárom") factory produces servers, networking equipment and data-centre hardware. It employs more than 2,600 people and has expanded production to meet growing demand for AI servers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-60)[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-61)

In Slovakia, Foxconn acquired a majority stake in a former [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony "Sony") television factory in [Nitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitra "Nitra") in 2010. The plant has manufactured televisions and printed circuit boards, but has undergone restructuring in recent years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-62)

### India

As of early 2015, Foxconn had tied up with [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony "Sony") for manufacturing their [televisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television "Television") and selling it all over [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"). Hence, they started a new plant called Competition Team Technology (India) Private Limited in Irungattukottai (near [Poonamallee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poonamallee "Poonamallee"), [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai "Chennai")) which was later moved to Oragadam ( [Kanchipuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanchipuram "Kanchipuram")) in 2019. As of mid-2015, Foxconn was in talks to manufacture [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.")'s [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone") in India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-63) In 2015, Foxconn announced that it would be setting up twelve factories in India and would create around one million jobs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64) It also discussed its intent to work with the [Adani Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adani_Group "Adani Group") for expansion in the country. In August 2015, Foxconn invested in [Snapdeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapdeal "Snapdeal"). In September 2016, Foxconn started manufacturing products with [Gionee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gionee "Gionee").[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-65) In 2017, Foxconn started the production of iPhones in [Sriperumbudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriperumbudur "Sriperumbudur"), near Chennai. In April 2019, Foxconn reported that they are ready to mass-produce newer iPhones in India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chennai-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-Chennai) Its Chairman, [Terry Gou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gou "Terry Gou"), said that the manufacturing will take place in the southern city of Chennai.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chennai-1)[\[66\]](#refnote-Chennai)

In September 2022, Foxconn signed a deal for a [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor") plant in [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat,_India "Gujarat, India") with an investment of $21 billion, by [Vedanta Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanta_Limited "Vedanta Limited").[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67) In July 2023, Foxconn made a decision to quit the project, citing a number of issues with Vedanta Group as well as including external ones.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-68) In August 2023, during its annual meeting, Foxconn reportedly stated that India at present accounts for more than 5% of the company's business and there is ample space for future investments.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-69) Foxconn has set a target to employ 2 million jobs and meet India's target of exporting mobile phones worth $10 billion, both by 2030. To meet these targets, as of September 2023, the company has three manufacturing plants under construction, all in southern India–a component and semiconductor plant near the company's existing plant in Chennai, and two plants each in [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore "Bangalore") (near [its airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempegowda_International_Airport "Kempegowda International Airport")) and [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad "Hyderabad") ( [Kongara Kalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongara_Kalan "Kongara Kalan")) for making iPhones, [iPads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad "IPad"), [iPods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod "IPod") and [AirPods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirPod "AirPod"). All three plants are projected to be completed and begin operations by the end of 2024. They will together employ around 400,000 people in the first five years of their operations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-70)[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-71)[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-72)[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-73)

In November 2023, Foxconn announced a $1.54 billion investment in India to "help it fulfil 'operational needs.'"[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-74) In August 2024, Foxconn showed interest in investing in Hyderabad, as confirmed by the [Telangana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana "Telangana") government. Chairman Young Liu met with Chief Minister [A Revanth Reddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revanth_Reddy "Revanth Reddy"), discussing plans for a new "fourth city" near Hyderabad.[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-75)

### United States

Further information: [Wisconn Valley Science and Technology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconn_Valley_Science_and_Technology_Park "Wisconn Valley Science and Technology Park")

Foxconn announced on 26 July 2017 that it would build a $10 billion TV manufacturing plant in southeastern [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin "Wisconsin") and would initially employ 3,000 workers (set to increase to 13,000).[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-85)[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-86) As part of the agreement, Foxconn was set to receive subsidies ranging from $3 billion to $4.8 billion (paid in increments if Foxconn met certain targets), which would be by far the largest subsidy ever given to a foreign firm in U.S. history.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-88)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-:5) Some estimate that Foxconn is expected to contribute $51.5 billion to Wisconsin's GDP over the next 15 years, which is $3.4 billion annually.[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-91) However, numerous economists have also expressed skepticism that the benefits would exceed the costs of the deal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-92)[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-93)[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-94)[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-95)[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-96) Others have noted that Foxconn has made similar claims about job creation in the past which did not come to fruition. Their office building in [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee "Milwaukee") is the only one in Wisconsin[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[87\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[89\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-97)

Foxconn was also exempted by Governor [Scott Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Walker_(politician) "Scott Walker (politician)") from filing an [environmental impact statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_statement "Environmental impact statement"), prompting criticism from environmentalists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-talton-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-talton) The plant was estimated to contribute significantly to air pollution in the region.[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-99) Environmentalists criticised the decision to allow Foxconn to draw 26,000 cubic metres (7×10 ~~^~~ 6USgal) of water per day from [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan "Lake Michigan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-2)[\[89\]](#refnote-:3) Given water concerns, Foxconn is spending $30 million on zero liquid discharge technology.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-auto) Foxconn is also required to replace wetlands at a higher ratio than other companies; Foxconn must restore 2 acres of wetland for every 1 acre disturbed instead of the ratio of 1.2 to 1 for other companies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[100\]](#refnote-auto)

As of 4 October 2017, Foxconn agreed to locate their plant in [Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pleasant,_Wisconsin "Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-101) and broke ground for the plant 28 June 2018. [President Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") was in attendance to promote American manufacturing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-102)[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-103)

In January 2019, Foxconn said it was reconsidering its initial plans to manufacture [LCD screens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCD_screens "LCD screens") at the Wisconsin plant, citing high labour costs in the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-104) Under a new agreement announced in April 2021, Foxconn will reduce its planned investment to $672 million with 1,454 new jobs. Tax credits available to the project were reduced to $8 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-105) In October 2021, [Lordstown Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordstown_Motors "Lordstown Motors") announced a $250 million deal to sell a former GM plant to Foxconn, which would become a contract assembler for the company's Endurance pickup truck. The deal was completed in May 2022 for a final price of $230 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-106) It was announced Foxconn would also invest $50 million into the company through a purchase of common stock.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Foldy-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-Foldy)

## Controversies

### Working conditions

Allegations of poor working conditions have been made on several occasions. News reports highlight the long working hours,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Economist-Foxconn-2)[\[36\]](#refnote-Economist-Foxconn)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bloom-1)[\[37\]](#refnote-bloom) discrimination against mainland Chinese workers by their [Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") co-workers,[&#8202;](#ref-back-management-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-management) and lack of working relationships at the company.[&#8202;](#ref-back-moore-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-moore) Although Foxconn was found to be compliant in the majority of areas when [Apple Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") audited the maker of its [iPods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod "IPod") and [iPhones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone") in 2007,[&#8202;](#ref-back-wsj-6)[\[7\]](#refnote-wsj) the audit did substantiate several of the allegations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-118-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-118) In May 2010, _[Shanghaiist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghaiist "Shanghaiist")_ reported that security guards had been caught beating factory workers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-119)

In reaction to a spate of negative press, particularly that involving worker suicides in which 14 people died[&#8202;](#ref-back-reutersthree-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-reutersthree) from January to May 2010, [Steve Jobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Jobs "Steve Jobs") defended Apple's relationship with the company in June 2010, citing that its Chinese partner is "pretty nice" and is "not a sweatshop".[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-121) Meanwhile, however, a report jointly produced by 20 universities in Hong Kong, Taiwan, and mainland China described Foxconn factories as [labour camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_camps "Labour camps")[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-122) with widespread worker abuse and illegal overtime.

Concerns increased in early 2012 by an article published in _The New York Times_ in October 2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-The_New_York_Times-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-The_New_York_Times) It reported evidence that substantiated some of the criticisms. The 2012 audit commissioned by [Apple Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") and performed by the [Fair Labor Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Labor_Association "Fair Labor Association") found that workers were routinely subjected to inhumane bouts of [overtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtime "Overtime") of up to 34 hours without a pay increase and suggested that debilitating workplace accidents and suicides may be common.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fla2012-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-fla2012)[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-125) A Hong Kong non-profit organisation, [Students and Scholars Against Corporate Misbehaviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_and_Scholars_Against_Corporate_Misbehaviour "Students and Scholars Against Corporate Misbehaviour"), has written numerous negative reports on Foxconn's treatment of its employees, such as in 2010 and 2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-126) These typically find far worse conditions than the 2012 Fair Labour Association audit did,[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-127) but they rely on a far smaller number of employee informants, circa 100 to 170.[&#8202;](#ref-back-128-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-128) The Fair Labor Association audit in 2012 used interviews with 35,000 Foxconn employees.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fla2012-1)[\[124\]](#refnote-fla2012)

In January 2012, about 150 Foxconn employees threatened to commit a [mass suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_suicide "Mass suicide") in protest of their working conditions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-129-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-129) One worker said the protest resulted from 600 workers being moved into a new "unbearable" factory location.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TECHT-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-TECHT) In September 2012, a fight at worker dormitories in [Taiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan "Taiyuan"), [Shanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanxi "Shanxi"), where a guard allegedly was beating a worker, escalated into a riot involving 2,000 people and was quelled by security.[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-131)

In October 2012, the company admitted that 14-year-old children had worked for a short time at a facility in [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai "Yantai"), [Shandong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Province "Shandong Province"), as part of an internship programme,[&#8202;](#ref-back-14years-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-14years) in violation of the minimum age of 16 for legal workers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14years-1)[\[132\]](#refnote-14years) Foxconn said that the workers had been brought in to help deal with a labor shortage, and _[Xinhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhua "Xinhua")_ quoted an official saying that 56 underage interns would be returned to their schools. _[Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters")_ quoted Foxconn saying that 2.7 percent of its workforce in China were long- or short-term interns. In response to the scrutiny, Foxconn said it would cut overtime from the current 20 hours per week to less than nine hours a week.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14years-2)[\[132\]](#refnote-14years)

Also in October 2012, there was a crisis concerning an injured worker in which 26-year-old Zhang Tingzhen[&#8202;](#ref-back-Lyn_Reuters-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-Lyn_Reuters) suffered an electric shock and fell in a factory accident[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tan_Ee_Lyn-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-Tan_Ee_Lyn) a year earlier. His doctors did immediate surgery to remove part of his brain,[&#8202;](#ref-back-WIRED-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-WIRED) "\[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ron_Dicker-0)[after which\] he lost his memory and can neither speak, walk".[\[136\]](#refnote-Ron_Dicker) When his father attempted to get compensation in 2012,[&#8202;](#ref-back-WIRED-1)[\[135\]](#refnote-WIRED) _Reuters_ reported that Foxconn told the family to transport and submit him for a disability assessment in [Huizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huizhou "Huizhou") 70km away, or it would cut off funding for his treatment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Lyn_Reuters-1)[\[133\]](#refnote-Lyn_Reuters) His doctors protested the move for fear of a [brain haemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_hemorrhage "Brain hemorrhage") en route,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ron_Dicker-1)[\[136\]](#refnote-Ron_Dicker) and the company stated that it was acting within labour laws.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tan_Ee_Lyn-1)[\[134\]](#refnote-Tan_Ee_Lyn)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Paris_Match-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-Paris_Match) His family later sued Foxconn in 2012 and argued in court that Tingzhen had been summoned to the wrong city.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Lyn_Reuters-2)[\[133\]](#refnote-Lyn_Reuters) In 2014, a court ruled that he had to be assessed in Huizhou to receive compensation, with Foxconn offering a settlement for the father to recant his criticisms, which was refused.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WIRED-2)[\[135\]](#refnote-WIRED)

In February 2015, _Beijing News_ reported that an official with the [All China Federation of Trade Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-China_Federation_of_Trade_Unions "All-China Federation of Trade Unions") (ACFTU), Guo Jun, said that Foxconn allegedly forced employees to work overtime, resulting in occasional death by [karōshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kar%C5%8Dshi "Karōshi") or [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide"). Jun also said that the illegal overtime resulted from a lack of investigation and light punishments. Foxconn, in return, issued a statement questioning Guo's allegations, arguing workers wanted to work overtime to earn more money.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DAILY-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-DAILY)

In November 2017, the _Financial Times_ reported that it had found several students working 11-hour days at the iPhone X plant in [Henan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan "Henan") province, violating the 40-hour-per-week mandate for children. In response, Foxconn announced that it has stopped the interns' illegal overtime work at the factory in which 3,000 students had been hired that September.[&#8202;](#ref-back-139-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-139)

Since 2016, Foxconn has been replacing its workforce with robots, which have replaced 50% of Foxconn's labor force in 2016, and there are plans for completely automated factories.[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[140\]](#refnote-140)

In 2019, a report was issued by Taiwan News stating that some of Foxconn's managers had fraudulently used rejected parts to build iPhones.[&#8202;](#ref-back-141-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-141)

In late 2022, working conditions were exacerbated by [Zero-COVID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-COVID "Zero-COVID") policies leading to [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_protests_in_China "COVID-19 protests in China").[&#8202;](#ref-back-142-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-142)[&#8202;](#ref-back-143-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-143)[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-144)

### Suicides

Main article: [Foxconn suicides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxconn_suicides "Foxconn suicides")

Suicides among Foxconn workers have attracted the media's attention.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNET-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-CNET) Among the first cases to attract attention in the press was the death of Sun Danyong, a 25-year-old man who committed suicide in July 2009 after reporting the loss of an [iPhone 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_4 "IPhone 4")[&#8202;](#ref-back-146-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-146) prototype in his possession.[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-147) According to _[The Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")_, Danyong had been beaten by security guards.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TECHT-1)[\[130\]](#refnote-TECHT)

There was also a series of suicides speculatively linked to low pay in 2010, though employees also noted that Foxconn paid higher wages than similar jobs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNET-1)[\[145\]](#refnote-CNET) In reaction to a spate of worker suicides in which 14 people died in 2010,[&#8202;](#ref-back-reutersthree-1)[\[120\]](#refnote-reutersthree) Foxconn installed suicide-prevention netting at the base of buildings in some facilities[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-148) and promised to offer substantially higher wages at its Shenzhen production bases.[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-149) In 2011, Foxconn also hired the PR firm [Burson-Marsteller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burson-Marsteller "Burson-Marsteller") to help deal with the negative publicity from the suicides. That year, the nets seemed to help lower the death rate, although at least four employees died by throwing themselves off buildings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TECHT-2)[\[130\]](#refnote-TECHT)

In January 2012, there was a protest by workers about conditions in [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan "Wuhan"), with 150 workers threatening to commit mass suicide if factory conditions were not improved.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TECHT-3)[\[130\]](#refnote-TECHT) In 2012 and into 2013, three young Foxconn employees were reported to have died by jumping off buildings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TECHT-4)[\[130\]](#refnote-TECHT) In September 2014, employee and poet [Xu Lizhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Lizhi_(poet) "Xu Lizhi (poet)") jumped to his death four days after signing a new three-year contract; his poems, published posthumously by friends, received widespread media attention.[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-150) In January 2018, another suicide was reported by a factory worker, after 31-year old Li Ming jumped to his death off a building in [Zhengzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengzhou "Zhengzhou"), where the [iPhone X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_X "IPhone X") was being manufactured.[&#8202;](#ref-back-TECHT-5)[\[130\]](#refnote-TECHT)

### The Wisconsin Valley Project

Main article: [Wisconn Valley Science and Technology Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconn_Valley_Science_and_Technology_Park "Wisconn Valley Science and Technology Park")

The project originally committed in 2017 to investing $10 billion and employing up to 13,000 workers but has now shrunk to $672 million with 1,454 jobs.

### Food poisoning

On 15 December 2021, 256 workers at Foxconn's [Sriperumbudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriperumbudur "Sriperumbudur") factory developed Acute Diarrhoeal Disease due to food poisoning after eating food at the company-provided hostel. As a result of which, 159 workers were hospitalized.[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-151) The workers were provided no information about this, due to which a rumor started spreading among the workers through WhatsApp that two workers had died.[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-152) By 17 December there were sit-in protests in worker dormitories, by 10 pm of the same day, thousands of women workers of the factory staged protests on the Chennai-Bengaluru national highway,[&#8202;](#ref-back-153-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-153) this was met by police detention of 67 women protestors and arrest of one journalist, with many of them being released a day later.[&#8202;](#ref-back-154-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-154) Following the protests the factory was shut down for a week, with the state government and district administration investigating the worker conditions.

On 22 December the Food Safety Department sealed the kitchen of the dormitory[&#8202;](#ref-back-155-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-155) finding rats and poor drainage. The rooms provided to workers were overcrowded, with them being forced to sleep on the floor, some even lacking toilets with a running water supply.[&#8202;](#ref-back-156-0)[\[156\]](#refnote-156) Following the revelation of substandard living conditions, on 29 December Apple put the Foxconn plant on probation, with both Apple and Foxconn issuing statements on the dormitory and dining rooms conditions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-157-0)[\[157\]](#refnote-157) In January 2022, after assuring Apple and the [Tamil Nadu government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Tamil_Nadu "Government of Tamil Nadu") that it had taken the necessary corrective measures, Foxconn began reopening its factory and resuming work in phases.[&#8202;](#ref-back-158-0)[\[158\]](#refnote-158)[&#8202;](#ref-back-159-0)[\[159\]](#refnote-159)

## Various corporate partnerships

The [Mobility in Harmony Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_in_Harmony_Consortium "Mobility in Harmony Consortium") was created in 2020 by Foxconn to promote a set of [open standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_standards "Open standards") for electric vehicles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-software_platform-0)[\[160\]](#refnote-software_platform)

In 2020, Foxconn initiated MIH Alliance to create an open EV ecosystem that promotes collaboration in the mobility industry, with more than 2,200 companies joining the open standard since its launch. The company announced plans to become more involved as a contract assembler of EVs. In the same year, Foxconn partnered with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles N.V. and Yulon Group for a move into EVs. Foxconn has been holding the Hon Hai Tech Day (HHTD) event since 2020 to showcase its latest achievements. In HHTD21, Foxconn introduced for the first time three self-developed EV models: the Model C recreational vehicle, the Model E sedan, and the Model T electric bus.

In January 2021, Foxconn and Geely Holding Group signed a strategic cooperative agreement and will establish a joint venture company to provide OEM and customized consulting services relating to whole vehicles, parts, intelligent drive systems, and automotive ecosystem platforms to global automotive enterprises and ridesharing companies. In February 2021, it announced an agreement with EV startup Fisker Inc. to jointly produce more than 250,000 vehicles a year. In March 2021, Foxtron, the JV company of Foxconn and Yulon, announced cooperation with Nidec to strengthen the power on EV key component development.

In July 2021, Foxconn teamed up with CTBC Financial Holding Co., Ltd to create a new fund targeting EV investments. In June 2021, Foxconn invested T$995.2 million ($36 million) in Gigasolar Materials Corp to develop EV battery materials. In September 2021, Foxconn collaborated with Thailand's state-owned oil supplier PTT Public Co. to invest US$1–2 billion in launching an EV joint venture in Thailand. In the same month, Foxconn and Gogoro formed a strategic technology and manufacturing partnership to introduce new levels of manufacturing capabilities and scale for Gogoro battery swapping technologies and Smart Scooters. In October 2021, it agreed to purchase a former GM auto plant from [Lordstown Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordstown_Motors "Lordstown Motors") and to purchase $50 million of the company's common stock. Under the agreement, Foxconn would use the plant to produce Lordstown's [Endurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordstown_Endurance "Lordstown Endurance") pickup truck. Fisker vehicles would also be made at the same plant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Foldy-1)[\[107\]](#refnote-Foldy)

In January 2022, Foxconn signed an MoU with the Indonesian Ministry of Investment/BKPM, IBC, Indika, and Gogoro to jointly develop a sustainable new energy ecosystem in Indonesia that focuses on electric batteries, electric mobility, and associated industries. In May 2022, Foxconn announced the completion of the Lordstown Motors facility purchase and further signed a contract manufacturing agreement and a joint venture agreement with LMC for product development.

In mid-2021, Foxconn announced that the company would enter into more [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor") production and will be expanding into supplying chips for [electric vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicles "Electric vehicles") (EVs) and electronics equipment used for healthcare.[&#8202;](#ref-back-161-0)[\[161\]](#refnote-161) In May 2021, Foxconn and Yageo Group entered into a joint venture agreement to form XSemi Corporation ("XSemi") to extend the businesses into the semiconductor industry, including product development and sales. Based in Hsinchu, Taiwan, XSemi aims to consolidate the strengths and resources of the two market leaders, in addition to the upcoming multifaceted collaborations with leading semiconductor companies in product design, process and capacity planning, and sales channel. In August 2021, Foxconn acquired a Macronix 6-inch Wafer Fab for US$90.8mn.

In February 2022, Foxconn formed a joint venture company with [Vedanta Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanta_Limited "Vedanta Limited"), one of India's leading multinational groups, to manufacture semiconductors in India. Foxconn dropped out of the deal in July 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-162-0)[\[162\]](#refnote-162) In April 2022, it was announced Foxconn had acquired the wireless telecommunications company, arQana Technologies – with the new organization being rebranded as "iCana".[&#8202;](#ref-back-163-0)[\[163\]](#refnote-163) Foxconn also announced a merger with the integrated circuit designing firm AchernarTek for an undisclosed amount. The acquisition and consolidation will help Foxconn develop semiconductors for the automotive sector and 5G infrastructure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-164-0)[\[164\]](#refnote-164) In September 2022, Foxtron, the automotive division of Foxconn works with [Luxgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxgen "Luxgen") to launch its first electric vehicle, Luxgen n7.[&#8202;](#ref-back-165-0)[\[165\]](#refnote-165)

In October 2026, Foxconn subsidiary Lin Yin International Investment agreed to acquire a 50% stake in Mitsubishi Electric Mobility from [Mitsubishi Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Electric "Mitsubishi Electric") for ¥115 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-166-0)[\[166\]](#refnote-166) The transaction was expected to close in October 2027, after which the companies would jointly operate the automotive-equipment business.[&#8202;](#ref-back-167-0)[\[167\]](#refnote-167)
