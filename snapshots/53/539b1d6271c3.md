# Emergency Meeting on Macroeconomic and Financial Stability (Oct. 23, 2022)

- DateOctober 23, 2022
- Tel+82 44 215 2750

**Emergency Meeting on Macroeconomic and Financial Stability**

**Government Works to Stabilize** **the Corporate Bond Market and Short-term Money Market**

Deputy Prime Minister Kyungho CHOO presided over the Emergency Meeting on Macroeconomic and Financial Stability with the BOK, FSC and FSS [\[1\]](#_ftn1) on October 23 to review the current trend of the corporate bond market and short-term money market and discuss market stabilization measures.

The following is a summary of DPM CHOO’s remarks.

The government and BOK agreed that as the current market situation has become grave due to multifaceted factors at home and abroad, the government will take timely responses to the market instability by mobilizing all available policy tools if needed. In particular, the government will push ahead with preemptive and adequate market stabilization measures through policy coordination with not only the BOK, FSC and FSS that participated in today’s meeting, but also related ministries including the Ministry of the Interior and Safety and the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport.

The government has devised six market stabilization measures as follows.

1\. The government will expand liquidity provision programs to over 50 trillion won in addition to the existing market stabilization measures with an aim to ease volatility and prevent liquidity shortages in the corporate bond and short-term money market.

Starting from October 24, the government will resume purchasing corporate bonds and CPs such as a project-financing asset-backed commercial paper (PF-ABCP) guaranteed by construction companies, primarily injecting possible resources worth 1.6 trillion won from a 20 trillion-won bond market stabilization fund.

Moreover, funds will be raised on a capital call basis from early November of 2022 with a rapid preparation for it, and additional funds will also be raised if necessary.

2\. The government will double the ceiling of its corporate bond and CP purchase programs run by state-run financial institutions [\[2\]](#_ftn2) to 16 trillion won from the existing 8 trillion won and include CPs issued by security firms in the programs’ purchase list in an effort to ease the market volatility associated with a real estate PF-ABCP.

3\. The government will supply 3 trillion won by utilizing the fund of the Korea Securities Finance Corporation to provide liquidity to securities firms experiencing temporary liquidity shortages due to the PF-ABCP refinancing difficulties, and work to secure additional liquidity as much as possible by pursuing cooperation with related institutions.

4\. The government will swiftly review measures to include quasi-sovereign bonds and financial bonds in addition to government bonds in the list of eligible collateral securities that satisfy the criteria designated by the BOK and other financial institutions at the BOK Monetary Policy Committee Meeting in an attempt to supply adequate liquidity to the market.

5\. The government will actively respond to concerns about the real estate market’s project financing (PF). As stated today by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety and the Ministry of Land, every Korean local government will fulfill its payment guarantee obligations linked to issuances of any asset-backed commercial paper (ABCP).

In addition, the government will increase the cap of Korea Housing & Urban Guarantee Corporation (KHUG) and Korea Housing Finance Corporation’s payment guaranteeprograms to 10 trillion won, hoping to provide refunding to get the project back on track and ease concerns related to the financing for the main PF. It will also rapidly devise measures to help facilitate the overall PF market such as deregulation in the housing market aimed at preventing unsold houses.

6\. The government will monitor the current market trends and swiftly deal with the difficulties by closely communicating with market participants including financial institutions and proactively respond to behaviors that disrupt and destabilize the market and spread false information.

Going forward, the government and BOK will stay alert on the latest changes to the market and take necessary actions for market stabilization in a bold and rapid manner.

* * *

[\[1\]](#_ftnref1) The meeting was joined by the Bank of Korea (BOK) Governor, the Chairman of the Financial Services Commission (FSC), the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) Governor and the Secretary to the President for Economic Affairs from the Office of the President.

[\[2\]](#_ftnref2) The Korea Development Bank (KDB), the Industrial Bank of Korea (IBK) and the Korea Credit Guarantee Fund (KODIT)
