## Sharing

![twitter icon](/legislative-train/icons/twitter-x-white.svg)

On 20 May, Parliament and Council reached a provisional agreement on Commission proposals for two regulations that implement the EU's tariff commitments under the 2025 EU-US Framework Agreement. The Agreement, also known as the "Turnberry deal", is a political agreement on tariff and trade reached on 27 July 2025 between US President Donald Trump and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in Turnberry, Scotland.

On 16 June, Parliament approved in Plenary the text of the provisional agreement. The acts were adopted by the Council on 25 June, and published in the Official Journal on 30 June.

On 28 August 2025, the Commission proposed two regulations that implement the tariff aspects of this agreement. Proposal (COM(2025)471)  eliminates customs duties on about 34% of EU industrial goods imports from the US in 2024 (the rest already were subject to zero tariffs). It also partially liberalises certain seafood and agricultural goods by opening tariff rate quotas (TRQs) for these goods.

The second proposal (COM(2025)472) prolongs an elimination of customs duties on certain types of lobster. This was originally granted in 2020 for a five-year period (until 31 July 2025) and the Commission proposes to expand the scope of this concession to processed lobster.

In Parliament, the file was referred to the Committee on International Trade (INTA) and Bernd Lange (S&D, Germany) is the rapporteur for both proposals. He published his draft report on both legislative files on 21 October 2025. Work was suspended in early 2026 following Trump's tariff threats in connection with the US approach to Greenland in January, and the US Supreme Court tariff ruling in February, but resumed shortly after. Both files were adopted in Committee on 19 March and in plenary on 26 March 2026.

This was the basis for the two legislative reports adopted in plenary on 26 March 2026. Regarding the proposal on the adjustment of customs duties and opening of tariff quotas for the import of certain goods, Parliament introduced a suspension clause allowing the Commission to suspend certain parts of the agreement. This can be done if the US undermines the objectives of improving the trade and investment relationship, imposes additional tariffs on goods from the EU that exceed the 15 % ceiling, or circumstances change. The EP also entrusted the Commission with monitoring the impact of the agreement and introduced a safeguard mechanism to protect the industrial and agricultural sectors, in case the increase in certain goods following the agreement causes serious injury to EU Member States. Parliament also introduced a 'sunrise' clause, delaying the application of tariffs until the US respects its commitments. It also added a 'sunset' clause, according to which, the agreement expires on 31 March 2028 and can be only extended following an impact assessment.  
Parliament equally  introduced the suspension and sunset clause amendments to the second proposal (on the non-application of customs duties on imports of certain goods).

The Council adopted its position on both proposals on 21 November 2025. While the Council adopted the Commission proposal relating to EU imports of lobster without modifications, it added three elements to the other Commission proposal regarding full and partial liberalisation of imports of US industrial and agricultural goods respectively.   
It strengthened the safeguard measures, emphasised the use of non-preferential rules of origin, and added a review clause and provisions on the monitoring of the economic impact of the trade liberalisation measures within the EU.

The trilogue agreement contains the following important elements: regarding the main regulation (industrial and agri-food imports), the agreed version includes a safeguard mechanism, which gives the EU the means to suspend partially or fully the application of the regulation, so as to address possible significant increases in imports from the US that cause –or threaten to cause– serious injury to domestic producers. Specifically, if following an examination, the Commission establishes (or has sufficient indications that such actions may occur in the future) that (i) the US fails to meet the commitments of the Joint Statement, (ii) it otherwise undermines the objectives pursued by the Joint Statement, (iii) disrupts the trade and investment relations with the EU, then it can suspend (in part or in full) the application of the regulation. The Commission is also empowered to suspend concessions concerning steel and aluminium products to the US, if by 31 December 2026 the US continues to apply a tariff rate higher than 15% on steel and aluminium derivative products imported from the EU (in August 2025, following the Turnberry deal, the US added 407 product categories to the list of derivative steel and aluminium products subject to tariffs).

In addition to the above, the agreed version provides for regular monitoring of the economic effects of the trade liberalisation measures on the EU economy. In June 2029, the Commission will also present a comprehensive assessment examining, among other things, the impact of the regulation on the EU-US trade flows, on trade patterns, on tariff revenue, and on SMEs. Where appropriate, the assessment will be accompanied by a legislative proposal to extend the application of the regulation. If no such action is taken, then the regulation will cease to apply at the end of 2029 (‘sunset clause’).

Concerning the second proposal (lobsters), Parliament and Council agreed on the EP's proposal to extend by five years the tariff-free imports of lobster until 31 July 2030.

### Glossary

#### Status type:

Legislative initiatives - by the European Parliament

#### Style:

#### Description:

Files with 'Legislative initiative' status represent explicit demands to the
European Commission for a proposal for new EU legislation expressed by the
European Parliament in plenary following a legislative-initiative report (INL).
Only INL procedures based on Article 225 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the
European Union can have this status.

#### Status type:

Announced - forthcoming initiatives

#### Style:

#### Description:

Files with 'Announced' status are initiatives expected from the European
Commission – in particular legislative proposals – which will contribute to
the implementation of the Commission’s priorities.

Most such files are announced in the Political Guidelines (of the incoming
Commission at the start of the mandate), in the Commission President's
yearly State of the Union speech and accompanying letter of intent, or in
the annual Commission work programme. Planned legislative proposals may also
be announced in a strategy, communication or action plan adopted by the
Commission, sometimes with an anticipated date of publication.

A few non-legislative files (communications from the European Commission) can
also be found with this status. These either announce future legislative
activity or are of major political importance.

#### Status type:

Tabled - proposals submitted to Parliament by the Commission

#### Style:

#### Description:

Once Parliament has received a legislative proposal – or significant
non-legislative initiative – and is able to start its consideration, the
file takes the status 'Tabled'. The move to this status from 'announced'
represents a significant change, going from a planned initiative, entirely
in the hands of the Commission, to a formal proposal dependent – for its
amendment and adoption – on the legislator (in most cases, under the
ordinary legislative procedure, the Parliament and Council jointly).

#### Status type:

Close to adoption - legislative procedure nearly completed

#### Style:

#### Description:

The status 'Close to adoption' applies to files for which the legislative
procedure will shortly be finalised. In the case of the ordinary legislative
procedure, this means that the European Parliament and Council have concluded
interinstitutional negotiations (trilogue) and reached a provisional agreement
on the text. Such provisional agreements need still to be formally adopted by
the two institutions.

#### Status type:

Blocked - proposals proceeding slowly or stalled

#### Style:

#### Description:

Files take on the status 'Blocked' when there has been no progress over at
least the previous nine months. In particular, when one or other
co-legislator has not formally started work on a proposal more than nine
months after it was tabled, or when no activity on the proposal has been
recorded in either the Council or Parliament for at least nine months.

In addition, where the Commission has announced an initiative would be
tabled on a certain date, but more than nine months after that date the
initiative has not been tabled, the file moves to this status.

#### Status type:

Adopted/Completed - legislation adopted or discussion ended

#### Style:

#### Description:

Once a legislative proposal is formally adopted, and has been signed by the
presidents of the European Parliament and the Council (or just the latter if
a special legislative procedure applies), the file moves to
'Adopted/Completed' status.

In the case of non-legislative initiatives from the Commission, this status
applies once the debate is finished – for example, once Parliament has
adopted a resolution on a Commission communication, or once the Commission
has tabled all the legislative proposals announced in the communication
concerned.

A Parliament legislative initiative takes this status once the Commission
has responded formally.

#### Status type:

Withdrawn - legislative proposal tabled but subsequently withdrawn

#### Style:

#### Description:

Files with 'Withdrawn' status are previously tabled proposals that have been
officially withdrawn by the European Commission, namely once the withdrawal is
published in the Official Journal.

#### Name:

TRAIN

#### Description:

Represents a main priority of the European Commission (i.e. one of the six
priorities of the von der Leyen Commission (2019-2024), or ten priorities of the
Juncker Commission (2014-2019)). A train is composed of a number of files – both
legislative and non-legislative – each of which are also known as CARRIAGES.
Separate trains cover all the files assigned to each parliamentary committee
(please see EP Committees).

#### Name:

CARRIAGE

#### Description:

Equals a legislative or non-legislative file which
forms part of a train. A carriage may cover more than one file in rare cases of
technical adjustments or minor changes to existing files.

#### Name:

PACKAGE

#### Description:

Represents a group of legislative and/or non-legislative files, based on the
indications of the European Commission. A package may, for example, cover all
files published under a strategy or an action plan. The grouping always reflects
the way the Commission has organised it.

#### Name:

STATUS

#### Description:

Indicates the level of advancement of legislative work on a file. There are
seven statuses which a given file may take on during the time it goes through
the legislative process.

#### Name:

EC PRIORITY

#### Description:

Politically defined priority set at the beginning of each European Commission
mandate (i.e. the six priorities of the von der Leyen Commission, ten priorities
of the Juncker Commission). Each priority is represented by a TRAIN composed of
separate files known as CARRIAGES.

#### Name:

EP COMMITTEES

#### Description:

A list of legislative and non-legislative files assigned to a parliamentary
committee. In total, 19 standing committees are represented on this website.

#### Name:

IN THE SPOTLIGHT

#### Description:

Important files grouped under a separate heading indicating a European policy
priority, a major current topic or any other theme that is key for European
policy.

#### Name:

SCHEDULE

#### Description:

General table presenting TRAINS (within EC Priorities or EP Committees) in a
summarised form with relevant statistical data by STATUS. Landing page of the
website.

#### Name:

PROCEDURE

#### Description:

Indicates procedure number which helps to find files quickly (e.g.
2021/XXXX(COD), 2020/XXXX(CNS), 20219/XXXX(APP)...)

#### Name:

COMMISSION WORK PROGRAMME

#### Description:

Allows to choose all the files presented in the annual Commission work
programme, for each year since 2019.
