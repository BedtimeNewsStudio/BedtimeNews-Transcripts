# When BSNL didn't have money for salaries:First time profit of 262 crores after 17 years; revenue 21 thousand crores

2 years ago

- Copy link

![](https://images.bhaskarassets.com/web2images/1884/2025/02/23/gif-17_1740301491.gif)

This is from 2019. It was just the second month of the new year. Around 1.76 lakh employees of Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited (BSNL) were waiting for their salary. Usually, the salary was received on the first date, but even after a week, money hadn't been credited to their accounts. This happened for the first time in the history of the government company BSNL. It had suffered a loss of 8 thousand crore rupees in 2018.

Anup Srivastava was then the CMD (Chairman and Managing Director) of BSNL. He was very worried. In a media report, he says - 'The company was drowning in debt. We had a cash shortage. We were raising funds to pay employees' salaries. Meanwhile, newspapers were publishing news that BSNL employees hadn't received their salaries even after 15 days. If this continued, it would have become difficult to get loans from banks.'

BSNL, which had been in losses for years, has turned profitable after 17 years. The company has earned a profit of 262 crore rupees in the third quarter of 2024-25. Currently, BSNL's revenue is 21 thousand crore rupees. It has more than 9 crore customers across the country.

Today, let's take a closer look at India's government telecom company, BSNL.

![BSNL office in Delhi. BSNL was established in September 2000.](https://images.bhaskarassets.com/web2images/1884/2025/02/23/facebook-cover-11_1740301013.png)

BSNL office in Delhi. BSNL was established in September 2000.

India's communication network traces its roots back to the British era. The East India Company introduced the telegraph in 1851, initially connecting Calcutta (now Kolkata) and Bombay (now Mumbai). In 1854, the British government took control of the telegraph system.

The Indian Telegraph Act was enacted in 1881, and by 1882, telephone services were introduced in major cities like Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras (now Chennai).

After independence in 1947, telecommunications came under the Government of India. The Department of Posts and Telegraph was established in 1951, overseeing both postal and telephone services. In 1986, telecommunications became a separate department, marking a new era for India’s telecom sector.

**BSNL made incoming free, people started seeking recommendations just to buy SIM cards**

In October 2000, under the NDA government led by Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, the Telecommunication Department was converted into a government-owned company, Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited (BSNL), with the government retaining full ownership. This was the era when some private companies had started mobile services in the country, but only a few people could use them. At that time, outgoing calls cost Rs 16 per minute and incoming calls cost Rs 8 per minute.

In 2002, BSNL also launched mobile services. Outgoing calls cost Rs 1.5 per minute while incoming calls were free. This was nothing short of a shock for private companies. People rushed to buy BSNL SIM cards. Recommendations were even needed to get a BSNL SIM, but then came the period when this government company started going into losses.

![Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee launching BSNL mobile service in Lucknow on October 19, 2002.](https://images.bhaskarassets.com/web2images/1884/2025/02/23/facebook-cover-12_1740301048.png)

Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee launching BSNL mobile service in Lucknow on October 19, 2002.

**Government accused of scam, shares bought at private rates, company incurred losses**

In 2006, BSNL had around 240 million mobile connections and a 22% market share. To expand, it issued a tender for 93 million additional lines, but delays in implementation slowed progress. Meanwhile, corruption scandals like the 2G scam impacted the government, and BSNL’s market share declined as private players surged ahead.

In 2010, when the 3G spectrum was auctioned, BSNL, being a government entity, was not allowed to bid but had to pay the same price as private companies—around ₹18,000 crores—draining its finances. This gave private players like Airtel, Vodafone, and Idea a competitive edge, pushing BSNL further behind in wireline, WiFi, and mobile services. By 2017, Jio emerged as India's largest telecom provider with 130 million users.

BSNL’s financial troubles deepened, and in 2018-19, it reported losses of ₹14,000 crores, with revenues falling by ₹19,000 crores compared to the previous year.

![](https://images.bhaskarassets.com/web2images/1884/2025/02/24/facebook-cover-10_1740388310.png)

**Why did BSNL remain continuously in losses, let's understand in 7 points:**

- In 2000, BSNL officials wanted to launch mobile services as quickly as possible to challenge private operators, but they did not receive the necessary government approval.
- Between 2006-12, while BSNL's capacity increased marginally, private operators moved far ahead.
- People switched from BSNL to private companies due to network congestion and other problems.
- In 2010, when 3G spectrum was auctioned, BSNL did not participate due to being a government company. Later, BSNL received spectrum at the same price as private companies.
- BSNL also had to pay a huge amount for WiMAX technology-based Broadband Wireless Access (BWA) spectrum. This directly affected BSNL's financial condition.
- With the mobile revolution gaining momentum in the country, landline connections declined rapidly. BSNL had 38 million landline customers in 2006-07, which decreased to 16 million in 2014-15.

BSNL was also kept out of the 4G spectrum auction. Due to this delay, private companies have rolled out 5G, but BSNL remains stuck on 4G.
