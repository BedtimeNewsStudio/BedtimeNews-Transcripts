On December 29, 2020, a major hacking incident occurred at the LuBian mining pool, resulting in the theft of a total of 127272.06953176 bitcoins (valued at approximately $3.5 billion at the time, now worth $15 billion). The holder of this large amount of bitcoin is Chen Zhi, the chairman of the Cambodian Prince Group. After the hacking incident, Chen Zhi and his Prince Group released multiple messages on the blockchain in early 2021 and July 2022, urging the hackers to return the stolen bitcoins and expressing a willingness to pay a ransom, but received no response. Strangely, after the theft of this large amount of bitcoin, it remained dormant in a bitcoin wallet address controlled by the attackers for as long as 4 years, with almost no movement, which obviously does not align with the typical behavior of hackers eager to cash out and pursue profit, but rather resembles a precise operation orchestrated by a 'national-level hacker organization.' It wasn't until June 2024 that this stolen bitcoin was transferred to a new bitcoin wallet address, and it has remained untouched since.

On October 14, 2025, the U.S. Department of Justice announced criminal charges against Chen Zhi and stated that 127,000 Bitcoin held by Chen and his Prince Group were seized. Various pieces of evidence show that this significant amount of Bitcoin, seized by the U.S. government, was indeed stolen from LuBian mining pool as early as 2020 by hacker attackers through technical means. In other words, the U.S. government may have seized the 127,000 Bitcoin held by Chen Zhi through hacker techniques as early as 2020, representing a typical 'black eat black' incident orchestrated by a state-level hacker organization. This report analyzes the key technical details of the incident from a technical perspective, focusing on the analysis of the background of the stolen Bitcoin, restoring the complete attack timeline, and assessing Bitcoin's security mechanisms to provide valuable security insights for the cryptocurrency industry and users.

1\. Event Background Information

LuBian Mining Pool (LuBian mining pool) was established in early 2020 and is a rapidly rising Bitcoin mining pool, primarily operating in China and Iran. In December 2020, LuBian mining pool suffered a massive hacker attack, resulting in over 90% of its Bitcoin holdings being stolen. The total amount stolen was 127272.06953176 BTC, which closely matches the 127271 BTC mentioned in the U.S. Department of Justice indictment.

The operational model of the LuBian mining pool includes centralized storage and distribution of mining rewards. The Bitcoin in the pool address is not stored in regulated centralized exchanges but exists in non-custodial wallets. From a technical perspective, non-custodial wallets (also known as cold wallets or hardware wallets) are considered the ultimate safe haven for crypto assets. Unlike exchange accounts that can be frozen by a decree, they are more like a bank vault that belongs solely to the holder, with the key (private key) only in the holder's possession.

Bitcoin, as a cryptocurrency, uses on-chain addresses to identify the ownership and flow of Bitcoin assets. Mastering the private key of an on-chain address allows complete control over the Bitcoin in that address. According to reports from on-chain analysis agencies, the significant Bitcoin controlled by the U.S. government and Chen Zhi highly overlaps with the LuBian mining pool hack incident. On-chain data records show that on December 29, 2020, Beijing time, the core Bitcoin wallet address of LuBian experienced an abnormal transfer, with a total amount of 127272.06953176 BTC, which closely matches the 127271 BTC mentioned in the U.S. Department of Justice indictment. After this batch of stolen Bitcoin was transferred abnormally, it remained dormant until June 2024. From June 22 to July 23, 2024, this batch of stolen Bitcoin was transferred again to new on-chain addresses and has remained untouched since. The well-known blockchain tracking tool platform ARKHAM has marked these final addresses as held by the U.S. government. Currently, the U.S. government has not disclosed how it obtained the private keys of Chen Zhi's significant Bitcoin on-chain addresses in the indictment.

2\. Attack Link Analysis

It is well known that in the world of blockchain, random numbers are the cornerstone of cryptographic security. Bitcoin employs asymmetric encryption technology, and Bitcoin private keys are a string of 256-bit binary random numbers, theoretically requiring 2^256 attempts to crack, which is nearly impossible. However, if this string of 256-bit binary private key is not entirely randomly generated, for example, if 224 bits follow a specified pattern and only 32 bits are randomly generated, it greatly reduces the strength of the private key, allowing brute force cracking with only 2^32 (approximately 4.29 billion) attempts. For instance, in September 2022, the British cryptocurrency market maker Wintermute was stolen $160 million due to a similar pseudo-random number vulnerability.

In August 2023, an overseas security research team, MilkSad, first publicly disclosed a vulnerability in a third-party key generation tool related to the pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) and successfully applied for a CVE number (CVE-2023-39910). In the research report published by the team, it was mentioned that the LuBian Bitcoin mining pool had a similar vulnerability, as all 25 Bitcoin addresses mentioned in the U.S. Department of Justice indictment were included among the addresses that were hacked.

![](https://public.bnbstatic.com/image/cms/crawler/BLOCKBEATS_NEWS/17baa6b9-443c-4243-9b3f-5447196731e6.png)

Figure 2: List of 25 Bitcoin wallet addresses in the U.S. Department of Justice indictment

LuBian Bitcoin mining pool, as a non-custodial wallet system, relies on a custom private key generation algorithm to manage funds, with Bitcoin wallet addresses dependent on this algorithm. The private key generation does not use the recommended 256-bit binary random number standard but relies on a 32-bit binary random number, which has a fatal flaw: it solely depends on a timestamp or weak inputs as seeds for the 'pseudo-random number generator' Mersenne Twister (MT19937-32). A pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) is equivalent to the randomness of a 4-byte integer and can be efficiently brute-forced in modern computing. Mathematically, the probability of cracking is 1/2^32; for example, if the attack script tests 1 million keys per second, the cracking time is about 4200 seconds (approximately 1.17 hours). In practice, optimization tools like Hashcat or custom scripts can further accelerate this process. Attackers exploited this vulnerability to steal a significant amount of Bitcoin from the LuBian mining pool.

Through technical tracing, the complete timeline and related details of the LuBian mining pool hack are as follows:

1\. Attack and Theft Phase: Beijing time December 29, 2020

Event: Hackers exploited the pseudo-random number vulnerability in the LuBian mining pool's Bitcoin wallet address private key generation to brute force over 5,000 weak random wallet addresses (wallet type: P2WPKH-nested-in-P2SH, prefix 3). Within about 2 hours, approximately 127272.06953176 BTC (then worth about $3.5 billion) was drained from these wallet addresses, leaving less than 200 BTC. All suspicious transactions shared the same transaction fee, indicating that the attack was executed by an automated batch transfer script.

Sender: LuBian mining pool weak random Bitcoin wallet address group (controlled by the LuBian mining operation entity, part of Chen Zhi's Prince Group);

Receiver: Group of Bitcoin wallet addresses controlled by the attacker (addresses not disclosed);

Transfer Path: Weak wallet address group → Attacker wallet address group;

Correlation Analysis: The total amount stolen was 127272.06953176 BTC, which closely matches the 127271 BTC mentioned in the U.S. Department of Justice indictment.

2\. Dormant Phase: Beijing time December 30, 2020, to June 22, 2024

Event: This batch of Bitcoin has been stored in Bitcoin wallet addresses controlled by the attacker for nearly 4 years since being stolen through the pseudo-random number vulnerability in 2020, remaining dormant with less than one ten-thousandth of a dust transaction possibly used for testing.

Correlation Analysis: This batch of Bitcoin remained almost untouched until June 22, 2024, when it was fully taken over by the U.S. government, which clearly does not conform to the typical nature of hackers eager to cash out and pursue profits but rather resembles a precise action orchestrated by a state-level hacking organization.

3\. Recovery Attempt Phase: Early 2021, July 4 and 26, 2022, Beijing time

Event: After this batch of Bitcoin was stolen, during its dormant period in early 2021, the LuBian pool sent over 1,500 messages via the Bitcoin OP\_RETURN feature (costing about 1.4 BTC in fees), embedded in the blockchain data area, pleading with hackers to return the funds. Example message: 'Please return our funds, we'll pay a reward.' On July 4 and 26, 2022, the LuBian pool again sent messages through the Bitcoin OP\_RETURN feature, with message examples: 'MSG from LB. To the whitehat who is saving our asset, you can contact us through 1228btc@gmail.com to discuss the return of asset and your reward.'

Sender: Lubian weak random Bitcoin wallet address (controlled by Lubian mining operation entity, part of Chen Zhi's Prince Group);

Receiver: Group of Bitcoin wallet addresses controlled by the attacker;

Transfer Path: Weak wallet address group → Attacker wallet address group; small transactions embedded in OP\_RETURN);

Correlation Analysis: After the theft incident occurred, these messages confirmed that the LuBian mining pool, as the sender, made multiple attempts to contact 'third-party hackers' to request the return of assets and discuss ransom matters.

4\. Activation and Transfer Phase: Beijing time June 22 to July 23, 2024

Event: Bitcoin in the group of wallet addresses controlled by the attacker was activated from a dormant state and transferred to the final Bitcoin wallet address. The final wallet address has been marked by the well-known blockchain tracking tool platform ARKHAM as held by the U.S. government.

Sender: Group of Bitcoin wallet addresses controlled by the attacker;

Receiver: Newly integrated final wallet address group (not disclosed but confirmed to be wallet addresses controlled by the U.S. government)

Transfer Path: Attacker-controlled Bitcoin wallet address group → U.S. government-controlled wallet address group;

Correlation Analysis: This batch of stolen significant Bitcoin remained dormant for almost 4 years without movement before ultimately being controlled by the U.S. government.

5\. Announcement of Seizure Phase: October 14, 2025, U.S. local time

Event: The U.S. Department of Justice issued a statement announcing charges against Chen Zhi and 'seizing' his 127,000 BTC.

At the same time, due to the public mechanism of blockchain, all Bitcoin transaction records are publicly traceable. Based on this, this report traces the source of the massive Bitcoin stolen from the LuBian weak random Bitcoin wallet address (controlled by the LuBian mining operation entity and possibly belonging to Chen Zhi's Prince Group), with a total of 127272.06953176 stolen Bitcoins. The sources include: approximately 17,800 from independent 'mining,' about 2,300 from mining pool wage income, and 107,100 from exchanges and other channels. Preliminary results show discrepancies with the U.S. Department of Justice indictment that claimed all sources came from illegal income.

3\. Vulnerability Technical Detail Analysis

1\. Bitcoin Wallet Address Private Key Generation:

The core of the LuBian mining pool vulnerability lies in its private key generator using a flaw similar to the 'MilkSad' defect in Libbitcoin Explorer. Specifically, the system employs the Mersenne Twister (MT19937-32) pseudo-random number generator initialized with only a 32-bit seed, leading to effective entropy of only 32 bits. This PRNG is not cryptographically secure, making it easy to predict and reverse-engineer. Attackers can enumerate all possible 32-bit seeds (0 to 2^32-1), generate corresponding private keys, and check for matches with the public key hash of known wallet addresses.

In the Bitcoin ecosystem, the private key generation process typically is: random seed → SHA-256 hash → ECDSA private key.

The implementation of the LuBian mining pool's base library may be based on custom code or open-source libraries (such as Libbitcoin), but it neglects the security of entropy. Similarities to the MilkSad vulnerability indicate that the 'bx seed' command of Libbitcoin Explorer also uses the MT19937-32 random number generator, relying only on timestamps or weak inputs as seeds, resulting in private keys that can be brute-forced. In the LuBian attack incident, over 5,000 wallets were affected, indicating that this vulnerability is systemic and may stem from code reuse during bulk wallet generation.

2\. Simulated Attack Process:

(1) Identify Target Wallet Addresses (through on-chain monitoring of LuBian mining pool activity);

(2) Enumerate 32-bit seeds: for seed in 0 to 4294967295;

(3) Generate Private Key: private\_key = SHA256(seed);

(4) Derive Public Keys and Addresses: using ECDSA SECP256k1 curve calculations;

(5) Match: If the derived address matches the target, use the private key to sign the transaction to steal funds;

Comparison with Similar Vulnerabilities: This vulnerability is similar to the 32-bit entropy flaw in Trust Wallet, which led to the mass cracking of Bitcoin wallet addresses; the 'MilkSad' vulnerability in Libbitcoin Explorer also exposed private keys due to low entropy. These cases stem from legacy issues in early codebases that did not adopt the BIP-39 standard (12-24 word seed phrases providing high entropy). The LuBian mining pool may have used a custom algorithm aimed at simplifying management but neglected security.

Defense Gaps: LuBian mining pool did not implement multisig, hardware wallets, or hierarchical deterministic wallets (HD wallets), all of which could enhance security. On-chain data shows that the attack covered multiple wallets, indicating a systemic flaw rather than a single point of failure.

3\. On-Chain Evidence and Recovery Attempts:

OP\_RETURN Message: LuBian mining pool sent over 1,500 messages via Bitcoin's OP\_RETURN feature, costing 1.4 BTC, pleading with attackers to return the funds. These messages were embedded in the blockchain, proving to be actions of the real owners rather than forgeries. Example messages include 'Please return the funds' or similar pleas, distributed across multiple transactions.

4\. Attack Correlation Analysis:

On October 14, 2025, the U.S. Department of Justice listed 25 Bitcoin wallet addresses in the criminal indictment against Chen Zhi (case number 1:25-cr-00416), which hold approximately 127,271 BTC, with a total value of about $15 billion, and have been seized. Through blockchain analysis and official document review, these addresses are highly correlated with the LuBian mining pool attack incident.

Direct Association: Blockchain analysis shows that the 25 addresses in the U.S. Department of Justice indictment are precisely the final holding addresses of the Bitcoin stolen in the 2020 attack on LuBian mining pool. Elliptic reports that this batch of Bitcoin was 'stolen' from LuBian mining pool's mining operations in 2020. Arkham Intelligence confirms that the funds seized by the U.S. Department of Justice directly originated from the LuBian mining pool theft incident.

Indictment Evidence Association: The U.S. Department of Justice indictment does not directly name the 'LuBian hack,' but mentions that the funds originated from 'the stolen attacks on Iranian and Chinese Bitcoin mining operations,' which aligns with the on-chain analysis from Elliptic and Arkham Intelligence.

Attack Behavior Association: From the perspective of the attack method, the significant Bitcoin from the LuBian mining pool was stolen after being technically attacked in 2020 and remained dormant for 4 years, with only less than one ten-thousandth of a dust transaction occurring during that time. It was not until 2024, just before being fully taken over by the U.S. government, that it moved almost not at all, which does not conform to the typical nature of hackers eager to cash out and pursue profits, indicating a precise action likely orchestrated by a state-level hacking organization. Analysis suggests that the U.S. government may have controlled this batch of Bitcoin as early as December 2020.

4\. Impact and Recommendations

The LuBian mining pool incident of 2020 has far-reaching implications, leading to the actual dissolution of the pool, with losses amounting to over 90% of total assets at that time, while the stolen Bitcoin's current value has risen to $15 billion, highlighting the amplified risks of price volatility.

The LuBian mining pool incident exposed systemic risks in random number generation within the cryptocurrency toolchain. To prevent similar vulnerabilities, the blockchain industry should use cryptographically secure pseudo-random number generators (CSPRNG); implement multi-layer defenses, including multisig, cold storage, and regular audits, to avoid custom private key generation algorithms; and pools should integrate real-time on-chain monitoring and abnormal transfer alert systems. Ordinary users should avoid using unverified key generation modules from open-source communities. This incident also reminds us that even with the high transparency of blockchain, weak security foundations can lead to disastrous consequences. It also highlights the importance of cybersecurity in the future development of the digital economy and digital currency.
