# Mindanao

Mindanao

Island in the Philippines

For the river, see [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao "Rio Grande de Mindanao"). For the 2019 film, see [Mindanao (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao_(film) "Mindanao (film)"). For the old sultanate, see [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao"). For other uses, see [Mindanao (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao_(disambiguation) "Mindanao (disambiguation)").

Not to be confused with [Mendanau Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendanau_Island "Mendanau Island").

Mindanao

**Mindanao** ([/ˌmɪndəˈnaʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/28/En-ca-Mindanao.oga/En-ca-Mindanao.oga.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:En-ca-Mindanao.oga "File:En-ca-Mindanao.oga"), [_MIN-də-NOW_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is the [second-largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_Philippines "List of islands of the Philippines") in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), after [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon "Luzon"), and [seventh-most populous island in the world.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_population "List of islands by population") Located in the southern region of the [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago "Archipelago"), the island is part of an island group of the same name that also includes its adjacent islands, notably the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago "Sulu Archipelago"). According to the 2020 census, Mindanao had a population of 26,252,442, while the entire island group had an estimated population of 27,384,138 as of 2024.

Mindanao is divided into six administrative regions: the [Zamboanga Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula "Zamboanga Peninsula"), [Northern Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mindanao "Northern Mindanao"), the [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga "Caraga") region, the [Davao region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Region "Davao Region"), [Soccsksargen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccsksargen "Soccsksargen"), and the autonomous region of [Bangsamoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro "Bangsamoro"). According to the 2020 census, [Davao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City "Davao City") is the most populous city on the island, with 1,776,949 people, followed by [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City") (pop. 977,234), [Cagayan De Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_De_Oro "Cagayan De Oro") (pop. 728,402), [General Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos "General Santos") (pop. 722,059), [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan "Butuan") (pop. 372,910), and [Cotabato City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_City "Cotabato City") (pop. 325,079).[\[5\]](#ref-5)

The vast majority of Mindanao's residents declare affiliation to various Christian groups, tantamounting roughly to two thirds of the population, while the Islamic religion sums up the island's (and the country's) largest minority, which has been reported or estimated to represent from 15 to 25%.[\[6\]](#ref-dd)[\[7\]](#ref-bb)[\[8\]](#ref-cc) Ethnic minority groups in Mindanao [whose members are predominantly Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people "Moro people") account for most of [Muslims in Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines "Islam in the Philippines").[\[9\]](#ref-DoS)[\[10\]](#ref-aa)[\[6\]](#ref-dd) The [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro "Bangsamoro") stands out as the most predominantly Islamic region, where, according to the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Philippine_census "2020 Philippine census"), above 89% of the population identified as Muslims.[\[7\]](#ref-bb) Reports on religious demographics are often considered unreliable or inaccurate, though, as Mindanao is a deeply-divided society.[\[10\]](#ref-aa) Humanists, atheists, Muslims, and other religious minorities are concerned about discrimination, and human rights abuses have been denounced.[\[9\]](#ref-DoS) Therefore, the appropriate methodology to access its ethno-religious diversity is disputed.[\[10\]](#ref-aa) The Philippine government's inherent failure to measure religious demographics is also argued by the [National Commission on Muslim Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Commission_on_Muslim_Filipinos "National Commission on Muslim Filipinos") (NCMF).[\[9\]](#ref-DoS)

Mindanao is considered the major [breadbasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadbasket "Breadbasket") of the Philippines.[\[6\]](#ref-dd)[\[11\]](#ref-11)[\[12\]](#ref-investvine)

## Etymology

The name _Mindanao_ is derived from a compound word in the [Maguindanao language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language "Maguindanao language"): _min_, a past tense verb prefix, and _danao_, meaning 'lake'. Together, they translate to 'land of where the lakes are found'. This name is also associated to the [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people "Maguindanao people") people, where the [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao") once held significant influence, and where lakes with varying depths are also common among the provinces of Mindanao.[\[1\]](#ref-mainit)

## History

### Prehistory

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Filippine%2C_provincia_di_agusan%2C_immagine_hindu%2C_statuetta_in_oro_massiccio%2C_xiii_secolo.jpg/500px-Filippine%2C_provincia_di_agusan%2C_immagine_hindu%2C_statuetta_in_oro_massiccio%2C_xiii_secolo.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Filippine,_provincia_di_agusan,_immagine_hindu,_statuetta_in_oro_massiccio,_xiii_secolo.jpg) The 21-karat gold [Agusan image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_image "Agusan image") statue (900–950 CE) discovered in 1917 on the banks of the [Wawa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wawa_River_(Agusan_del_Sur) "Wawa River (Agusan del Sur)") near [Esperanza, Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanza,_Agusan_del_Sur "Esperanza, Agusan del Sur")[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/Krieger_1926_Philippine_ethnic_weapons_Plate_20.png/500px-Krieger_1926_Philippine_ethnic_weapons_Plate_20.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Krieger_1926_Philippine_ethnic_weapons_Plate_20.png) A 1926 photograph of Bagobo (Manobo) warriors

Archaeological findings on the island point to evidence of human activity dating back about ten thousand years. Around 1500 BC, [Austronesian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people "Austronesian people") spread throughout the Philippines.

The [Subanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subanen_people "Subanen people") are believed to have settled in the [Zamboanga Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula "Zamboanga Peninsula") during the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") era c. 4500–2000 BC.\[ _[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\][\[13\]](#ref-Subanon_History)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] Evidence of stone tools in [Zamboanga del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_del_Norte "Zamboanga del Norte") may indicate a late Neolithic presence. Ceramic burial jars, both unglazed and glazed, Chinese [celadons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celadon "Celadon"), gold ornaments, beads, and bracelets have been found in caves. Many of the ceramic objects are from the [Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty") and [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") periods. Evidently, there was a long history of trade between the Subanon and the Chinese.

### Rajahnates and Hindu-Buddhism

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Spanish_map_of_Mindanao.jpg/500px-Spanish_map_of_Mindanao.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Spanish_map_of_Mindanao.jpg) An old Spanish map of Mindanao island.

In the [classic epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(900%E2%80%931521) "History of the Philippines (900–1521)") of Philippine history (900 AD onwards), the people of Mindanao were heavily exposed to [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") and [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist "Buddhist") influence and beliefs from Indonesia and Malaysia. Indianized [abugida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abugida "Abugida") scripts such as [Kawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawi_alphabet "Kawi alphabet") and [baybayin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baybayin "Baybayin") were introduced from Java and an extinct intermediate from Sulawesi or Borneo respectively. Cultural icons of the [sarong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarong "Sarong") (known as _[malong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malong "Malong")_ or _patadyong_), the _pudong_ [turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban "Turban"), [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk "Silk"), and [batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik "Batik") and [ikat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikat "Ikat") weaving and dyeing methods were introduced. Artifacts found from this era include a golden [kinnara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinnara "Kinnara"), a [golden image believed by some to be a Tara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_image "Agusan image"), and a [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha "Ganesha") pendant. These cultural traits passed from Mindanao into the [Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayas "Visayas") and [Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon "Luzon"), but were subsequently lost or heavily modified after the Spanish arrival in the 16th century.

Hindu-Buddhist cultural influence took root in coastal settlements, syncretizing with indigenous [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animist "Animist") beliefs and customs among the tribes of the interior. The [Butuan Rajahnate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Butuan "Rajahnate of Butuan"), a Hinduized kingdom mentioned in Chinese records as a tributary state in the 10th century, was concentrated along the northeastern coast of [Butuan Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan_Bay "Butuan Bay").[\[14\]](#ref-slate)\[ _[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] The Rajahnate of [Sanmalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanmalan "Sanmalan") in Zamboanga, was also in Mindanao. The _[Darangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darangen "Darangen")_ epic of the Maranao people harkens back to this era as the most complete local version of the _[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")_. The Maguindanao at this time also had strong Hindu beliefs, evidenced by the Ladya Lawana (Rajah [Ravana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravana "Ravana")) epic saga that survives to the present, albeit highly Islamized from the 17th century onward.

The spread of Islam in the Philippines began in the 14th century, mostly through the influence of Muslim merchants from the western [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago "Malay Archipelago"). The first [mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque "Mosque") in the Philippines was built in the mid-14th century in the town of [Simunul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simunul "Simunul"), [Tawi-Tawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawi-Tawi "Tawi-Tawi").[\[14\]](#ref-slate) Around the 16th century, the Muslim sultanates of [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu "Sultanate of Sulu"), [Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_Lanao "Confederate States of Lanao") and [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao") were established from formerly Hindu-Buddhist rajahnates.[\[15\]](#ref-15)

As Islam colonised Mindanao, the natives of the sultanates had to either convert to Islam or pay tribute to their new Muslim rulers. The largest of the Muslim polities in mainland Mindanao was the [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao"), which controlled the southern floodplains of the [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao "Rio Grande de Mindanao") and most of the coastal area of [Illana Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illana_Bay "Illana Bay"), [Moro Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Gulf "Moro Gulf"), [Sarangani Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_Bay "Sarangani Bay") and [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf "Davao Gulf"). The name Mindanao was derived from this sultanate. But most of Mindanao remained animist, especially the [Lumad people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad_people "Lumad people") in the interior. Most of the northern, eastern, and southern coastal regions inhabited by [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans "Visayans") ( [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_people "Surigaonon people") and [Butuanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuanon_people "Butuanon people")) and other groups were later converted to Christianity by the Spanish. Mindanao was then embroiled between a conflict with the Boholano (Visayan) [Dapitan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapitan_Kingdom "Dapitan Kingdom") and the Moluccan [Sultanate of Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ternate "Sultanate of Ternate"). Dapitan which was originally at Bohol was destroyed by an expeditionary force from the Ternate Sultanate and Dapitenyos were forced to relocate to Northern Mindanao where they waged war against the Sultanate of Lanao and established a new Dapitan there.[\[16\]](#ref-16) Mindanaoans then spread out of Mindanao across Southeast Asia, Historian William Henry Scott, quoting the Portuguese manuscript Summa Orientalis, noted that [Mottama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mottama "Mottama") in [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma "Burma") (Myanmar) had a large presence of merchants from Mindanao.[\[17\]](#ref-17)

### Spanish colonization and Christianity

See also: [Spanish–Moro conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93Moro_conflict "Spanish–Moro conflict")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6e/Christian_Filipinos_under_Spanish_army_in_Mindanao_in_their_battle_against_the_Moro_Muslim%2C_circa_1887.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Christian_Filipinos_under_Spanish_army_in_Mindanao_in_their_battle_against_the_Moro_Muslim,_circa_1887.jpg) [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_the_Philippines "Christianity in the Philippines") [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos "Filipinos"), who served under the Spanish Army, searching for [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people "Moro people") rebels during the [Spanish–Moro conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93Moro_conflict "Spanish–Moro conflict"), c. 1887. The insurgency in Mindanao can be traced to the early 16th century.[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Two_Spanish_missionaries_baptise_a_Moro_convert_to_Roman_Catholic%2C_circa_1890.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Two_Spanish_missionaries_baptise_a_Moro_convert_to_Roman_Catholic,_circa_1890.jpg) Two Spanish missionaries [baptizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism "Baptism") a Moro convert to [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism "Catholicism"), circa 1890.

In 1521 Antonio Pigafetta wrote an account of reaching 'Maingdano.' He was with Magellan on the first circumnavigation of the globe and sailing for the king of Spain.[\[18\]](#ref-18)

On February 2, 1543, [Ruy López de Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_L%C3%B3pez_de_Villalobos "Ruy López de Villalobos") was the first Spaniard to reach Mindanao.[\[19\]](#ref-19) He called the island _"Caesarea Caroli"_ after Charles V of the Holy Roman Empire (and I of Spain). Shortly after Spain's colonization of Cebu, it moved on to colonize the [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga "Caraga") region in northeast Mindanao and discovered a significant Muslim presence on the island. Over time a number of tribes in Mindanao converted to Catholicism and built settlements and forts throughout the coastal regions. These settlements endured despite attacks from neighboring Muslim sultanates. The most heavily fortified of them, apart from a short period in 1662 when Spain sent soldiers from the city to Manila after a threat of invasion from the Chinese general [Koxinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koxinga "Koxinga"), was [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City")[\[20\]](#ref-:0)\[ _[unreliable source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources "Wikipedia:Reliable sources")_\] which was settled by soldiers from Peru and Mexico.[\[21\]](#ref-Peru) The sultanates resisted Spanish pressure and attempts to convert them to Christianity during this period.[\[22\]](#ref-22) The [Sultanate of Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ternate "Sultanate of Ternate") of the Maluku Islands formed a close alliance with the sultanates of Mindanao, especially [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao").[\[23\]](#ref-23) Ternate regularly sent military reinforcements to Mindanao to assist the local sultanates in their war against Spanish-controlled Manila.[\[24\]](#ref-GuerreroNakpil2003)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\]

By the late 18th century Spain had geographic dominance over the island, having established settlements and forts in most of Mindanao, including [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City") and [Misamis Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Occidental "Misamis Occidental") to the northwest, [Iligan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan "Iligan"), [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental "Misamis Oriental"), [Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukidnon "Bukidnon"), and [Camiguin Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camiguin#Spanish_colonial_era "Camiguin") to the north, [Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City "Surigao City") and Agusan in the [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga#History "Caraga") region to the east, and [Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City "Davao City") in the island's gulf coast. Spain continued to engage in battles with Muslim sultanates until the end of the 19th century.[\[20\]](#ref-:0)

At the same time as the Philippine revolution against Spain, the [Republic of Zamboanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Zamboanga "Republic of Zamboanga") rose as a revolutionary state in Mindanao before it was absorbed by the oncoming Americans.

### American occupation and Philippine Commonwealth

See also: [Moro Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Rebellion "Moro Rebellion")

In the [Treaty of Paris in 1898](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_in_1898 "Treaty of Paris in 1898") Spain sold the entire Philippine archipelago to the United States for $20 million. The [1900 Treaty of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Washington_(1900) "Treaty of Washington (1900)") and the [1930 Convention Between the United States and Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_Between_the_United_States_and_Great_Britain_(1930) "Convention Between the United States and Great Britain (1930)") clarified the borders between Mindanao and Borneo.

In the early 1900s the Commonwealth government (led by Americans) encouraged citizens from Luzon and Visayas to migrate to Mindanao. Consisting mostly of [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people "Ilocano people"), Cebuanos, and [Ilonggos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people "Hiligaynon people"). Settlers streaming into [Soccsksargen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccsksargen "Soccsksargen") led to the displacement of the [Blaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaan_people "Blaan people") and [Tboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tboli_people "Tboli people") tribes.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Together with [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan "Palawan"), Mindanao was part of an acquisition proposal in 1910 between then- [United States ambassador to Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Ambassador_to_Denmark "United States Ambassador to Denmark") [Maurice Francis Egan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Francis_Egan "Maurice Francis Egan") and several of his Danish friends. Under the proposal, the United States was to trade Mindanao and Palawan to Denmark for [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland "Greenland") and the [Danish West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_West_Indies "Danish West Indies"), and Denmark could then trade Mindanao and Palawan to [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") for [Northern Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Schleswig "Northern Schleswig"), which was then under German rule.[\[25\]](#ref-25)[\[26\]](#ref-26)[\[27\]](#ref-27) Egan's suggestion faded away when [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") erupted, and Denmark eventually regained Northern Schleswig after the German defeat in World War I following the [1920 Schleswig plebiscites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Schleswig_plebiscites "1920 Schleswig plebiscites").[\[28\]](#ref-28)

### World War II

In April 1942 Mindanao, along with the rest of the Philippines, officially entered [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") after [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan") soldiers invaded key cities in the islands.[\[29\]](#ref-29) Many towns and cities were burned to the ground in Mindanao, most notably Davao City, Zamboanga City, Lanao, Cagayan de Oro, Iligan City, and Butuan.[\[30\]](#ref-30) In the months of April and May 1942, Japanese forces defeated US troops commanded by [William F. Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Sharp "William F. Sharp") and [Guy Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Fort "Guy Fort"), in a battle that started at [Malabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabang,_Lanao_del_Sur "Malabang, Lanao del Sur") (a town close to [Gandamatu Macadar, Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Sur "Lanao del Sur")) and ended close to the town of [Ganassi, Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganassi,_Lanao_del_Sur "Ganassi, Lanao del Sur").[\[31\]](#ref-31) Davao City was among the earliest to be occupied by the invading Japanese forces. They immediately fortified the city as a bastion of the Japanese defense system.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Davao City was subjected by the returning forces of Gen. [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur "Douglas MacArthur") to constant bombing before the American Liberation Forces landed in [Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte "Leyte") in October 1944.[\[32\]](#ref-32) Filipino soldiers and local [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Fertig "Wendell Fertig") fighters were actively fighting Japanese forces until liberation at the conclusion of the [Battle of Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mindanao "Battle of Mindanao").[\[33\]](#ref-33)

### Postwar era and Philippine independence

Mindanao was peaceful and increasingly progressive in the postwar period, including the 1950s and the mid-1960s. Ethnic tensions were minimal, and there was essentially no presence of secessionists groups in Mindanao.[\[34\]](#ref-MiclatinArguillas)

### Marcos era (1965–1986)

Main articles: [Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos "Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos"), [Ilaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaga "Ilaga"), [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre "Jabidah massacre"), and [Moro conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_conflict "Moro conflict")

Under [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos")'s administration, Christian groups began to settle in Mindanao, displacing many locals. The population boom resulted in conflicts as the original owners sought their ancestral land domains.[\[35\]](#ref-35)[\[36\]](#ref-36)

The [Marcos administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ferdinand_Marcos "Presidency of Ferdinand Marcos") encouraged new settlers who had emigrated to Mindanao to form a militia, which was eventually called the [Ilaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaga "Ilaga"). Anecdotal evidence states that the Ilaga often committed human rights abuses by targeting the [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people "Moro people") and [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad "Lumad") people, as well as attempting to seize additional territory. It resulted in a lingering animosity between Moro and Christian communities. Mistrust and a cycle of violence are still felt today due to the creation of the Ilaga.[\[37\]](#ref-Case)[\[38\]](#ref-Mackerras_2003)

The [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre "Jabidah massacre") in 1968 is commonly cited as the major flashpoint that ignited the Moro insurgency, and the ensuing ethnic tensions led to the formation of secessionist movements,[\[39\]](#ref-39) such as the [Muslim Independence Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Independence_Movement "Muslim Independence Movement") and the [Bangsamoro Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Liberation_Organization "Bangsamoro Liberation Organization"). These movements were largely political in nature, but the prohibition of political parties after Marcos' 1972 declaration of Martial Law led to the founding and dominance of armed groups such as the [Moro National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_National_Liberation_Front "Moro National Liberation Front") (MNLF), and the [Moro Islamic Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Islamic_Liberation_Front "Moro Islamic Liberation Front") (MILF).[\[38\]](#ref-Mackerras_2003) Ethnic conflicts continued to escalate, leading to incidents like the [1971 Manili massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manili_massacre "Manili massacre"), the [Pata Island massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pata_Island_massacre "Pata Island massacre"), and the [Palimbang massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palimbang_massacre "Palimbang massacre").[\[38\]](#ref-Mackerras_2003)

Additionally, an economic crisis in late 1969 led to social unrest throughout the country, and violent crackdowns on protests led to the radicalization of many students,[\[40\]](#ref-40) with some joining the [New People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People's_Army "New People's Army"), bringing the [New People's Army rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People's_Army_rebellion "New People's Army rebellion") to Mindanao for the first time.[\[34\]](#ref-MiclatinArguillas)

Marcos' declaration led to the shuttering of press outlets – television stations, national newspapers, weekly magazines, community newspapers, and radio stations – throughout the country, including in Mindanao.[\[41\]](#ref-GazetteMLInfographic) The remaining years of the Marcos dictatorship led to the killings of many Mindanao journalists, with prominent examples being [Alex Orcullo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Orcullo "Alex Orcullo") of _Mindanao Currents_ and _Mindaweek_,[\[42\]](#ref-BantayogProfileOrcullo)[\[43\]](#ref-HowieSeverino20170225) and [Jacobo Amatong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobo_Amatong "Jacobo Amatong") of the _Mindanao Observer_.[\[44\]](#ref-BantayogProfileAmatong)[\[45\]](#ref-Maslog1993)

### Fifth Republic (1986–present)

In 1989, the [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao "Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao") (ARMM) was established, constituted by several provinces in Western Mindanao.

In March 2000, President [Joseph Estrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Estrada "Joseph Estrada") declared an "All Out War" against the MILF after it committed a series of terrorist attacks on government buildings, civilians, and foreigners. A number of livelihood intervention projects, from organisations such as [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development "United States Agency for International Development") and the Emergency Livelihood Assistance Program (ELAP), aided in the reconstruction of areas affected by constant battles on the island.[\[46\]](#ref-46)

In December 2009, President [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo "Gloria Macapagal Arroyo") officially placed [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao "Maguindanao") under a state of martial law following the [Maguindanao massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_massacre "Maguindanao massacre").

On September 9, 2013, an [MNLF faction attempted to raise the flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City_crisis "Zamboanga City crisis") of a self-proclaimed Bangsamoro Republik at [Zamboanga City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City_Hall "Zamboanga City Hall") in an armed incursion into parts of the city.

On January 25, 2015, [a shootout took place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamasapano_clash "Mamasapano clash") during a police operation by the Special Action Force (SAF) of the Philippine National Police (PNP) in Tukanalipao, Mamasapano, Maguindanao. The operation, codenamed Oplan Exodus, was intended to capture or kill wanted Malaysian terrorist and bomb-maker Zulkifli Abdhir and other Malaysian terrorists or high-ranking members of the Bangsamoro Islamic Freedom Fighters (BIFF).

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Lanao_sultans.jpg/500px-Lanao_sultans.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lanao_sultans.jpg) Lanao sultans making an open letter to Duterte urging for the quick resolution of the Marawi crisis

In May 2017, President [Rodrigo Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Duterte "Rodrigo Duterte") declared martial law on the entire island group of Mindanao following the [Marawi siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi_crisis "Marawi crisis") by the [Maute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maute_Group "Maute Group") terrorist group.[\[47\]](#ref-47) More than 180,000 people were forced to evacuate [Marawi City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi "Marawi").[\[48\]](#ref-48) Around 165 security forces and 47 residents were confirmed killed in the battle, although Marawi residents believe the number of civilians killed was far higher. The official death toll in the five-month conflict is 1,109, most of which were members of a militant alliance which drew fighters from radical factions of domestic Islamist groups.[\[49\]](#ref-49)

In 2019, the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao "Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao") was established, replacing the former ARMM.

In 2024, former President [Rodrigo Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Duterte "Rodrigo Duterte") called for Mindanao to secede from the Philippines,[\[50\]](#ref-50) reviving the movement started by congressman [Pantaleon Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantaleon_Alvarez "Pantaleon Alvarez").[\[51\]](#ref-51) Secession calls resurfaced just before 2026, as proposed by some personalities in [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro "Cagayan de Oro");[\[52\]](#ref-52) the inclusion of Palawan in the map planned by these individuals for submission to the United Nations [Special Committee on Decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Committee_on_Decolonization "Special Committee on Decolonization") was denounced by Palawan officials the next month.[\[53\]](#ref-53)

Mindanao's economy accounts for 14% of the country's gross domestic product. The region grew 4.9% in 2016 against Luzon's 5.5% and Visayas' 9.1%.

Agriculture, forestry and fishing make up more than 40% of Mindanao's market, being the country's largest supplier of major crops such as pineapples and bananas.[\[54\]](#ref-Mindanao_fold)

There is one defined growth corridor in the island, namely [Metro Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Davao "Metro Davao"). Other growth centers are: Cagayan de Oro, General Santos, Zamboanga City, Cotabato City, and Pagadian City.[\[54\]](#ref-Mindanao_fold)

Being the top-performing economy in Mindanao, [Davao Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Region "Davao Region") has the 5th-biggest economy in the country and the second-fastest-growing economy next to Cordillera Autonomous Region.[\[55\]](#ref-55) While the region's economy is predominantly agri-based, it is now developing into a center for agro-industrial business, trade and tourism. Its competitive advantage is in agri-industry as its products, papayas, mangoes, bananas, pineapples, fresh asparagus, flowers, and fish products are exported internationally. The region can be a vital link to markets in other parts of Mindanao, [Brunei Darussalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei") and parts of [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia").[\[56\]](#ref-56)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\]

There is also a growing call center sector in the region, mostly centered in [Davao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City "Davao City").

### Upcoming developments

Some 2,130 government-led infrastructure projects worth P547.9 billion have also been lined up for Mindanao until 2022.

NEDA officials said that 68% of that budget will be allotted to the transportation sector, while 16% will go to water resources, and 6% to social infrastructure.

Of this amount, 18 infrastructure projects have been identified as "flagship projects", five of them having already been approved by President Rodrigo Duterte.

The projects include the [₱](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_peso "Philippine peso") 35.26 billion Tagum-Davao-Digos Segment of the [Mindanao Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao_Railway "Mindanao Railway"), the ₱40.57 billion Davao airport, the ₱14.62 billion Laguindingan airport, the ₱4.86 billion Panguil Bay Bridge Project, and the ₱5.44 billion Malitubog-Maridagao Irrigation Project, Phase II.

Projects in the pipeline are the second and third phases of the Mindanao Railway; the Agus-Pulangi plant rehabilitation; the Davao expressway; the Zamboanga Fish Port Complex rehabilitation; the Balo-i Plains Flood Control Project; Asbang Small Reservoir Irrigation Project; the Ambal Simuay Sub-Basin of the Mindanao River Basin Flood Control and River Protection Project; as well as the Road Network Development Project in Conflict-Affected Areas in Mindanao project.[\[54\]](#ref-Mindanao_fold)

### Air transport

[Francisco Bangoy International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Bangoy_International_Airport "Francisco Bangoy International Airport") serves as the main gateway to Mindanao, it is the busiest airport on the island and the [third busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_the_Philippines "List of the busiest airports in the Philippines") in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") in 2022.

[Laguindingan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguindingan_Airport "Laguindingan Airport") is the second-busiest airport along with [Zamboanga International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_International_Airport "Zamboanga International Airport") as third-busiest in the island.

There are also smaller airports across Mindanao such as [Bancasi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bancasi_Airport "Bancasi Airport"), [General Santos International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos_International_Airport "General Santos International Airport"), [Labo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labo_Airport "Labo Airport"), [Sayak Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayak_Airport "Sayak Airport") and [Surigao Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_Airport "Surigao Airport").

## Administrative divisions

Further information: [Political divisions of Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_divisions_of_Mindanao "Political divisions of Mindanao")

The island consists of six [administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_Philippines "Regions of the Philippines"),[\[57\]](#ref-57) 23 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_Philippines "Provinces of the Philippines"), and 30 [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines "Cities of the Philippines") (28 provinces and 33 cities if [associated islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_groups_of_the_Philippines "Island groups of the Philippines") are included).

[Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu "Sulu") was a part of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao, but was excluded after the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines "Supreme Court of the Philippines") declared its inclusion in Bangsamoro unconstitutional as the majority of its residents voted against it during the [2019 plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bangsamoro_autonomy_plebiscite "2019 Bangsamoro autonomy plebiscite").[\[58\]](#ref-58) Sulu was officially reverted to Zamboanga Peninsula, its original region before the establishment of the now-defunct Autonoumous Region of Muslim Mindanao, by the virtue of Executive Order No. 91, signed by President [Bongbong Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos "Bongbong Marcos") on July 30, 2025.[\[59\]](#ref-59)

## Geography

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Carte_topographique_de_Mindanao.svg/500px-Carte_topographique_de_Mindanao.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Carte_topographique_de_Mindanao.svg) Geofeatures map of Mindanao

Mindanao is the second-largest [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island "Island") in the Philippines at 97,530 square kilometres (37,660sqmi),[\[2\]](#ref-unep) and is the [seventh-most populous island in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_population "List of islands by population"). The island is mountainous, and is home to [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo "Mount Apo"), the highest mountain in the country. Mindanao is surrounded by four seas: the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea "Sulu Sea") to the west,[\[63\]](#ref-68) the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea "Philippine Sea") to the east, the [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea "Celebes Sea") to the south, and the [Mindanao Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol_Sea "Bohol Sea") to the north.

The island itself is part of an [island group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_groups_of_the_Philippines "Island groups of the Philippines") of the same name, which consists of the mainland, the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago "Sulu Archipelago") and the outlying islands of [Camiguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camiguin "Camiguin"), [Dinagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinagat_Island "Dinagat Island"), [Siargao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siargao "Siargao"), and [Samal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samal,_Davao_del_Norte "Samal, Davao del Norte").

### Mountains

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Foggy_Morning_At_Panimahawa_Ridge.jpg/500px-Foggy_Morning_At_Panimahawa_Ridge.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Foggy_Morning_At_Panimahawa_Ridge.jpg) Mountains in the province of [Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukidnon "Bukidnon")

The mountains of Mindanao can be grouped into ten ranges, including both complex structural mountains and volcanoes. The structural mountains on the extreme eastern and western portions of the island show broad exposures of [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic "Mesozoic") rock, and Ultrabasic rocks at the surface in many places along the east coast. Other parts of the island consist mainly of [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic "Cenozoic") and [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary "Quaternary") volcanic or sedimentary rocks.

In the eastern portion of the island, from [Bilas Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilas_Point?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bilas Point") in [Surigao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_del_Norte "Surigao del Norte") to [Cape San Agustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_San_Agustin?action=edit&redlink=1 "Cape San Agustin") in [Davao Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Oriental "Davao Oriental"), is a range of complex mountains, called the Eastern Pacific Cordillera, known in their northern portion as the [Diwata Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwata_Mountains?action=edit&redlink=1 "Diwata Mountains"). This range is low and rolling in its central portion. A proposed road connecting [Bislig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislig "Bislig") on the east coast with the [Agusan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_River "Agusan River") would pass through 16 kilometers (9.9mi) of broad saddle across the mountains at a maximum elevation of less than 250 meters (820ft); while the existing east–west road from [Lianga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianga,_Surigao_del_Sur "Lianga, Surigao del Sur"), 48km (30mi) north of Bislig, reaches a maximum elevation of only 450m (1,480ft). The Diwata Mountains, north of these low points, are considerably higher and more rugged, reaching an elevation of 2,012m (6,601ft) in [Mount Hilong-Hilong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hilong-Hilong?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mount Hilong-Hilong"), 17 miles (27km) along the eastern portion of [Cabadbaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabadbaran "Cabadbaran"). The southern portion of this range is broader and even more rugged than the northern section. In Davao Oriental, the highest peak is Mount Pandadagsaan (2,670m (8,760ft)).

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Tuktok_ng_Pilipinas.jpg/500px-Tuktok_ng_Pilipinas.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tuktok_ng_Pilipinas.jpg) [Mt. Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Apo "Mt. Apo"), the highest peak in the Philippines

The east-facing coastal regions of Davao and Surigao del Sur are marked by a series of small coastal lowlands separated from each other by rugged forelands which extend to the water's edge. Offshore are numerous [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reefs "Coral reefs") and tiny [islets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islets "Islets"). This remote and forbidding coast is made doubly difficult to access during the months from October to March by heavy surf driven before the northeast trade winds. A few miles offshore is found the [Philippine Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Deep "Philippine Deep"). This ocean trench, reaching measured depths of 34,696 feet (10,575m), is the third-deepest trench, (after the [Mariana Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Trench "Mariana Trench") and [Tonga Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga_Trench "Tonga Trench")) on the earth's surface.

A second north–south mountain range extends from [Talisayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talisayan,_Misamis_Oriental "Talisayan, Misamis Oriental") in the north, to [Tinaca Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinaca_Point "Tinaca Point") in the southernmost point of Mindanao. This mountain range runs along the western borders of the [Agusan del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Norte "Agusan del Norte"), [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur "Agusan del Sur"), and [Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Region "Davao Region") provinces. This range is mainly structural in origin, but it also contains at least three active volcano peaks. The central and northern portions of this range contain several peaks between 2,000 and 2,600m (6,600 and 8,500ft), and here the belt of mountains is about 30 miles (48km) across.

West of [Davao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City "Davao City") stand two inactive volcanoes: [Mount Talomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Talomo "Mount Talomo") at 2,674 meters (8,773ft), and [Mount Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo "Mount Apo") at 2,964m (9,724ft). Mount Apo is the highest point in the Philippines. South of Mount Apo, this central mountain belt is somewhat lower than it is to the north, with peaks averaging only 1,100 to 1,800m (3,600 to 5,900ft).

In Western Mindanao, a range of complex structural mountains form the long, hand-like [Zamboanga Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula_(geographical_region) "Zamboanga Peninsula (geographical region)"). These mountains, reaching heights of only 1,200 meters (3,900 feet), are not as high as the other structural belts in Mindanao. There are several places in the Zamboanga Mountains where small inter-mountain basins have been created, with some potential for future agricultural development. The northeastern end of this range is marked by the twin peaks of the highest mountain in the Zamboanga Peninsula, the now-extinct volcano [Mount Malindang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Malindang "Mount Malindang"), that towers over [Ozamiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozamiz "Ozamiz") at a height of 2,425m (7,956ft). [Batorampon Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batorampon_Point "Batorampon Point") is the highest mountain at the southernmost end of the peninsula, reaching a height of only 1,335m (4,380ft); it is located in the boundary of [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City").

A series of volcanic mountains is located within the vicinity of [Lake Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lanao "Lake Lanao") forming a broad arc through the [Lanao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Sur "Lanao del Sur"), [Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato "Cotabato") and [Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukidnon "Bukidnon") provinces. At least six of the twenty odd peaks in this area are active and several stand in semi-isolation. The [Butig Peaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butig_Peaks?action=edit&redlink=1 "Butig Peaks"), with their four crater lakes, are easily seen from Cotabato. [Mount Ragang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ragang "Mount Ragang"), an active volcano cone reaching 2,815m (9,236ft), is the most isolated, while the greatest height is reached by [Mount Kitanglad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kitanglad "Mount Kitanglad") at 2,889m (9,478ft).

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Philippinen_mindanao_boot_ph06p73.jpg/500px-Philippinen_mindanao_boot_ph06p73.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Philippinen_mindanao_boot_ph06p73.jpg) Mindanao coast

In [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato "South Cotabato"), is another range of volcanic mountains, this time paralleling the coast. These mountains have a maximum extent of 110 miles (180km) from northwest to southeast and measure some 30 miles (48km) across. One of the well-known mountains here is [Mount Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Parker_(Cotabato) "Mount Parker (Cotabato)"), whose almost circular [crater lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_crater_lake "Volcanic crater lake") measures a mile-and-a-quarter in diameter and lies 300m (980ft) below its 2,040m (6,690ft) summit. [Mount Matutum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Matutum "Mount Matutum") is a protected area and is considered one of the major landmarks in the South Cotabato province.

### Plateaus

Another important physiographic division of Mindanao is the series of upland plateaus in the [Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukidnon "Bukidnon") and [Lanao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Sur "Lanao del Sur") provinces. These [plateaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau "Plateau") are rather extensive and almost surround several [volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanoes "Volcanoes") in this area. The plateaus are made up of basaltic lava flows inter-bedded with volcanic ash and tuff. Near their edges, the plateaus are cut by deep [canyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyons "Canyons"), and at several points waterfalls drop down to the narrow coastal plain. These falls hold considerable promise for the development of hydroelectric energy. Indeed, one such site at [Maria Cristina Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Cristina_Falls "Maria Cristina Falls") has already become a major producer. The rolling plateaus lie at an elevation averaging 700 meters above sea level, and offer relief from the often oppressive heat of the coastal lowlands.

### Lakes and waterfalls

Lake Lanao occupies a large portion of one such plateau in [Lanao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Sur "Lanao del Sur"). The lake is the largest in Mindanao and the second largest in the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"). Roughly triangular in shape, it lies at an elevation of about 780 metres (2,560 ft) above sea level and is surrounded by mountains that rise to more than 2,300 metres (7,500 ft).[\[64\]](#ref-69)

Another of Mindanao's waterfall sites is located in Malabang, 15 miles (24km) south of [Lake Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lanao "Lake Lanao"). Here the Jose Abad Santos Falls are present at the gateway to a 200-hectare national park development.

The Limunsudan Falls, with an approximate height of 800ft (240m), is the highest waterfall in the Philippines; it is located in [Iligan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan_City "Iligan City").

### Valleys, rivers, and plains

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao_%28Cotabato_City%29.jpg/500px-Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao_%28Cotabato_City%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao_(Cotabato_City).jpg) [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao "Rio Grande de Mindanao")

Mindanao contains two large lowland areas in the valleys of the [Agusan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_River "Agusan River") in Agusan, and the [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao "Rio Grande de Mindanao") in [Cotabato City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_City "Cotabato City").

There is some indication that the Agusan Valley occupies a broad syncline between the central mountains and the east-coast mountains. This valley measures 110 miles (180km) from south to north and varies from 20 to 30 miles (32 to 48km) in width. 35 miles (56km) north of the head of [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf "Davao Gulf") lies the watershed between the Agusan and the tributaries of the Libuganon River, which flows to the gulf. The elevation of this divide is well under 200m (660ft), indicating the almost continuous nature of the lowland from the [Mindanao Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol_Sea "Bohol Sea") on the north to Davao Gulf.

The [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao "Rio Grande de Mindanao") and its main tributaries, the Catisan and the [Pulangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River "Pulangi River"), form a valley with a maximum length of 120 miles (190km) and a width which varies from 12 miles (19km) at the river mouth to about 60 miles (97km) in central [Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato "Cotabato"). The southern extensions of this Cotabato Valley extend uninterrupted across a 350-meter (1,150ft) [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_divide "Drainage divide") from [Illana Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illana_Bay "Illana Bay") on the northwest to [Sarangani Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarangani_Bay "Sarangani Bay") on the southeast.

Other lowlands of a coastal nature are to be found in various parts of Mindanao. Many of these are tiny isolated pockets, along the northwest coast of [Zamboanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula "Zamboanga Peninsula"). In other areas such as the Davao Plain, these coastal lowlands are 16km (9.9mi) wide and several times in length.

From [Dipolog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipolog "Dipolog"), eastward along the northern coast of Mindanao approaching [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan "Butuan"), extends a rolling coastal plain of varying width. In [Misamis Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Occidental "Misamis Occidental"), the now dormant [Mount Malindang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Malindang "Mount Malindang") has created a lowland averaging 13km (8.1mi) in width. Shallow [Panguil Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panguil_Bay "Panguil Bay") divides this province from [Lanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Norte "Lanao del Norte"), and is bordered by low-lying, poorly drained lowlands and extensive mangroves. In [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental "Misamis Oriental"), the plain is narrower and in places whittles into rugged capes that reach the sea. East of [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro "Cagayan de Oro"), a rugged peninsula extends into the [Mindanao Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol_Sea "Bohol Sea").

### Climate change

[Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change") is expected to have adverse effects on Mindanao's population, environment, and agriculture.[\[65\]](#ref-70)[\[66\]](#ref-71) Mindanao is already experiencing [severe climate events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change "Effects of climate change") attributed to changes in the Earth's temperature. These climate events include typhoons such as [Typhoon Washi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Washi "Tropical Storm Washi"), [Typhoon Bopha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Bopha "Typhoon Bopha") and [Typhoon Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Rai "Typhoon Rai") as of December 2021. Those storms had a severe impact on the island of Mindanao.[\[67\]](#ref-72)

## Demographics

In 2017, Mindanao had a population of over 25 million. This represented 22 percent of the entire population of the country.[\[68\]](#ref-honshu2)

An American census conducted in the early 1900s noted that the island was inhabited by people "greatly divided in origin, temperament and religion".[\[69\]](#ref-74) Evidence of the island's cultural diversity can be seen in the buildings and ruins of old Spanish settlements in the northwestern peninsula that span eastwards to the southern gulf coast, the site of the ancient [Rajahnate of Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Butuan "Rajahnate of Butuan") in the northeast region ( [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga "Caraga")), the sultanates in the southwest ( [Sultanate of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu "Sultanate of Sulu"), Sultanate of Lanao, [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao "Sultanate of Maguindanao")), a number of Buddhist and Taoist temples, and the numerous indigenous tribes.

Today around 25.8 percent of the household population in Mindanao classified themselves as Cebuanos. Other ethnic groups included Bisaya/Binisaya (18.4%), Hiligaynon/Ilonggo (8.2%), Maguindanaon (5.5%), and Maranao (5.4%). The remaining 36.6 percent belonged to other ethnic groups, including individuals from Luzon and the Lumad people (indigenous peoples of Mindanao). Cebuano registered the highest proportion of ethnic groups in Northern Mindanao and Davao Region with 35.59 percent and 37.76 percent, respectively. In Soccsksargen, it was Hiligaynon/Ilonggo (31.58%), Binisaya/Bisaya (33.10%) in Zamboanga Peninsula, Maranao (26.40%) in BARMM, and Surigaonon (25.67%) in Caraga.[\[70\]](#ref-:1)

Like elsewhere, assimilation from one ethnic group into another is not uncommon in Mindanao. Over the last decades, many Christian migrants from Luzon and Lumad tribes on the island integrated and assimilated into the majority Cebuano-speaking society in Mindanao (Hiligaynon-speaking in the case of Soccsksargen). They identified themselves as Visayans as a result of learning to speak Cebuano or Hiligaynon fluently from their Cebuano or Hiligaynon neighbors. Despite this, many of them still know and retain their non-Visayan roots and some speak their ancestors' language fluently as their second or third language since Mindanao is a melting pot of cultures as a result of southward migration from Luzon and Visayas since the 20th century. Descendants of these migrant Luzon ethnic groups and Lumads, especially newer generations (as Mindanao-born natives), now speak Cebuano or Hiligaynon fluently as their main language with little or no knowledge of their ancestors' native tongues at the time of their southward journey to Mindanao and Lumads developed contact with Cebuano and Hiligaynon speakers. Because the island's inhabitants converted to Christianity and assimilated into the Christian communities, it cannot be always determined whether or not the residents were of indigenous Mindanaoan heritage.[\[71\]](#ref-76)

Dozens of languages are spoken in Mindanao; among them, [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language "Cebuano language"), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language "Hiligaynon language"), [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language "Maguindanao language"), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language "Maranao language"), [Surigaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigaonon_language "Surigaonon language"), [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language "Tausug language"), and [Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano "Chavacano") are most widely spoken. Of the seven aforementioned regional languages, Cebuano (often referred to as _Bisaya_) has the largest number of speakers, being spoken throughout Northern Mindanao (except the southern parts of [Lanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Norte "Lanao del Norte")), the Davao region, the western half of the Caraga region (as well as the city of [Bislig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislig "Bislig") and the municipalities surrounding it in [Surigao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_del_Sur "Surigao del Sur")), the entirety of the Zamboanga Peninsula (with the exception of Zamboanga City), and southern Soccsksargen.

Hiligaynon is the main language of Soccsksargen, where majority of the inhabitants are of ethnic Hiligaynon stock. [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language "Maguindanao language"), [Manobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manobo_language "Manobo language"), [Tboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tboli_language "Tboli language") and [Blaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaan_language "Blaan language") are the indigenous languages spoken in Soccsksargen. [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language "Ilocano language"), a native language of the Luzon [ethnic group of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people "Ilocano people"), is also spoken in some areas in Soccsksargen, where they also share residency with Hiligaynons. Surigaonon is spoken in the eastern half of the Caraga region, mainly by the eponymous Surigaonons. Tausug is widely spoken specifically in the Sulu Archipelago, which comprises the provinces of [Basilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilan "Basilan"), [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu "Sulu"), and [Tawi-Tawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawi-Tawi "Tawi-Tawi"), with a community of speakers residing in Zamboanga City.

Maranao and Maguindanaon are the dominant languages of the eastern territories of the Bangsamoro, respectively, with the former being spoken in [Lanao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Sur "Lanao del Sur") as well as the southern areas of Lanao del Norte, and the latter in the eponymous provinces of [Maguindanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_del_Norte "Maguindanao del Norte") and [Maguindanao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_del_Sur "Maguindanao del Sur") and also in adjacent areas which are part of Soccsksargen. Chavacano is the native language of Zamboanga City and is also the lingua franca of Basilan; it is also spoken in the southernmost fringes of [Zamboanga Sibugay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Sibugay "Zamboanga Sibugay"). It is also spoken, albeit as a minority language, in [Cotabato City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_City "Cotabato City") and Davao City, where dialects of it, respectively, exist, namely Cotabateño and Castellano Abakay, both of which evolved from the variant of the language spoken in Zamboanga City.

[English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English "Philippine English") and [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language "Filipino language") are also widely understood and spoken, with the former being highly utilized in business and academia, and the latter being used to communicate with visitors from Luzon and other parts of Visayas. Filipino is also the main lingua franca of Cotabato City and in BARMM as a whole.

## Tourism

Major tourist spots are scattered throughout Mindanao, consisting mostly of beach resorts, scuba diving resorts, surfing, museums, nature parks, mountain climbing, and river rafting. [Siargao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siargao "Siargao"), best known for its surfing tower in Cloud 9, also has caves, pools, waterfalls, and lagoons.[\[74\]](#ref-79) There are archaeological sites, historical ruins, and museums in [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan "Butuan").[\[75\]](#ref-80) White Island is a popular tourist spot in [Camiguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camiguin "Camiguin"). The Duka Bay[\[76\]](#ref-81) and the Matangale[\[77\]](#ref-82) dive resorts in [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental "Misamis Oriental") offer glass bottomed boat rides and scuba diving lessons. [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro "Cagayan de Oro") has beach resorts, the Mapawa Nature Park, white water rafting and kayaking, museums, and historical landmarks.[\[78\]](#ref-83) Ziplining is the main attraction at the Dahilayan Adventure Park and rock wall climbing at Kiokong in [Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukidnon "Bukidnon").[\[79\]](#ref-84) [Iligan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan "Iligan") has the Maria Cristina Falls, Tinago Falls, nature parks, beaches, and historical landmarks.[\[80\]](#ref-85) There are parks, historical buildings, the Vinta Ride at Paseo del Mar, boat villages, 11 Islands (commonly called as _Onçe Islas_), 17th-century [Fort Pilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pilar "Fort Pilar") Shrine and Museum and the world-renowned [_Pink Sand Beach_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Santa_Cruz_Island "Great Santa Cruz Island") of Sta. Cruz in [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City").[\[81\]](#ref-86)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] There are festivals, fireworks, and the Beras Bird Sanctuary in [Takurong City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacurong "Tacurong").[\[82\]](#ref-87) [Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Region "Davao Region") has [Mt Apo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Apo "Mount Apo"), parks, museums, beaches, historical landmarks, and scuba diving resorts.[\[83\]](#ref-88)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\]

Many areas in Mindanao suffer rotating 12-hour blackouts due to the island's woefully inadequate power supply.[\[84\]](#ref-89) The island is forecast to continue suffering from a 200-megawatt power deficit until 2015, when the private sector begins to operate new capacity. [Aboitiz Equity Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboitiz_Equity_Ventures "Aboitiz Equity Ventures"), a publicly listed holding company, has committed to supplying 1,200 megawatts through a coal-fired plant on the border of Davao City and Davao del Sur that is slated for operation by 2018.[\[85\]](#ref-autogenerated1) The [Agus-Pulangui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agus-Pulangui?action=edit&redlink=1 "Agus-Pulangui") hydropower complex, which supplies more than half of Mindanao's power supply, is currently producing only 635 megawatts of its 982 megawatt capacity due to the heavy siltation of the rivers that power the complex. [Zamboanga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_City "Zamboanga City"), an urbanized center in southwest Mindanao, is expected to begin experiencing daily three-hour brownouts due to the [National Power Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Power_Corporation "National Power Corporation")'s decision to reduce power supply in the city by 10 megawatts.[\[86\]](#ref-power1)

The [Manila Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Electric_Company "Manila Electric Company") (Meralco), the largest power distributor in the Philippines, and [Global Business Power Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Business_Power_Corp?action=edit&redlink=1 "Global Business Power Corp") (GBPC), also a major provider, have announced plans to enter Mindanao for the first time to establish solutions for the power problems within the island.[\[86\]](#ref-power1)
