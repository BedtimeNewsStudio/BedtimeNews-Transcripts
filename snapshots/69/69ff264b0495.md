The **Syria–Lebanon campaign**, also known as **Operation Exporter**, was the liberation of [Syria and Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon "French Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon") from German control in June and July 1941 by [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth "British Commonwealth") forces during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").

On 1 April 1941, after the [Iraqi coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1941 Iraqi coup d'état"), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq "Kingdom of Iraq") was controlled by Iraqi nationalists led by [Rashid Ali al-Gaylani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Ali_al-Gaylani "Rashid Ali al-Gaylani"), who appealed for Italian and German support. The [Anglo-Iraqi War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War "Anglo-Iraqi War") (2–31 May 1941) led to the overthrow of the Ali regime and the installation of a pro-British government. During this conflict, Admiral [François Darlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Darlan "François Darlan") allowed German aircraft to use Vichy airfields in Syria for attacks against the British in Iraq.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-3) The British invaded Syria and Lebanon in June to prevent the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers") from using the [Syrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Syrian_Republic "Mandatory Syrian Republic") and [French Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon "Greater Lebanon") as bases for attacks on [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Egypt "Kingdom of Egypt"), during an invasion scare in the aftermath of the Axis victories in the [Battle of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Greece "Battle of Greece") (6–30 April 1941) and the [Battle of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Crete "Battle of Crete") (20 May – 1 June). In the [Western Desert Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Desert_Campaign "Western Desert Campaign") (1940–1943) in North Africa, the British fought [Operation Battleaxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Battleaxe "Operation Battleaxe") to end the [siege of Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tobruk "Siege of Tobruk") and the [East African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Campaign_(World_War_II) "East African Campaign (World War II)") (10 June 1940 – 27 November 1941) in Ethiopia and Eritrea.

The French conducted a vigorous defence of Syria but, on 10 July, as the [21st Australian Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Brigade_(Australia) "21st Brigade (Australia)") was on the verge of entering [Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut "Beirut"), the French sought an armistice. At one minute past midnight on 12 July, a ceasefire came into effect and ended the campaign.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Playfair_2004,_pp._221,_335%E2%80%93337-4) The [Armistice of Saint Jean d'Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Saint_Jean_d'Acre "Armistice of Saint Jean d'Acre") (Convention of [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel "Acre, Israel")) was signed on 14 July at the Sidney Smith Barracks on the outskirts of the city. While the surrender was being held, _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ magazine referred to the Syria–Lebanon campaign as a "mixed show", and the campaign to this day remains relatively unknown, even in the countries that participated in it.

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

On 28 May 1941, Admiral [François Darlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Darlan "François Darlan"), on behalf of [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France "Vichy France"), signed the [Paris Protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Protocols "Paris Protocols"), an agreement with the Germans which granted Germany access to military facilities in Vichy-controlled Syria.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-5) The protocols remained unratified, but [Charles Huntziger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Huntziger "Charles Huntziger"), the [Vichy Minister of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_(France) "Minister of Defence (France)"), sent orders to [Henri Dentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Dentz "Henri Dentz"), the [High Commissioner for the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioners_of_French-mandated_Syria_and_Lebanon "High Commissioners of French-mandated Syria and Lebanon"), to allow aircraft of the German _[Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftwaffe "Luftwaffe")_ and the Italian _[Regia Aeronautica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regia_Aeronautica "Regia Aeronautica")_ to refuel in Syria. Marked as Iraqi aircraft, Axis aircraft under _[Fliegerführer Irak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fliegerf%C3%BChrer_Irak "Fliegerführer Irak")_ landed in Syria _en route_ to the [Kingdom of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq "Kingdom of Iraq") during the Anglo-Iraqi War. Darlan, a confirmed Anglophobe, allowed the German and Italian aircraft to use Syrian airfields partly because of attacks on Vichy French ships by the British. He calculated that, since July 1940, 167 French ships had been seized by British forces.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Sutherland35-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-7)

Under the Paris Protocols, an agreement was also made for the French to launch an offensive against the British-held Iraqi oilfields, a proposal put forward by Darlan.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Sutherland35-6) As well as the use of Syrian airfields, the Germans also requested permission from the Vichy authorities to use Syrian railways to send armaments to Iraqi nationalists in [Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul "Mosul"). In return for Darlan's enthusiastic pro-Axis gestures, the Germans released 7,000 French prisoners of war, many of whom were professional officers and non-commissioned officers.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Sutherland35-6) Despite government prodding, General [Archibald Percival Wavell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Wavell,_1st_Earl_Wavell "Archibald Wavell, 1st Earl Wavell"), the [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief") of [Middle East Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Command "Middle East Command"), was reluctant to intervene in Syria, because of the situation in the Western Desert, the imminent German attack on Crete and doubts about the [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French "Free French") intentions.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTERaugh1993216%E2%80%93218-8)

Dentz was Commander in Chief of the _Armée du Levant_ ( [Army of the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Levant "Army of the Levant")), which had regular metropolitan colonial troops and _troupes spéciales_ (special troops, indigenous Syrian and Lebanese soldiers).[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Mollo144-9) There were seven infantry battalions of regular French troops at his disposal, which included the [6th Foreign Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Foreign_Infantry_Regiment "6th Foreign Infantry Regiment") of the [French Foreign Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Foreign_Legion "French Foreign Legion"), the [24th Colonial Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24th_Colonial_Infantry_Regiment?action=edit&redlink=1 "24th Colonial Infantry Regiment (page does not exist)") and eleven infantry battalions of "special troops", including at least 5,000 cavalry in horsed and motorized units, two artillery groups and supporting units.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Mollo144-9) The Army had 35,000 troops with 35,000 regulars including 8,000 French and 25,000 Syrian and Lebanese infantry. The French had 90 tanks (according to British estimates), the [_Armée de l'air_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_French_Air_Force "Vichy French Air Force") had 90 aircraft (increasing to 289 aircraft after reinforcement) and the _Marine nationale_ ( [French Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Navy "French Navy")) had two [destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A9pard_class_destroyer "Guépard class destroyer"), [_Guépard_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_destroyer_Gu%C3%A9pard "French destroyer Guépard") and [_Valmy_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_destroyer_Valmy "French destroyer Valmy") as well as one sloop, [_Élan_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_sloop_%C3%89lan "French sloop Élan"), and three submarines.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-11)

On 14 May 1941, a [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force "Royal Air Force") (RAF) [Bristol Blenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Blenheim "Bristol Blenheim") bomber crew flying a reconnaissance mission over [Palmyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmyra_(modern) "Palmyra (modern)"), in central Syria, spotted a [Junkers Ju 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_90 "Junkers Ju 90") transport taking off, with more German and Italian aircraft seen later that day. An attack on the airfield was authorised later that evening.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-12) Attacks against German and Italian aircraft staging through Syria continued, and the British claimed six [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers") aircraft destroyed by 8 June. Vichy French forces shot down a Blenheim on 28 May, killing the crew, and forced down another on 2 June.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Sutherland43-13) French [Morane-Saulnier M.S.406](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morane-Saulnier_M.S.406 "Morane-Saulnier M.S.406") fighters also escorted German [Junkers Ju 52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_52 "Junkers Ju 52") aircraft into Iraq on 28 May.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Sutherland43-13) The RAF shot down a Vichy [Martin 167F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Maryland "Martin Maryland") bomber over the British [Mandate of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine") on 6 June.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-14) While German interest in the French mandates of Syria and Lebanon was limited, [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") permitted reinforcement of the French troops by allowing French aircraft _en route_ from [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria "Algeria") to Syria to fly over Axis-controlled territory and refuel at the German-controlled [Eleusina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleusina "Eleusina") air base in [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-15) The activity of German aircraft based in Greece and the [Dodecanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecanese_Islands "Dodecanese Islands") [Italian Islands of the Aegean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Islands_of_the_Aegean "Italian Islands of the Aegean") was interpreted by the British as support for Vichy troops, but although Dentz briefly considered accepting German assistance, he rejected the offer on 13 June.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-16) By the end of the Anglo-Iraqi War, all 14 of the original German [Messerschmitt Bf 110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Bf_110 "Messerschmitt Bf 110") aircraft sent to Syria and five [Heinkel He 111](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinkel_He_111 "Heinkel He 111") and a large number of transport aircraft had been destroyed by the British.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-17)

### Palestine and Iraq

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Palestine and Iraq")\]

See also: [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine") and [Kingdom of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq "Kingdom of Iraq")

The British-led invasion of Syria and Lebanon aimed at preventing Germany from using the [Mandatory Syrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Syrian_Republic "Mandatory Syrian Republic") and [Greater Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon "Greater Lebanon"), controlled by Vichy France, for attacks on [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Egypt "Kingdom of Egypt") as the British fought the [Western Desert Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Desert_Campaign "Western Desert Campaign") (1940–1943) against Axis forces in [North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa "North Africa"). In September 1936, the French had ceded autonomy to Syria but it retained rights to maintain some armed forces and two airfields in the territory. The British were concerned about potential attacks by Nazi Germany from Syria and Lebanon, or that the Germans might gain access to airfields there. An additional concern related to the possibility of German troops on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II) "Eastern Front (World War II)") linking up with Vichy forces if Germany defeated the Soviet Union, by advancing south through the Caucasus. Both contingencies were unlikely, but would have exposed Allied forces in Egypt to a northern front at a time when all available resources were needed to halt Axis advances from the west.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames201799-18) On 1 April 1941, after a _[coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1941 Iraqi coup d'état")_, Iraq, on the eastern border of Syria, came under the control of nationalists led by [Rashid Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Ali_al-Gaylani "Rashid Ali al-Gaylani"), who was willing to appeal for German support. The [Anglo-Iraqi War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War "Anglo-Iraqi War") (2–31 May 1941) led to the installation of a pro-British government.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTERaugh1993211%E2%80%93216-19)

British forces to the south of Syria in Mandate Palestine were under the command of General Sir [Henry Maitland Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Maitland_Wilson "Henry Maitland Wilson") and consisted of the [7th Australian Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_7th_Division "Australian 7th Division") (minus the 18th Brigade, which was in North Africa, besieged at the [siege of Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tobruk "Siege of Tobruk")), _Gentforce_ with two Free French brigades of the [1st Free French Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Free_French_Division "1st Free French Division") (including two battalions of the [13th Foreign Legion Demi-Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Foreign_Legion_Demi-Brigade "13th Foreign Legion Demi-Brigade") attached to the 1st Free French Brigade) and the [5th Indian Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Indian_Infantry_Brigade "5th Indian Infantry Brigade") ( [4th Indian Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Indian_Infantry_Division "4th Indian Infantry Division")) with artillery, engineers and other support services attached to form the 5th Indian Brigade Group. In northern and central Syria, _[Iraq Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqforce "Iraqforce")_ (Lieutenant-General Sir [Edward Quinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Quinan "Edward Quinan")) was used in the campaign to attack from the east, consisting of the [10th Indian Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Indian_Infantry_Division "10th Indian Infantry Division"), elements of the [17th Indian Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Indian_Infantry_Brigade "17th Indian Infantry Brigade") ( [8th Indian Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Indian_Infantry_Division "8th Indian Infantry Division")) and [Habforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habforce "Habforce"), the [4th Cavalry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Cavalry_Brigade_(United_Kingdom) "4th Cavalry Brigade (United Kingdom)") and the [Arab Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Legion "Arab Legion"), under [John Glubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glubb "John Glubb") (Glubb Pasha).[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-20) [Commando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commando "Commando") and raiding operations were undertaken by [No. 11 (Scottish) Commando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._11_(Scottish)_Commando "No. 11 (Scottish) Commando") from [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus"),[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-21) as well as [Palmach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmach "Palmach") paramilitary and [Mista'arvim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mista'arvim "Mista'arvim") squads from Mandatory Palestine.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-22)

Air support was provided by squadrons from the RAF and the [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force "Royal Australian Air Force") (RAAF). Ground forces on the coast were supported by bombardments from [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") (RN) and [Royal Australian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Navy "Royal Australian Navy") (RAN) units of the [Mediterranean Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Fleet_(United_Kingdom) "Mediterranean Fleet (United Kingdom)"). At the beginning, Air Commodore L. O. Brown, the [Air officer commanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_officer_commanding "Air officer commanding") (AOC) [HQ RAF Palestine and Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AHQ_Levant "AHQ Levant") had the understrength [11 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._11_Squadron_RAF "No. 11 Squadron RAF") (Blenheim Mk IV), [80 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._80_Squadron_RAF "No. 80 Squadron RAF"), re-equipping with [Hawker Hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hurricane "Hawker Hurricane"), [3 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._3_Squadron_RAAF "No. 3 Squadron RAAF") RAAF, converting to [Curtiss Tomahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtiss_P-40_Warhawk "Curtiss P-40 Warhawk"), [208 (Army Co-operation) Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._208_Squadron_RAF "No. 208 Squadron RAF") with a flight of Hurricanes and X Flight ( [Gloster Gladiators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloster_Gladiator "Gloster Gladiator")). A detachment of [Fleet Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Arm "Fleet Air Arm") (FAA) [815 Naval Air Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/815_Naval_Air_Squadron "815 Naval Air Squadron") ( [Fairey Swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairey_Swordfish "Fairey Swordfish") torpedo bombers) in Cyprus and [84 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._84_Squadron_RAF "No. 84 Squadron RAF") (Blenheims) in Iraq were to co-operate.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-23)

British forces in reserve included the [6th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Infantry_Division_(United_Kingdom) "6th Infantry Division (United Kingdom)") (with the [Czechoslovak 11th Infantry Battalion–East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_11th_Infantry_Battalion "Czechoslovak 11th Infantry Battalion") attached to the [23rd Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Infantry_Brigade_(United_Kingdom) "23rd Infantry Brigade (United Kingdom)")) and the [17th Australian Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Brigade_(Australia) "17th Brigade (Australia)").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlayfair2004209-24) In mid-June, the division with its two infantry brigades came into the line as reinforcements, mainly on the [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus "Damascus") front, and the southern force was placed under the command of the [1st Australian Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_I_Corps "Australian I Corps") on 19 June.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoslen200350-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlayfair2004211-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChappell198719-27) At the beginning of Operation Exporter, the British and Commonwealth force consisted of about 34,000 men(18,000 Australians,9,000 British,2,000 Indian and 5,000 Free French troops).[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Long_1953,_p._526-28) The RAF and RAAF had about 50 aircraft, and the navy contributed the landing ship [HMS _Glengyle_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Glengyle "HMS Glengyle"), five [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser "Cruiser") and eight destroyers.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-29)

### British plan of attack

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: British plan of attack")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/The_British_Army_in_the_Middle_East_1941_E3839.jpg/250px-The_British_Army_in_the_Middle_East_1941_E3839.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_British_Army_in_the_Middle_East_1941_E3839.jpg) Indian troops outside Damascus

The British plan of attack devised by Wilson called for four lines of invasion, in Damascus and Beirut, in Palestine, in northern Syria and [Palmyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmyra "Palmyra"), in central Syria, from Iraq, and in [Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli,_Lebanon "Tripoli, Lebanon") (in northern Lebanon) also from Iraq.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-30)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames2017119-31) The 5th Indian Brigade Group ( [Brigadier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier "Brigadier") [Wilfrid Lewis Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfrid_Lewis_Lloyd "Wilfrid Lewis Lloyd")) was ordered to cross the Syrian border from Palestine and take [Quneitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quneitra "Quneitra") and [Deraa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deraa "Deraa"). This was anticipated to open the way for the 1st Free French Division to advance to Damascus. Four days after the commencement of the operation, the force was brought under unified command and was named _Gentforce_ after its French commander, [Major-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major-General "Major-General") [Paul Louis Le Gentilhomme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Legentilhomme "Paul Legentilhomme").[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-32) The 7th Australian Division (Major-General [John Lavarack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lavarack "John Lavarack"), succeeded by Major-General [Arthur "Tubby" Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Samuel_Allen "Arthur Samuel Allen") on 18 June when Lavarack took over [Australian I Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Corps_(Australia) "I Corps (Australia)")) advanced from Palestine along the coastal road from [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa "Haifa") towards Beirut.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-33) The [Australian 21st Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Brigade_(Australia) "21st Brigade (Australia)") was to take Beirut, advancing along the coast from [Tyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre,_Lebanon "Tyre, Lebanon"), over the [Litani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litani_River "Litani River") towards [Sidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidon "Sidon").[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-34) The Australian [25th Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Brigade_(Australia) "25th Brigade (Australia)") was to attack the large Vichy French airbase at [Rayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayak_(Riyaq) "Rayak (Riyaq)") by advancing along a route further inland from the 21st Brigade.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-35) The operation was also to include a supporting commando landing from Cyprus at the south of the Litani River.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-36)

Once the two southern prongs were well engaged, a third force of formations drawn from _Iraq Command_, was planned to invade Syria. The bulk of the 10th Indian Infantry Division (Major-General [William "Bill" Slim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Slim "William Slim")) was to advance north-west, up the [Euphrates River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_River "Euphrates River") from [Haditha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haditha "Haditha") in Iraq (upstream from Baghdad), toward [Deir ez Zor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez_Zor "Deir ez Zor") and thence to [Raqqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raqqa "Raqqa") and [Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo "Aleppo"). The manoeuvre was intended to threaten the communication and supply lines of Vichy forces defending Beirut from the Australians advancing from the south, particularly the railway line running northwards through Aleppo to [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), which was thought by some British strategists to be sympathetic to Vichy and Germany.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTERaugh1993221%E2%80%93222-37) A group of two infantry battalions from the [20th Indian Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Indian_Infantry_Brigade "20th Indian Infantry Brigade") (10th Indian Division) and two from the 17th Indian Infantry Brigade (8th Indian Infantry Division) would operate independently to capture all the territory in north-eastern Syria. The 20th Indian Infantry Brigade were to make a feint from Mosul, and the 17th Indian Infantry Brigade would advance into the _Bec du Canard_ (Duck's Bill) region, through which a railway from Aleppo ran eastward to Mosul and [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad "Baghdad").[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-38)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-39) Habforce was in Iraq attached to _Iraq Command_ because it had struck across the desert from the [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Transjordan "Emirate of Transjordan") border as part of the relief of [RAF Habbaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Habbaniya "RAF Habbaniya") during the Anglo-Iraqi War.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTERaugh1993222-40) Habforce consisted of the 4th Cavalry Brigade, the 1st Battalion, [Essex Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Essex_Regiment "The Essex Regiment") and the Arab Legion Mechanized Regiment supported by field, anti-tank and anti-aircraft artillery units, to gather in western Iraq between [Rutbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutbah "Rutbah") and the Transjordan border.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-41) As the thrust up the Euphrates took place, Habforce would meanwhile advance north-westerly to take Palmyra, Syria, and secure [the oil pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkuk-Haifa_oil_pipeline "Kirkuk-Haifa oil pipeline") from Haditha to Tripoli.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTERaugh1993222-40)

The invasion would be preceded by the infiltration of commandos from the [Palmach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmach "Palmach"), the strike arm of the [Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah "Haganah"), the main underground militia of the Jewish community of [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine"). Prior to the invasion, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language")-speaking Palmach personnel were sent in to gather intelligence and mine key bridges. Just before the operation began two Palmach companies were sent in to perform last-minute reconnaissance and divided into twelve squads. They cut wires, ambushed Vichy troops guarding bridges over the Litani River, destroyed culverts, sabotaged roads, and would act as guides to Australian forces once the invasion began.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-42)

- [Battle of Deir ez-Zor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Deir_ez-Zor_(1941) "Battle of Deir ez-Zor (1941)") (3 July): part of the advance on central and northern Syria from Iraq

- [Battle of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Beirut_(1941) "Battle of Beirut (1941)") (12 July): part of the advance on Beirut from Palestine

### War in the air

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: War in the air")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/11_Squadron_RAF_Blenheims_bombing_Beirut_1941_IWM_HU_93073.jpg/250px-11_Squadron_RAF_Blenheims_bombing_Beirut_1941_IWM_HU_93073.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:11_Squadron_RAF_Blenheims_bombing_Beirut_1941_IWM_HU_93073.jpg)

11 Squadron RAF [Bristol Blenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Blenheim "Bristol Blenheim") bombing Beirut, 1941

The initial advantage that the Vichy French Air Force ( _Armée de l'Air de Vichy_) enjoyed did not last long. The Vichy French lost most of their aircraft destroyed on the ground where the flat terrain, the absence of infrastructure and the absence of modern [anti-aircraft (AA) artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft_warfare "Anti-aircraft warfare") made them vulnerable to air attacks.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Mollo146-43) On 26 June, a [strafing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strafe "Strafe") run by Tomahawks of 3 Squadron RAAF, on [Homs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homs "Homs") airfield, destroyed five [Dewoitine D.520s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewoitine_D.520 "Dewoitine D.520") of Fighter Squadron II/3 ( _Groupe de Chasse II/3_) and damaged six more.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-44)

On 10 July, five D.520s attacked Bristol Blenheim bombers of [45 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._45_Squadron_RAF "No. 45 Squadron RAF") RAF, which were being escorted by seven Tomahawks from 3 Squadron RAAF.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Herington94-45) The French pilots claimed three Blenheims but at least four D.520s were destroyed by the Australians.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Herington94-45)[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-46) The following day, a Dewoitine pilot shot down a Tomahawk from 3 Squadron, the only one lost during the campaign.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Herington94-45) By the end of the campaign, the Vichy forces had lost 179 aircraft from about 289 committed to the Levant, with remaining aircraft with the range to do so evacuating to [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes "Rhodes").[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-47)

### War at sea

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: War at sea")\]

The war at sea was not a major part of Operation Exporter, although some significant actions were fought. During the [Battle of the Litani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Litani_River "Battle of the Litani River"), rough seas kept commandos from landing along the coast on the first day of battle. On 9 June 1941, the French destroyers _Valmy_ and _Guépard_ fired on the advancing Australians at the Litani River before being driven off by shore-based artillery-fire. The French destroyers then exchanged fire with the British destroyer [HMS _Janus_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Janus_(F53) "HMS Janus (F53)"). The [Royal New Zealand Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Navy "Royal New Zealand Navy") light cruiser [HMNZS _Leander_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Leander "HMNZS Leander") came to the aid of _Janus_ along with six British destroyers and the French retired.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Pieka%C5%82kiewicz144-48) The _Luftwaffe_ attempted to come to the aid of the French naval forces on 15 June. [Junkers Ju 88s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_88 "Junkers Ju 88") of _II./LG_ 1 (2nd Group, [_Lehrgeschwader_ 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehrgeschwader_1 "Lehrgeschwader 1")), attacked British warships forces off the Syrian coast and hit the destroyers [HMS _Ilex_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Ilex "HMS Ilex") and [_Isis_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Isis_(D87) "HMS Isis (D87)"). That evening, French aircraft of the 4th Naval Air Group bombed British naval units off the Syrian coast.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Pieka%C5%82kiewicz144-48)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/AWM_010546_allen_lebanon.jpg/250px-AWM_010546_allen_lebanon.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:AWM_010546_allen_lebanon.jpg)

[Hammana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammana "Hammana"), September 1941. With terrain typical of the region in the background, Maj. Gen. [A. S. Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Samuel_Allen "Arthur Samuel Allen") (centre), commander of the [Australian 7th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Division_(Australia) "7th Division (Australia)"), inspects some of his men. [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations") units [garrisoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison "Garrison") [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon") and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria") for several months, following the end of the campaign.

On 16 June, British torpedo aircraft sank the French destroyer [_Chevalier Paul_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_destroyer_Chevalier_Paul_(1932) "French destroyer Chevalier Paul (1932)"), which had been en route from [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon "Toulon") to Syria, carrying ammunition from [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France "Metropolitan France"). The following day, British bombers attacked another French destroyer in the port of Beirut which was also carrying ammunition.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Pieka%C5%82kiewicz144-48) On the night of 22/23 June, _Guépard_ fought a brief engagement with two British cruisers and six destroyers off the Syrian coast, before the French destroyer retired under the cover of darkness.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-49) The French suffered further losses on 25 June, when the British submarine [HMS _Parthian_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Parthian_(N75) "HMS Parthian (N75)") torpedoed and sank the French submarine [_Souffleur_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_submarine_Souffleur_(1924) "French submarine Souffleur (1924)") off the Lebanese coast; shortly afterwards, the French tanker _Adour_, which was carrying the entire fuel supply for the French forces in the Middle East, was attacked by British torpedo aircraft and badly damaged.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-50)

### Armistice

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Armistice")\]

On 10 July, as the Australian 21st Brigade was on the verge of entering Beirut, Dentz sought an armistice. At one minute past midnight on 12 July, a ceasefire came into effect and ended the campaign. The [Armistice of Saint Jean d'Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Saint_Jean_d'Acre "Armistice of Saint Jean d'Acre") (also known as the "Convention of Acre") was signed on 14 July at the [Sidney Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sidney_Smith "William Sidney Smith") Barracks on the outskirts of the city of [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akko "Akko").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Playfair_2004,_pp._221,_335%E2%80%93337-4)

## Aftermath

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Aftermath")\]

### Analysis

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Analysis")\]

Wavell had not wanted the Syrian distraction, given that British forces in the Mediterranean were already overstretched. However, political factors, including pressure from Churchill and CIGS in addition to guarantees by the Free French that any operation into Syria and Lebanon would meet with little resistance, forced his hand. In the event, the Vichy government ordered its soldiers to resist the invasion and its troops offered stiff resistance to the advancing British Empire forces. The Vichy government also conducted an effective propaganda campaign within France, encouraging the people to fight the "hereditary enemy" (Britain) and equating the defence of Syria as a matter of national honour.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-51) As a result of the unexpected resistance British forces quickly required reinforcements, which could only be provided piecemeal. Many of the British and Commonwealth troops were novices and the hot, dry, mountainous terrain was a severe test, in which Indian Army units excelled. The Australian contingent had to cope with the worst country but conducted the most effective attack, "with a good plan carried through with great determination". The achievement of air superiority was delayed by the lack of aircraft but the urgency of the situation made it impossible for the naval and ground forces to wait. Vichy French airmen concentrated their attacks on ships and ground targets, which were highly effective until they were forced to move north. The scare caused by the German success in Crete had been exaggerated because the German parachute and glider invasions of The Netherlands and Crete had been very costly and there was little chance of the Germans gaining a bridgehead in Syria. The Germans withdrew from Syria to preserve their forces and to deprive the British of a pretext for invasion. The British invaded Syria anyway and gained naval and air bases far north of Suez, thus increasing the security of the oil route from [Basra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra "Basra") to Baghdad in Iraq to Haifa in Palestine.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-52)

### Casualties

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Casualties")\]

In August, the Vichy authorities announced 6,352 casualties of whom 521 men had been killed, 1,037 were missing, 1,790 wounded and 3,004 men had been taken prisoner. After the war, Dentz stated that 1,092 men had been killed, which would mean 1,790 wounded,466 missing and 3,004 prisoners against a British claim of 8,912 casualties of all natures.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Long_1953,_p._526-28) The Vichy Air Force lost 179 aircraft, most destroyed on the ground, the navy lost one submarine and 5,668 men [defected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defected "Defected") to the Free French.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Mollo146-43)[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-53) The armistice agreement led to the repatriation to France of 37,563 military and civilian personnel in eight convoys, consisting of three [hospital ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_ship "Hospital ship") and a "gleaner" ship, from 7 August to 27 September.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-54) Prisoners taken by the Vichy French forces were returned but several British prisoners of war had been sent out of Syria, some after the armistice. The delay in obtaining the return of these prisoners led to the detention of Dentz and 29 senior officers in Palestine who were released when the British prisoners were returned to Syria.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-55) British and Commonwealth casualties were about 4,652; the Australians suffered 1,552 casualties,(416 men killed and 1,136 wounded.) The Free French incurred about c.1,300 losses and 1,100 men taken prisoner; British and Indian casualties were 1,800 wounded,1,200 men captured and 3,150 sick, including 350 malaria cases.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Long_1953,_p._526-28) The RAF and RAAF lost 27 aircraft.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-56)

### Subsequent events

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Subsequent events")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Allied_leaders_meet_in_the_Middle_East.jpg/250px-Allied_leaders_meet_in_the_Middle_East.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Allied_leaders_meet_in_the_Middle_East.jpg)

Allied leaders meet in Syria. Left to right: [Air Chief Marshal Longmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Longmore "Arthur Longmore"), [General Wavell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Wavell "Archibald Wavell"), [General de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle"), [General Catroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Catroux "Georges Catroux")

Operations against the Vichy regime in Syria could only be conducted with troops withdrawn from the Western Desert, a dispersal that contributed to the defeat of [Operation Battleaxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Battleaxe "Operation Battleaxe") and made the Syrian campaign take longer than necessary. Churchill had decided to sack Wavell in early May over his reluctance to divert forces to Iraq. Wavell was relieved on 22 June and relinquished command on 5 July, leaving for India two days afterwards.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-FOOTNOTERaugh1993222,_238%E2%80%93239-57) In late July 1941, de Gaulle flew from [Brazzaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazzaville "Brazzaville") to congratulate the victors.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-58) Free French General [Georges Catroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Catroux "Georges Catroux") was placed in control of Syria and Lebanon and on 26 November, shortly after assuming this post, Catroux recognized the independence of Syria and Lebanon in the name of the Free French movement.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-59) After elections on 8 November 1943, Lebanon became an independent state on 22 November 1943 and on 27 February 1945, declared war on Germany and the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan").[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-60)

By 1945, however, continued French presence in the Levant saw nationalist demonstrations which the French attempted to quell. With heavy Syrian casualties, notably in Damascus, Churchill opposed French action but after being rebuffed by Charles de Gaulle, he ordered British forces into Syria from Jordan with orders to fire on the French. Known as the [Levant Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant_Crisis "Levant Crisis"), British armored cars and troops reached Damascus, following which the French were escorted and confined to their barracks. With political pressure added, de Gaulle ordered a ceasefire and France withdrew from Syria the following year.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign#cite_note-Luce-61)
