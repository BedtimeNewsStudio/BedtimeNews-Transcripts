The **Rwandan Civil War** was a large-scale [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_war "Civil war") in [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda") which was fought between the [Rwandan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Armed_Forces "Rwandan Armed Forces"), representing the incumbent [Third Rwandese Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Rwandese_Republic "Third Rwandese Republic"), and the rebel [Rwandan Patriotic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Patriotic_Front "Rwandan Patriotic Front") (RPF) from 1October 1990 to 18 July 1994. The war arose from the long-running dispute between the [Hutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu "Hutu") and [Tutsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutsi "Tutsi") groups within the Rwandan population. The [Rwandan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Revolution "Rwandan Revolution"), which broke out in 1959, had replaced the [Tutsi monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Rwanda "Kingdom of Rwanda") with a Hutu-led republic, forcing more than 336,000 Tutsis to seek refuge in neighbouring countries. A group of these refugees in [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda "Uganda") founded the RPF which, under the leadership of [Fred Rwigyema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Rwigyema "Fred Rwigyema") and [Paul Kagame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kagame "Paul Kagame"), became a battle-ready army by the late 1980s.

The war began on 1 October 1990, when the RPF invaded north-eastern [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Rwandese_Republic "Third Rwandese Republic"), advancing 60km (37mi) into the country. They suffered a major setback when Rwigyema was killed in action on the second day. The Rwandan Army, assisted by [expeditionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_warfare "Expeditionary warfare") troops from France, gained the upper hand and the RPF were largely defeated by the end of October. Kagame, who had been in the United States during the invasion, returned to take command. He withdrew troops to the [Virunga Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virunga_Mountains "Virunga Mountains") for several months before attacking again. The RPF began an [insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency "Insurgency"), which continued until mid-1992 with neither side able to gain the upper hand. A series of protests forced Rwandan President [Juvénal Habyarimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana "Juvénal Habyarimana") to begin peace negotiations with the RPF and domestic opposition parties. Despite disruption and killings by [Hutu Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu_Power "Hutu Power"), a group of extremists opposed to any deal, and a fresh RPF offensive in early 1993, the negotiations were successfully concluded with the signing of the [Arusha Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arusha_Accords_(Rwanda) "Arusha Accords (Rwanda)") in August 1993.

An uneasy peace followed, during which the terms of the accords were gradually implemented. RPF troops were deployed to a compound in Kigali and the peace-keeping [United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Assistance_Mission_for_Rwanda "United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda") (UNAMIR) was sent to the country. The Hutu Power movement was steadily gaining influence and planned a "final solution" to exterminate the Tutsi. This plan was put into action following the [assassination of President Habyarimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira "Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira") on 6April 1994. Over the course of about a hundred days, between 500,000 and 1,000,000 Tutsi and moderate Hutu were killed in the [Rwandan genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_genocide "Rwandan genocide"). The RPF quickly resumed the civil war. They captured territory steadily, encircling cities and cutting off supply routes. By mid-June they had surrounded the capital, [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali "Kigali"), and on 4July they seized it. The war ended later that month when the RPF captured the last territory held by the interim government, forcing the government and _[genocidaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9nocidaires "Génocidaires")_ into [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire "Zaire").

The victorious RPF assumed control of the country, with Paul Kagame as _de facto_ leader. Kagame served as [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Rwanda "Vice President of Rwanda") from 1994 and as president from 2000. The RPF began a programme of rebuilding the infrastructure and economy of the country, bringing genocide perpetrators to trial, and promoting reconciliation between Hutu and Tutsi. In 1996 the RPF-led Rwandan government launched an offensive against refugee camps in Zaire, home to exiled leaders of the former regime and millions of Hutu refugees. This action started the [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War "First Congo War"), which removed long-time dictator [Mobutu Sese Seko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko "Mobutu Sese Seko") from power. As of 2026[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit), Kagame and the RPF remain the dominant political force in Rwanda.

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

### Pre-independence Rwanda and origins of Hutu, Tutsi, and Twa

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Pre-independence Rwanda and origins of Hutu, Tutsi, and Twa")\]

Main article: [Origins of Hutu, Tutsi and Twa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_Hutu,_Tutsi_and_Twa "Origins of Hutu, Tutsi and Twa")

[![Photograph of King's palace in Nyanza, Rwanda depicting main entrance, front and conical roof](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Rwanda_Nyanza_Mwami_Palace.jpg/250px-Rwanda_Nyanza_Mwami_Palace.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rwanda_Nyanza_Mwami_Palace.jpg) A reconstruction of the [King of Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Rwanda "King of Rwanda")'s palace at [Nyanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyanza,_Rwanda "Nyanza, Rwanda")

The earliest inhabitants of what is now [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda") were the [Twa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Twa "Great Lakes Twa"), Indigenous [pygmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy_peoples "Pygmy peoples") hunter-gatherers who settled in the area between 8000 BC and 3000 BC and remain in Rwanda today.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien200344-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien200344)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani200261-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani200261) Between 700 BC and 1500 AD, [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples "Bantu peoples") groups migrated into the region and began to clear forest land for agriculture.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien200358-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien200358)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani200261-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani200261) The forest-dwelling Twa lost much of their land and moved to the slopes of mountains.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKing200775-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKing200775) Historians have several theories regarding the Bantu migrations. One theory is that the first settlers were [Hutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu "Hutu"), and the [Tutsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutsi "Tutsi") migrated later and formed a distinct racial group, possibly originating from the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPottier200212-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPottier200212)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199916-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199916) An alternative theory is that the migration was slow and steady, with incoming groups integrating into rather than conquering the existing society.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani200258-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani200258)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani200261-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani200261) Under this theory, the Hutu and Tutsi are a later class, rather than racial, distinction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien200369-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien200369)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEShyaka200410–11-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEShyaka200410–11)

The population coalesced, first into [clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clans_of_Rwanda "Clans of Rwanda") ( _ubwoko_)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien200388–89-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien200388–89) and into around eight kingdoms by 1700.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003482-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003482) The [Kingdom of Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Rwanda "Kingdom of Rwanda"), ruled by the Tutsi Nyiginya clan, became dominant from the mid-eighteenth century,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003160-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003160) expanding through conquest and assimilation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDorsey199438-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDorsey199438) It achieved its greatest extent under the reign of [Kigeli Rwabugiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigeli_IV_of_Rwanda "Kigeli IV of Rwanda") in 1853–1895. Rwabugiri expanded the kingdom west and north, and initiated administrative reforms which caused a rift to grow between the Hutu and Tutsi populations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani200269-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani200269)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003160-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003160) These included _uburetwa_, a system of forced labour which Hutu had to perform to regain access to land seized from them,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPottier200213-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPottier200213) and _[ubuhake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubuhake "Ubuhake")_, under which Tutsi patrons ceded cattle to Hutu or Tutsi clients in exchange for economic and personal service.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199913–14-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199913–14) Rwanda and neighbouring [Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi "Burundi") were assigned to Germany by the [Berlin Conference of 1884](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference_(1884) "Berlin Conference (1884)"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAppiahGates2010218-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAppiahGates2010218) and Germany established a presence in 1897 with the formation of an alliance with the King.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTECarney201324-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTECarney201324) German policy was to rule through the Rwandan monarchy, enabling colonisation with fewer European troops.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199925-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199925) The colonists favoured the Tutsi over the Hutu when assigning administrative roles, believing them to be migrants from Ethiopia and racially superior.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEJones200117-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEJones200117) The Rwandan King welcomed the Germans, and used their military strength to reinforce his rule and expand the kingdom.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003217–218-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003217–218) In World War I, Belgian forces took control of Rwanda and Burundi during the [Tabora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabora_offensive "Tabora offensive") and [Mahenge offensives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahenge_offensive "Mahenge offensive"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199925–26-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199925–26) and from 1926 began a policy of more direct colonial rule.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199926-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199926)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003260-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEChrétien2003260) The Belgian administration, in conjunction with Catholic clerics, modernised the local economy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199932–35-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199932–35) They also increased taxes and imposed forced labour on the population.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199935-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199935) Tutsi supremacy remained, reinforced by Belgian support of two monarchies, leaving the Hutu disenfranchised.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199938–39-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199938–39) In 1935, Belgium introduced identity cards classifying each individual as Tutsi, Hutu, Twa, or Naturalised. It had previously been possible for wealthy Hutu to become honorary Tutsi, but the identity cards prevented further movement between the groups.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGourevitch200056–57-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGourevitch200056–57)

### Revolution, exile of Tutsi, and the Hutu republic

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Revolution, exile of Tutsi, and the Hutu republic")\]

Main article: [Rwandan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Revolution "Rwandan Revolution")

After 1945, a Hutu counter-elite developed,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002108-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002108) demanding the transfer of power from Tutsi to Hutu.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199945–46-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199945–46) The Tutsi leadership responded by trying to negotiate a speedy independence on their terms but found that the Belgians no longer supported them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199943-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199943)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKroslak200724-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKroslak200724) There was a simultaneous shift in the Catholic Church,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199943–44-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199943–44) with prominent conservative figures in the early Rwandan church replaced by younger clergy of working-class origin. Of these, a greater proportion were [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people "Flemish people") rather than [Walloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloons "Walloons") Belgians and sympathised with the plight of the Hutu.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002113-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002113) In November 1959, the Hutu began a series of riots and arson attacks on Tutsi homes, following false rumours of the death of a Hutu sub-chief in an assault by Tutsi activists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTECarney2013124–125-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTECarney2013124–125) Violence quickly spread across the whole country, beginning the [Rwandan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Revolution "Rwandan Revolution").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTECarney2013125-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTECarney2013125) The King and Tutsi politicians launched a counter-attack[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTENewbury1988196-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTENewbury1988196) in an attempt to seize power and ostracise the Hutu and Belgians,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTENewbury1988195–196-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTENewbury1988195–196) but were thwarted by Belgian Colonel [Guy Logiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Logiest "Guy Logiest"), who was brought in by the colonial Governor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTENewbury1988196-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTENewbury1988196) Logiest re-established law and order and began a programme of overt promotion and protection of the Hutu elite.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTECarney2013127-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTECarney2013127) He replaced many Tutsi chiefs with Hutu and effectively forced [King KigeliV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigeli_V_of_Rwanda "Kigeli V of Rwanda") into exile.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTESabar2013-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTESabar2013)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_%281980%29.jpg/250px-Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_%281980%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_(1980).jpg) Hutu army officer [Juvénal Habyarimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana "Juvénal Habyarimana") (pictured) became President of Rwanda after [a 1973 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Rwandan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1973 Rwandan coup d'état").

Logiest and Hutu leader [Grégoire Kayibanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A9goire_Kayibanda "Grégoire Kayibanda") declared the country [an autonomous republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_of_Gitarama "Coup of Gitarama") in 1961 and it became independent in 1962.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199953–54-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199953–54) More than 336,000 Tutsi left Rwanda by 1964 to escape the Hutu purges,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199962-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199962)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMayersen201021-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMayersen201021) mostly to the neighbouring countries of Burundi, Uganda, [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania") and Zaire.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002160–161-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002160–161) Many of the Tutsi exiles lived as refugees in their host countries,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199963–64-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199963–64) and sought to return to Rwanda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199955-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199955) Some supported the new Rwandan Government, but others formed [armed groups and launched attacks on Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inyenzi_movement "Inyenzi movement"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199955-1)[\[49\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199955) the largest of which advanced close to Kigali in 1963.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199956-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199956) These groups were known in [Kinyarwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinyarwanda "Kinyarwanda") as the _inyenzi_ (cockroaches).[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199954-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199954) Historians do not know the origin of this term—it is possible the rebels coined it themselves, the name reflecting that they generally attacked at night.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200834-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200834) The _inyenzi_ label resurfaced in the 1990s as a highly derogatory term for the Tutsi, used by Hutu hardliners to dehumanise them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200834-1)[\[52\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200834) The _inyenzi_ attacks of the 1960s were poorly equipped and organised and the government defeated them. The [last significant attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugesera_invasion "Bugesera invasion") was made in desperation from Burundi in December 1963 but failed due to bad planning and lack of equipment. The government responded to this attack with the slaughter of an estimated 10,000 Tutsi within Rwanda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199956-1)[\[50\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199956)

Kayibanda presided over a Hutu republic for the next decade, imposing an autocratic rule similar to the pre-revolution feudal monarchy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199957-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199957) In 1973 Hutu army officer [Juvénal Habyarimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana "Juvénal Habyarimana") toppled Kayibanda in [a coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Rwandan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat "1973 Rwandan coup d'état").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199974–76-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199974–76) He founded the [National Republican Movement for Democracy and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Republican_Movement_for_Democracy_and_Development "National Republican Movement for Democracy and Development") (MRND) party in 1975,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTETwagilimana2007117-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTETwagilimana2007117) and promulgated a new constitution following a [1978 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_constitutional_referendum,_1978 "Rwandan constitutional referendum, 1978"), making the country a [one-party state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_state "One-party state") in which every citizen had to belong to the MRND.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTETwagilimana2007116-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTETwagilimana2007116) Anti-Tutsi discrimination continued under Habyarimana but the country enjoyed greater economic prosperity and reduced anti-Tutsi violence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199974–76-1)[\[54\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199974–76) A coffee price collapse in the late 1980s caused a loss of income for Rwanda's wealthy elite, precipitating a political fight for power and access to [foreign aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_aid "Foreign aid") receipts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199984-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199984) The family of first lady [Agathe Habyarimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathe_Habyarimana "Agathe Habyarimana"), known as the _[akazu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akazu "Akazu")_, were the principal winners in this fight.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199985-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199985) The family had a more respected lineage than that of the President, having ruled one of the independent states near [Gisenyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisenyi "Gisenyi") in the nineteenth century.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199986-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199986) Habyarimana therefore relied on them in controlling the population of the north-west.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199986-1)[\[59\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199986) The _akazu_ exploited this to their advantage, and Habyarimana was increasingly unable to rule without them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199986-2)[\[59\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199986) The economic situation forced Habyarimana to greatly reduce the [national budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_budget "National budget"), which led to civil unrest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199987–89-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199987–89) On the advice of French president [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand "François Mitterrand"), Habyarimana declared a commitment to [multi-party politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system") but took no action to bring this about.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199990-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199990) Student protests followed and by late 1990 the country was in crisis.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199990-1)[\[61\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199990)

### Formation of the RPF and preparation for war

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Formation of the RPF and preparation for war")\]

The organisation which became the [Rwandan Patriotic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Patriotic_Front "Rwandan Patriotic Front") (RPF) was founded in 1979 in Uganda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199967-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199967) It was initially known as the Rwandan Refugees Welfare Association and then from 1980 as the Rwandan Alliance for National Unity (RANU).[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199967-1)[\[62\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199967) It formed in response to persecution and discrimination against the Tutsi refugees by the regime of Ugandan President [Milton Obote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Obote "Milton Obote").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199967-2)[\[62\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199967) Obote accused the Rwandans of collaboration with his predecessor, [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin "Idi Amin"), including occupying the homes and stealing the cattle of Ugandans who had fled from Amin.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDash1983-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDash1983) Meanwhile, Tutsi refugees [Fred Rwigyema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Rwigyema "Fred Rwigyema") and [Paul Kagame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kagame "Paul Kagame") had joined [Yoweri Museveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoweri_Museveni "Yoweri Museveni")'s rebel [Front for National Salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_for_National_Salvation "Front for National Salvation") (FRONASA).[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199968-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199968) Museveni [fought alongside Obote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda%E2%80%93Tanzania_War "Uganda–Tanzania War") to defeat Amin in 1979 but withdrew from the government following Obote's disputed victory in the [1980 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_general_election,_1980 "Ugandan general election, 1980").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200839-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200839) With Rwigyema and Kagame he formed a new rebel army, the [National Resistance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resistance_Army "National Resistance Army") (NRA).[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAssociated_Press_(I-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAssociated_Press_(I)1981-66)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200839-1)[\[65\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200839) The NRA's goal was to overthrow Obote's government, in what became known as the [Ugandan Bush War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Bush_War "Ugandan Bush War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200839-2)[\[65\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200839)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTENganda2009-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTENganda2009) President Obote remained hostile to the Rwandan refugees throughout his presidency and RANU was forced into exile in 1981, relocating to [Nairobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi "Nairobi") in Kenya.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199963-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199963) In 1982, with the authority of Obote,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199969-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199969) local district councils in the [Ankole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankole "Ankole") region issued notices requiring refugees to be evicted from their homes and settled in camps.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199919-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199919) These evictions were violently implemented by Ankole youth militia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199969-1)[\[69\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199969) Many displaced Rwandans attempted to cross the border to Rwanda, but the Habyarimana regime confined them to isolated camps and closed the border to prevent further migration.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199922-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199922) Faced with the threat of statelessness,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199922-1)[\[71\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199922) many more Tutsi refugees in Uganda chose to join Museveni's NRA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199924-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199924)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199970-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199970)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/ReaganMuseveni.jpg/250px-ReaganMuseveni.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ReaganMuseveni.jpg) Ugandan president [Yoweri Museveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoweri_Museveni "Yoweri Museveni") with [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") at the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") in October 1987

In 1986, the NRA [captured Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kampala "Battle of Kampala") with a force of 14,000 soldiers, including 500 Rwandans, and formed a new government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200847-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200847) After Museveni was inaugurated as president he appointed Kagame and Rwigyema as senior officers in the new Ugandan army.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200850–51-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200850–51)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTESimpson_(I-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTESimpson_(I)2000-76) The experience of the Bush War inspired Rwigyema and Kagame to consider an attack against Rwanda, with the goal of allowing the refugees to return home.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200851–52-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200851–52) As well as fulfilling their army duties, the pair began building a covert network of Rwandan Tutsi refugees within the army's ranks, intended as the nucleus for such an attack.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200851–52-1)[\[77\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200851–52) With the pro-refugee Museveni in power, RANU was able to move back to Kampala.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEWellsFellows201629-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEWellsFellows201629) At its 1987 convention it renamed itself to the Rwandan Patriotic Front and it too committed to returning the refugees to Rwanda by any means possible.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199972–73-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199972–73) In 1988, a leadership crisis within the RPF prompted Fred Rwigyema to intervene in the organisation and take control, replacing [Peter Bayingana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bayingana?action=edit&redlink=1 "Peter Bayingana (page does not exist)") as RPF president.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBamurangirwa201380-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBamurangirwa201380) Kagame and other senior members of Rwigyema's Rwandan entourage within the NRA also joined, Kagame assuming the vice presidency.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBamurangirwa201380-1)[\[80\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBamurangirwa201380) Bayingana remained as the other vice president but resented the loss of the leadership.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBamurangirwa201380-2)[\[80\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBamurangirwa201380) Bayingana and his supporters attempted to start the war with an invasion in late 1989 without the support of Rwigyema,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199934-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199934) but this was quickly repelled by the Rwandan Army.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199931-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199931)

Rwandan President Habyarimana was aware of the increasing number of Tutsi exiles in the Ugandan Army and made representations to President Museveni on the matter.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200853-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200853) At the same time many native Ugandans and [Baganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baganda "Baganda") officers in the NRA began criticising Museveni over his appointment of Rwandan refugees to senior positions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199933-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199933)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002175-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002175) He therefore demoted Kagame and Rwigyema in 1989.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200853-1)[\[83\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200853) They remained _de facto_ senior officers but the change in official status, and the possibility that they might lose access to the resources of the Ugandan military,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199934-1)[\[81\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199934) caused them to accelerate their plans to invade Rwanda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200853–54-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200853–54) In 1990 a dispute in south-western Uganda between Ugandan ranch owners and squatters on their land, many of whom were Rwandans,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002176-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002176) led to a wider debate on indigeneity and eventually to the explicit labeling of all Rwandan refugees as non-citizens.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002182-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002182) Realising the precariousness of their own positions, the opportunity afforded by both the renewed drive of refugees to leave Uganda, and the instability on the Rwandan domestic scene, Rwigyema and Kagame decided in mid-1990 to effect their invasion plans immediately.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200857-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200857) It is likely President Museveni knew of the planned invasion but did not explicitly support it.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199997–98-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199997–98) In mid-1990 Museveni ordered Rwigyema to attend an officer training course at the [Command and General Staff College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_General_Staff_College "Command and General Staff College") in [Fort Leavenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Leavenworth "Fort Leavenworth") in the United States, and was also planning overseas deployments for other senior Rwandans in the army.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200861-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200861) This may have been a tactic to reduce the threat of an RPF invasion of Rwanda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200861-1)[\[91\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200861) After two days of discussion Rwigyema persuaded Museveni that following years of army duty he needed a break and was allowed to remain in Uganda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200861–62-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200861–62) Museveni then ordered Kagame to attend instead. The RPF leadership allowed him to go, to avoid suspicion, even though it meant his missing the beginning of the war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200862-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200862)

## Course of the war

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Course of the war")\]

### 1990 invasion and death of Rwigyema

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 1990 invasion and death of Rwigyema")\]

On 1 October 1990 fifty RPF rebels deserted their Ugandan Army posts and crossed the border from Uganda into Rwanda, killing a Rwandan customs guard at the Kagitumba border post and forcing others to flee.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199993-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199993) They were followed by hundreds more rebels, dressed in the uniforms of the [Ugandan national army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_People's_Defence_Force "Uganda People's Defence Force") and carrying stolen Ugandan weaponry, including [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_gun "Machine gun"), [autocannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocannon "Autocannon"), [mortars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_(weapon) "Mortar (weapon)"), and Soviet [BM-21 multiple rocket launchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-21_Grad "BM-21 Grad").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199993-1)[\[94\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199993) According to RPF estimates, around 2,500 of the Ugandan Army's 4,000 Rwandan soldiers took part in the invasion,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199993-2)[\[94\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199993) accompanied by 800 civilians, including medical staff and messengers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200865-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200865) Both President Yoweri Museveni of Uganda and President Habyarimana of Rwanda were in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") attending the United Nations [World Summit for Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Summit_for_Children "World Summit for Children").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBiles1990-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBiles1990) In the first few days of fighting, the RPF advanced 60km (37mi) south to Gabiro.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199994-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199994) Their [Rwandan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Armed_Forces "Rwandan Armed Forces") opponents, fighting for Habyarimana's government, were numerically superior, with 5,200 soldiers, and possessed armoured cars and helicopters supplied by France, but the RPF benefited from the element of surprise.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199994-1)[\[97\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199994) The Ugandan government set up roadblocks across the west of Uganda, to prevent further desertions and to block the rebels from returning to Uganda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199994-2)[\[97\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199994)

On 2 October the RPF leader Fred Rwigyema was shot in the head and killed. The exact circumstances of Rwigyema's death are disputed; the official line of Kagame's government,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGovernment_of_Rwanda2009-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGovernment_of_Rwanda2009) and the version mentioned by historian [Gérard Prunier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Prunier "Gérard Prunier") in his 1995 book on the subject, was that Rwigyema was killed by a stray bullet.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199995–96-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199995–96) In his 2009 book _Africa's World War_, Prunier says Rwigyema was killed by his subcommander Peter Bayingana, following an argument over tactics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier200913–14-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier200913–14) According to this account, Rwigyema was conscious of the need to move slowly and attempt to win over the Hutu in Rwanda before assaulting Kigali, whereas Bayingana and fellow subcommander [Chris Bunyenyezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Bunyenyezi?action=edit&redlink=1 "Chris Bunyenyezi (page does not exist)") wished to strike hard and fast, to achieve power as soon as possible. The argument boiled over, causing Bayingana to shoot Rwigyema dead.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier200913–14-1)[\[100\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier200913–14) Another senior RPF officer, [Stephen Nduguta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Nduguta?action=edit&redlink=1 "Stephen Nduguta (page does not exist)"), witnessed this shooting and informed President Museveni; Museveni sent his brother [Salim Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salim_Saleh "Salim Saleh") to investigate, and Saleh ordered Bayingana's and Bunyenyezi's arrests and eventual executions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier200914-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier200914)

When news of the RPF offensive broke, Habyarimana requested assistance from France in fighting the invasion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEWallis200624–25-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEWallis200624–25) The French president's son, [Jean-Christophe Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Christophe_Mitterrand "Jean-Christophe Mitterrand"), was head of the government's Africa Cell and promised to send troops.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEWallis200624–25-1)[\[102\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEWallis200624–25) On the night of 4October, gunfire was heard in Kigali in a mysterious attack, which was attributed to RPF commandos. The attack was most likely staged by the Rwandan authorities, seeking to convince the French the regime was in imminent danger.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[note 1\]](#refnote-105) As a result, 600 French soldiers arrived in Rwanda the following day, twice as many as initially pledged.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEWallis200624–25-2)[\[102\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEWallis200624–25) The French operation was code-named [Noroît](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Noro%C3%AEt?action=edit&redlink=1 "Opération Noroît (page does not exist)") and its official purpose was to protect French nationals.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEWallis200627-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEWallis200627) In reality the mission was to support Habyarimana's regime and the French parachute companies immediately set up positions blocking the RPF advance to the capital and [Kigali International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali_International_Airport "Kigali International Airport").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern200014-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern200014) Belgium and Zaire also sent troops to Kigali in early October.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999101-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999101) The Belgian troops were deployed primarily to defend the country's citizens living in Rwanda but after a few days it became clear they were not in danger. Instead, the deployment created a political controversy as news reached Brussels of [arbitrary arrests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrary_arrest_and_detention "Arbitrary arrest and detention") and massacres by the Habyarimana regime and its failure to deal with the underlying causes of the war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999107-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999107) Faced with a growing domestic dispute over the issue, and with no obvious prospect of achieving peace, the Belgian government withdrew its troops by the beginning of November.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999107–108-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999107–108) Belgium provided no further military support to the Habyarimana government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199988-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199988) Zairian President [Mobutu Sese Seko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko "Mobutu Sese Seko")'s contribution was to send several hundred troops of the elite [Special Presidential Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Presidential_Division "Special Presidential Division") (DSP).[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999101-1)[\[107\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999101) Unlike the French, the Zairian troops went straight to the front line and began fighting the RPF,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999101-2)[\[107\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999101) but their discipline was poor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999109-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999109) The Zairian soldiers raped Rwandan civilians in the north of the country and looted their homes,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999109-1)[\[111\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999109) prompting Habyarimana to expel them back to Zaire within a week of their arrival.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200878-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200878) With French assistance, and benefiting from the loss of RPF morale after Rwigyema's death, the Rwandan Army enjoyed a major tactical advantage. By the end of October they had regained all the ground taken by the RPF and pushed the rebels all the way back to the Ugandan border.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199996-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199996) Many soldiers deserted; some crossed back into Uganda and others went into hiding in the [Akagera National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akagera_National_Park "Akagera National Park").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199996-1)[\[113\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199996) Habyarimana accused the Ugandan Government of supplying the RPF, establishing a "rear command" for the group in Kampala, and "flagging off" the invasion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016116-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016116) The Rwandan Government announced on 30 October that the war was over.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199996-2)[\[113\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199996)

The Rwandan Government used the attack on Kigali on 4October as the pretext for the arbitrary arrest of more than 8,000 mostly Tutsi political opponents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201534-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201534) Tutsi were increasingly viewed with suspicion; [Radio Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Rwanda "Radio Rwanda") aired incitement to ethnic hatred and a [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom "Pogrom") was organised by local authorities on 11 October in the Kibilira commune of [Gisenyi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisenyi_Province "Gisenyi Province"), killing 383 Tutsi.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201534–36-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201534–36)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern200416-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern200416) The burgomaster and the _sous-préfet_ were dismissed from their posts and jailed, but released soon thereafter.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEHuman_Rights_Watch199213–14-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEHuman_Rights_Watch199213–14) It was the first time in nearly twenty years that massacres against Tutsi were perpetrated,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTELongman2017126-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTELongman2017126) as anti-Tutsi violence under the Habyarimana regime had been only low level up to that point.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEEncyclopaedia_Britannica-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEEncyclopaedia_Britannica)

### Kagame's reorganisation of the RPF

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Kagame's reorganisation of the RPF")\]

[![Photograph of a lake with one of the Virunga Mountains behind, partially in cloud](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/RwandaVolcanoAndLake_cropped2.jpg/250px-RwandaVolcanoAndLake_cropped2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:RwandaVolcanoAndLake_cropped2.jpg) The [Virunga Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virunga_Mountains "Virunga Mountains"), the RPF base from 1990 to 1991

Paul Kagame was still in the United States at the time of the outbreak of war, attending the military training course in Fort Leavenworth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200864-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200864) He and Rwigyema had been in frequent contact by telephone throughout his stay in Kansas, planning the final details for the October invasion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200863-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200863) At the end of September Kagame informed the college that he was leaving the course, and was settling his affairs ready to return to Africa as the invasion began.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200864-1)[\[120\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200864) The college allowed him to leave with several textbooks, which he later used in planning tactics for the war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200864-2)[\[120\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200864) When Kagame learned of Rwigyema's death on 5October, he departed immediately to take command of the RPF troops.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200867-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200867) He flew through [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") and [Addis Ababa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addis_Ababa "Addis Ababa") to [Entebbe Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe_International_Airport "Entebbe International Airport"), where he was given safe passage by a friend in the Ugandan secret service;[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200875–76-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200875–76) the police considered arresting him, but with Museveni out of the country and no specific orders, they allowed him to pass.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876) Ugandan associates drove Kagame to the border and he crossed into Rwanda early on 15 October.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876-1)[\[124\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876)

The RPF were in disarray by the time Kagame arrived, with troop morale very low.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876-2)[\[124\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876) He later described his arrival as one of the worst experiences of his life; the troops lacked organisation following Rwigyema's death and were demoralised after their losses in the war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876-3)[\[124\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200876) Kagame was well known to the RPF troops, many of whom had fought with him in the Ugandan Army, and they welcomed his arrival in the field.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200878–79-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200878–79) He spent the following weeks gathering intelligence with senior officers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200879-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200879) By the end of October, with the RPF forced back to the Ugandan border, Kagame decided it was futile to continue fighting.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200879-1)[\[126\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200879) He therefore withdrew most of the army from north-eastern Rwanda, moving them to the [Virunga Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virunga_Mountains "Virunga Mountains"), along the northwestern border.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200879-2)[\[126\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200879) Kagame knew that the rugged terrain of the Virungas offered protection from attacks, even if the RPF's position was discovered.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999114–115-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999114–115) The march west took almost a week during which the soldiers crossed the border into Uganda several times, with the permission of President Museveni, taking advantage of personal friendships between the RPF soldiers and their ex-colleagues in the Ugandan Army.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200880-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200880)

Meanwhile, some RPF soldiers remained as a [decoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoy#Military_decoy "Decoy") to carry out small-scale attacks on the Rwandan Army, who remained unaware of the Front's relocation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200880-1)[\[128\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200880) The reorientation towards [guerrilla warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_warfare "Guerrilla warfare") began with a raid on a Rwandan customs post across the border from [Katuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katuna "Katuna").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016112–113-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016112–113) Following the attack, the Rwandan Government accused Uganda of deliberately sheltering the RPF.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016114-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016114) The RPF's new tactics inflicted heavy casualties on the Rwandan Army, which reacted by shelling Ugandan territory. Ugandan civilians were killed and a significant amount of damage to property was incurred, and there were reports of Rwandan troops crossing the border to loot and abduct locals.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016115–116-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016115–116)

Conditions in the Virungas were very harsh for the RPF. At an altitude of almost 5,000 metres (16,000ft),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115) there was no ready availability of food or supplies and, lacking warm clothing, several soldiers froze to death or lost limbs in the [high-altitude cold climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate "Alpine climate").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200880-2)[\[128\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200880)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115-1)[\[132\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115) Kagame spent the next two months reorganising the army, without carrying out any military operations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115-2)[\[132\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115) [Alexis Kanyarengwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Kanyarengwe "Alexis Kanyarengwe"), a Hutu colonel who had worked with Habyarimana but had fallen out with him and gone into exile, joined the RPF and was appointed chairman of the organisation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115-3)[\[132\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115) Another Hutu, [Seth Sendashonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Sendashonga "Seth Sendashonga"), became the RPF's liaison with Rwandan opposition parties.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDes_Forges1999[httpswwwhrworgreports1999rwandaGeno15-8-03htm_The_Rwandan_Patriotic_Front]-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDes_Forges1999[httpswwwhrworgreports1999rwandaGeno15-8-03htm_The_Rwandan_Patriotic_Front]) Most of the other senior recruits at the time were Ugandan-based Tutsi.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115-4)[\[132\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115) Personnel numbers grew steadily, volunteers coming from the exile communities in Burundi, Zaire and other countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999116-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999116) Kagame maintained tight discipline in his army, enforcing a regimented training routine, as well as a large set of rules for soldier conduct.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200883-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200883) Soldiers were expected to pay for goods purchased in the community, refrain from alcohol and drugs, and to establish a good reputation for the RPF amongst the local population.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200883-1)[\[135\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200883) The RPF punished personnel who broke these rules, sometimes with beatings, while more serious offences such as murder, rape, and desertion, were punishable by death.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200883-2)[\[135\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200883)

The RPF carried out a major fundraising programme, spearheaded by Financial Commissioner [Aloisia Inyumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloisia_Inyumba "Aloisia Inyumba") in Kampala.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115-5)[\[132\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999115) They received donations from Tutsi exiles around the world,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200882-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200882) as well as from businessmen within Rwanda who had fallen out with the government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999117-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999117) The sums involved were not enormous but, with tight financial discipline and a leadership willing to lead frugal lives, the RPF was able to grow its operational capability.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999118-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999118) It obtained its weapons and ammunition from a variety of sources, including the open market, taking advantage of a surplus of weaponry at the end of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999118-1)[\[138\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999118) It is likely they also received weaponry from officers in the Ugandan Army; according to Gérard Prunier, Ugandans who had fought with Kagame in the Bush War remained loyal to him and secretly passed weaponry to the RPF.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119) Museveni likely knew of this but was able to claim ignorance when dealing with the international community.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119-1)[\[139\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119) Museveni later said that "faced with \[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002183-0)[a\] _fait accompli_ situation by our Rwandan brothers", Uganda went "to help the RPF, materially, so that they are not defeated because that would have been detrimental to the Tutsi people of Rwanda and would not have been good for Uganda's stability".[\[140\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMamdani2002183) Journalist Justus Muhanguzi Kampe reported that the taking of military equipment by deserted Tutsi members of the Ugandan Army meant the national arsenal "nearly got depleted"; he suspected the war "must have had a tremendous financial impact on the Ugandan government, especially Uganda's military budget", costing the country "trillions of shillings".[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016117,_121-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016117,_121)

### Attack on Ruhengeri, January 1991

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Attack on Ruhengeri, January 1991")\]

[![Photograph of Ruhengeri, Rwanda, with buildings, a street, and people visible, and mountains in the background, partially in cloud](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Ruhengeri.jpg/250px-Ruhengeri.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ruhengeri.jpg) The town of [Ruhengeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhengeri "Ruhengeri"), with the [Virunga Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virunga_Mountains "Virunga Mountains") in the background

After three months of regrouping, Kagame decided in January 1991 that the RPF was ready to fight again.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200887-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200887) The target for the first attack was the northern city of [Ruhengeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhengeri "Ruhengeri"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200887-1)[\[142\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200887) south of the Virunga mountains.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEITMB_Publishing-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEITMB_Publishing) The city was the only provincial capital that could be attacked quickly from the Virungas while maintaining an element of surprise.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119-2)[\[139\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119) Kagame also favoured an attack on Ruhengeri for cultural reasons. President Habyarimana, as well as his wife and her powerful family, came from the north-west of Rwanda and most Rwandans regarded the region as the heartland of the regime.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119-3)[\[139\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119) An attack there guaranteed the population would become aware of the RPF's presence and Kagame hoped this would destabilise the government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888)

During the night of 22 January, seven hundred RPF fighters descended from the mountains into hidden locations around the city, assisted by RPF sympathisers living in the area.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888-1)[\[144\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888) They attacked on the morning of 23 January.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999120-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999120) The Rwandan forces were taken by surprise and were mostly unable to defend against the invasion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888-2)[\[144\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888) The Rwandan Police and army succeeded in briefly repelling the invasion in areas around their stations, killing large numbers of rebel fighters in the process.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888-3)[\[144\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888) It is likely the Rwandan Army forces were assisted by French troops, as the French Government later rewarded around fifteen French paratroopers for having taken part in the rearguard.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888-4)[\[144\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888) By noon, the defending forces were defeated and the RPF held the whole city.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889) Most of the civilian population fled.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016127-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016127)

One of the principal RPF targets in Ruhengeri was the prison, which was Rwanda's largest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119-4)[\[139\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119) When he learnt of the invasion the warden, [Charles Uwihoreye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Uwihoreye?action=edit&redlink=1 "Charles Uwihoreye (page does not exist)")\[ [fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles%20Uwihoreye "fr:Charles Uwihoreye")\], telephoned the government in Kigali to request instructions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999120-1)[\[145\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999120) He spoke to Colonel [Elie Sagatwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Sagatwa?action=edit&redlink=1 "Elie Sagatwa (page does not exist)"), one of the _akazu_, who ordered him to kill every inmate in the prison to avoid escape and defections during the fighting.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888-5)[\[144\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888) He also wanted to prevent high-profile political prisoners and former insiders from sharing secret information with the RPF.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119-5)[\[139\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119) Uwihoreye refused to obey, even after Sagatwa called him and repeated the order, having confirmed it with the president.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888-6)[\[144\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200888) Eventually, the RPF stormed the buildings and the prisoners were liberated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889-1)[\[146\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889) Several prisoners were recruited into the RPF, including [Théoneste Lizinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9oneste_Lizinde?action=edit&redlink=1 "Théoneste Lizinde (page does not exist)"), a former close ally of President Habyarimana, who had been arrested following a failed coup attempt in 1980.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889-2)[\[146\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119-6)[\[139\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999119)

The RPF forces held Ruhengeri through the afternoon of 23 January, before withdrawing into the mountains for the night.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889-3)[\[146\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889) The raid undermined the Rwandan Government's claims that the RPF had been ejected from the country and had been reduced to conducting guerrilla operations from Uganda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016127-1)[\[147\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016127) The government sent troops to the city the following day and a [state of emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_emergency "State of emergency") was declared, with strict curfews in Ruhengeri and the surrounding area.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889-4)[\[146\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889) The RPF raided the city almost every night for several months, fighting with Rwandan army forces, and the country was back at war for the first time since the October invasion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889-5)[\[146\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200889)

### Guerrilla war, 1991–1992

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Guerrilla war, 1991–1992")\]

Following the action in Ruhengeri the RPF again began to wage guerrilla war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999135-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999135) The Rwandan Army massed troops across the north of the country, occupying key positions and shelling RPF hideouts in the Virunga mountains, but the mountainous terrain prevented them from launching an all-out assault.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200890-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200890) Paul Kagame's troops attacked the Rwandan Army forces repeatedly and frequently, keen to ensure the diplomatic and psychological effect of the RPF's resurgence was not lost.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200891-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200891) Kagame employed tactics such as attacking simultaneously in up to ten locations across the north of the country, to prevent his opponents from concentrating their force in any one place.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200891-1)[\[150\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200891) This low intensity war continued for many months, both sides launching successful attacks on the other, and neither able to gain the upper hand in the war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200891-2)[\[150\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200891) The RPF made some territorial gains including capturing the border town of [Gatuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatuna "Gatuna").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999135-1)[\[148\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999135) This was significant as it blocked Rwanda's access to the port of [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa "Mombasa") via the [Northern Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Corridor "Northern Corridor"), forcing all trade to go through Tanzania via the longer and costlier [Central Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Corridor_(Africa) "Central Corridor (Africa)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999135-2)[\[148\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999135) By late 1991 the RPF controlled 5% of Rwanda, setting up its new headquarters in an abandoned tea factory near [Mulindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulindi "Mulindi"), Byumba province.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200896-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200896) Many Hutu civilians in areas captured by the RPF fled to government-held areas, creating a large population of [internally displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_persons "Internally displaced persons") in the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999136-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999136)

The renewed warfare had two effects in Rwanda. The first was a resurgence of violence against Tutsi still in the country. Hutu activists killed up to 1,000 Tutsi in attacks authorised by local officials, starting [with the slaughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/With_the_slaughter?action=edit&redlink=1 "With the slaughter (page does not exist)")\[ [fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/massacres%20des%20Bagogwe "fr:massacres des Bagogwe")\] of 30–60 [Bagogwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagogwe?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bagogwe (page does not exist)") Tutsi pastoralists near [Kinigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinigi "Kinigi") and then moving south and west to Ruhengeri and Gisenyi.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999136–137-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999136–137) These attacks continued until June 1991, when the government introduced measures to allow potential victims to move to safer areas such as Kigali.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999136–137-1)[\[153\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999136–137) The _akazu_ also began a major propaganda campaign, broadcasting and publishing material designed to persuade the Hutu population that the Tutsi were a separate and alien people, non-Christians seeking to re-establish the old Rwandan feudal monarchy with the final goal of enslaving the Hutu.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200898-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200898) This included the [Hutu Ten Commandments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu_Ten_Commandments "Hutu Ten Commandments"), a set of "rules" published in the _[Kangura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangura "Kangura")_ magazine, mandating Hutu supremacy in all aspects of Rwandan life.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200892–94-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200892–94) In response the RPF opened its own propaganda radio station, [Radio Muhabura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Muhabura "Radio Muhabura"), which broadcast from Uganda into Rwanda. This was never hugely popular but gained listenership during 1992 and 1993.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDes_Forges1999[httpswwwhrworgreports1999rwandaGeno1-3-10htm_Propaganda_and_Practice]-0)[\[156\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDes_Forges1999[httpswwwhrworgreports1999rwandaGeno1-3-10htm_Propaganda_and_Practice])

The second development was that President Habyarimana announced that he was introducing multi-party politics into the country, following intense pressure from the international community, including his most loyal ally France.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200897-0)[\[157\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200897) Habyarimana had originally promised this in mid-1990, and opposition groups had formed in the months since, including the [Republican Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Democratic_Movement "Republican Democratic Movement") (MDR), [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_(Rwanda) "Social Democratic Party (Rwanda)") (PSD) and the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(Rwanda) "Liberal Party (Rwanda)") (PL),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999123–124-0)[\[158\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999123–124) but the one-party state law had remained in place.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999127-0)[\[159\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999127) In mid-1991 Habyarimana officially allowed multi-party politics to begin, a change that saw a plethora of new parties come into existence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999127-1)[\[159\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999127)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199966–67-0)[\[160\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199966–67) Many had manifestos which favoured full democracy and rapprochement with the RPF,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200897-1)[\[157\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200897) but these were quite ineffective and had no political influence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999127-2)[\[159\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999127) The older opposition groups registered themselves as official parties and the country was notionally moving towards a multi-party inclusive cabinet with proper representation, but progress was continually hampered by the regime. The last opposition party to form was the [Coalition for the Defence of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_the_Defence_of_the_Republic "Coalition for the Defence of the Republic") (CDR),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199969-0)[\[161\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke199969) which was more hardline Hutu than Habyarimana's own party and had close links to the _akazu_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200898-1)[\[154\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200898)

Progress remained slow in 1991 and 1992. A cabinet set up in October 1991 contained almost no opposition, and the administrative hierarchy across the country recognised the authority of only Habyarimana's National Republican Movement for Democracy and Development party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999134-0)[\[162\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999134) Another one-party cabinet was announced in January 1992 which prompted large scale protests in Kigali, forcing Habyarimana to make real concessions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008103-0)[\[163\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008103) He announced his intention to negotiate with the RPF,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008103-1)[\[163\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008103) and formed a multi-party cabinet in April. This was still dominated by Habyarimana's party, but with opposition figures in some key positions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999145-0)[\[164\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999145) The opposition members of this cabinet met with the RPF, and negotiated a ceasefire. In July 1992 the rebels agreed to stop fighting, and the parties began peace negotiations in the Tanzanian city of [Arusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arusha "Arusha").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999150-0)[\[165\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999150)

### Peace process, 1992–1993

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Peace process, 1992–1993")\]

The peace process was complicated by the fact that four distinct groups were involved, each with its own agenda. The Hutu hardliners, centred around the family of Agathe Habyarimana, were represented by the CDR as well as extremists within the president's own MRND party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999161-0)[\[166\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999161) The second group was the official opposition, which excluded the CDR. They had much more democratic and conciliatory aims but were also deeply suspicious of the RPF, whom they saw as trying to upset the "democratic" policy of Hutu rule established in the 1959 revolution.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999151-0)[\[167\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999151) The third group was the RPF. Paul Kagame engaged with the peace process against the advice of some of his senior officers, in the knowledge that many of those on the other side of the table were hardliners who were not sincerely interested in negotiations. He feared that shunning the opportunity for peace would weaken the RPF politically and lose them international goodwill.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer200897-2)[\[157\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer200897) Finally there was the group representing President Habyarimana himself, who sought primarily to hold on to his power in whatever form he could. This meant publicly striving for a middle ground compromise solution, but privately obstructing the process and trying to delay change to the status quo for as long as possible.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999161-1)[\[166\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999161) Habyarimana recognised the danger posed to him by the radical Hutu faction and attempted in mid-1992 to remove them from senior army positions. This effort was only partially successful; _akazu_ affiliates [Augustin Ndindiliyimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Ndindiliyimana "Augustin Ndindiliyimana") and [Théoneste Bagosora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9oneste_Bagosora "Théoneste Bagosora") remained in influential posts, providing them with a link to power.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999167-0)[\[168\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999167)

The delegates at the negotiations in Arusha made some progress in the latter half of 1992, despite wrangling between Habyarimana and hardline members of his party that compromised the government officials' negotiating power.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999162-0)[\[169\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999162) In August the parties agreed to a "pluralistic transitional government", which would include the RPF.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999162-1)[\[169\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999162) The CDR and hardline faction of the MRND reacted violently to this. Feeling sidelined by the developing Arusha process,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999163-0)[\[170\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999163) they began killing Tutsi civilians in the [Kibuye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibuye,_Rwanda "Kibuye, Rwanda") area; 85 were killed, and 500 homes burnt.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999162-2)[\[169\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999162) Historian Gérard Prunier names late 1992 as the time when the idea of a genocidal "final solution" to kill every Tutsi in Rwanda was first mooted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999169-0)[\[171\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999169) Hardliners were busy setting up parallel institutions within the official organs of state, including the army, from which they hoped to effect a move away from the more conciliatory tone adopted by Habyarimana and the moderate opposition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999169-1)[\[171\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999169) Their goal was to take over from Habyarimana's government as the perceived source of power in the country amongst the Hutu masses, to maintain the line that the RPF and Tutsi more generally were a threat to Hutu freedoms, and to find a way to thwart any agreement negotiated in Arusha.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999170-0)[\[172\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999170)

The situation deteriorated in early 1993 when the teams in Arusha signed a full power-sharing agreement, dividing government positions between the MRND, RPF and other major opposition parties, but excluding the CDR.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173-0)[\[173\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999140–141-0)[\[174\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999140–141) This government was supposed to rule the country under a transitional constitution until free and fair elections could be held.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173-1)[\[173\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173) The agreement reflected the balance of power at the time; Habyarimana, the mainstream opposition, and the RPF all accepted it, but the CDR and hardline MRND officers were violently opposed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173-2)[\[173\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173) MRND national secretary [Mathieu Ngirumpatse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu_Ngirumpatse "Mathieu Ngirumpatse") announced that the party would not respect the agreement, contradicting the president and the party's negotiators in Arusha.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173-3)[\[173\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999173) The MRND hardliners organised demonstrations across the country and mobilised their supporters within the army and populace to begin a much larger killing spree than those that had previously occurred.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-0)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174) The violence engulfed the whole north-west of Rwanda and lasted for six days; many houses were burned and hundreds of Tutsi killed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-1)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104-0)[\[176\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141-0)[\[177\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141)

### RPF offensive, February 1993

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: RPF offensive, February 1993")\]

[![Map showing the partition of Rwanda between government, RPF, and demilitarised zones](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/RwandaTerritoryAfterFeb1993.png/500px-RwandaTerritoryAfterFeb1993.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:RwandaTerritoryAfterFeb1993.png) The partition of Rwanda following the RPF offensive in February 1993.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003xx-0)[\[178\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003xx) For the first time, the Rwandan Government acknowledged that it had lost part of the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106-0)[\[179\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106)

Paul Kagame responded by pulling out of the Arusha process and resuming the war, ending the six-month cease-fire.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-2)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201580-0)[\[180\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201580)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104-1)[\[176\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104) The RPF cited the CDR and MRND-hardliner violence as its reason for this, but according to foreign policy scholar [Bruce D. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_D._Jones "Bruce D. Jones") the offensive may actually have been intended primarily to increase the rebels' bargaining power at the peace talks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141-1)[\[177\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141) The next subject for the negotiations was the proportion of troops and officers to be allocated to each side in the new unified army. By demonstrating its military power in the field, through a successful offensive against the Rwandan Government forces, the RPF was able to secure an increased percentage of troops in the agreement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141-2)[\[177\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141)

The RPF began its offensive on 8February, fighting southwards from the territory it already held in Rwanda's northern border regions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-3)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174) In contrast to the October 1990 and 1991–1992 campaigns, the RPF advance in 1993 was met by weak resistance from the Rwandan Army forces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-4)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174) The likely reason was a significant deterioration in morale and military experience within the government forces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-5)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174) The impact of the long-running war on the economy, and a heavy devaluation of the [Rwandan franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_franc "Rwandan franc"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999159-0)[\[181\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999159) had left the government struggling to pay its soldiers regularly.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-6)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174) The armed forces had also expanded rapidly, at one point growing from less than 10,000 troops to almost 30,000 in one-year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern200420-0)[\[182\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern200420) The new recruits were often poorly disciplined and not battle ready,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern200420-1)[\[182\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern200420) with a tendency to get drunk and carry out abuse and rapes of civilians.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174-7)[\[175\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999174)

The RPF advance continued unchecked in February, its forces moving steadily south and gaining territory without opposition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175-0)[\[183\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175) They took Ruhengeri on the first day of fighting,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175-1)[\[183\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175) and later the city of [Byumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byumba "Byumba"). Local Hutu civilians fled _en masse_ from the areas the RPF were taking, most of them ending up in refugee camps on the outskirts of Kigali.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201581-0)[\[184\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201581)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175-2)[\[183\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175) The civilian cost of the offensive is unclear; according to André Guichaoua several thousand were killed,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201581-1)[\[184\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGuichaoua201581) while Prunier labelled the RPF killing as "small-scale".[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175-3)[\[183\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175) This violence alienated the rebels from their potential allies in the democratic Rwandan opposition parties.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999176-0)[\[185\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999176)

When it became clear that the Rwandan Army was losing ground to the RPF, Habyarimana requested urgent assistance from France.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141-3)[\[177\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141) Fearing that the RPF could soon be in a position to seize Kigali, the French immediately dispatched 150 troops to Rwanda,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141-4)[\[177\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141) along with arms and ammunition, to bolster the Rwandan Army forces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999176-1)[\[185\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999176) A further 250 French soldiers were sent on 20 February.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141-5)[\[177\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999141) The arrival of French troops in Kigali significantly changed the military situation on the ground. The RPF now found themselves under attack, French shells bombarding them as they advanced southwards.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008105-0)[\[186\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008105)

By 20 February the RPF had advanced to within 30km (19mi) of the capital, [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali "Kigali"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999177-0)[\[187\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999177) and many observers believed an assault on the city was imminent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175-4)[\[183\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999175) The assault did not take place, and the RPF instead declared a cease-fire.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999177-1)[\[187\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999177) Whether or not the RPF intended to advance on the capital is unknown. Kagame later said his aim at this point was to inflict as much damage as possible on Rwandan Army forces, capture their weapons, and gain ground slowly, but not to attack the capital or seek to end the war with an outright RPF victory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104-2)[\[176\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104) Kagame told journalist and author [Stephen Kinzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Kinzer "Stephen Kinzer") such a victory would have ended international goodwill towards the RPF and led to charges that the war had simply been a bid to replace the Hutu state with a Tutsi one.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104-3)[\[176\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008104) The increased presence of French troops and the fierce loyalty of the Hutu population to the government meant an invasion of Kigali would not have been achieved with the same ease that the RPF had conquered the north. Fighting for the capital would have been a much more difficult and dangerous operation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999178-0)[\[188\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999178) Several of Kagame's senior officers urged him to go for outright victory but he overruled them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008105-1)[\[186\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008105) By the end of the February war more than a million civilians, mostly Hutu, had left their homes in the country's largest exodus to date.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008105-2)[\[186\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008105)

### Arusha Accords and rise of Hutu Power, 1993–1994

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Arusha Accords and rise of Hutu Power, 1993–1994")\]

Main article: [Arusha Accords (Rwanda)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arusha_Accords_(Rwanda) "Arusha Accords (Rwanda)")

[![Photograph showing the front entrance of the Arusha International Conference Centre](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/AICC_Arusha.jpg/250px-AICC_Arusha.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:AICC_Arusha.jpg) The [Arusha International Conference Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arusha_International_Conference_Centre "Arusha International Conference Centre"), venue for peace talks to end the war

The RPF cease-fire was followed by two days of negotiations in the Ugandan capital [Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala "Kampala"), attended by RPF leader Paul Kagame, and involving President Museveni and representatives of European nations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106-1)[\[179\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106) The Europeans insisted that RPF forces withdraw to the zone they had held before the February offensive. Kagame responded that he would agree to this only if the Rwandan army were forbidden from re-entering the newly conquered territory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106-2)[\[179\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106) Following a threat by Kagame to resume fighting and potentially take even more territory, the two sides reached a compromise deal. This entailed the RPF withdrawing to its pre-February territory, but also mandated the setting up of a demilitarised zone between the RPF area and the rest of the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106-3)[\[179\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106) The deal was significant because it marked a formal concession by Habyarimana's regime of the northern zone to the rebels, recognising the RPF hold on that territory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106-4)[\[179\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106) There were many within the RPF senior command who felt Kagame had ceded too much, because the deal meant not only withdrawal to the pre-February boundaries, but also a promise not to encroach on the demilitarised zone. This therefore ended RPF ambitions of capturing more territory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106-5)[\[179\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008106) Kagame used the authority he had accumulated through his successful leadership of the RPF to override these concerns, and the parties returned once more to the negotiating table in Arusha.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008107-0)[\[189\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008107)

Despite the agreement and ongoing negotiations President Habyarimana, supported by the French Government,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999178-1)[\[188\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999178) spent the subsequent months forging a "common front" against the RPF.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999179-0)[\[190\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999179) This included members of his own party and the CDR and also factions from each of the other opposition parties in the power-sharing coalition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999179-1)[\[190\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999179) At the same time other members of the same parties issued a statement, in conjunction with the RPF, in which they condemned French involvement in the country and called for the Arusha process to be respected in full.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999179-2)[\[190\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999179) The hardline factions within the parties became known as [Hutu Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu_Power "Hutu Power"), a movement which transcended party politics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999188-0)[\[191\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999188) Apart from the CDR there was no party that was exclusively part of the Power movement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999181–182-0)[\[192\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999181–182) All major parties, save the [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_(Rwanda) "Social Democratic Party (Rwanda)"), had "moderate" and "Power" wings, with members of both camps claiming to represent the legitimate leadership of that party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999181–182-1)[\[192\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999181–182) Even the ruling party contained a Power wing, consisting of those who opposed Habyarimana's intention to sign a peace deal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999182-0)[\[193\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999182) Several radical youth militia groups emerged, attached to the Power wings of the parties; these included the _[Interahamwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interahamwe "Interahamwe")_, which was attached to the MRND,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003129-0)[\[194\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003129) and the CDR's _[Impuzamugambi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impuzamugambi "Impuzamugambi")_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999165-0)[\[195\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999165) The youth militia began actively carrying out massacres across the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern200425-0)[\[196\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern200425) The army trained the militias, sometimes in conjunction with the French, who were unaware the training they provided was being used to perpetrate the mass killings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999165-1)[\[195\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999165)

By June President Habyarimana had come to view Hutu Power, rather than the mainstream opposition, as the biggest threat to his leadership.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999186-0)[\[197\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999186) This led him to change tactics and engage fully with the Arusha peace process, giving it the impetus it needed to draw to a completion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999186-1)[\[197\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999186) According to Prunier this support was more symbolic than genuine. Habyarimana believed he could maintain power more easily through a combination of limited concessions to the opposition and RPF than he could if Hutu Power were allowed to disrupt the peace process.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999186-2)[\[197\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999186) The negotiation of troop numbers was protracted and difficult; twice the talks almost collapsed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999142–143-0)[\[198\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999142–143) The Rwandan Government wanted to allocate only 15% of the officer corps to the RPF, reflecting the proportion of Tutsi in the country, while the RPF was arguing for a 50/50 split.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999142–143-1)[\[198\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999142–143) The RPF were in a superior position following their successful February campaign and were backed in their demands by Tanzania, which was chairing the talks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999142–143-2)[\[198\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAdelmanSuhrke1999142–143) The government eventually agreed to their demands.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999193-0)[\[199\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999193) As well as 50% of the officer corps, the RPF was allocated up to 40% of the non-command troops.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999193-1)[\[199\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999193) The deal also mandated large-scale demobilisation; of the 35,000 Rwandan Army and 20,000 RPF soldiers at the time of the accords, only 19,000 would be drafted into the new national army.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEIPEP200049–50-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEIPEP200049–50) With all details agreed the [Arusha Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arusha_Accords_(Rwanda) "Arusha Accords (Rwanda)") were finally signed on 4August 1993 at a formal ceremony attended by President Habyarimana as well as heads of state from neighbouring countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999191-0)[\[200\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999191)

An uneasy peace was once again entered into, which would last until 7April of the following year. The agreement called for a United Nations [peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping "Peacekeeping") force; this was titled the [United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Assistance_Mission_for_Rwanda "United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda") (UNAMIR), and was in place in Rwanda by October 1993[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire200398-0)[\[201\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire200398) under the command of Canadian General [Roméo Dallaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rom%C3%A9o_Dallaire "Roméo Dallaire").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire200342-0)[\[202\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire200342) Another stipulation of the agreement was that the RPF would station diplomats in Kigali at the _Conseil national de développement_ (CND), now known as the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_(Rwanda) "Chamber of Deputies (Rwanda)"), Rwanda's Parliament building.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003130-0)[\[203\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003130) These men were protected by 600–1,000 RPF soldiers, who arrived in Kigali through UNAMIR's [Operation Clean Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Clean_Corridor?action=edit&redlink=1 "Operation Clean Corridor (page does not exist)") in December 1993.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003130-1)[\[203\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003130) Meanwhile, the Hutu Power wings of the various parties were beginning plans for a [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide "Genocide").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200-0)[\[204\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200) The President of Burundi, [Melchior Ndadaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchior_Ndadaye "Melchior Ndadaye"), who had been elected in June as the country's first ever Hutu president, [was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Burundian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt "1993 Burundian coup d'état attempt") by extremist Tutsi army officers in October 1993.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999199-0)[\[205\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999199) The assassination reinforced the notion among Hutus that the Tutsi were their enemy and could not be trusted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200-1)[\[204\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200) The CDR and the Power wings of the other parties realised they could use this situation to their advantage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200-2)[\[204\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200) The idea of a "final solution", which had first been suggested in 1992 but had remained a fringe viewpoint, was now top of their agenda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200-3)[\[204\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999200) An informant from the _Interahamwe_ told UNAMIR officials a group of Hutu extremists were planning on disrupting the peace process and killing Tutsis in Kigali.[&#8202;](#ref-back-207-0)[\[206\]](#refnote-207)

### Military operations during the 1994 genocide

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Military operations during the 1994 genocide")\]

Main article: [Rwandan genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_genocide "Rwandan genocide")

[![Map showing the advance of the RPF during the Rwandan genocide of 1994](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/RPF_advance_Rwandan_Genocide_1994.svg/500px-RPF_advance_Rwandan_Genocide_1994.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:RPF_advance_Rwandan_Genocide_1994.svg)

The cease-fire ended abruptly on 6April 1994 when President Habyarimana's plane [was shot down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira "Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira") near [Kigali Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali_Airport "Kigali Airport"), killing both Habyarimana and the new [President of Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Burundi "List of Presidents of Burundi"), [Cyprien Ntaryamira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprien_Ntaryamira "Cyprien Ntaryamira").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTENational_Assembly_of_France1998-0)[\[207\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTENational_Assembly_of_France1998)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(I-0)[\[208\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(I)2010-209) The pair were returning home from a regional summit in [Dar es Salaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam "Dar es Salaam") at which the leaders of Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania, had urged Habyarimana to stop delaying the implementation of the Arusha accords.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999211-0)[\[209\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999211) The attackers remain unknown. Prunier, in his book written shortly after the incident, concluded that it was most likely a coup carried out by extreme Hutu members of Habyarimana's government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999222–223-0)[\[210\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999222–223) This theory was disputed in 2006 by French judge [Jean-Louis Bruguière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Brugui%C3%A8re "Jean-Louis Bruguière") and in 2008 by Spanish judge [Fernando Andreu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Andreu "Fernando Andreu").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEWilkinson2008-0)[\[211\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEWilkinson2008) Both alleged that Kagame and the RPF were responsible.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBruguière20061-0)[\[212\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBruguière20061) At the end of 2010 the judges succeeding Bruguière ordered a more thorough scientific examination, which employed experts in ballistics and acoustics. This report seemed to reaffirm the initial theory that Hutu extremists assassinated Habyarimana.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(IV-0)[\[213\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(IV)2012-214) But the report did not lead the judges to drop the charges against the RPF suspects;[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEReyntjens2014-0)[\[214\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEReyntjens2014) this was finally done in 2018, due to lack of evidence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(VI-0)[\[215\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(VI)2018-216)

The shooting down of the plane served as the catalyst for the [Rwandan genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_genocide "Rwandan genocide"), which began within a few hours. A crisis committee was formed by the military, headed by Colonel Théoneste Bagosora, which refused to recognise Prime Minister [Agathe Uwilingiyimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathe_Uwilingiyimana "Agathe Uwilingiyimana") as leader, even though she was legally next in the line of political succession.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003224-0)[\[216\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003224) UN commander General Dallaire labelled this a coup and insisted that Uwilingiyimana be placed in charge, but Bagosora refused.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMcGreal2008-0)[\[217\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMcGreal2008)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003224-1)[\[216\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003224) The Presidential Guard killed Uwilingiyimana and her husband during the night, along with ten Belgian UNAMIR soldiers charged with her protection[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEGourevitch2000114-0)[\[218\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEGourevitch2000114) and other prominent moderate politicians and journalists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003231-0)[\[219\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003231)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999230-0)[\[220\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999230) The crisis committee appointed an interim government, still effectively controlled by Bagosora,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999268-0)[\[221\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999268) which began ordering the systematic killing of huge numbers of Tutsi, as well as some politically moderate Hutu, through well-planned attacks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003386-0)[\[222\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003386) Over the course of approximately 100 days between 500,000 and 1,000,000 were killed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEHenley2007-0)[\[223\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEHenley2007)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003386-1)[\[222\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003386)

On 7 April, as the genocide started, RPF commander Paul Kagame warned the interim government and the United Nations peacekeepers that he would resume the civil war if the killing did not stop.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003247-0)[\[224\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003247) The next day Rwandan Army forces attacked the national parliament building from several directions but RPF troops stationed there successfully fought back.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003264–265-0)[\[225\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003264–265) The RPF then crossed the demilitarised zone from their territory in the north and began an attack on three fronts, leaving their opponents unsure of their true intentions or whether an assault on Kigali was imminent. UNAMIR contingents in the demilitarised zone withdrew to their camps to avoid being caught in the fighting.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003269-0)[\[226\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003269) Kagame refused to talk to the interim government, believing it was just a cover for Bagosora's rule and not committed to ending the genocide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999268-1)[\[221\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999268) Over the next few days the RPF moved steadily south through the eastern part of the country, capturing Gabiro and large areas of the countryside to the north and east of Kigali.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003288-0)[\[227\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003288) Their unit stationed in Kigali was isolated from the rest of their forces but a unit of young soldiers successfully crossed government-held territory to link up with them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003288-1)[\[227\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003288) They avoided attacking Kigali or Byumba at this stage but conducted manoeuvres designed to encircle the cities and cut off supply routes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003299-0)[\[228\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003299) The RPF also allowed Tutsi refugees from Uganda to settle behind the front line in the RPF controlled areas.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003299-1)[\[228\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003299)

In April there were numerous attempts by the United Nations forces to establish a cease-fire, but Kagame insisted each time that the RPF would not stop fighting unless the killings stopped.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003300-0)[\[229\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003300) In late April the RPF secured the whole of the Tanzanian border area and began to move west from Kibungo, to the south of Kigali.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003326–327-0)[\[230\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003326–327) They encountered little resistance except around Kigali and Ruhengeri.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999268-2)[\[221\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999268) By 16 May they had cut the road between Kigali and [Gitarama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitarama "Gitarama"), the temporary home of the interim government, and by 13 June had taken Gitarama itself. The taking of Gitarama followed an unsuccessful attempt by the Rwandan Army forces to reopen the road. The interim government was forced to relocate to Gisenyi in the far north-west.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003410-0)[\[231\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003410) As well as fighting the war Kagame recruited heavily at this time to expand the RPF. The new recruits included Tutsi survivors of the genocide and Rwandan Tutsi refugees who had been living in Burundi, but they were less well trained and disciplined than the earlier recruits.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999270-0)[\[232\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999270)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/French_milouf_DF-ST-99-05514.JPEG/250px-French_milouf_DF-ST-99-05514.JPEG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:French_milouf_DF-ST-99-05514.JPEG) French soldiers in Kigali, August 1994

In late June 1994, France launched _[Opération Turquoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Turquoise "Opération Turquoise")_, a UN-mandated mission to create safe humanitarian areas for [displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_person "Displaced person"), [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee "Refugee"), and civilians in danger.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations1996-0)[\[233\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations1996) From bases in the Zairian cities of [Goma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goma "Goma") and [Bukavu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukavu "Bukavu"), the French entered south-western Rwanda and established the _Turquoise_ zone, within the [Cyangugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyangugu "Cyangugu")–Kibuye– [Gikongoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gikongoro "Gikongoro") triangle, an area occupying approximately a fifth of Rwanda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations1996-1)[\[233\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations1996) Radio France International estimates that _Turquoise_ saved around 15,000 lives,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTERFI2014-0)[\[234\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTERFI2014) but with the genocide coming to an end and the RPF's ascendancy, many Rwandans interpreted _Turquoise_ as a mission to protect the Hutus from the RPF, including some who had participated in the genocide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEFassbender201127-0)[\[235\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEFassbender201127) The French remained hostile to the RPF and their presence held up the RPF's advance in the south-west of the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMcGreal2007-0)[\[236\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMcGreal2007) _Opération Turquoise_ remained in Rwanda until 21 August 1994.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTERucyahana2007137-0)[\[237\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTERucyahana2007137) French activity in Rwanda during the civil war later became a subject of much study and dispute, and generated an unprecedented debate about French foreign policy in Africa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTELott2018364–365-0)[\[238\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTELott2018364–365)

Having completed the encirclement of Kigali, the RPF spent the latter half of June fighting for the capital.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421-0)[\[239\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421) The Rwandan Army forces had superior manpower and weapons, but the RPF steadily gained territory and conducted raids to rescue civilians from behind enemy lines.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421-1)[\[239\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421) According to Dallaire, this success was due to Kagame's being a "master of psychological warfare";[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421-2)[\[239\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421) he exploited the fact that the Rwandan Army were concentrating on the genocide rather than the fight for Kigali and exploited the government's loss of morale as it lost territory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421-3)[\[239\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003421) The RPF finally defeated the Rwandan Army in Kigali on 4July[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003459-0)[\[240\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003459) and on 18 July took Gisenyi and the rest of the north-west, forcing the interim government into Zaire. This RPF victory ended the genocide as well as the civil war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999298–299-0)[\[241\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999298–299) At the end of July 1994 Kagame's forces held the whole of Rwanda except for the _Turquoise_ zone in the south-west.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003474–475-0)[\[242\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003474–475) The date of the fall of Kigali, 4July, was later designated [Liberation Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Day "Liberation Day") by the RPF and is commemorated as [a public holiday in Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Rwanda "Public holidays in Rwanda").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMINFOTRA2015-0)[\[243\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMINFOTRA2015)

The UN peacekeeping force, UNAMIR, was in Rwanda during the genocide, but its [Chapter VI mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_VI_of_the_United_Nations_Charter "Chapter VI of the United Nations Charter") rendered it powerless to intervene militarily.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999261-0)[\[244\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999261) Efforts by General Dallaire to broker peace were unsuccessful,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999236–237-0)[\[245\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999236–237) and most of UNAMIR's Rwandan staff were killed in the early days of the genocide, severely limiting its ability to operate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003247-1)[\[224\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003247) Its most significant contribution was to provide refuge for thousands of Tutsi and moderate Hutu at its headquarters in [Amahoro Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amahoro_Stadium "Amahoro Stadium"), as well as other secure UN sites,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003270-0)[\[246\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003270) and to assist with the evacuation of foreign nationals. The Belgian Government, which had been one of the largest troop contributors to UNAMIR,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999204-0)[\[247\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999204) pulled out in mid-April following the deaths of its ten soldiers protecting Prime Minister Uwilingiliyimana.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern2004197-0)[\[248\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern2004197) In mid-May the UN conceded that "acts of genocide may have been committed",[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPBS-0)[\[249\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPBS) and agreed to reinforcement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern2004229-0)[\[250\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern2004229) The new soldiers started arriving in June,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMelvern2004411-0)[\[251\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMelvern2004411) and following the end of the genocide in July they stayed to maintain security and stability, until the termination of their mission in 1996.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations1996-2)[\[233\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations1996) Fifteen UN soldiers were killed in Rwanda between April and July 1994, including the ten Belgians, three Ghanaians, a Uruguayan, and Senegalese [Mbaye Diagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbaye_Diagne "Mbaye Diagne") who risked his life repeatedly to save Rwandans.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003400-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDallaire2003400)

## Aftermath

[![Overhead view of Kagame and Perry seated on leather seats with a large microphone visible and another army member in the background](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/DF-ST-98-01731.jpg/250px-DF-ST-98-01731.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:DF-ST-98-01731.jpg) Vice president and _de facto_ Rwandan leader [Paul Kagame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kagame "Paul Kagame") with United States Secretary of Defense [William Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_J._Perry "William J. Perry") in July 1994

The victorious RPF assumed control of Rwanda following the genocide, and as of 2026 remain the dominant political force in the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(V-0)[\[252\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(V)2013-253)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPark2020-0)[\[253\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPark2020)[&#8202;](#ref-back-255-0)[\[254\]](#refnote-255) They formed a government loosely based on the Arusha Accords, but Habyarimana's party was outlawed and the RPF took over the government positions allocated to it in the accords.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999299–300-0)[\[255\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999299–300) The military wing of the RPF was renamed as the [Rwandan Patriotic Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Patriotic_Army "Rwandan Patriotic Army") (RPA) and became the national army.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEWallis2006ix-0)[\[256\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEWallis2006ix) Paul Kagame assumed the dual roles of [Vice President of Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Rwanda "Vice President of Rwanda") and [Minister of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_(Rwanda) "Minister of Defence (Rwanda)"); [Pasteur Bizimungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteur_Bizimungu "Pasteur Bizimungu"), a Hutu who had been a civil servant under Habyarimana before fleeing to join the RPF, was appointed president.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier199990-2)[\[61\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier199990)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999300-0)[\[257\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999300) Bizimungu and his cabinet had some control over domestic affairs but Kagame remained commander-in-chief of the army and _de facto_ ruler of the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999369-0)[\[258\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999369)

### Domestic situation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Domestic situation")\]

The civil war severely disrupted Rwanda's formal economy, bringing coffee and tea cultivation to a halt, decimating tourism, diminishing food production, and diverting government spending towards defence and away from other priorities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTELegum1994B-0)[\[259\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTELegum1994B) Rwanda's infrastructure and economy suffered further during the genocide. Many buildings were uninhabitable and the former regime had taken all currency and moveable assets when they fled the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008181-0)[\[260\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008181) Human resources were severely depleted, with over 40% of the population having fled or been killed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008181-1)[\[260\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008181) Women constituted about 70 percent of the population, as many men had fled or been killed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-262-0)[\[261\]](#refnote-262) Outside of civilian deaths, 7,500 combatants had been killed during the war.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTECunningham2011137-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTECunningham2011137)[&#8202;](#ref-back-264-0)[\[note 2\]](#refnote-264) Many of the remainder were traumatised: most had lost relatives, witnessed killings, or participated in the genocide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEUnited_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum2011-0)[\[263\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEUnited_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum2011) The long-term effects of [war rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape_during_the_Rwandan_genocide "Rape during the Rwandan genocide") included social isolation, [sexually transmitted diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexually_transmitted_diseases "Sexually transmitted diseases") and unwanted pregnancies and babies, some women resorting to [self-induced abortions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-induced_abortion "Self-induced abortion").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEde_Brouwer200514-0)[\[264\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEde_Brouwer200514) The army, led by Paul Kagame, maintained law and order while the government began the work of rebuilding the country's institutions and infrastructure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBonner1994-0)[\[265\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBonner1994)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008187-0)[\[266\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008187)

[Non-governmental organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organisation "Non-governmental organisation") began to move back into the country but the international community did not provide significant assistance to the new regime. Most [international aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid "Aid") was routed to the refugee camps which had formed in Zaire following the exodus of Hutu from Rwanda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999327–328-0)[\[267\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999327–328) Kagame strove to portray the government as inclusive and not Tutsi-dominated. He directed removal of ethnicity from citizens' national identity cards and the government began a policy of downplaying the distinctions between Hutu, Tutsi, and Twa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBonner1994-1)[\[265\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBonner1994)

During the genocide and in the months following the RPF victory, RPF soldiers killed many people they accused of participating in or supporting the genocide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008189-0)[\[268\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008189) The scale, scope, and source of ultimate responsibility of these killings is disputed. [Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch "Human Rights Watch"), as well as scholars such as Prunier, allege that the death toll might be as high as 100,000,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999360-0)[\[269\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999360) and that Kagame and the RPF elite either tolerated or organised the killings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEDes_Forges1999[httpswwwhrworgreports1999rwandaGeno15-8-03htm_The_Rwandan_Patriotic_Front]-1)[\[133\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEDes_Forges1999[httpswwwhrworgreports1999rwandaGeno15-8-03htm_The_Rwandan_Patriotic_Front]) In an interview with Stephen Kinzer, Kagame acknowledged that killings had occurred but said they were carried out by rogue soldiers and had been impossible to control.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008191-0)[\[270\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008191) The killings gained international attention after the 1995 [Kibeho massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibeho_massacre "Kibeho massacre"), in which soldiers opened fire on a camp for internally displaced persons in [Butare Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butare_Province "Butare Province").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTELorch1995-0)[\[271\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTELorch1995) Australian soldiers serving as part of UNAMIR estimated at least 4,000 people were killed;[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEAustralian_War_Memorial-0)[\[272\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEAustralian_War_Memorial) the Rwandan Government claimed the death toll was 338.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier200942-0)[\[273\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier200942)

Paul Kagame took over the presidency from Pasteur Bizimungu in 2000 and began a large-scale national development drive, launching [a programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vision_2020_(Rwanda) "Vision 2020 (Rwanda)") to develop Rwanda as a [middle income country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_income_country "Middle income country") by 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(III-0)[\[274\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(III)2000-276)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008226–227-0)[\[275\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008226–227) The country began developing strongly on key indicators, including the [human development index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_index "Human development index"), health care, and education. Annual growth between 2004 and 2010 averaged 8% per year,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMurdock2010-0)[\[276\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMurdock2010) the poverty rate reduced from 57% to 45% between 2006 and 2011,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTENational_Institute_of_Statistics_of_Rwanda2012-0)[\[277\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTENational_Institute_of_Statistics_of_Rwanda2012) and [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy "Life expectancy") rose from 46.6 years in 2000[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEUNDP20132-0)[\[278\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEUNDP20132) to 64.3 years in 2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTECIA_(I-0)[\[279\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTECIA_(I)-281) A period of reconciliation began as well as the establishment of courts for trying genocide suspects. These included the [International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Tribunal_for_Rwanda "International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda") (ICTR) and _[Gacaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gacaca "Gacaca")_, a traditional village court system reintroduced to handle the large caseloads involved.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBowcott2014-0)[\[280\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBowcott2014) As women represented a larger share of the post-war population were not as frequently implicated in the genocide, they were entrusted by the regime with more tasks of reconciliation and reconstruction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-283-0)[\[281\]](#refnote-283)

### Refugee crisis, insurgency, and Congo wars

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rwandan_Civil_War&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Refugee crisis, insurgency, and Congo wars")\]

Main articles: [Great Lakes refugee crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_refugee_crisis "Great Lakes refugee crisis"), [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War "First Congo War"), and [Second Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Congo_War "Second Congo War")

[![View of refugee camp on foggy day, showing tents of various colours and the refugees](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Rwandan_refugee_camp_in_east_Zaire.jpg/250px-Rwandan_refugee_camp_in_east_Zaire.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rwandan_refugee_camp_in_east_Zaire.jpg) [Refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_camp "Refugee camp") in [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire "Zaire"), 1994

Following the RPF victory, approximately two million Hutu fled to refugee camps in neighbouring countries, particularly Zaire, fearing RPF reprisals for the Rwandan genocide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999312-0)[\[282\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999312) The camps were crowded and squalid and tens of thousands of refugees died in disease epidemics, including [cholera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholera "Cholera") and [dysentery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysentery "Dysentery").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEUNHCR2000-0)[\[283\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEUNHCR2000)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTESiddique1994-0)[\[284\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTESiddique1994) They were set up by the [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_High_Commissioner_for_Refugees "United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees") (UNHCR) but were effectively controlled by the army and government of the former Hutu regime, including many leaders of the genocide,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999313–314-0)[\[285\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999313–314) who began to rearm in a bid to return to power in Rwanda.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999381–382-0)[\[286\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999381–382)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997-0)[\[287\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997)

By late 1996, Hutu militants from the camps were launching regular cross-border incursions and the RPF-led Rwandan Government launched a counter-offensive.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999382-0)[\[288\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999382) Rwanda provided troops and military training[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997-1)[\[287\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997) to the [Banyamulenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyamulenge "Banyamulenge"), a Tutsi group in the Zairian [South Kivu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kivu "South Kivu") province,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999384–385-0)[\[289\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999384–385) helping them to defeat Zairian security forces. Rwandan forces, the Banyamulenge, and other Zairian Tutsi, then attacked the refugee camps, targeting the Hutu militia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999384–385-1)[\[289\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier1999384–385)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997-2)[\[287\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997) These attacks caused hundreds of thousands of refugees to flee;[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009118-0)[\[290\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009118) many returned to Rwanda despite the presence of the RPF, while others ventured further west into Zaire.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009122–123-0)[\[291\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009122–123) The refugees fleeing further into Zaire were relentlessly pursued by the RPA under the cover of the [AFDL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Democratic_Forces_for_the_Liberation_of_Congo "Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Congo") rebellion,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEReyntjens2013110–115-0)[\[292\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEReyntjens2013110–115) killing an estimated 232,000 people.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEEmizet2000163,_178–179-0)[\[293\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEEmizet2000163,_178–179) The defeated forces of the former regime continued a cross-border insurgency campaign,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008209-0)[\[294\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008209) supported initially by the predominantly Hutu population of Rwanda's north-western provinces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008216-0)[\[295\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008216) By 1999 a programme of propaganda and Hutu integration into the [national army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Defence_Forces "Rwandan Defence Forces") succeeded in bringing the Hutu to the government side and the insurgency was defeated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBrittain1999-0)[\[296\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBrittain1999)[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008215–218-0)[\[297\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEKinzer2008215–218)

As well as dismantling the refugee camps, Kagame began planning a war to remove Mobutu.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997-3)[\[287\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPomfret1997) Mobutu had supported the _genocidaires_ based in the camps and was also accused of allowing attacks on Tutsi people within Zaire.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBymanChalkHoffmanRosenau200118-0)[\[298\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBymanChalkHoffmanRosenau200118) The Rwandan and Ugandan governments supported an alliance of four rebel groups headed by [Laurent-Désiré Kabila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent-D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Kabila "Laurent-Désiré Kabila"), which began waging the [First Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Congo_War "First Congo War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009113–116-0)[\[299\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009113–116) The rebels quickly took control of North and South Kivu provinces and then advanced west, gaining territory from the poorly organised and demotivated Zairian army with little fighting.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009128–133-0)[\[300\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009128–133) They controlled the whole country by May 1997.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009136-0)[\[301\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009136) Mobutu fled into exile and the country was renamed the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo") (DRC).[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(II-0)[\[302\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(II)-304) Rwanda fell out with the new Congolese regime in 1998 and Kagame supported a fresh rebellion, leading to the [Second Congo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Congo_War "Second Congo War").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009182–183-0)[\[303\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEPrunier2009182–183) This lasted until 2003 and caused millions of deaths and severe damage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(II-1)[\[302\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBBC_News_(II)-304) A 2010 United Nations report accused the [Rwandan Patriotic Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Patriotic_Army "Rwandan Patriotic Army") of wide-scale human rights violations and crimes against humanity during the two Congo wars, charges denied by the Rwandan Government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMcGreal2010-0)[\[304\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMcGreal2010)

In 2015 the Rwandan government paid reparations to Uganda for damage inflicted during the civil war to its border regions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016120-0)[\[305\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEMuhanguzi_Kampe2016120)
