# Tony Clark investigated on 'inappropriate' relationship with sister-in-law

The surprise resignation of MLB Players Association boss [Tony Clark](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/mlb/2026/02/17/tony-clark-resigns-mlbpa-lockout/88716162007/) occurred after an internal investigation uncovered an "inappropriate" relationship with his sister-in-law, who had been hired by the union in 2023, [according to an ESPN](https://go.skimresources.com/?id=83224X1595658&url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.espn.com%2Fmlb%2Fstory%2F_%2Fid%2F47954830%2Fsources-tony-clark-resign-mlbpa-executive-director&xcust=SC1-USAT&sref=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.usatoday.com%2Fstory%2Fsports%2Fmlb%2F2026%2F02%2F17%2Ftony-clark-sister-in-law-mlbpa%2F88724652007%2F) report.

Clark's departure [leaves the MLBPA without an executive director](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/mlb/columnist/gabe-lacques/2026/02/17/tony-clark-mlbpa-baseball-mlb-lockout/88718205007/) less than 10 months before baseball's current collective bargaining agreement expires, with a contentious labor battle expected to spill over into 2027.

The MLBPA officially announced Clark's resignation Tuesday night, about 12 hours after the initial reporting on his exit. A 15-year MLB veteran, Clark became the union's executive director in 2013 after the death of previous chief Michael Weiner.

The union held a call with players on Tuesday afternoon but there was no vote taken on who would succeed Clark in the interim. MLBPA leadership had to delay its team-by-team tour a day amidst the upheaval, but was scheduled to meet with the [Kansas City Royals](https://sportsdata.usatoday.com/baseball/mlb/teams/kansas-city-royals/231) on Wednesday morning in Surprise, Arizona.

"It's tough, obviously, the stuff that I've seen so far, but it doesn't really change the state of the union," said [Detroit Tigers](https://sportsdata.usatoday.com/baseball/mlb/teams/detroit-tigers/230) ace Tarik Skubal, who sits on the MLBPA's executive subcommittee. "I think we're still as strong as ever. The union has always been about the players, and it's up to us to accomplish what we want to accomplish."

The MLBPA was also under federal investigation due to its role in OneTeam Partners, a licensing company co-owned with the NFLPA and three other sports unions.
