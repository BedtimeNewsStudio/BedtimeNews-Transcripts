Bust of Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik unveiled at INA Complex at Moirang   
- 21st October 2025 at INA Martyrs' Memorial Hall at Moirang Kangla -

Dr Syed Ahmed \*

![ Bust of Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik unveiled at INA Complex, Moirang  ](https://www.e-pao.org/galleries/images/misc/2025/11/Ali_20251109_1.jpg)   
*Bust of Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik unveiled at INA Complex, Moirang*

The 82nd Anniversary of the Provisional Government of Free India (Arzi Hukumat E-Azad Hind) was observed on 21st October 2025 at the INA Martyrs' Memorial Hall at Moirang Kangla by the INA War Museum cum Netaji Library Moirang and INA Local Committee Moirang.
  
  
The commemoration event included paying floral tributes to the statue of Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose, and the unveiling of a bust statue of Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik, the commander of the INA’s Bahadur Group, who hoisted the INA’s official flag in the liberated territory of Manipur at Moirang Kangla. The statue was donated by Paresh Kumar Nandi, Founder and President of Netaji Subhash Manch, Jamshedpur, in memory of his father.
  
  
The event was graced by MLA of Moirang Assembly Constituency & Chairman, INA Local Advisory Committee, Moirang, Th. Shanti Singh, INA Local Advisory Committee member Dr. Th. Gourabhidhu Singh, Retired Principal of Moirang College, A. Sharatchandra Sharma and Founder and President of Netaji Subhash Manch, Jamshedpur, Jharkhand, Paresh Kumar Nandi as Chief Guest, President, Guest of Honour and Special Guest.
  
  
A book authored by Paresh Kumar, entitled "*The Last Fight for Freedom, Moirang*," was also released.
  
  
**Formation of INA’s Provincial Government in Manipur**
  
  
On May 10 and 16, 1942, Japanese bombed Imphal city. It was their first air-raid. Many people, including women and children died. There was jail-break, looting of banks, most of the non-Manipuri businessmen who opened shops in and around the Khwaraimband Keithel fled, there was acute shortage of essential goods (price of salt and kerosene begin to cost Rs. 5 per seer and Rs. 150 per tin respectively).
  
  
Around two lakhs of Indian refugees entered Manipur from Burma (now Myanmar). In March 1944, three Japanese Army Divisions entered the North-Eastern Frontier crossing the hills, dense jungles and rivers. INA’s Subash Brigade was attached to the Japanese forces.
  
  
The 31st Japanese Division and a force of INA reached Kohima town in Naga Hills. After a fierce fight with the British Indian force, the town was taken over. The Imphal Dimapur Road was blocked for almost four weeks, which forced the British to airlift the basic needs.
  
  
The 15th Japanese Division and a regiment of INA attacked towards Tamu (a town near Moreh) and Ukhrul. The latter was captured. The combined force marched towards Imphal-Dimapur Road. A section of the Division advanced up to Pallel, but failed to reach Imphal.
  
  
The 33rd Japanese Mountain Gun Regiment and INA’s Bahadur (Intelligence) Group led by Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik entered Manipur crossing the southern hill ranges of Churachandpur District and reached Potsangbam evacuating the areas from British control.
  
  
It may be mentioned that Colonel Shaukat Malik was assisted by a Manipuri in his Intelligence Group, whose name was Naki Ahmed Choudhury (1914-1996), a Manipuri Muslim from Keikhu, now in Imphal East. He was a former British Indian Army who joined INA in Singapore.
  
  
As he knew the language and terrain of Manipur, he worked closely with the Colonel (for his role, see Dr. Syed Ahmed, ‘INA’s Moirang Campaign and Naki Ahmed Choudhury,’ *Shodh Sanchar*, Oct.-Dec. 2020).
  
  
By 13th April 1944, the withdrawal of the 17th British Division was complete. By then the advancing Japanese and INA force was joined by many Manipuris, which included M. Koireng Singh, L. Sanaba Singh, K. Kanglen Singh, M. Mani Singh, among others. They all were all from Moirang. On 14th April, a meeting was held at Trongloubi to discuss the situation and make plans for occupation.
  
  
The same day, at around 5 pm, Colonel Shaukat Ali hoisted the INA’s Tricolour flag, which had the springing tiger, at the Kangla at Moirang in the presence of the Japanese military commander and soldiers.
  
  
It was a historic moment in the history of India’s freedom struggle against the British. Numerous arms and ammunitions left by the British force were collected by the local people and handed to Colonel Shaukat Ali. H. Nilamani offered his house to Colonel Shaukat Ali, who made it the Head Quarter of INA. The house is preserved still today. (1)
  
  
Captain Ito, the commander of the 33rd Japanese Mountain Gun Regiment and Colonel Shaukat Ali delivered speech after planting the flag to the officers, soldiers and the local people who had gathered for the historic occasion.
  
  

![ Bust of Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik unveiled at INA Complex, Moirang ](https://www.e-pao.org/galleries/images/misc/2025/11/Ali_20251109_2.jpg)   
*Bust of Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik unveiled at INA Complex, Moirang*

M. Koireng Singh (2) translated the speech delivered by Colonel Shaukat Ali. A summary of the speech given by Colonel Shaukat goes:
  
  
*The Provisional Government of Azad Hind had declared war on England and America with a commitment to complete the creation of Greater East Asia and bring welfare to the people of India by defeating Anglo-American forces. The Indian National Army with the support of the Japanese Government has now entered into the sacred soil of India’s mainland.
  
  
In the course of its struggle for the liberation of the people of India from British slavery, we have now reached Moirang, the ancient citadel of the Manipur kingdom. Our commitment is to march to Delhi and unfurl the Tri-Colour flag there at Lal Killa (Red Fort in Delhi). Many had died on our way to reach here and many would die on our way to Delhi.
  
  
However, the expulsion of the enemy from the sacred soil of India is a must for us. We shall fight. We appeal to the brave and patriotic people of Manipur to support and provide supplies. Nothing about us should be told to the enemy; everything about the enemy must be informed to us. This is how people can contribute to our single-minded mission of expelling the Imperialist forces from India.
  
  
Freedom of India is very near; near at hand; we shall win. There would be progress and prosperity for the people of India after it…So give us your helping hands in our collective efforts of freeing India free from slavery.*
  
  
(See Priyadarshini Mukherji, Netaji Subash Chandra Bose: Contemporary Anecdotes, Reminiscences & Wartime Reportage, 2009)
  
  
The crowd rejoiced hearing the speech. The people of Moirang began to provide the essential provisions to the Indo-Japanese force, and extended all sorts of support. Many more Manipuris from Imphal came and joined INA.
  
  
It may be mentioned that Netaji had delivered a letter with the message appealing the leaders and workers of the Indian National Congress and the general public of Manipur to extend full support to the Indo-Japanese force which had entered Manipur. The letter was brought from a base camp in Chamol by a Kuki, named Lungdam.
  
  
He delivered the letter to a Meitei Medical officer posted at Sugnu, named Dr. Gulapchand Singh. It was finally handed to a political leader and Member of Nikhil Manipuri Mahasabha, Thokchom Angou Singh, a resident of Singjamei (2). Angou Singh shared Netaji’s message to the other members of the Mahasabha. Thirteen members of the body, led by Angou Singh, covertly reached Moirang and joined INA.
  
  
The area liberated by the Indo-Japanese force extended up to Pallel in the south-east: Kameng, Sekmai, Kangpokpi, and Kanglatongbi in the northeast; Torbung and Moirang in the south-west; Bishnupur and Irengbam/Maibam Lokpaching (Red Hill) in the heart of the valley.
  
  
Just a small portion of the Imphal Valley and Tamenglong could not be liberated. This vast area was came under the direct control of the INA for nearly three months, till the Japanese and the INA force withdrew by mid July 1944.
  
  
According to L. Nishikanta Singh (‘[Three Months of Provincial INA Government, Part II](https://www.e-pao.net/epSubPageSelector.asp?src=3_months_of_Provisional_INA_Govt_in_Manipur_Part_2_By_L_Nishikanta&ch=manipur&sub1=History_of_Manipur&sub2=Historical_War_Manipur)’ e-pao.net), 17 prominent INA freedom-fighters of Manipur (including two women) are
  
  
M. Koireng Singh,   
Hemam Nilamani Singh,   
K. Gopal Singh,   
L. Sanaba Singh,   
P. Tomal Singh,   
Th. Angou Singh,   
S. Ibohal Singh,   
M. Amuba Singh,   
L. Irabot Singh,   
Kh. Jugeshor Singh,   
L. Kanhai Singh,   
L. Bijoy Singh,   
I. Tombi Singh,   
W. Gyaneshwar Singh,   
M. Jatra Singh,   
M. Randhoni Devi and   
O. Keina Devi.
  
  
The last two women joined the Jhansi Rani Regiment. They all got freedom fighters’ pension.
  
  
**Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik** 
  
  
Colonel Shaukat Ali belonged to the Bawahalpore State forces and at the time of the fall of Singapore, held the rank of a Captain. He was a staunch supporter of the INA movement from the very beginning, and went to Bangkok Conference as a delegate. After the formation of INA, he was given the command of the Intelligence Group that he commanded and trained with remarkable skill in Singapore.
  
  
In early September 1943, he left Singapore for Burma. His group was one of the first INA units to go to action during the operations in Manipur. He also became the first to hoist the Indian INA flag on the Indian soil at Moirang Kangla on 14th April 1944. Due to health issues, he and his soldiers went back to their base camp in Rangoon.
  
  
After the improvement of his health, he was commissioned to the front in February 1945. Once during a military operation in Burma, he and his force was surrounded by the British, but the force break through and returned to their base in Rangoon. He accompanied Netaji on his trip from Rangoon to Bangkok on 24th April 1945.
  
  
For his military engagements and achievements, he was conferred the Sardar-e-Jung (Leader of Battle), the two highest gallantry medal of honor given by Azad Hind Government. The award was given to Malik for the capture of Moirang.
  
  
He faced the Red Fort trail. He was a special invitee in the Advisory Committee formed for the welfare of the members of the INA. After partition, Col. Shaukat Malik became a citizen of Pakistan as he was a native of western Punjab. Sadly, his achievements are not properly documented.
  
  
**End-Notes**
  
  
**(1) Mairembam Koireng Singh (1915-1994)** joined INA in 1944. He was one of the founder members of Manipur State Congress. He became a member of the Legislative Assembly in 1948, Electoral College in 1952, President of Manipur State Congress (1955 - 56) and Territorial Council (1957-1962). He served as Chief Minister of Manipur (1963-69). He was elected to the Legislative Assembly in 1972, 1980 and 1985.
  
  
**(2) Hemam Nialamani Singh (1912-2002)** joined the INA at Moirang in 1944. He joined the INA’s retreat to Burma and reached Yangon. There he worked with Netaji for almost a year. He was captured along with other INA and put in jail. They all were released on 17th February 1946. He later joined Congress. He became a member of the Territorial Council in 1957.
  
  
He was elected to the State Legislative Assembly in 1972 and became Deputy Minister for Education. He was a pioneer of co-operative movement in Manipur (established Marketing Trading and Co-operative Bank at Moirang among others).
  
  
He established Hindi Training College at Imphal. He was founder member of Moirang College. He began to work (since 1955) as a Member of INA martyrs Memorial Committee (Azad Hind Fauz Saheed Smarak Committee) and Netaji Library.
  
  
**(3) Thokchom Angou Singh (1889-1976)** was a political leader associated with Praja Samelan. He even served as President of the party. He joined INA after receiving the letter signed by Netaji. He later accompanied the INA, who were retreating to Burma by the middle of 1944.
  
  
At Yangon, Angou and several other INA were made captured and put in jail by the British army. They were finally released on 17th February 1946. He later served as a member of the Territorial Council (1957-63) and served the public.

(See Priyadarshini Mukherji, Netaji Subash Chandra Bose: Contemporary Anecdotes, Reminiscences & Wartime Reportage, 2009)
  
  
The crowd rejoiced hearing the speech. The people of Moirang began to provide the essential provisions to the Indo-Japanese force, and extended all sorts of support. Many more Manipuris from Imphal came and joined INA.
  
  
It may be mentioned that Netaji had delivered a letter with the message appealing the leaders and workers of the Indian National Congress and the general public of Manipur to extend full support to the Indo-Japanese force which had entered Manipur. The letter was brought from a base camp in Chamol by a Kuki, named Lungdam.
  
  
He delivered the letter to a Meitei Medical officer posted at Sugnu, named Dr. Gulapchand Singh. It was finally handed to a political leader and Member of Nikhil Manipuri Mahasabha, Thokchom Angou Singh, a resident of Singjamei (2). Angou Singh shared Netaji’s message to the other members of the Mahasabha. Thirteen members of the body, led by Angou Singh, covertly reached Moirang and joined INA.
  
  
The area liberated by the Indo-Japanese force extended up to Pallel in the south-east: Kameng, Sekmai, Kangpokpi, and Kanglatongbi in the northeast; Torbung and Moirang in the south-west; Bishnupur and Irengbam/Maibam Lokpaching (Red Hill) in the heart of the valley.
  
  
Just a small portion of the Imphal Valley and Tamenglong could not be liberated. This vast area was came under the direct control of the INA for nearly three months, till the Japanese and the INA force withdrew by mid July 1944.
  
  
According to L. Nishikanta Singh (‘[Three Months of Provincial INA Government, Part II](https://www.e-pao.net/epSubPageSelector.asp?src=3_months_of_Provisional_INA_Govt_in_Manipur_Part_2_By_L_Nishikanta&ch=manipur&sub1=History_of_Manipur&sub2=Historical_War_Manipur)’ e-pao.net), 17 prominent INA freedom-fighters of Manipur (including two women) are

**Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik** 
  
  
Colonel Shaukat Ali belonged to the Bawahalpore State forces and at the time of the fall of Singapore, held the rank of a Captain. He was a staunch supporter of the INA movement from the very beginning, and went to Bangkok Conference as a delegate. After the formation of INA, he was given the command of the Intelligence Group that he commanded and trained with remarkable skill in Singapore.
  
  
In early September 1943, he left Singapore for Burma. His group was one of the first INA units to go to action during the operations in Manipur. He also became the first to hoist the Indian INA flag on the Indian soil at Moirang Kangla on 14th April 1944. Due to health issues, he and his soldiers went back to their base camp in Rangoon.
  
  
After the improvement of his health, he was commissioned to the front in February 1945. Once during a military operation in Burma, he and his force was surrounded by the British, but the force break through and returned to their base in Rangoon. He accompanied Netaji on his trip from Rangoon to Bangkok on 24th April 1945.
  
  
For his military engagements and achievements, he was conferred the Sardar-e-Jung (Leader of Battle), the two highest gallantry medal of honor given by Azad Hind Government. The award was given to Malik for the capture of Moirang.
  
  
He faced the Red Fort trail. He was a special invitee in the Advisory Committee formed for the welfare of the members of the INA. After partition, Col. Shaukat Malik became a citizen of Pakistan as he was a native of western Punjab. Sadly, his achievements are not properly documented.
  
  
**End-Notes**
  
  
**(1) Mairembam Koireng Singh (1915-1994)** joined INA in 1944. He was one of the founder members of Manipur State Congress. He became a member of the Legislative Assembly in 1948, Electoral College in 1952, President of Manipur State Congress (1955 - 56) and Territorial Council (1957-1962). He served as Chief Minister of Manipur (1963-69). He was elected to the Legislative Assembly in 1972, 1980 and 1985.
  
  
**(2) Hemam Nialamani Singh (1912-2002)** joined the INA at Moirang in 1944. He joined the INA’s retreat to Burma and reached Yangon. There he worked with Netaji for almost a year. He was captured along with other INA and put in jail. They all were released on 17th February 1946. He later joined Congress. He became a member of the Territorial Council in 1957.
  
  
He was elected to the State Legislative Assembly in 1972 and became Deputy Minister for Education. He was a pioneer of co-operative movement in Manipur (established Marketing Trading and Co-operative Bank at Moirang among others).
  
  
He established Hindi Training College at Imphal. He was founder member of Moirang College. He began to work (since 1955) as a Member of INA martyrs Memorial Committee (Azad Hind Fauz Saheed Smarak Committee) and Netaji Library.
  
  
**(3) Thokchom Angou Singh (1889-1976)** was a political leader associated with Praja Samelan. He even served as President of the party. He joined INA after receiving the letter signed by Netaji. He later accompanied the INA, who were retreating to Burma by the middle of 1944.
  
  
At Yangon, Angou and several other INA were made captured and put in jail by the British army. They were finally released on 17th February 1946. He later served as a member of the Territorial Council (1957-63) and served the public.
