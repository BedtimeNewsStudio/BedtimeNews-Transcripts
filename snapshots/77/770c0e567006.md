# NOTA DE ALERTA:

## Alert Note

*El Salvador: Meteorological and agricultural drought in the context of the El* 
*Niño phenomenon, 13 October 2026.*

**Completed by:** Christian Association for Education and Development (ALFALIT) and Salvadoran 
Lutheran Synod (SLS).

**Date completed:** 13 October 2026

Forum: ACT El Salvador Forum

Type of emergency: Drought (slow onset emergency), in the context of the El Niño phenomenon

### 1. Nature of the emergency

El Salvador is facing a slow-onset emergency caused by meteorological and agricultural drought, 
characterised by cumulative rainfall deficits, prolonged dry spells and extreme temperatures, 
affecting agricultural and livestock production, water availability, food security and rural 
livelihoods, particularly in the Dry Corridor. Three episodes of meteorological drought were 
recorded during 2026, including one beginning on 7 August that lasted up to 19 consecutive dry 
days. In August, accumulated national rainfall was 134.9 mm, compared with a historical average 
of 313.6 mm, equivalent to a 57% deficit, the most severe for that month since 1971.

The severity of the situation prompted the <u>declaration of a Red Alert</u> for meteorological and 
agricultural drought on 25 August 2026 and, subsequently, the Legislative Assembly's approval of a 
six-month <u>State of National Emergency</u> on 7 October 2026. Food insecurity is an additional 
concern: the <u>Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis</u>, published in June 2026, 
projected that approximately 157,000 people (23% of the population analysed) would face acute 
food insecurity at Phase 3 or above between July and October in the tri-national border area of the 
Lempa River, shared by El Salvador, Guatemala and Honduras.

The activation of an immediate humanitarian response is justified by cumulative agricultural losses, 
declining incomes and the progressive depletion of food reserves among affected households, as 
the dry season approaches. The emergency declaration of 7 October, together with needs identified 
through the ACT El Salvador Forum's territorial assessments, provides the basis for a timely and 
targeted intervention to prevent further deterioration in food security, access to water and 
livelihoods among the prioritised families.

The intervention will focus on vulnerable communities in territories prioritised by the ACT Forum, 
mainly in the eastern and central-eastern parts of the country, where damage to agricultural 
production and rural livelihoods is exacerbating household food insecurity.

### 2. Impact and scale of the emergency

The 2026 drought has significantly affected rural communities in El Salvador's Dry Corridor, 
especially families dependent on subsistence farming, staple crop production and agricultural day 
labour. Although its effects extend nationwide, the ACT El Salvador Forum proposes a targeted, 
medium-scale humanitarian response for communities with the greatest needs and limited 
capacity to recover.

Needs assessments conducted during 2026 show deteriorating livelihoods and food security. The 
<u>rapid assessment by Oxfam and partner organisations</u>, conducted between July and August, 
covered 718 households in five departments: Ahuachapán, La Libertad, Morazán, San Miguel and 
Usulután. The ACT El Salvador Forum's Needs Assessment, conducted in October 2026, collected 
information from 187 households in 44 communities, identifying crop losses, reduced income, 
difficulties accessing food and water, and harmful coping strategies, including reducing meals, 
taking on debt and selling assets.<sup>1</sup> Among surveyed respondents who answered questions on crop 
losses, 55% reported total losses; likewise, 77.5% indicated that drought had severely affected 
their ability to obtain income or food. These findings provide direct evidence of needs in the

The impact is particularly severe for low-income rural households, small-scale producers and 
families dependent on agricultural day labour who have lost their crops and have no food reserves

Under these circumstances, the ACT El Salvador Forum plans to prioritise 800 families (3,200 
people), estimated to include 1,760 women (55%) and 1,440 men (45%), according to criteria 
related to drought impacts, food insecurity, socioeconomic vulnerability and recovery capacity. 
Geographic targeting will be based on identifying affected communities in La Paz, San Miguel, 
Usulután, Chalatenango, Morazán, La Unión, Sonsonate and Santa Ana, taking into account 
available community records and the Forum organisations' presence in the territories. Selection will 
be informed by needs assessments, community presence and identification of households facing 
the greatest difficulties in coping with the emergency. In the short term, the intervention seeks to 
help safeguard families' food consumption, access to water and well-being; in the medium term, it 
aims to prevent further depletion of their assets and livelihoods.
**3. Local and national capacity**
Rural households affected by the drought have increasingly limited coping capacity. According to 
the ACT El Salvador Forum's needs assessment, conducted in the departments of Morazán, San 
Miguel, La Unión, La Paz, Santa Ana and Sonsonate, 68.1% of surveyed households sought 
alternative income sources, 25.8% reduced the quantity or frequency of meals, 24.2% used savings 
or incurred debt, and 14.0% sold livestock, tools or other assets.<sup>2</sup> These strategies demonstrate 
worsening food security and the risk of progressive loss of livelihoods.
At community level, support structures include Community Development Associations (ADESCO), 
water management boards, producer organisations, churches and local Civil Protection 
commissions, which help identify needs and facilitate assistance. The ACT El Salvador Forum also 
has a presence in the territories, community relationships and an Emergency Preparedness and 
Response Plan (EPRP), updated in July 2026. In the prioritised territories, the requesting 
organisations have community ties and coordination arrangements with ADESCO, churches, local 
leaders, municipal authorities and government institutions, including the National Centre for 
Agricultural and Forestry Technology (CENTA), the Ministry of Governance and Territorial 
Development, and local Civil Protection structures. However, these capacities require additional 
resources to meet the needs of affected households.
At government level, the <u>National Civil Protection System</u> coordinates preparedness and response 
activities through its national and territorial structures. The Red Alert of 25 August and the <u>State of</u> 
<u>National Emergency</u> of 7 October 2026 established measures to assess damage, protect food 
security and mobilise assistance. The Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MAG) has also 
implemented productive support programmes and targeted food distributions. In some prioritised
communities, technical assistance from CENTA has been reported, although this does not include 
targeted humanitarian assistance for drought-affected households. Nevertheless, the available 
information does not allow the overall coverage of these actions to be determined or establish 
whether they reach all households that have lost crops or lack resources to recover.
The international humanitarian response includes anticipatory action mechanisms and targeted 
assistance. These include USD 2.5 million from the Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF) to 
protect up to 20,000 people in Ahuachapán and Santa Ana; allocations from the Regional 
Humanitarian Fund for Latin America and the Caribbean for food assistance; and activities by the 
Salvadoran Red Cross targeting around 10,000 people in Morazán and La Unión. These actalliance

interventions provide relevant capacity but have specific geographic, population and time coverage, 
so they cannot be assumed to address all needs identified by the ACT Forum.

National coordination is organised through the National Civil Protection System and, 
internationally, through the Humanitarian Country Team, with the participation of United Nations 
agencies and humanitarian organisations in sectors such as food security, WASH, health and 
protection. In this context, the response proposed by the ACT El Salvador Forum, through ALFALIT 
and the Salvadoran Lutheran Synod, seeks to complement existing activities and address unmet 
needs in prioritised communities through a targeted intervention coordinated with the relevant 
territorial mechanisms.

The requesting organisations will establish internal coordination mechanisms to harmonise family 
targeting, avoid duplication and complement response activities in the prioritised territories.

### 4. Main needs and gaps

The main needs identified in drought-affected communities concern food security, access to safe 
water, psychosocial well-being and protection. The ACT El Salvador Forum's needs assessment 
shows a significant deterioration in livelihoods: 55.0% of those responding to questions about 
agricultural losses reported losing their entire crops, 16.1% were unable to plant, and 77.5% 
reported substantial impacts on their ability to obtain food or income. In addition, 118 households 
indicated that they had staple food reserves sufficient for one week or less. These findings highlight 
the urgency of facilitating access to food and resources to meet essential needs, in line with the 
principles of the <u>Sphere Standards</u>.

Regarding water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), 23.1% of surveyed respondents identified drinking 
water as a priority need, and 12.9% reported greater difficulties or costs in obtaining it. These 
conditions call for measures to promote safe access to water, its storage and treatment, as well as 
appropriate hygiene practices. The Sphere Standards establish a reference minimum of 15 litres of 
water per person per day for drinking, food preparation and personal hygiene, subject to the specific 
conditions and needs of the population.

Protection and psychosocial well-being needs are also significant. Of respondents, 40.5% identified 
worry, anxiety or constant stress among the main household problems; 89.1% reported increased 
domestic and caregiving responsibilities, and 54.3% reported a permanent or occasional increase 
in family tensions. These findings justify including psychosocial support and measures to prevent 
and mitigate risks of gender-based violence (GBV), with particular attention to women, girls and 
other groups in vulnerable situations, without assuming that such tensions in themselves 
constitute confirmed cases of violence.

Although government and humanitarian interventions exist in food security, productive support and 
access to water, their geographic and population coverage is specific. Accordingly, the proposed 
response will prioritise the 800 families in communities with the most severe impacts and unmet 
needs, through cash transfers, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) interventions, psychosocial 
support and prevention of gender-based violence. Geographic and household selection will be 
based on needs assessments and coordination with other humanitarian actors to avoid 
duplication. The experience and territorial presence of the requesting organisations indicate 
favourable preliminary conditions for linking cash transfers with local markets and financial 
services. Centralised procurement of kits containing essential hygiene and personal care items for 
women affected by the drought is also planned, through direct purchase in accordance with 
established procedures and with the participation of the ACT El Salvador Forum's procurement 
committee.
