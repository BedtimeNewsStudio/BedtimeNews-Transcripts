$Trump

**$Trump** (stylized in all caps) is a [meme coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meme_coin "Meme coin") [cryptocurrency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") associated with United States president [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"), hosted on the [Solana blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solana_(blockchain_platform) "Solana (blockchain platform)").[\[5\]](#ref-5) One billion coins were created; 800 million remain owned by two Trump-owned companies, after 200 million were publicly released in an [initial coin offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_coin_offering "Initial coin offering") (ICO) on January 17, 2025. Less than a day later, the aggregate market value of all coins was more than $27 billion, valuing Trump's holdings at more than $20 billion. A March 2025 _[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")_ analysis found that the crypto project netted at least $350 million through sales of tokens and fees.[\[6\]](#ref-Asgari-2025)

The venture has faced widespread condemnation from ethics experts for Donald Trump's [conflicts of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest "Conflict of interest") related to the project and his presidential duties. During the [second Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Trump_administration "Second Trump administration"), Trump has promoted $Trump and taken actions that have raised the value of the meme coin, contributing to a substantial increase in his net worth.[\[7\]](#ref-7)

January 17, 2025[\[8\]](#ref-8)

The Trump [meme coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meme_coin "Meme coin") was launched on January 17, 2025, three days before Trump was [inaugurated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump "Second inauguration of Donald Trump") as president of the United States. The lack of a public announcement initially led to concerns that the [cryptocurrency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") was a scam and might have no association to the president-elect.[\[9\]](#ref-9) Several hours later, Trump announced $Trump on his [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") and [Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_Social "Truth Social") [social accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media_use_by_Donald_Trump "Social media use by Donald Trump").[\[10\]](#ref-USATodayInfo) The meme coin website described it as the "only official Trump meme".[\[11\]](#ref-Ishmael)[\[12\]](#ref-BBCJan1825) Its logo is a digitally-stylized image of Trump raising his fist after surviving the [attempted assassination in July 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Donald_Trump_in_Pennsylvania "Attempted assassination of Donald Trump in Pennsylvania").[\[13\]](#ref-NYTJan1825) A disclaimer said that the coin was "not intended to be, or the subject of" an investment opportunity or a security and was "not political and has nothing to do with" any political campaign, political office or government agency.[\[12\]](#ref-BBCJan1825) The terms of the offering prohibit coin buyers from joining any class-action lawsuits against the project and assert indemnity against any claims.[\[14\]](#ref-Huang-2025) Trump promoted the coin on the night of its ICO while a "Crypto Ball" was underway.[\[15\]](#ref-15)

_[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ reported in January 2025 that Trump affiliates controlled an additional 800 million tokens that, hypothetically, could be worth over $56 billion at the current market value, potentially making Trump one of the [richest people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_of_Donald_Trump "Wealth of Donald Trump") in the world at an estimated net worth of $63.8 billion.[\[16\]](#ref-16)

In March 2025, a _Financial Times_ analysis found that the crypto project netted at least $350 million ($314 million from the sale of the tokens and $36 million from fees).[\[6\]](#ref-Asgari-2025) However, by July 2025, _[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(magazine) "New York (magazine)")_ reported that the president's holdings in the coin were only worth about $93 million.[\[17\]](#ref-17) This was contradicted by reporting in _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_ in August 2025 suggesting that holdings held by Trump through a partnership "are potentially worth several billion dollars" and that actualized profits from the meme coin were $385 million.[\[18\]](#ref-:0)

### 2025 private dinner

In April 2025, the top 220 holders of the coin were offered dinner with the president, and the very top 25 holders would receive a special VIP White House tour. Following the announcement, the coin jumped more than 50%.[\[19\]](#ref-19)[\[20\]](#ref-20) Analysis found that leaked information about the promotion allowed certain traders to make bets on the coin before it was publicly announced. According to _The New York Times_, certain buyers in interviews and statements said they "bought the coins or entered the dinner contest with the intention of securing an action by Mr. Trump to affect United States policy".[\[21\]](#ref-LY-B2025a) On May 13, _The New York Times_ reported that GD Culture Group, a small company with ties to China, no reported revenue in 2024, and an affiliation with [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok"), announced it would spend $300 million on purchasing [Bitcoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin "Bitcoin") and $Trump using proceeds from "a stock sale to an unnamed entity in the [British Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Virgin_Islands "British Virgin Islands")".[\[22\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEYaffe-BellanyLipton2025a) The purchase was the first known instance of a China-linked firm buying Trump's cryptocurrency. TikTok is facing a [ban in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restrictions_on_TikTok_in_the_United_States "Restrictions on TikTok in the United States"), although Trump has postponed the decision several times.[\[23\]](#ref-23)

According to _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_, at least twenty investors sold or transferred their holdings in $Trump on May 12. The bidding process allowed certain investors to profit; in one instance, an investor, known only as "Noah", purchased US$2.2 million in $Trump before the contest and sold it at the end of the contest, profiting over US$950,000. In an apparent effort to stop the sell-off, the social media account associated with $Trump promised to give investors who kept their coins a [non-fungible token](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-fungible_token "Non-fungible token") known as "Trump Diamond Hand" and that they would earn reward points.[\[24\]](#ref-FOOTNOTELiptonFreedmanYaffe-Bellany2025) Several investors were able to attend the dinner for only the cost of trading fees by shorting $Trump.[\[25\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEKharif2025a) According to [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News "NBC News"), the winners of the contest collectively spent US$394 million.[\[26\]](#ref-FOOTNOTECollier2025) References to a tour of the White House were later removed from the dinner's website; a senior Trump official stated that the administration was not conducting a tour for the investors.[\[27\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEYaffe-BellanyLipton2025b)

The "black-tie optional"[\[28\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEIngramEgwuonwu2025) dinner was held at [Trump National Golf Club Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_National_Golf_Club_Washington,_D.C. "Trump National Golf Club Washington, D.C.") in [Sterling, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling,_Virginia "Sterling, Virginia").[\[22\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEYaffe-BellanyLipton2025a) Before the dinner, protesters had gathered to condemn the gathering, shouting, "Shame!"[\[29\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEKharifFaux2025) The menu featured [filet mignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filet_mignon "Filet mignon"), pan-seared [halibut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halibut "Halibut"), and a "Trump organic field green salad".[\[22\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEYaffe-BellanyLipton2025a) An attendee who spoke to _[The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verge "The Verge")_ described "pretty light" security,[\[30\]](#ref-FOOTNOTENguyen2025) Trump's limited presence and his speech—delivered behind a podium with the [presidential seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_the_president_of_the_United_States "Seal of the president of the United States")[\[31\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEJansen2025)—and a private afterparty at the Washington Marriott Capitol Hill hosted by Memecore.[\[30\]](#ref-FOOTNOTENguyen2025) Trump's speech was approximately fifteen[\[28\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEIngramEgwuonwu2025) to twenty minutes long.[\[32\]](#ref-FOOTNOTETreene2025) Four of the highest-paying investors, including [Justin Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Sun "Justin Sun"), were given US$100,000 Tourbillon watches.[\[30\]](#ref-FOOTNOTENguyen2025) The dinner was Sun's first visit to the United States since being investigated by the [United States attorney for the Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York "United States Attorney for the Southern District of New York") in 2021.[\[33\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEBerwickKowsmannGe_Huang2025)

MemeCore, a cryptocurrency company, invested US$19.3 million to become the second-largest holder; Cherry Hsu, the company's chief business officer, stated that he was interested in sharing its "vision and mission" with Trump and other attendees. The largest holder was an account belonging to [HTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTX_(cryptocurrency_exchange) "HTX (cryptocurrency exchange)") that was believed to be associated with Justin Sun. Javier Selgas, the chief executive of Fr8Tech, told the [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Commission "Securities and Exchange Commission") that he intended to purchase US$20 million in $Trump in an effort to convince Trump to lower [tariffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariffs_in_the_second_Trump_administration "Tariffs in the second Trump administration") on Mexico.[\[21\]](#ref-LY-B2025a) Through a guest list and social media, the _Times_ later identified many of the attendees, including Sun and the former basketball player [Lamar Odom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar_Odom "Lamar Odom").[\[34\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEFahrentholdLiptonShaoFreedman2025)

The dinner posed ethics concerns and opportunities for [foreign influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Emoluments_Clause "Foreign Emoluments Clause").[\[35\]](#ref-FOOTNOTENeuman2025)[\[21\]](#ref-LY-B2025a) [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming "Wyoming") senator [Cynthia Lummis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Lummis "Cynthia Lummis") and [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina") senator [Thom Tillis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_Tillis "Thom Tillis") expressed concerns over the dinner.[\[28\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEIngramEgwuonwu2025) [Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_for_Responsibility_and_Ethics_in_Washington "Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington")'s Donald Sherman described it as "one of the most blatant and appalling instances of selling access to the presidency".[\[36\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEAndersonGarrison2025) [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts") senator [Elizabeth Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Warren "Elizabeth Warren") described the dinner as an "orgy of corruption", while [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon "Oregon") senator [Jeff Merkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Merkley "Jeff Merkley") referred to it as "the [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest") of American corruption."[\[37\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEShapero2025)[\[38\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEHarty2025)

Merkley and Senate minority leader [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer "Chuck Schumer")[\[28\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEIngramEgwuonwu2025) introduced a bill to ban senior executive branch officials—including Trump—and their family from profiting off of cryptocurrency. Merkley additionally asked the [Office of Government Ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Government_Ethics "Office of Government Ethics") to investigate the dinner.[\[24\]](#ref-FOOTNOTELiptonFreedmanYaffe-Bellany2025) The [Senate Homeland Security Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Homeland_Security_Permanent_Subcommittee_on_Investigations "United States Senate Homeland Security Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations")'s investigation into cryptocurrency businesses involving Trump broadened to include the dinner.[\[39\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEKharif2025b) [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland") representative [Jamie Raskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Raskin "Jamie Raskin"), the ranking member of the [House Committee on the Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_the_Judiciary "United States House Committee on the Judiciary"), later initiated an inquiry into the dinner.[\[40\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEZakrzewski2025) The late-night television hosts [Jimmy Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Fallon "Jimmy Fallon") and [Jimmy Kimmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel "Jimmy Kimmel") ridiculed the dinner for its attendees and its apparent corruption, respectively.[\[41\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEVineyard2025)

### 2026 private event

In April 2026, the top buyers of the $Trump coin attended a [Mar-a-Lago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar-a-Lago "Mar-a-Lago") event where they got potential facetime with Trump.[\[42\]](#ref-:1) The [Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_for_Responsibility_and_Ethics_in_Washington "Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington") (CREW) condemned the event and the $Trump coin as unethical.[\[42\]](#ref-:1)

## Token distribution

According to the meme coin's website, ownership of the token is primarily concentrated in two Trump owned entities: CIC Digital LLC and Fight Fight Fight LLC, which together hold 80 percent of the coins remaining after the ICO.[\[43\]](#ref-43)[\[6\]](#ref-Asgari-2025) Trump's personal profit under this ownership structure is unclear.[\[6\]](#ref-Asgari-2025) The holdings are scheduled for gradual release over three years.[\[44\]](#ref-44)

## Value

After its launch in January 2025, the price of the meme coin soared by over 300% overnight.[\[45\]](#ref-45) Within two days, it became the 19th most valuable form of cryptocurrency in the world, with a total trading value of nearly $13 billion, and a total of $29 billion worth of trades based on a $64 value of each of the 200 million tokens issued by the afternoon of January 19.

In February 2025, a forensic analysis commissioned by _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ concluded that 813,294 wallets lost $2 billion by trading the coin while the president's company and partners profited about $100 million from trading fees.[\[46\]](#ref-Rogelberg)[\[47\]](#ref-:2) According to [_Fortune_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_(magazine) "Fortune (magazine)"), "Less than three weeks after its release, President Donald Trump's meme coin has produced more losers than winners. For every dollar in trading fees the Trump crypto creators raked in, investors lost $20."[\[46\]](#ref-Rogelberg)

By early July 2026, the coin was trading at less than $US2; it was down 97 percent from its peak price of $75.35. Two out of three buyers had lost money, either when selling the coin, or based on its early July market price, if still holding the coin. About 990,000 buyers had lost a total of $3.81 billion.[\[48\]](#ref-48)

## Reception

Some crypto executives and investors said Trump had undermined the credibility of the industry they had worked hard to build by selling coins known for their speculative nature and extreme volatility. They also noted Trump's brazen [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest "Conflict of interest") by setting crypto market policies while directly benefiting from participating in the market. Some noted its similarity to a " [rug pull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_scam "Exit scam")", in which a coin is launched and quickly abandoned, leaving early investors with steep losses. _[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ reported "Even the most ardent Trump supporters reached a breaking point when the $MELANIA token launched less than 48 hours after" $Trump, with one urging Trump to fire the advisor who recommended it. [Erik Voorhees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Voorhees "Erik Voorhees"), a prominent Bitcoin investor, criticized the coin as "stupid and embarrassing".[\[14\]](#ref-Huang-2025)[\[49\]](#ref-49)[\[50\]](#ref-50)

Others discussed its lack of any utility beyond enriching Trump.

> $TRUMP doesn't purport to hold value in the way that bitcoin or stablecoins do. Nor does $TRUMP entitle a buyer to a vote on a company's future direction, as World Liberty's initial token does. It does not even convey the right to own a digital cartoon of Trump. It's a meme coin, a novelty, a bit of fun—the fun, for those who enjoy it, of paying Donald Trump.

— [David D. Kirkpatrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_D._Kirkpatrick "David D. Kirkpatrick") in _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_[\[18\]](#ref-:0)

Ethics experts criticized the crypto venture and [government watchdogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchdog_journalism "Watchdog journalism").[\[51\]](#ref-Lipton_01192025) The venture and the possibility of foreign governments buying the coin and enriching Trump was highlighted as possibly violating the Constitution's [foreign emoluments clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Emoluments_Clause "Foreign Emoluments Clause").[\[52\]](#ref-52) Critics said it could allow special interests and foreign governments to seek to buy influence with the president.[\[53\]](#ref-Suderman-2025)

The release of $Trump was condemned by ethics lawyers, including Adav Noti, the director of the [Campaign Legal Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_Legal_Center "Campaign Legal Center"),[\[54\]](#ref-NYTimesInfo) and Jordan Libowitz, the vice president for communications at [Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_for_Responsibility_and_Ethics_in_Washington "Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington").[\[55\]](#ref-PoliticoInfo) Nick Tomaino, a former executive at [Coinbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coinbase "Coinbase"), described Trump's ownership and the timing of the cryptocurrency as "predatory".[\[54\]](#ref-NYTimesInfo) [Anthony Scaramucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Scaramucci "Anthony Scaramucci"), a former [White House Communications Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Communications_Director "White House Communications Director") during the [first Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "First presidency of Donald Trump") and current crypto investor, described the coin as " [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin "Idi Amin") level corruption" and said that the launch of the coin was bad for the cryptocurrency industry. He added, "Now anyone in world can essentially deposit money into bank account of President of USA with a couple clicks."[\[56\]](#ref-PoliticoJan1825)[\[53\]](#ref-Suderman-2025) Brendan Fischer, the deputy executive director at _Documented,_ an investigative news outlet, said, "The timing of this launch cannot be a coincidence. It comes just after Trump's campaign ended, and just before he formally takes office and is fully subject to federal ethics rules."[\[57\]](#ref-57) With the coin having slumped in value as of the end of early February 2025, the circumstances around the coin have led to wide condemnation from ethics experts and concerns over conflicts of interest as well as allegations from members of Congress that have called for an investigation.[\[58\]](#ref-58)[\[59\]](#ref-59)[\[60\]](#ref-60)

Some analysts described it as a [pump and dump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump_and_dump "Pump and dump") scheme,[\[61\]](#ref-61) and a "disaster".[\[62\]](#ref-62) A forensic analysis commissioned by _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ concluded that 813,294 wallets lost $2 billion by trading the coin while the president's company and partners profited about $100 million from trading fees.[\[46\]](#ref-Rogelberg)[\[47\]](#ref-:2) According to [_Fortune_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_(magazine) "Fortune (magazine)"), "Less than three weeks after its release, President Donald Trump's meme coin has produced more losers than winners. For every dollar in trading fees the Trump crypto creators raked in, investors lost $20."[\[46\]](#ref-Rogelberg)

Representative [Sam Liccardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Liccardo "Sam Liccardo"), a Democrat who represents [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley "Silicon Valley"), introduced the Modern Emoluments and Malfeasance Enforcement Act (MEME Act), which would prohibit the issuance or endorsement of any financial asset by the president, senior White House officials, or members of Congress, or their spouses or children. Unlike the [Hatch Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatch_Act "Hatch Act"), it would introduce a [private right of action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_right_of_action "Private right of action") for anyone harmed by purchasing such assets.[\[63\]](#ref-63)

## $Melania

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/20250120_Market_price_of_Melania_meme_coin.svg/500px-20250120_Market_price_of_Melania_meme_coin.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:20250120_Market_price_of_Melania_meme_coin.svg) The Melania meme coin experienced extreme volatility in its first days, after which it declined in price.[\[2\]](#ref-CBS_20250424)

After the launch of the $Trump meme coin, Trump's wife [Melania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melania_Trump "Melania Trump") launched her own meme coin, $Melania on January 19, 2025.[\[51\]](#ref-Lipton_01192025) The launch of the $Melania token was partially handled by Hayden Davis, who later played a key role in orchestrating the [$Libra cryptocurrency scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/$Libra_cryptocurrency_scandal "$Libra cryptocurrency scandal").[\[64\]](#ref-bb2) Davis admitted to his involvement in "sniping" the $Melania and $Libra tokens, a legally-questionable practice similar to [front running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_running "Front running").[\[65\]](#ref-65)

In October 2025, founders of decentralized crypto exchange Meteora were named in a class action lawsuit alleging that Melania Trump's meme coin, among fifteen tokens, was part of a scheme utilizing "weaponized fame to disarm diligence".[\[66\]](#ref-66) From [President Trump's 2024 inauguration day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump "Second inauguration of Donald Trump") to the end of November 2025, the price of the $Melania token fell 99%, while the price of the $Trump token fell 86%.[\[67\]](#ref-67)

## House Judiciary Committee report

On November 25, 2025, U.S. Representative [Jamie Raskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Raskin "Jamie Raskin") released a report finding that following an investigation by Democrats on the [U.S. House Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_the_Judiciary "United States House Committee on the Judiciary"), it was determined that the cryptocurrency policies of Trump were in fact used to benefit Trump and his family, with Trump in fact adding billions of dollars to his net worth through cryptocurrency schemes which were entangled with foreign governments, corporate allies, and criminal actors. The report, titled _Trump, Crypto, and a New Age of Corruption_, also found that Trump dismantled anti-corruption and financial integrity safeguards and pardoned people who were regarded as "corporate cronies" in order build his cryptocurrency empire.[\[68\]](#ref-68)[\[69\]](#ref-69)[\[70\]](#ref-70)
