# Caught between Shein and Vinted, the fashion market risks pauperisation

**By**
[![](https://media.fashionnetwork.com/m/7180/0ae2/b4d1/8d68/b677/2b19/ce7d/d6b3/1645/fb9e/60x60/fb9e.png)
Matthieu Guinebault](/auteur/Matthieu-Guinebault.html "Matthieu Guinebault")

**Published**

After [a weak year](https://fr.fashionnetwork.com/news/Les-ventes-francaises-de-textile-habillement-sont-reparties-a-la-baisse-en-2025,1803190.html#carolina-herrera) for the textiles and clothing sector [in 2025](https://fr.fashionnetwork.com/news/Les-ventes-francaises-de-textile-habillement-sont-reparties-a-la-baisse-en-2025,1803190.html#carolina-herrera), and with the sector expecting a further [0.5% contraction in 2026](https://fr.fashionnetwork.com/news/En-2026-la-mode-francaise-s-attend-a-une-stagnation-des-ventes,1786870.html#carolina-herrera), the [Institut Français de la Mode](/tags-institut-francais-de-la-mode "Institut Français de la Mode") takes stock of an industry in which ultra-fast fashion and second-hand clothing now account for a quarter of the market.

![](https://media.fashionnetwork.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=contain,width=1000,height=1000,format=auto/m/62bc/4243/04a3/945f/4b8a/f655/5f76/65ef/cdf0/9318/9318.jpg)

IFM / ©Louis Canadas

In the ranking of the largest players by volume and average price, [Vinted](/tags-vinted "Vinted"), [Shein](/tags-shein "Shein"), and [Temu](/tags-temu "Temu") place first, fifth, and eleventh respectively. Notably, the two Chinese players post the lowest price floors, each at €9, putting pressure on the other ranked players, including [Kiabi](/tags-kiabi "Kiabi"), [Amazon](/tags-amazon "Amazon"), [Decathlon](/tags-decathlon "Decathlon"), H&M, [Intersport](/tags-intersport "Intersport"), and [Bershka](/tags-bershka "Bershka"), as well as [E.Leclerc](/tags-e-leclerc "E.Leclerc") and [Carrefour](/tags-carrefour "Carrefour").

This is pulling down the average purchase price, which in 2025 stands at €22. The gender disparities are striking. Men and women spend an average of €16 and €12 respectively on a T-shirt, the most-purchased item. By contrast, men spend €49 on jeans- their second most-purchased item- versus €32 for women, for whom jeans rank third after dresses.

"More than ever, we're seeing a widening gap between very low prices and the market average, which is a very important factor," says [Gildas Minvielle](/tags-gildas-minvielle "Gildas Minvielle"), director of the IFM Economic Observatory. "It's also important in qualitative terms for a consumer whose perception of prices has been modified, even distorted, by the very strong prevalence of low prices."

The erosion of price benchmarks is nothing new, and the near-permanent promotions offered by major chains have been criticised for the past decade. The IFM notes that this shift in perception is now being reinforced by the second-hand market, which makes the prices of new items harder for French consumers to accept.

This matters given that second-hand now represents 19% of clothing sales by volume. Added to this is the 6% market share now captured by ultra-low-cost Chinese players such as Shein, Temu, and AliExpress. In value terms, these two categories account for 11% and 2% of the market respectively.

"We must also bear in mind that the share of ultra-fast fashion products sold on second-hand platforms keeps rising," notes IFM managing director [Xavier Romatet](/tags-xavier-romatet "Xavier Romatet"), urging professionals to see this for themselves in just a few clicks.

30.4% of sales online

The day before the IFM figures were presented, the online sales federation ([Fevad](/tags-fevad "Fevad")) unveiled its 2025 report. It shows that while [fashion remains the leading category in online sales](https://fr.fashionnetwork.com/news/Le-retail-en-decalage-avec-ses-ambitions-de-transformation-technologique-,1805785.html#carolina-herrera), it is also the major sector suffering the sharpest drop in sales over the year.

![](https://media.fashionnetwork.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=contain,width=1000,height=1000,format=auto/m/5364/d2e4/33b9/0be8/0f02/9c4d/b768/479f/cbe0/f395/f395.jpg)

IFM

"All age groups combined, e-commerce accounts for 30.4% of fashion purchases by value," says Gildas Minvielle, noting that online sales of textiles and clothing have risen over the past three half-year periods, and are even up 12.4% on 2019, whereas store sales have contracted by 12.5% over the same period. In 2025, while store sales declined by 2.7%, fashion e-commerce grew by 1.2%.

This growth has also benefited bricks-and-mortar retailers, with chains capturing 35% of online fashion sales, compared with 48% for pure players such as Amazon, Shein and [Zalando](/tags-zalando "Zalando"), and 17% for second-hand platforms such as Vinted and [Vestiaire Collective](/tags-vestiaire-collective "Vestiaire Collective").

By volume, French online clothing sales are dominated by Vinted, Amazon, Shein, Temu, and Zalando, followed by [Adidas](/tags-adidas "Adidas"), Kiabi, AliExpress, LeBonCoin, and Decathlon. The average price points in this ranking once again underscore the market’s polarisation, at €9–€10 for the Chinese players- above which sits an accessible "classic" such as Kiabi (€13)- while Adidas and Zalando stand at €43 and €44 respectively.

What could curb Chinese players?

The IFM estimates that in 2025, ultra-fast fashion accounted for around 19% of the volume of online fashion purchases, but only 8% by value. Across total French fashion sales, these players now account for 6% of the market by volume and 2% by value.

![](https://media.fashionnetwork.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=contain,width=1000,height=1000,format=auto/m/5739/0592/1325/041e/0b5a/db10/340c/9cb3/c71e/0b18/0b18.jpg)

IFM

As a survey conducted by the Institute in July found, the appeal is particularly strong among young female consumers, with 56% of 16- to 24-year-olds and 54% of 25- to 34-year-olds having already purchased ultra-fast fashion, compared with 38% of the population as a whole. These items are valued for their low prices (78%), product diversity (63%) and, notably, a wide range of sizes (43%).

Could taxes on small parcels curb the phenomenon? Yes- according to 57% of respondents, starting from as little as €2; rising to 82% for a €5 levy.

Risk of market pauperisation

The IFM data show another notable trend in 2025. While Shein remained the fifth-largest fashion seller by volume throughout the year, Temu rose from 24th to 11th place between the beginning and the end of the year.

This is mirrored in market shares by volume. Shein began the year with a 2.6% market share in the first quarter and ended with 2.7% in the final quarter. Over the same period, Temu increased its market share from 0.9% to 2.2%.

"There is a real risk of market pauperisation," warns Gildas Minvielle. "Certainly, volumes can rise because prices are very low, but few consumers are willing to increase the value of their spending. This points to a downward trend in market value, which would then decline even further than it has already."
