The **_Autobahn_** (IPA:[\[ˈaʊtoˌbaːn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/12/De-Autobahn2.ogg/De-Autobahn2.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Autobahn2.ogg "File:De-Autobahn2.ogg"); German pl._Autobahnen_, pronounced[\[ˈaʊtoˌbaːnən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/53/De-Autobahnen.ogg/De-Autobahnen.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Autobahnen.ogg "File:De-Autobahnen.ogg")) is the federal [controlled-access highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway "Controlled-access highway") system in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"). The official term is **_Bundesautobahn_** (abbreviated _BAB_), which translates as 'federal motorway'. The literal meaning of the word _Bundesautobahn_ is 'Federal Auto(mobile) Track'.

Much of the system has no [speed limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_limit "Speed limit") for some classes of vehicles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wa_16-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-wa_16-09) However, limits are posted and enforced in areas that are urbanised, substandard, prone to collisions, or under construction. On speed-unrestricted stretches, an [advisory speed limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advisory_speed_limit "Advisory speed limit") (_[Richtgeschwindigkeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advisory_speed_limit#Germany "Advisory speed limit")_) of 130 kilometres per hour (81mph) applies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) While driving faster is not illegal in the absence of a speed limit, it can cause an increased liability in the case of a collision (which mandatory auto insurance has to cover); courts have ruled that an "ideal driver" who is exempt from absolute liability for "inevitable" [tort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tort "Tort") under the law would not exceed the advisory speed limit.

A 2017 report by the Federal Road Research Institute reported that in 2015, 70.4% of the Autobahn network had only the advisory speed limit, 6.2% had temporary speed limits due to weather or traffic conditions, and 23.4% had permanent speed limits.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) In 2006, measurements from the German state of [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg "Brandenburg") showed average speeds of 142km/h (88mph) on a 6-lane section of Autobahn in free-flowing conditions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-studie_tempolimit-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-studie_tempolimit)

## Names

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Names")\]

Only federally built controlled-access highways with certain construction standards including at least two lanes per direction are called _Bundesautobahn_. They have their own white-on-blue signs and numbering system. In the 1930s, when construction began on the system, the official name was _[Reichsautobahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsautobahn "Reichsautobahn")_. Various other controlled-access highways exist on the federal ( _[Bundesstraße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesstra%C3%9Fe "Bundesstraße")_), state ( _[Landesstraße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landesstra%C3%9Fe "Landesstraße")_), district, and municipal level but are not part of the _Autobahn_ network and are officially referred to as _Kraftfahrstraße_ (with rare exceptions, like [A 995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_995 "Bundesautobahn 995") Munich-Giesing–Brunntal until 2018). These highways are considered _autobahnähnlich_ (autobahn-like) and are sometimes colloquially called _Gelbe Autobahn_ (yellow autobahn) because most of them are _Bundesstraßen_ (federal highways) with yellow signs. Some controlled-access highways are classified as "Bundesautobahn" in spite of not meeting the autobahn construction standard (for example, the [A62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_62 "Bundesautobahn 62") near [Pirmasens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirmasens "Pirmasens")).

Similar to some other German words, the term _autobahn_ when used in English is usually understood to refer specifically to the national highway system of Germany, whereas in German the word _autobahn_ is applied to any controlled highway in any country. For this reason in German, the more specific term _Bundesautobahn_ is strongly preferred when the intent is to make specific reference to Germany's Autobahn network.

## Construction

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Construction")\]

Similar to high-speed motorways in other countries, autobahns have multiple lanes of traffic in each direction, separated by a central barrier with [grade-separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_separation "Grade separation") junctions and access restricted to motor vehicles with a top speed greater than 60km/h (37mph). Nearly all exits are to the right; rare left-hand exits result from incomplete interchanges where the "straight-on" leads into the exit. The earliest motorways were flanked by [shoulders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_(road) "Shoulder (road)") about 60 centimetres (24in) in width, constructed of varying materials; right-hand shoulders on many autobahns were later retrofitted to 120 centimetres (47in) in width when it was realized cars needed the additional space to pull off the autobahn safely. In the postwar years, a thicker [asphaltic concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphaltic_concrete "Asphaltic concrete") cross-section with fully paved hard shoulders came into general use. The top design speed was approximately 160km/h (99mph) in flat country but lower design speeds were used in hilly or mountainous terrain. A flat-country autobahn that was constructed to meet standards during the [Nazi period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") could support speeds of up to 150km/h (93mph) on curves.

### Numbering system

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Numbering system")\]

Further information: [List of autobahns in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_autobahns_in_Germany "List of autobahns in Germany")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Deutschland_Autobahnen_Nummerierung.svg/250px-Deutschland_Autobahnen_Nummerierung.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Deutschland_Autobahnen_Nummerierung.svg) Numbering pattern of autobahns in Germany: single digit autobahns in black and colored first digit regions for two or three digit autobahns

The current autobahn numbering system in use in Germany was introduced in 1974. All autobahns are named by using the capital letter A, which simply stands for "Autobahn" followed by a blank and a number (for example [A8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_8 "Bundesautobahn 8")). The main autobahns going all across Germany have a single-digit number. Shorter autobahns that are of regional importance (e.g. connecting two major cities or regions within Germany) have a double-digit number (e.g. [A24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_24 "Bundesautobahn 24"), connecting Berlin and Hamburg). The system is as follows:

- [A 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_10 "Bundesautobahn 10") to [A 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_19 "Bundesautobahn 19") are in eastern Germany (Berlin, [Saxony-Anhalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony-Anhalt "Saxony-Anhalt"), parts of [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony "Saxony") and [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg "Brandenburg"))
- [A 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_20 "Bundesautobahn 20") to [A 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_29 "Bundesautobahn 29") are in northern and northeastern Germany
- [A 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_30 "Bundesautobahn 30") to [A 39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_39 "Bundesautobahn 39") are in [Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Saxony "Lower Saxony") (northwestern Germany) and Thuringia
- [A 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_40 "Bundesautobahn 40") to [A 49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_49 "Bundesautobahn 49") are in the [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr "Rhine-Ruhr") to [Frankfurt Rhine-Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhine-Main "Frankfurt Rhine-Main")
- [A 52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_52 "Bundesautobahn 52") to [A 59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_59 "Bundesautobahn 59") are in the [Lower Rhine region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Rhine_region "Lower Rhine region") to [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne "Cologne")
- [A 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_60 "Bundesautobahn 60") to [A 67](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_67 "Bundesautobahn 67") are in [Rhineland-Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland-Palatinate "Rhineland-Palatinate"), [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland "Saarland"), [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse "Hesse") and northern [Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg "Baden-Württemberg")
- [A 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_70 "Bundesautobahn 70") to [A 73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_73 "Bundesautobahn 73") are in [Thuringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuringia "Thuringia"), northern [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria "Bavaria") and parts of [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony "Saxony")
- [A 81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_81 "Bundesautobahn 81") is in [Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg "Baden-Württemberg")
- [A 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_92 "Bundesautobahn 92") to [A 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_99 "Bundesautobahn 99") are in (southern) [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria "Bavaria")
- [A 98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_98 "Bundesautobahn 98") is in [Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg "Baden-Württemberg")

There are also some very short autobahns built just for local traffic (e.g. [ring roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road "Ring road") or the [A555](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_555 "Bundesautobahn 555") from Cologne to Bonn) that usually have three digits for numbering. The first digit used is similar to the system above, depending on the region.

East–west routes are even-numbered, north–south routes are odd-numbered. The north–south autobahns are generally numbered from west to east; that is to say, the more easterly roads are given higher numbers. Similarly, the east–west routes are numbered from north (lower numbers) to south (higher numbers).

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: History")\]

See also: [Reichsautobahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsautobahn "Reichsautobahn")

### Weimar Republic: 1918-1933

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Weimar Republic: 1918-1933")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Funkturm_Berlin_View_14.jpg/250px-Funkturm_Berlin_View_14.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Funkturm_Berlin_View_14.jpg) Part of the [AVUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AVUS "AVUS") road in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"), the first automobile-only road and forefather of the Autobahn.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

The idea for the construction of the autobahn was first conceived in the mid-1920s during the days of the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic"), but the construction was slow, and most projected sections did not progress much beyond the planning stage due to economic problems and a lack of political support. One project was the private initiative _[HaFraBa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaFraBa "HaFraBa")_ which planned a "car-only road" crossing Germany from [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg") in the north via central [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt") to [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel "Basel") in Switzerland. Parts of the _HaFraBa_ were completed in the late 1930s and early 1940s, but construction eventually was halted by [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). The first public road of this kind was completed in 1932 between Cologne and Bonn and opened by [Konrad Adenauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer "Konrad Adenauer") ( [Lord Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mayor "Lord Mayor") of Cologne and future [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Germany_(Federal_Republic_of_Germany) "Chancellor of Germany (Federal Republic of Germany)") of West Germany) on 6 August 1932.[&#8202;](#ref-back-myth-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-myth) Today, that road is the [Bundesautobahn 555](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_555 "Bundesautobahn 555").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-wdr-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-wdr)[&#8202;](#ref-back-dhm-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-dhm) This road was not yet called _Autobahn_ and lacked a centre median like modern motorways, but instead was termed a _Kraftfahrstraße_ ("motor vehicle road") with two lanes each direction without intersections, pedestrians, bicycles, or animal-powered transportation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

### Third Reich: 1933-1945

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Third Reich: 1933-1945")\]

See also: [Urban planning in Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning_in_Nazi_Germany "Urban planning in Nazi Germany")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-H04560%2C_%C3%96sterreich%2C_Reichsautobahn%2C_Adolf_Hitler%2C_Spatenstich.jpg/250px-Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-H04560%2C_%C3%96sterreich%2C_Reichsautobahn%2C_Adolf_Hitler%2C_Spatenstich.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-H04560,_%C3%96sterreich,_Reichsautobahn,_Adolf_Hitler,_Spatenstich.jpg) Hitler ceremonially starts the excavation works for the first Austrian autobahn (1938).[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Bundesarchiv_Bild_146-1979-025-30A%2C_Reichsautobahn_mit_zwei_KdF-Wagen.jpg/250px-Bundesarchiv_Bild_146-1979-025-30A%2C_Reichsautobahn_mit_zwei_KdF-Wagen.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_Bild_146-1979-025-30A,_Reichsautobahn_mit_zwei_KdF-Wagen.jpg)"Reichsautobahn" in 1943

Just days after the [1933 Nazi takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler's_rise_to_power "Adolf Hitler's rise to power"), [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") enthusiastically embraced an ambitious autobahn construction project, appointing [Fritz Todt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Todt "Fritz Todt"), the Inspector General of German Road Construction, to lead it. By 1936, 130,000 workers were directly employed in construction, as well as an additional 270,000 in the supply chain for construction equipment, steel, concrete, signage, maintenance equipment, etc. In rural areas, new camps to house the workers were built near construction sites.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) The [job creation program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_creation_program "Job creation program") aspect was not especially important because [full employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_employment "Full employment") was almost reached by 1936.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] However, according to one source autobahn workers were often conscripted through the compulsory Reich Labor Service (and thereby removed from the unemployment registry).[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnnt_2020-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-cnnt_2020-12)

The autobahns were not primarily intended as major infrastructure improvement of special value to the military as sometimes stated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) Their military value was limited as all large-scale military transportation in Germany was done by train to save fuel. The [propaganda ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Ministry_of_Public_Enlightenment_and_Propaganda "Reich Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda") turned the construction of the autobahns into a major media event that attracted international attention.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

The autobahns formed the first limited-access, high-speed road network in the world, with the first section from Frankfurt am Main to [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt "Darmstadt") opening in 1935. This straight section was used for high-speed record attempts by the [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motor_racing "Grand Prix motor racing") racing teams of [Mercedes-Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz "Mercedes-Benz") and [Auto Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_Union "Auto Union") until a fatal crash involving popular German race driver [Bernd Rosemeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Rosemeyer "Bernd Rosemeyer") in early 1938. The world record of 432 kilometres per hour (268mph) set by [Rudolf Caracciola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Caracciola "Rudolf Caracciola") on this stretch just prior to the crash remains one of the highest speeds ever achieved on a public motorway. In the 1930s, a ten-kilometre stretch of what is today [Bundesautobahn 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_9 "Bundesautobahn 9") just south of [Dessau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dessau "Dessau")—called the [_Dessauer Rennstrecke_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_9#The_Dessauer_Rennstrecke "Bundesautobahn 9")—had bridges with no piers and was designed for cars like the [Mercedes-Benz T80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_T80 "Mercedes-Benz T80") to attempt to make [land speed records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_speed_record "Land speed record"). The T80 was to make a record attempt [in January 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_T80#Projections_for_the_1940_land_speed_record_attempt "Mercedes-Benz T80"), but plans were abandoned after the outbreak of [World War II in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Europe "World War II in Europe") in September 1939.

#### World War II: 1939-1945

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: World War II: 1939-1945")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Polish_Army_1945.jpg/250px-Polish_Army_1945.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Polish_Army_1945.jpg) Polish Army tanks riding to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin "Battle of Berlin") using the German Autobahn at the end of World War II in 1945.

During World War II, many of Germany's workers were required for various war production tasks. Therefore, construction work on the autobahn system increasingly relied on forced workers and concentration camp inmates, and working conditions were very poor. As of 1942, when [the war turned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa "Operation Barbarossa") against the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Reich "Third Reich"), only 3,800km (2,400mi) out of a planned 20,000km (12,000mi) of autobahn had been completed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnnt_2020-1)[\[16\]](#refnote-cnnt_2020-12)

Meanwhile, the [median strips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_strip "Median strip") of some autobahns were paved over to allow their conversion into [auxiliary airstrips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_strip "Highway strip"). Aircraft were either stashed in numerous tunnels or camouflaged in nearby woods. However, for the most part during the war, the autobahns were not militarily significant. Motor vehicles, such as trucks, could not carry goods or troops as quickly or in as much bulk and in the same numbers as trains could, and the autobahns could not be used by [tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank "Tank") as their weight and [caterpillar tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar_track "Caterpillar track") damaged the road surface. The general shortage of petrol in Germany during much of the war, as well as the low number of trucks and motor vehicles needed for direct support of military operations, further decreased the autobahn's significance. As a result, most military and economic freight was carried by rail. After the war, numerous sections of the autobahns were in bad shape, severely damaged by heavy [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") [bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II "Strategic bombing during World War II") and military demolition. Furthermore, thousands of kilometres of autobahns remained unfinished, their construction brought to a halt by 1943 due to the increasing demands of the war effort.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)

### West Germany: 1949–1990

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: West Germany: 1949–1990")\]

In [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany "West Germany") (FRG), most existing autobahns were repaired soon after the war. During the 1950s, the West German government restarted the construction program. It invested in new sections and in improvements to older ones. Finishing the incomplete sections took longer, with some stretches opened to traffic by the 1980s. Some sections cut by the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain "Iron Curtain") in 1945 were only completed after [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification "German reunification") in 1990. Others were never completed, as more advantageous routes were found. An example is [Strecke 46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strecke_46?action=edit&redlink=1 "Strecke 46 (page does not exist)")\[ [de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strecke%2046 "de:Strecke 46")\] between Bad Brückenau and Gemünden am Main on the Fulda-Würzburg route, which was replaced by [A7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_7 "Bundesautobahn 7").

### East Germany: 1949–1990

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: East Germany: 1949–1990")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Autobahn_bei_Oranienburg.jpg/250px-Autobahn_bei_Oranienburg.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Autobahn_bei_Oranienburg.jpg) East German Autobahn

In 1956, the speed limit was set to 100 kilometres per hour (62mph) in the new version of the Rules of the Road (Straßenverkehrsordnung), which adopted a lot of rules that corresponded with the international standards of the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) The reasons for this speed limit are unknown. Oftentimes it is argued that the roads were in a poor state, however, there is no proof that the road conditions were a relevant factor in introducing the speed limit, especially since the roads were not much used in the first 20 years after the Second World War and the majority of the road network was based on the Reichsautobahn of Nazi-Germany just like in West Germany, and thus were in a good state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) Speed limit violations on the autobahns of the GDR were rare because most cars didn't have the engine power to go much faster than the set limit. For example, the most common car of the GDR, the [Trabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabant "Trabant"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-IHT-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-IHT) could reach a maximum of only 110 kilometres per hour (68mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

### Reunification: 1990–present day

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Reunification: 1990–present day")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/08/Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F088783-0003%2C_Bei_Bad_Honnef%2C_Ferienverkehr_auf_der_A_3.jpg/250px-Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F088783-0003%2C_Bei_Bad_Honnef%2C_Ferienverkehr_auf_der_A_3.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F088783-0003,_Bei_Bad_Honnef,_Ferienverkehr_auf_der_A_3.jpg) [A 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_3 "Bundesautobahn 3") in 1991

The last 4 kilometres (2.5mi) of the remaining original _Reichsautobahn_, a section of [A 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_11 "Bundesautobahn 11") northeast of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") near [Gartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gartz "Gartz") built in 1936—the westernmost remainder of the never-finished [Berlinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlinka "Berlinka")— was scheduled for replacement around 2015.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)\[ _[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\] Roadway condition is described as "deplorable"; the 25 metres (82ft)-long concrete slabs, too long for proper expansion, are cracking under the weight of the traffic as well as the weather.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

### German-built _Reichsautobahnen_ in other countries

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: German-built Reichsautobahnen in other countries")\]

The first autobahn in Austria was the [West Autobahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Autobahn "West Autobahn") from [Wals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wals-Siezenheim "Wals-Siezenheim") near [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg "Salzburg") to [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna"). Building started by command of [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") shortly after the _[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss "Anschluss")_ in 1938. It extended the _Reichsautobahn 26_ from Munich (the present-day [A 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_8 "Bundesautobahn 8")), however only 16.8km (10.4mi) including the branch-off of the planned [Tauern Autobahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauern_Autobahn "Tauern Autobahn") was opened to the public on 13 September 1941.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) Construction works discontinued the next year and were not resumed until 1955.

There are sections of the former German _Reichsautobahn_ system in the [former eastern territories of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_eastern_territories_of_Germany "Former eastern territories of Germany"), _i.e._ [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia "East Prussia"), [Farther Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farther_Pomerania "Farther Pomerania"), and [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia "Silesia"); these territories became parts of Poland and the Soviet Union with the implementation of the [Oder–Neisse line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder%E2%80%93Neisse_line "Oder–Neisse line") after World War II. Parts of the planned autobahn from Berlin to [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigsberg "Königsberg") (the _[Berlinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlinka "Berlinka")_) were completed as far as Stettin ( [Szczecin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczecin "Szczecin")) on 27 September 1936. After the war, they were incorporated as the [A6 autostrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_autostrada_(Poland) "A6 autostrada (Poland)") of the [Polish motorway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_in_Poland "Highways in Poland"). A single-carriageway section of the _Berlinka_ east of the former " [Polish Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Corridor "Polish Corridor")" and the [Free City of Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Danzig "Free City of Danzig") opened in 1938; today it forms the Polish [S22 expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressway_S22_(Poland) "Expressway S22 (Poland)") from [Elbląg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbl%C4%85g "Elbląg") (Elbing) to the border with the Russian [Kaliningrad Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad_Oblast "Kaliningrad Oblast"), where it is continued by the R516 regional road. Also on 27 September 1936, a section from Breslau ( [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw "Wrocław")) to Liegnitz ( [Legnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legnica "Legnica")) in Silesia was inaugurated, which today is part of the Polish [A4 autostrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4_autostrada_(Poland) "A4 autostrada (Poland)"), followed by the (single vehicle) _Reichsautobahn 9_ from Bunzlau ( [Bolesławiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82awiec "Bolesławiec")) to Sagan ( [Żagań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBaga%C5%84 "Żagań")) the next year, today part of the Polish [A18 autostrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A18_autostrada_(Poland) "A18 autostrada (Poland)").

After the [German occupation of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Czechoslovakia "German occupation of Czechoslovakia"), plans for a motorway connecting Breslau with Vienna via [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno "Brno") (Brünn) in the " [Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia "Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia")" were carried out from 1939 until construction works discontinued in 1942. A section of the former _Strecke 88_ near Brno is today part of the [D52 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R52_expressway_(Czech_Republic) "R52 expressway (Czech Republic)") of the Czech Republic. Also, there is the isolated and abandoned twin-carriageway [Borovsko Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borovsko_Bridge "Borovsko Bridge") southeast of Prague, on which construction started in July 1939 and halted after the assassination of Reinhard Heydrich by former Czechoslovak army soldiers at the end of May 1942.

## Network size

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Network size")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/2013-06-08_Projekt_Hei%C3%9Fluftballon_DSCF0923.jpg/250px-2013-06-08_Projekt_Hei%C3%9Fluftballon_DSCF0923.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2013-06-08_Projekt_Hei%C3%9Fluftballon_DSCF0923.jpg) [BAB 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAB_7 "BAB 7"), the longest national motorway in the European Union, approaching [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg")[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Garching_Bundesautobahn_9.jpg/250px-Garching_Bundesautobahn_9.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Garching_Bundesautobahn_9.jpg) [Autobahn 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobahn_9 "Autobahn 9") near Munich with 8 lanes

As of 2021[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit), Germany's autobahn network has a total length of about 13,192 kilometres (8,197mi).[&#8202;](#ref-back-length-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-length) Longer similar systems can be found [in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System "Interstate Highway System") (77,960 kilometres or 48,440 miles)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30) [and in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressways_of_China "Expressways of China") (149,600 kilometres or 93,000 miles).[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31) However both the U.S. and China have an area nearly 30 times bigger than Germany, which demonstrates the high density of Germany's highway system.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32).

Most sections of Germany's autobahns have two or three, sometimes four lanes in each direction in addition to an emergency lane ( [hard shoulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_shoulder "Hard shoulder")). A few sections have only two lanes in each direction without emergency lanes, and short slip-roads and ramps. Since 2009 Germany has embarked on a massive widening and rehabilitation project which aims to increase the number of lanes on many of its major arterial roads of the highway network, such as the [A 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_5 "Bundesautobahn 5") in the southwest and [A 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_8 "Bundesautobahn 8") going east–west.

The motorway density in Germany is 36 kilometers per thousand square kilometer in 2016, close to that of the smaller countries nearby (Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland, Slovenia).[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33)

Emergency telephone

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/HectoReflecto_D_59k200.jpg/250px-HectoReflecto_D_59k200.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:HectoReflecto_D_59k200.jpg)

About 17,000 [emergency telephones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_telephone "Emergency telephone") are distributed at regular intervals all along the autobahn network, with triangular stickers on the armco barriers pointing the way to the nearest one. Despite the increasing use of mobile phones, there are still about 150 calls made each day on average (after some 700 in 2013). This still equals four calls per kilometre each year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34) The location of the caller is automatically sent to the operator.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)

### Parking, rest areas, and truck stops

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Parking, rest areas, and truck stops")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/BAB-A6-km-565.jpg/250px-BAB-A6-km-565.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BAB-A6-km-565.jpg) Road kilometre sign on [A 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_6 "Bundesautobahn 6"), near [Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim "Mannheim")

For breaks during longer journeys, parking sites, [rest areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rest_area "Rest area"), and [truck stops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_stop "Truck stop") are distributed over the complete Autobahn network. Parking on the autobahn is prohibited in the strictest terms outside these designated areas. There is a distinction between "managed" and "unmanaged" rest areas. ( [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language"): _bewirtschaftet_ / _unbewirtschaftet_).

[![Parking sign](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Zeichen_314-50_-_Parkplatz%2C_StVO_2013.svg/60px-Zeichen_314-50_-_Parkplatz%2C_StVO_2013.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zeichen_314.svg "Parking sign") Parking sign

Unmanaged rest areas are basically only parking spaces, sometimes with toilets. They form a part of the German highway system; the plots of land are federal property. Autobahn exits leading to such parking areas are marked at least 200 metres (660ft) (mostly 500 metres (1,600ft)) in advance with a blue sign with the white letter "P". They are usually found every few kilometres. Some of them bear local or historic names.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Vorank%C3%BCndigung_einer_Autobahnrastst%C3%A4tte_in_Kombination_mit_dem_Hinweis_auf_eine_bleifreie_Tankstelle%2C_1988.svg/60px-Vorank%C3%BCndigung_einer_Autobahnrastst%C3%A4tte_in_Kombination_mit_dem_Hinweis_auf_eine_bleifreie_Tankstelle%2C_1988.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Vorank%C3%BCndigung_einer_Autobahnrastst%C3%A4tte_in_Kombination_mit_dem_Hinweis_auf_eine_bleifreie_Tankstelle,_1988.svg)

A managed rest area (German: _Autobahnraststätte_ or _Raststätte_ (German:[\[ˈʁastˌʃtɛtə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/84/De-Rastst%C3%A4tte.ogg/De-Rastst%C3%A4tte.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Rastst%C3%A4tte.ogg "File:De-Raststätte.ogg")) for short) usually also includes a [filling station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filling_station "Filling station"), [charging station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charging_station "Charging station"), lavatories, toilets, and baby changes. Most rest areas also have restaurants, shops, public telephones, Internet access, and a playground. Some have hotels. Mandated every 50 kilometres (31mi) or so, rest areas are usually open all night.

Both kinds of rest areas are directly on the autobahn, with their own exits, and any service roads connecting them to the rest of the road network are usually closed to general traffic. Apart from rare exceptions, the autobahn must not be left nor entered at rest areas.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Zeichen_448.1_-_Autohof%2C_StVO_2000.svg/120px-Zeichen_448.1_-_Autohof%2C_StVO_2000.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zeichen_448.1_-_Autohof,_StVO_2000.svg)

Truck stops (German: _Autohof_ (German:[\[ˈaʊ̯toˌhoːf\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/90/De-Autohof.ogg/De-Autohof.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Autohof.ogg "File:De-Autohof.ogg")), plural _Autohöfe_ (German:[\[ˈaʊ̯toˌhøːfə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f1/De-Autoh%C3%B6fe.ogg/De-Autoh%C3%B6fe.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Autoh%C3%B6fe.ogg "File:De-Autohöfe.ogg"))) are large filling stations located at general exits, usually at a small distance from the autobahn, combined with fast food facilities and/or restaurants, but have no ramps of their own. They mostly sell fuel at normal price level while the _Raststätten_ fuel prices are significantly higher.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Scandinavian-Park_Handewitt_Luftbild.jpg/250px-Scandinavian-Park_Handewitt_Luftbild.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Scandinavian-Park_Handewitt_Luftbild.jpg)

## Speed limits

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Speed limits")\]

Main article: [Speed limits in Germany §Autobahns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_limits_in_Germany#Autobahns "Speed limits in Germany")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Bundesautobahn_5-FFM.jpg/250px-Bundesautobahn_5-FFM.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesautobahn_5-FFM.jpg) Autobahn with three separate lanes in each direction and an emergency lane[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Zeichen_393_-_Informationstafel_an_Grenz%C3%BCbergangsstellen%2C_StVO_2013.svg/250px-Zeichen_393_-_Informationstafel_an_Grenz%C3%BCbergangsstellen%2C_StVO_2013.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zeichen_393_-_Informationstafel_an_Grenz%C3%BCbergangsstellen,_StVO_2013.svg) Advisory speed limit (_Richtgeschwindigkeit_) of 130km/h on autobahns[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Zeichen_282_-_Ende_s%C3%A4mtlicher_Streckenverbote%2C_StVO_1970.svg/250px-Zeichen_282_-_Ende_s%C3%A4mtlicher_Streckenverbote%2C_StVO_1970.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zeichen_282.svg)"Limits no longer apply" (_Ende aller Streckenverbote_) sign, indicating a return to the default speed, while lifting all other limits as well (All limits are indicated by round signs with red border, as seen above.)[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Coloring_GPS_Tracks_According_to_Speed.png/250px-Coloring_GPS_Tracks_According_to_Speed.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Coloring_GPS_Tracks_According_to_Speed.png) GPS tracks colored according to speed show considerable speed differences at an autobahn crossing.

Germany's autobahns are famous for being among the few public roads in the world without blanket [speed limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_limit "Speed limit") for cars and motorcycles. As such, they are important German cultural identifiers, "often mentioned in hushed, reverential tones by motoring enthusiasts and looked at with a mix of awe and terror by outsiders."[&#8202;](#ref-back-wa_16-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-wa_16-09) Some speed limits are implemented on different autobahns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)

Certain limits are imposed on some classes of vehicles:

| 60km/h (37mph) | - Buses carrying standing passengers<br>- Motorcycles pulling trailers |
| --- | --- |
| 80km/h (50mph) | - Vehicles with maximum allowed weight exceeding 3.5 t (except passenger cars)<br>- Passenger cars pulling trailers<br>- Trucks<br>- Buses |
| 100km/h (62mph) | - Passenger cars pulling trailers certified for 100km/h<br>- Buses certified for 100km/h not towing trailers[&#8202;](#ref-back-german_speed_limit_law-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-german_speed_limit_law) |

Additionally, speed limits are posted at most on- and off-ramps and interchanges[&#8202;](#ref-back-Starnberg_Crash_2013-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-Starnberg_Crash_2013) and other danger points like sections under construction or in need of repair.

Where no general limit exists, the advisory speed limit is 130km/h (81mph), referred to in German as the _[Richtgeschwindigkeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richtgeschwindigkeit "Richtgeschwindigkeit")_. The advisory speed is not enforceable; however, being involved in a collision driving at higher speeds can lead to the driver being deemed at least partially responsible due to "increased operating danger" ( _Erhöhte Betriebsgefahr_).

The Federal Road Research Institute ( _Bundesanstalt für Straßenwesen_) solicited information about speed regulations on autobahns from the [sixteen States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany#Constituent_states "Germany") and reported the following, comparing the years 2006 and 2008:

| Parameter[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40) | 2006 | 2008 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autobahn total length | 24,735km | 25,240km | +505km |
| No speed limit (advisory limit only) | 69.2% | 65.5% | -580km |
| Variable speed limit (with advisory maximum) | 4.2% | 4.1% | -5km |
| Permanent or temporary speed limit | 26.7% | 30.4% | +1,090km |

Except at construction sites, the general speed limits, where they apply, are usually between 100km/h (62mph) and 130km/h (81mph); construction sites usually have a speed limit of 80km/h (50mph) but the limit may be as low as 60km/h (37mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41) In rare cases, sections may have limits of 40km/h (25mph),[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42) or on one ramp 30km/h (19mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43) Certain stretches have lower speed limits during wet weather. Some areas have a speed limit of 120km/h (75mph) in order to reduce noise pollution during overnight hours (usually 10pm– 6am) or because of increased traffic during daytime (6am– 8pm).

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Verkehrszeichen_auf_der_Bundesautobahn_20090320_001.JPG/250px-Verkehrszeichen_auf_der_Bundesautobahn_20090320_001.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Verkehrszeichen_auf_der_Bundesautobahn_20090320_001.JPG) Dynamic traffic signs on an autobahn

Some limits were imposed to reduce pollution and noise. Limits can also be temporarily put into place through dynamic traffic guidance systems that display the corresponding message. More than half of the total length of the German autobahn network has no speed limit, about one third has a permanent limit, and the remaining parts have a temporary or conditional limit.

Some cars with very powerful engines can reach speeds of well over 300km/h (190mph). Major German car manufacturers, except [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche "Porsche"), follow a [gentlemen's agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentlemen's_agreement "Gentlemen's agreement") by [electronically limiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_(device)#Cars "Governor (device)") the top speeds of their cars—with the exception of some top of the range models or engines—to 250km/h (155mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44) These limiters can be deactivated, so speeds up to 300km/h (190mph) might arise on the German autobahn, but due to other traffic, such speeds are generally not attainable except during certain times like between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. or on Sundays (when truck drivers have to rest by law). Furthermore, there are certain autobahn sections which are known for having light traffic, making such speeds attainable during most days (especially some of those located in Eastern Germany). Most unlimited sections of the autobahn are located outside densely populated areas.

Vehicles with a top speed less than 60km/h (37mph) (such as [quads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-terrain_vehicle "All-terrain vehicle"), low-end microcars, and agricultural/construction equipment) are not allowed to use the autobahn, nor are motorcycles and scooters with low engine capacity regardless of top speed (mainly applicable to [mopeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moped "Moped") which are typically limited to 25 kilometres per hour (16mph) or 45 kilometres per hour (28mph) anyway). To comply with this limit, heavy-duty trucks in Germany (e.g. mobile cranes, tank transporters etc.) often have a maximum design speed of 62km/h (39mph) (usually denoted by a round black-on-white sign with "62" on it), along with flashing orange beacons to warn approaching cars that they are travelling slowly. There is no general minimum speed but drivers are not allowed to drive at an unnecessarily low speed as this would lead to significant traffic disturbance and an increased collision risk.

### Public debate

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Public debate")\]

German national speed limits have a historical association[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45) with war-time restrictions and deprivations, the Nazi era, and the Soviet era in East Germany. After the Nazi dictatorship, German society was happy to overcome the traumas of war by freeing itself from most government restrictions, prohibitions and regulations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46) "Free driving for free citizens" ("freie Fahrt für freie Bürger"), a slogan promoted by the [German Auto Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADAC "ADAC") since the 1970s,[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47) is a popular slogan among those opposing autobahn speed restrictions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49)[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50) [Tarek Al-Wazir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarek_Al-Wazir "Tarek Al-Wazir"), head of the Green Party in Hesse, and currently the Hessian Transport Minister has stated that "the speed limit in Germany has a similar status as the [right to bear arms in the American debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_keep_and_bear_arms_in_the_United_States "Right to keep and bear arms in the United States"). At some point, a speed limit will become reality here, and soon we will not be able to remember the time before. It's like the smoking ban in restaurants."[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51)

#### Early history

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Early history")\]

The [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic") had no federally required speed limits. The first crossroads-free road for motorized vehicles only, now [A 555](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_555 "Bundesautobahn 555") between Bonn and Cologne, had a 120km/h (75mph) limit when it opened in 1932.[&#8202;](#ref-back-myth-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-myth) In October 1939, the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") instituted the first national maximum speed limit, throttling speeds to 80km/h (50mph) in order to conserve gasoline for the war effort.[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-52) After the war, the [four Allied occupation zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Germany_(1945%E2%80%9390)#Four_occupation_zones "History of Germany (1945–90)") established their own speed limits until the divided [East German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany "East Germany") and [West German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany "West Germany") republics were constituted in 1949; initially, the Nazi speed limits were restored in both East and West Germany.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-53)

#### After the World Wars

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: After the World Wars")\]

In December 1952 the West German legislature voted to [abolish all national speed limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_limits_in_Germany#History "Speed limits in Germany"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-54) reverting to State-level decisions. National limits were reestablished incrementally. The 50km/h (31mph) urban limit was enacted in 1956, effective in 1957.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-55) The 100km/h (62mph) limit on rural roads—except autobahns—became effective in 1972.

#### Oil crisis of the 1970s

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Oil crisis of the 1970s")\]

Just prior to the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis "1973 oil crisis"), Germany, Switzerland,[&#8202;](#ref-back-amtsdruckschriften.bar.admin.ch-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-amtsdruckschriften.bar.admin.ch) and Austria[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-auto)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ÖAMTC/Sicherheit-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-ÖAMTC/Sicherheit) all had no general speed restriction on autobahns. During the crisis, like other nations, Germany imposed temporary speed restrictions; for example, 100km/h (62mph) on autobahns effective 13 November 1973.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-59) Automakers projected a 20% plunge in sales, which they attributed in part to the lowered speed limits.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-60) The 100km/h limit championed by Transportation Minister [Lauritz Lauritzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauritz_Lauritzen "Lauritz Lauritzen") lasted 111 days.[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-61) Adjacent nations with unlimited speed autobahns, Austria[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[57\]](#refnote-auto)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ÖAMTC/Sicherheit-1)[\[58\]](#refnote-ÖAMTC/Sicherheit) and Switzerland,[&#8202;](#ref-back-amtsdruckschriften.bar.admin.ch-1)[\[56\]](#refnote-amtsdruckschriften.bar.admin.ch) imposed permanent 130km/h (81mph) limits after the crisis.

However, after the crisis eased in 1974, the [upper house of the German parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesrat_of_Germany "Bundesrat of Germany"), which was controlled by [conservative parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDU/CSU "CDU/CSU"), successfully resisted the imposition of a permanent mandatory limit supported by [Chancellor Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Brandt#1972_crisis "Willy Brandt").[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-62) The upper house insisted on a 130km/h (81mph) recommended limit until a thorough study of the effects of a mandatory limit could be conducted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-63) Accordingly, the Federal Highway Research Institute conducted a multiple-year experiment, switching between mandatory and recommended limits on two test stretches of autobahn. In the final report issued in 1977, the Institute stated the mandatory speed limit could reduce the autobahn death toll but there would be economic impacts, so a political decision had to be made due to the trade-offs involved.[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64) At that time, the federal government declined to impose a mandatory limit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-65) The fatality rate trend on the German autobahn mirrored those of other nations' motorways that imposed a general speed limit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-66)

#### Environmental concerns of the 1980s

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Environmental concerns of the 1980s")\]

In the mid-1980s, [acid rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_rain "Acid rain") and sudden [forest destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_dieback#History "Forest dieback") renewed debate on whether or not a general speed limit should be imposed on autobahns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67)[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-68) A car's fuel consumption increases with high speed, and fuel conservation is a key factor in reducing air pollution. Environmentalists argued that enforcing limits of 100km/h (62mph) limit on autobahns and 80km/h (50mph) on other rural roads would save lives as well as the forest, reducing the annual death toll by 30% (250 lives) on autobahns and 15% (1,000 lives) on rural roads;[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-69) the German motor vehicle death toll was about 10,000 at the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-http://www.bast.de_2014-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-http://www.bast.de_2014) The federal government sponsored a large-scale experiment with a 100km/h (62mph) speed limit in order to measure the impact of reduced speeds on emissions and compliance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-71) Afterward, again, the federal government declined to impose a mandatory limit, deciding the modest measured emission reduction would have no meaningful effect on forest loss.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-72) By 1987, all restrictions on test sections had been removed, even in Hesse where the state government was controlled by a ["red-green" coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red%E2%80%93green_alliance "Red–green alliance").[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-73)

#### German reunification

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: German reunification")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-1990-0510-033%2C_Wartha-Herleshausen%2C_Gewerkschafter_blockieren_Autobahn.jpg/250px-Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-1990-0510-033%2C_Wartha-Herleshausen%2C_Gewerkschafter_blockieren_Autobahn.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-1990-0510-033,_Wartha-Herleshausen,_Gewerkschafter_blockieren_Autobahn.jpg) Trade unionists blocked the motorway near the [Wartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wartha_(Eisenach) "Wartha (Eisenach)")- [Herleshausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herleshausen "Herleshausen") border crossing in protest against impending social cutbacks in 1990.

Prior to [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification "German reunification") in 1990, [eastern German states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_states_of_Germany "New states of Germany") focused on restrictive traffic regulation such as a 100km/h (62mph) autobahn speed limit and of 80km/h (50mph) on other rural roads. Within two years after the opening, availability of high-powered vehicles and a 54% increase in motorized traffic led to a doubling of annual traffic deaths,[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-74) despite "interim arrangements \[which\] involved the continuation of the speed limit of 100km/h (62mph) on autobahns and of 80km/h (50mph) outside cities". An extensive program of the four _E_ s (enforcement, education, engineering, and [emergency response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services "Emergency medical services")) brought the number of traffic deaths back to pre-unification levels after a decade of effort while traffic regulations were conformed to western standards (e.g., 130km/h (81mph) freeway advisory limit, 100km/h (62mph) on other rural roads, and [0.05 percent BAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Alcohol_Content#Limits_by_country_.28BAC:_Blood_Alcohol_Content.29 "Blood Alcohol Content")).[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-75)
Before reunification, East Germany prioritized traffic control over mobility, limiting car ownership and emphasizing public transport. Roads were less developed, and vehicle quality was lower. After reunification, rapid motorization overwhelmed infrastructure. The resulting spike in accidents prompted major safety reforms and modernization of traffic laws, aligning with West German standards.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-76)

#### Since reunification

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Since reunification")\]

In 1993, the Social Democratic-Green Party coalition controlling the State of Hesse experimented with a 90km/h (56mph) limit on autobahns and 80km/h (50mph) on other rural roads.[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-77) These limits were attempts to reduce [ozone pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone#Health_effects "Ozone").

During his term of office (1998 to 2005) as Chancellor of Germany, [Gerhard Schröder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Schr%C3%B6der "Gerhard Schröder") opposed an autobahn speed limit, famously referring to Germany as an _Autofahrernation_ (a "nation of drivers").

In October 2007, at a party congress held by the [Social Democratic Party of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany "Social Democratic Party of Germany"), delegates narrowly approved a proposal to introduce a blanket speed limit of 130km/h (81mph) on all German autobahns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-78) While this initiative is primarily a part of the SPD's general strategic outline for the future and, according to practices, not necessarily meant to affect immediate government policy, the proposal had stirred up a debate once again; Germany's chancellor since 2005, [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel "Angela Merkel"), and leading cabinet members expressed outspoken disapproval of such a measure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-79)

In 2008, the [Social Democratic-Green Party coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_state_election,_2007 "Bremen state election, 2007") controlling Germany's smallest state, [the paired City-State of Bremen and Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_(state) "Bremen (state)"), imposed a 120-kilometre-per-hour (75mph) limit on its last 11 kilometres (6.8 miles) of speed-unlimited autobahn[&#8202;](#ref-back-Der_Spiegel-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-Der_Spiegel) in hopes of leading other States to do likewise.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rp-online.de-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-rp-online.de)

In 2011, the first-ever [Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg_state_election,_2011 "Baden-Württemberg state election, 2011") minister-president of any German state, [Winfried Kretschmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winfried_Kretschmann "Winfried Kretschmann") of [Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg "Baden-Württemberg") initially argued for a similar, state-level 120 kilometres per hour (75mph) limit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-82) However, Baden-Württemberg is an important location for the German motor industry, including the headquarters of [Daimler AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimler_AG "Daimler AG") and [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche "Porsche");[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-83) the ruling coalition ultimately decided _against_ a state-level limit on its 675 kilometres (419 miles) of speed-unlimited roads—arguing for nationwide speed limit instead.[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-84)[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-85)[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-86)

In 2014, the [conservative-liberal ruling coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony_state_election,_2009 "Saxony state election, 2009") of [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony "Saxony") confirmed its rejection of a general speed limit on autobahns, instead advocating dynamic traffic controls where appropriate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-87) Between [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse_state_election,_2009 "Hesse state election, 2009") and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse_state_election,_2013 "Hesse state election, 2013") in the State of [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse "Hesse"), transportation ministers Dieter Posch[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-88) and his successor[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-89) Florian Rentsch,[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-90) both members of the [Free Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_(Germany) "Free Democratic Party (Germany)"), removed or raised speed limits on several sections of autobahn following regular 5-year reviews of speed limit effectiveness; some sections just prior to the installation of [Tarek Al-Wazir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarek_Al-Wazir "Tarek Al-Wazir") ( [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_'90/The_Greens "Alliance '90/The Greens")) as Transportation Minister in January 2014[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-91)[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-92) as part of an uneasy CDU-green coalition government. In 2015, the left-green coalition government of [Thuringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuringia "Thuringia") declared that a general autobahn limit was a federal matter; Thuringia would not unilaterally impose a general statewide limit, although the Thuringian environmental minister had recommended a 120 kilometres per hour (75mph) limit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-93)

In late 2015, Winfried Hermann, Baden-Württemberg's Green minister of transportation, promised to impose a trial speed limit of 120 kilometres per hour (75mph) on about 10% of the state's autobahns beginning in May 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-94) However, the ruling Green-Social Democratic coalition lost its majority in the March 2016 elections;[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-95) while Mr Hermann retained his post in the new Green-Christian Democratic government, he put aside preparations for a speed limit due to opposition from his new coalition partners.[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-96)

In 2019, the Green Party introduced a motion to introduce a hard 130km/h speed limit on the autobahn, but it was defeated in the [Bundestag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundestag "Bundestag").[&#8202;](#ref-back-DW-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-DW) A second attempt to reopen debate on the issue was made by [the Left Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left_(Germany) "The Left (Germany)") in 2022, rejected by the majority of the opposition [CDU/CSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDU/CSU "CDU/CSU") and [Alternative for Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_for_Germany "Alternative for Germany") (AfD) and the governing [Free Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_(Germany) "Free Democratic Party (Germany)") (FDP). However, [Alliance 90/The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens "Alliance 90/The Greens") and the [SPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany "Social Democratic Party of Germany") were obliged by their [traffic light coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholz_cabinet "Scholz cabinet") with the FDP to reject the proposal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-98)

## Safety

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Safety")\]

In 2014, autobahns carried 31% of motorized road traffic while accounting for 11% of Germany's traffic deaths. The autobahn fatality rate of 1.6 deaths per billion travel-kilometres compared favorably with the 4.6 rate on urban streets and 6.5 rate on rural roads.[&#8202;](#ref-back-http://www.bast.de_2014-1)[\[70\]](#refnote-http://www.bast.de_2014) However, these types of roads are not comparable according to German traffic researcher [Bernhard Schlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Schlag?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bernhard Schlag (page does not exist)")\[ [de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard%20Schlag "de:Bernhard Schlag")\]: "You don't have some of the problems that are accident-prone there at all. No cyclists, no pedestrians, no crossing traffic, hardly any direct oncoming traffic. In that sense, it's not surprising that autobahns are relatively safe roads \[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[compared to other road types\]."[\[103\]](#refnote-103)

According to official statistics from 2018, unlimited highways in Germany claimed about 71% of fatalities on highways.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-:12) However, autobahns without speed limits also account for 70% of the entire autobahn network, which puts the high proportion of collision fatalities on stretches without speed limits into perspective.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-1)[\[104\]](#refnote-:12) The often resulting thinking that speed limits would not make roads significantly safer, however, is a fallacy, since it is precisely those roads that have a high volume of traffic and thus a high risk of collisions that are given speed limits.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-105)[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-106)[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-107)

According to Schlag, unsafe and older drivers, in particular, would avoid the autobahn because they perceive the large speed differentials and very fast drivers as scary, and instead congregate on rural roads where the risk of collisions is higher anyway.[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-108)

In contrast to other road types, where the number of collisions has continuously decreased, the number of collisions on autobahns has remained relatively stable or even increased for several years since 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-109)

According to a report by the [Federal Statistical Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Statistical_Office_of_Germany "Federal Statistical Office of Germany"), fast driving is the main cause of collisions on autobahns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-:9)

According to the 2018 edition of the European Road Safety Observatory's _Traffic Safety Basic Facts_ report, an above-average number of crashes end in fatalities on a 1000-kilometer stretch of highway in Germany compared to other EU countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-1)[\[110\]](#refnote-:9)

Although Germany has a very low total [traffic-related death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_traffic-related_death_rate "List of countries by traffic-related death rate"), if only the mortality rate on highways is considered, Germany is in the rear midfield in a Europe-wide comparison of the number of traffic fatalities per thousand kilometers driven on highways in 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-111) In addition, Germany's percentage of fatalities that occurred on highways is above the EU average.[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-112)

An evaluation by the [Deutscher Verkehrssicherheitsrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_Verkehrssicherheitsrat?action=edit&redlink=1 "Deutscher Verkehrssicherheitsrat (page does not exist)")\[ [de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher%20Verkehrssicherheitsrat "de:Deutscher Verkehrssicherheitsrat")\] shows that in 2016 statistically 26% fewer people died on autobahns with a speed limit per kilometer than on autobahns without.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-:1) A similar trend could be observed in the number of serious injuries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[113\]](#refnote-:1)

Between 1970 and 2010, overall German road [fatalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death "Death") decreased by almost 80% from 19,193 to 3,648; over the same time period, autobahn deaths halved from 945 to 430 deaths.[&#8202;](#ref-back-http://www.bast.de_2014-2)[\[70\]](#refnote-http://www.bast.de_2014) Statistics for 2013 show total German traffic deaths had declined to the lowest count ever recorded: 3,340 (428 on autobahns); a representative of the Federal Statistical Office attributed the general decline to harsh winter weather that delayed the start of the motorcycle-riding season.[&#8202;](#ref-back-http://www.bast.de_2014-3)[\[70\]](#refnote-http://www.bast.de_2014)[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-114) In 2014, there was a total of 3,377 road fatalities, while autobahn deaths dropped to 375.[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-115)

| Road class | Injury crashes | Fatalities | Injury rate\* | Fatality rate\* | Fatalities per 1000 injury crashes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autobahn | 18,901 | 375 | 0.082 | 1.6 | 19.8 |
| Urban | 209,618 | 983 | 1.052 | 4.9 | 4.7 |
| Rural | 73,916 | 2,019 | 0.238 | 6.5 | 27.3 |
| Total | 302,435 | 3,377 | 0.408 | 4.6 | 11.2 |

\* per 1,000,000,000 travel-kilometres

In 2012, the leading cause of autobahn crashes was " [excessive speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_limit#Excessive_speed "Speed limit") (for conditions)": 6,587 so-called "speed related" crashes claimed the lives of 179 people, which represents almost half (46.3%) of 387 autobahn fatalities that year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.destatis.de_2012-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-www.destatis.de_2012) However, "excessive speed" does not mean that a speed limit has been exceeded, but that police determined at least one party travelled too fast for existing road[&#8202;](#ref-back-Starnberg_Fatality_2013-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-Starnberg_Fatality_2013) or weather conditions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.destatis.de_2012-1)[\[116\]](#refnote-www.destatis.de_2012) On autobahns 22 people died per 1,000 injury crashes, a lower rate than the 29 deaths per 1,000 injury collisions on conventional rural roads, which in turn is five times higher than the risk on urban roads—speeds are higher on rural roads and autobahns than urban roads, increasing the severity potential of a crash.[&#8202;](#ref-back-www.destatis.de_2012-2)[\[116\]](#refnote-www.destatis.de_2012)

## Road tolls

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Road tolls")\]

On 1 January 2005, a new system came into effect which imposed mandatory [road tolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_tolls "Road tolls") _(Mautpflicht)_ on heavy trucks (those weighing more than 12 t) which are using the German Autobahn system ( _[LKW-Maut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LKW-Maut "LKW-Maut")_). The German government contracted with a private company, [Toll Collect GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_Collect "Toll Collect"), to operate the toll collection system, which has involved the use of vehicle-mounted transponders and roadway-mounted sensors installed throughout Germany. The toll is calculated depending on the toll route, as well as based on the pollution class of the vehicle, its weight and the number of axles on the vehicles. Certain vehicles, such as emergency vehicles and buses, are exempt from the toll. An average user is charged €0.15 per kilometre, or about $0.31 per mile (Toll Collect, 2007).

## Traffic laws and enforcement

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Traffic laws and enforcement")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/Zeichen_277_-_%C3%9Cberholverbot_f%C3%BCr_Kraftfahrzeuge_mit_einem_zul%C3%A4ssigen_Gesamtgewicht_%C3%BCber_2%2C8_t%2C_einschlie%C3%9Flich_ihrer_Anh%C3%A4nger%2C_StVO_1992.svg/250px-Zeichen_277_-_%C3%9Cberholverbot_f%C3%BCr_Kraftfahrzeuge_mit_einem_zul%C3%A4ssigen_Gesamtgewicht_%C3%BCber_2%2C8_t%2C_einschlie%C3%9Flich_ihrer_Anh%C3%A4nger%2C_StVO_1992.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zeichen_277_-_%C3%9Cberholverbot_f%C3%BCr_Kraftfahrzeuge_mit_einem_zul%C3%A4ssigen_Gesamtgewicht_%C3%BCber_2,8_t,_einschlie%C3%9Flich_ihrer_Anh%C3%A4nger,_StVO_1992.svg)

One of the most common signs in the Autobahn (No passing for vehicles over 3.5 t) indicates truck drivers restricted for overtaking.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Sattelschlepper_Scania_--_Autobahn_4_bei_Eschweiler.JPG/250px-Sattelschlepper_Scania_--_Autobahn_4_bei_Eschweiler.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sattelschlepper_Scania_--_Autobahn_4_bei_Eschweiler.JPG)

Trucks (Lorries) in Germany are often referred to as " **LKW**", short for **_Lastkraftwagen_** and mostly drive on the right lane.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/FuStw-A6-APITH.jpg/250px-FuStw-A6-APITH.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:FuStw-A6-APITH.jpg) Contemporary patrol car, an [Audi A6 Avant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A6#C8_(Typ_4K,_2018%E2%80%93present) "Audi A6"), used for policing on Autobahns in [Thuringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuringia "Thuringia")

Driving in Germany is regulated by the Straßenverkehrs-Ordnung ( _road traffic regulations_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-119) abbreviated StVO). Enforcement on the federal Autobahnen is handled by each state's [highway patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_patrol "Highway patrol") (_[Autobahnpolizei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobahnpolizei "Autobahnpolizei")_), often using unmarked police cars and motorcycles and usually equipped with [video cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road-rule_enforcement_camera "Road-rule enforcement camera"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-120-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-120)[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-121) thus allowing easier enforcement of laws such as [tailgating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailgating "Tailgating").

### Notable laws

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Autobahn&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Notable laws")\]

- The right lane should be used when it is free (_Rechtsfahrgebot_) and the left lane is generally intended only for overtaking unless traffic is too dense to justify driving only on the right lane. It is legal to give a short horn or light signal (flashing headlights or _Lichthupe_) in order to indicate the intention of overtaking, but a safe distance to the vehicle in front must be maintained,[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-122) otherwise this might be regarded as an act of [coercion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coercion "Coercion").
- Trucks and buses only drive on the right lane, this is common throughout Europe wherever two travel lanes in a direction are present. Trucks are well known for doing the "Elephant Race" (_Elefantenrennen_[\[eleˈfantn̩ˌʁɛnən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/43/De-Elefantenrennen.ogg/De-Elefantenrennen.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Elefantenrennen.ogg "File:De-Elefantenrennen.ogg")), which has little to do with actual elephants. This occurs when one truck tries to overtake another with a minimum speed difference. However on sections with three or more travel lanes in a direction, trucks and buses are prohibited from using the farthest left lane, except to access a route inaccessible from the right and/or center lanes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-123-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-123) In some places which are indicated by signs, truck drivers are not allowed to overtake at all.
- Penalties for [tailgating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailgating "Tailgating") were increased in May 2006 to a maximum of €375 and three months' license suspension:[&#8202;](#ref-back-124-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-124) "drivers must keep a distance in metres that is equal to half their speed. For example, a driver going 100 km/h on the autobahn must keep a distance of at least 50 metres (165 feet)". The penalty increase followed uproar after an [infamous fatal crash on Autobahn 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accident_on_the_Bundesautobahn_5 "Accident on the Bundesautobahn 5") in 2003.[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-125) In a traffic jam, drivers must form a rescue lane (_Rettungsgasse_[\[ˈʁɛtʊŋsˌɡasə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/3b/De-Rettungsgasse.ogg/De-Rettungsgasse.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Rettungsgasse.ogg "File:De-Rettungsgasse.ogg")) to allow emergency services to reach the scene of a crash. This emergency corridor is to be created on the dividing line between the two leftmost lanes;[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-126) following the guiding principle of _if on the left, drive left, else drive right_, vehicles may cross into another lane if need be.
- It is unlawful to stop for any reason on the autobahn, except for emergencies and when unavoidable, like traffic jams or being involved in a collision. This includes stopping on emergency lanes. Running out of fuel is considered an avoidable occurrence, as by law there are petrol stations directly on the autobahn approximately every 50–55km (31–34mi). Drivers may face fines and up to six months' suspension, should it come to a stop that was deemed unnecessary by the police. In some cases (if there is a direct danger to life and limb or property e.g. cars and highway infrastructure) it may also be considered a crime and the driver could receive a prison sentence (up to 5 years).
- Overtaking on the right (undertaking) is strictly forbidden, except when stuck in traffic jams. Up to a speed of 60km/h (37mph), if the left lane is crowded or driving slowly, it is permitted to pass cars on the right side if the speed difference is not greater than 20km/h (12mph)[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-127) or the vehicle on the left lane is stationary.[&#8202;](#ref-back-128-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-128) This is not referred to as overtaking, but driving past because of the limited speed differential. Even if the car overtaken is illegally occupying the left-hand lane, it is not an acceptable excuse; in such cases, the police will routinely stop and fine both drivers. However, exceptions can and have sometimes been made.
