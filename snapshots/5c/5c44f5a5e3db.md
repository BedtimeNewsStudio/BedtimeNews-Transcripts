**Gustav Adolf Graf von Götzen** (12 May 1866 – 1 December 1910) was a German explorer, colonial administrator, and military officer who served as [Reichskommissar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissar "Reichskommissar") of [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa "German East Africa"). He came to [Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda "Rwanda") in 1894 becoming the second European to enter the territory, since [Oscar Baumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Baumann "Oscar Baumann")’s brief expedition in 1892, and later, he became the first European to cross the entire territory of Rwanda.

During the [Maji Maji Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maji_Maji_Rebellion "Maji Maji Rebellion") of 1905, Götzen commanded the [Schutztruppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutztruppe "Schutztruppe") against several rebelling African tribes in the [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa "German East Africa") colony, quelling the uprising. The rebellion and famine that followed resulted in the deaths of up to 300,000 people.

## Early life and education

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Adolf_von_G%C3%B6tzen&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")]

Count von Götzen was born into a comital family at their main residence, [Scharfeneck Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarny_Court_and_Park "Sarny Court and Park"), back then in the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia "Kingdom of Prussia"). In present-day [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland") and now called Sarny Castle, the castle and the adjoining summer palace, as well as the castle chapel in which he may have been baptized, still exist despite decades of disrepair in the communist era.

Von Götzen studied [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence "Jurisprudence") at the universities of [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"), [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_University "Berlin University") and [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel_University "Kiel University") between 1884 and 1887. He then joined the army, and became (in 1887) a [Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant "Lieutenant") in the 2nd Garde-Ulanen regiment. Between 1890 and 1891 he was stationed in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") and it was from there that he made his first African trip, in a hunting expedition to [Mount Kilimanjaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kilimanjaro "Mount Kilimanjaro").

In 1892, having been made an officer in the War academy, Götzen travelled to the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") with Major [Walther von Diest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_von_Diest?action=edit&redlink=1 "Walther von Diest (page does not exist)") [[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther%20von%20Diest "de:Walther von Diest")].

## Military career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Adolf_von_G%C3%B6tzen&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Military career")]

### Expeditions to Africa (1892–1896)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Adolf_von_G%C3%B6tzen&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Expeditions to Africa (1892–1896)")]

From 1885, [Carl Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Peters "Carl Peters") had begun claiming areas of [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa") for Germany. The Tanganyikan coast proved relatively easy, but conquest of the inland areas of the colony - right up to the [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo "Belgian Congo") - was more difficult as large parts were still unexplored. For this reason, Götzen led an expedition to claim these hinterlands. He took with him [Georg von Prittwitz und Gaffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_von_Prittwitz_und_Gaffron?action=edit&redlink=1 "Georg von Prittwitz und Gaffron (page does not exist)") [[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg%20von%20Prittwitz%20und%20Gaffron "de:Georg von Prittwitz und Gaffron")] and [Hermann Kersting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Kersting?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hermann Kersting (page does not exist)") [[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann%20Kersting%20(Afrikaforscher) "de:Hermann Kersting (Afrikaforscher)")].

The group set off from [Pangani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangani "Pangani"), on the Tanganyikan coast, on 21 December 1893. After travelling through [Maasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasai_people "Maasai people") areas, they eventually arrived, on 2 May 1894, at [Rusumo Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusumo_Falls "Rusumo Falls") on the Kagera river. By crossing the river, the group entered in the [Kingdom of Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Rwanda "Kingdom of Rwanda"), at the time, it was one of the most organised and centralised kingdoms in the region. Götzen became the second European to set foot in Rwanda since [Oscar Baumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Baumann "Oscar Baumann")'s 1892 expedition. His group travelled right through Rwanda, meeting the [mwami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Rwanda "King of Rwanda") (king) [Kigeli IV Rwabugiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigeli_IV_Rwabugiri "Kigeli IV Rwabugiri") at his palace in [Kageyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/,_Rwanda?action=edit&redlink=1 ", Rwanda (page does not exist)"), and eventually reaching [Lake Kivu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kivu "Lake Kivu"), the western edge of the kingdom.

After encountering and climbing some of the [Virunga Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virunga_Mountains "Virunga Mountains"), Götzen decided to continue west through the Congolese jungle. With great effort, they managed to reach the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River "Congo River") on 21 September, which they then followed downstream, eventually reaching the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") on 29 November. In January 1895, Götzen returned to Germany.

### Service in America (1896–1901)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Adolf_von_G%C3%B6tzen&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Service in America (1896–1901)")]

Between 1896 and 1898 Götzen worked as an [attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attaché "Attaché") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), and he served as an observer with [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt") during the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish–American_War "Spanish–American War"). While Götzen was there, he fell in love with the American widow of William Matthews Lay (1845-1893), May Loney (1857-1931), and married her. They had a daughter, Wanda Luise von Götzen, in 1898.[[1]](#cite_note-1)[[2]](#cite_note-2) Afterwards, he joined the general staff of the army in Berlin, where he was promoted in 1900 to the rank of [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(land_and_air) "Captain (land and air)").

### German East Africa and Maji Maji Rebellion (1901–1906)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Adolf_von_G%C3%B6tzen&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: German East Africa and Maji Maji Rebellion (1901–1906)")]

Due to his knowledge of local conditions, Götzen was appointed governor of German East Africa in March 1901. There had already been rebellions by the native population in the 1880s and 1890s, and in 1905 Götzen was faced with outbreak of the [Maji Maji Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maji_Maji_Rebellion "Maji Maji Rebellion"), which quickly took over about half of the colony. This was similar in severity to the [Herero Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herero_Wars "Herero Wars") taking place in [German South-West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_South-West_Africa "German South-West Africa"), but was noticed less by the German public. Götzen sent for reinforcements, and suppressed the rebellion by force. Götzen's troops lost 15 Europeans and 389 African soldiers, according to official data. Estimates of the numbers of Africans who died in the famine following the uprising range from 75,000, to 100-120,000, to 300,000, depending on the source consulted.[[3]](#cite_note-3)

On 14 June 1906, Götzen later returned to Germany after submitting a report to the Foreign Office outlining what he believed were the causes of the Maji Maji Rebellion. The Foreign Office, keen to avoid a colonial scandal, accepted Götzen's report, and he retired ostensibly on the grounds of ill health.[[4]](#cite_note-4) Following his retirement, the German government began to administer its overseas possessions under civilian control.

## Later life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Adolf_von_G%C3%B6tzen&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Later life")]

Götzen's diplomatic career was almost at an end after his work as Governor. In early 1907 he was offered the minor post of a German envoy to [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg") in which his duties would involve mostly accompanying the [Kaiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser "Kaiser") through the city. This lighter amount of work gave Götzen the opportunity to publish a book *DEUTSCH-OSTAFRIKA IM AUFSTAND, 1905-1906*. In it, he attempted to justify his involvement in the [Maji Maji Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maji_Maji_Rebellion "Maji Maji Rebellion") and the causes that prompted his response to quell the rebellion. This work was later published in 1909. Götzen still took an interest in the colonial politics of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire"); he was an active member of the committee for the German Colonial Society until his death on December 1, 1910 in a hospital in [Schöneberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schöneberg "Schöneberg") near Berlin.[[5]](#cite_note-5) His wife outlived him by over two decades. They were buried together at the [Ohlsdorf Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohlsdorf_Cemetery "Ohlsdorf Cemetery") in Hamburg.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

## Legacy

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gustav_Adolf_von_G%C3%B6tzen&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Legacy")]

The German passenger ship *Graf von Götzen* was named after Götzen and used as part of Germany's war effort on [Lake Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika "Lake Tanganyika") in World War I. She was scuttled in July 1916 off the mouth of the [Malagarasi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malagarasi_River "Malagarasi River") to prevent her falling into the hands of the Belgian troops. In 1924 on instructions from [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill"), salvage operations by the *[Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy")* succeeded in refloating the ship and in 1927 she returned to service as the *[M.V. Liemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.V._Liemba "M.V. Liemba")* and is still running today as a passenger cargo ferry.[[7]](#cite_note-7)

Comparing von Götzen's actions to the [Herero and Nama genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herero_and_Nama_genocide "Herero and Nama genocide"), [Mark Levene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Levene "Mark Levene") writes: "Operating with many fewer Schutzgruppe, considerably less money and appreciably less self-aggrandisement, von Götzen achieved essentially the same results by an entirely more effective but much less spectacular strategy than that initially adopted by his South-West African counterpart: starvation."[[8]](#cite_note-8)
