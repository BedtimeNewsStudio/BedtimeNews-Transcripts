# Answer given by Ms Bieńkowska on behalf of the European Commission

23.9.2019

[Written question](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/P-9-2019-002312_EN.html)

Galileo initial navigation and timing services were affected in July 2019 by a technical incident related to the Galileo ground infrastructure. The Search and Rescue Service (SAR) remained unaffected.

Galileo has provided ‘initial services’ since December 2016, where signals are used in combination with the ones of other satellite navigation systems. This initial ‘pilot’ phase allows for the detection of technical issues before the system becomes fully operational.

A team of experts from the European Global Navigation Satellite Systems Agency (GSA), industry, the European Space Agency and the Commission worked together to restore the services quickly. During the incident, regular updates on the situation were provided to Galileo users via technical notices (NAGU) [\[1\]](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/P-9-2019-002312-ASW_EN.html#def1) and services notices [\[2\]](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/P-9-2019-002312-ASW_EN.html#def2). In parallel, the Commission updated Member States via five information notes and on 19 July 2019 held a dedicated information session with Member States. The Commission presented a state of play to the ITRE (Industry, Research and Energy) Committee on 23 July 2019.

Galileo initial navigation and timing services are now fully restored and work on the causes of the incident and provide recommendations to avoid similar situations.
