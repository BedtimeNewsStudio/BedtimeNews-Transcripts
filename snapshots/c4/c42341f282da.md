Crew Dragon Demo-2

**Crew Dragon Demo-2** (officially **Crew Demo-2**, **SpaceX Demo-2**, or **Demonstration Mission-2**)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-11) was the first crewed [test flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_flight "Test flight") of the [Crew Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2") spacecraft. The spacecraft, named _[Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour "Crew Dragon Endeavour")_, launched on May 30, 2020[&#8202;](#ref-back-SFN20200520-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-SFN20200520)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wattles_2020-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-Wattles_2020)[&#8202;](#ref-back-spnow-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-spnow) on a [Falcon 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_9 "Falcon 9") rocket, and carried [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") astronauts [Doug Hurley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Hurley "Doug Hurley") and [Bob Behnken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Behnken "Bob Behnken") to the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") in the first crewed orbital spaceflight launched from the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") since the [final Space Shuttle mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-135 "STS-135") in 2011, and the first ever operated by a commercial provider.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bloomberg-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-bloomberg) Demo-2 was also the first two-person orbital spaceflight launched from the United States since [STS-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-4 "STS-4") in 1982. Demo-2 completed the validation of crewed spaceflight operations using [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX") hardware and received [human-rating certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human-rating_certification "Human-rating certification") for the spacecraft,[&#8202;](#ref-back-TechCrunch01-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-TechCrunch01)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Clark-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-Clark) including astronaut testing of Crew Dragon capabilities on orbit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Clark-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-Clark)

Docking was autonomously controlled by the Crew Dragon, but monitored by the flight crew in case manual intervention became necessary.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASA_DM2-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-NASA_DM2) The spacecraft [soft docked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docking_and_berthing_of_spacecraft "Docking and berthing of spacecraft") with the International Space Station on May 31, 2020, nineteen hours after launch. Following soft capture, 12 hooks were closed to complete a hard capture 11 minutes later.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hard_docking-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-Hard_docking) Hurley and Behnken worked alongside the crew of [Expedition 63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_63 "Expedition 63") for 62 days, including four [spacewalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extravehicular_activity "Extravehicular activity") by Behnken with fellow American astronaut [Chris Cassidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Cassidy "Christopher Cassidy") to replace batteries brought up by a [Japanese cargo vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-II_Transfer_Vehicle "H-II Transfer Vehicle"). _Endeavour_ autonomously undocked from the station on August 1, 2020, and thirteen hours later returned the astronauts to [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth") in the first [water landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splashdown "Splashdown") by astronauts since 1975.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FoustSplashdownCoverage-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-FoustSplashdownCoverage)

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

After the [Space Shuttle program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_program "Space Shuttle program") was [brought to an end](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-135 "STS-135") in 2011, [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") no longer had a spacecraft system capable of sending [humans to space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight"). As a result, it was forced to fly its astronauts to the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") (ISS) aboard the Russian [Soyuz space vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_programme "Soyuz programme"), at a cost of up to US$80 million per astronaut. As an alternative, NASA contracted with private companies such as [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX") for the [Commercial Crew Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Crew_Program "Commercial Crew Program"), which is expected to cost 50% less than Soyuz once in regular operation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-19) Up to the launch, NASA has awarded a total of US$3.1 billion for the development of the [Dragon 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2").[&#8202;](#ref-back-program_stats-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-program_stats) The Demo-2 mission was SpaceX's last major test before NASA certified it for regular [crewed spaceflights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight").[&#8202;](#ref-back-bloomberg-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-bloomberg) Prior to that, SpaceX had sent twenty cargo missions to the ISS, but never a crewed one.[&#8202;](#ref-back-program_stats-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-program_stats) [Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing "Boeing") was separately working on crewed [orbital spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_spaceflight "Orbital spaceflight") under the same NASA effort.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bloomberg-2)[\[13\]](#refnote-bloomberg)

## Crew

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Crew")\]

Doug Hurley and Bob Behnken were announced as the primary crew on August 3, 2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nasa-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-nasa) Both astronauts are veterans of the Space Shuttle program,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Forbes-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-Forbes) and the Demo-2 flight was the third trip to space for both of them. The lead flight director for this mission was Zebulon Scoville.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-23)

NASA astronaut [Kjell Lindgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kjell_Lindgren "Kjell Lindgren") was the sole backup crew member for the flight, backing up both Hurley and Behnken for the mission.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-24)

## Insignia and livery

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Insignia and livery")\]

The mission insignia was designed by artist Andrew Nyberg from [Brainerd, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainerd,_Minnesota "Brainerd, Minnesota"), a nephew of spacecraft commander Hurley.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-25) The insignia features the logos of the [Commercial Crew Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Crew_Program "Commercial Crew Program"), [Falcon 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_9 "Falcon 9"), [Crew Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2"), and the red chevron of [NASA's "meatball" insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_insignia "NASA insignia"). Also depicted are the [American flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_United_States "Flag of the United States") and a graphic representation of the ISS. The words _NASA, SPACEX, FIRST CREWED FLIGHT_ and _DM-2_ are printed around the border along with the surnames of the astronauts. The insignia outline is in the shape of the Crew Dragon capsule.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-26)

The Falcon 9 rocket used to launch _[Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour "Crew Dragon Endeavour")_ displayed NASA's ["worm" insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_insignia "NASA insignia"), the first time the logo had been used officially since it was retired in 1992.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-27) [NASA TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_TV "NASA TV") and media coverage of the launch was branded as " [Launch America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launch_America "Launch America")", with its own logo.[&#8202;](#ref-back-KCBD-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-KCBD)[&#8202;](#ref-back-USMissionVienna-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-USMissionVienna)

The SpaceX patch shows the top of the company's spacesuit, with the ISS and North America portrayed on the helmet shield and a white star denoting the [launch site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceport "Spaceport") at [Cape Canaveral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral "Cape Canaveral"). Behind the suit are the American flag, and around the insignia's black border _SPACEX DRAGON_ and _NASA DEMO-2_ are written in white, alongside the names of the two astronauts at the bottom; with a [cloverleaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clover "Clover") between the two names.

## Pre-launch processing

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Pre-launch processing")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/DM-2_Falcon_Rollout_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-DM-2_Falcon_Rollout_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:DM-2_Falcon_Rollout_(cropped).jpg) [Falcon 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_9 "Falcon 9") and [Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon "Crew Dragon") rolls out to the launch pad, bearing the [NASA "worm" logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_insignia "NASA insignia").[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/KSC-20200527-PH-KLS02_0180~orig.jpg/250px-KSC-20200527-PH-KLS02_0180~orig.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KSC-20200527-PH-KLS02_0180~orig.jpg) SpaceX CEO [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") and NASA administrator [Jim Bridenstine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bridenstine "Jim Bridenstine") greet Behnken and Hurley at Kennedy, while wearing face masks and practicing social distancing amid the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States "COVID-19 pandemic in the United States").

NASA calculated the loss-of-crew (LOC) probability for the test flight as 1-in-276, better than the commercial crew program requirement threshold of 1-in-270. The 1-in-276 number included mitigations to reduce the risk, such as on-orbit inspections of the [Crew Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2") [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft "Spacecraft") once it was [docked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docking_and_berthing_of_spacecraft "Docking and berthing of spacecraft") to the space station to look for damage from [micrometeoroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrometeoroid "Micrometeoroid") and [orbital debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_debris "Space debris") (MMOD). NASA pegged the overall risk of a loss of mission (LOM) as 1-in-60, covering scenarios where the Crew Dragon does not reach the space station as planned, but the crew safely returns to Earth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-30)

The Crew Dragon Demo-2 mission was initially planned for launch in July 2019 as part of the [Commercial Crew Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Crew_Program "Commercial Crew Program") contract with a crew of two on a 14-day test mission to the ISS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASAblogcc-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-NASAblogcc)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nasa-1)[\[20\]](#refnote-nasa) The Crew Dragon capsule from the [Crew Dragon Demo-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Demo-1 "Crew Dragon Demo-1") mission was destroyed while its [SuperDraco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperDraco "SuperDraco") thrusters were undergoing static fire testing on April 20, 2019, ahead of its planned use for [the in-flight abort test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_In-Flight_Abort_Test "Crew Dragon In-Flight Abort Test").[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-32)[&#8202;](#ref-back-berger-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-berger) SpaceX traced the cause of the anomaly to a component that leaked [oxidizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidizing_agent "Oxidizing agent") into the high-pressure helium lines, which then solidified and damaged a valve. The valves were since switched for [burst discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burst_disc "Burst disc") to prevent another anomaly.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-34) On January 19, 2020, a Crew Dragon [capsule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_capsule "Space capsule") successfully completed an in-flight abort test.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-35) NASA administrator [Jim Bridenstine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bridenstine "Jim Bridenstine") said on April 9, 2020, that he was "fairly confident" that astronauts could fly to the ISS aboard SpaceX's Crew Dragon spaceship at the end of May or in early June 2020, pending final parachute tests, data reviews and a training schedule that could escape major impacts from the ongoing [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").[&#8202;](#ref-back-SFN20200413-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-SFN20200413)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/SpaceX_DM-2_Commercial_Crew_Launch_America_Poster.jpg/250px-SpaceX_DM-2_Commercial_Crew_Launch_America_Poster.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SpaceX_DM-2_Commercial_Crew_Launch_America_Poster.jpg) SpaceX DM-2 Promotional Poster

On April 17, 2020, NASA and SpaceX announced the launch date as May 27, 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Clark-2)[\[15\]](#refnote-Clark) The arrival of the [Crew Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2") will have raised the station's crew size from three to five. Bob Behnken and Doug Hurley will have performed duties and conducted experiments as crew on board the ISS for several months. Hurley and Behnken were expected to live and work aboard the space station for two or three months, and then return to Earth for a splashdown in the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") east of [Cape Canaveral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Air_Force_Station "Cape Canaveral Air Force Station").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Clark-3)[\[15\]](#refnote-Clark)[&#8202;](#ref-back-SFN20200413-1)[\[35\]](#refnote-SFN20200413) NASA administrator [Jim Bridenstine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bridenstine "Jim Bridenstine") urged space enthusiasts not to travel to the Kennedy Space Center to view the launch and asked people to instead watch the launch on television or online. Bridenstine explained that maintenance crews were working in cohesive shifts, to mitigate workers' exposure to [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_2 "Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2").[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-37) On May 1, 2020, SpaceX successfully demonstrated the Mark 3 parachute system, a critical milestone for the mission approval.[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-38) Crew Dragon Demo-2 marked the first crewed United States spaceflight mission not to include the presence of the public at the [Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Visitor_Complex "Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex") due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States "COVID-19 pandemic in the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-39) As the mission was previously delayed, the Visitor Complex opened as of May 28, 2020, with limited capacity for publicly viewing the launch. Admissions sold out almost immediately.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-40) To engage the public, notably the Class of 2020, who were unable to attend their graduations due to the COVID-19 pandemic, both NASA and SpaceX invited students and graduates to submit their photos to be flown to the ISS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-41)

Behnken and Hurley arrived at [Kennedy Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center "Kennedy Space Center") on May 20, 2020, in preparation for the launch. On May 21, 2020, the Falcon 9 rocket was [rolled out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transporter_erector_launcher "Transporter erector launcher") to the launch pad, and a [static fire test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_fire_test "Static fire test") was conducted on May 22, 2020, a major milestone ahead of the launch.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-42) The mission used a [Tesla Model X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_X "Tesla Model X") to transport Hurley and Behnken to [LC-39A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Launch_Complex_39A "Kennedy Space Center Launch Complex 39A").[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-43)

### Launch attempts

An official launch weather forecast for Dragon Crew Demo-2 by the [45th Weather Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Weather_Squadron "45th Weather Squadron") of the [U.S. Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force "United States Space Force"), for the original launch time at 20:33:33UTC on May 27, 2020, predicted a 50% probability of favorable conditions. ## Mission

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Mission")\]

### Launch and orbit

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Launch and orbit")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/SpaceX_Demonstration_Mission_2_Launch_%2849963535768%29.jpg/250px-SpaceX_Demonstration_Mission_2_Launch_%2849963535768%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SpaceX_Demonstration_Mission_2_Launch_(49963535768).jpg) Vice President [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence "Mike Pence") and President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") watch the Crew Dragon Demo-2 Falcon 9 rocket launch from Kennedy Space Center.

Three days after the first launch attempt was scrubbed, the [Crew Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2") [_Endeavour_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour "Crew Dragon Endeavour") launched atop a [Falcon 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_9 "Falcon 9") rocket from [Kennedy Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center "Kennedy Space Center") [Launch Complex 39A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Launch_Complex_39A "Kennedy Space Center Launch Complex 39A") at 19:22:45UTC on May 30, 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-51)[&#8202;](#ref-back-M20-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-M20) The first stage booster (serial number [B1058](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_9_B1058 "Falcon 9 B1058")) landed autonomously on the floating barge _[Of Course I Still Love You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Course_I_Still_Love_You "Of Course I Still Love You")_, which was prepositioned in the Atlantic Ocean.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Launch360-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-Launch360) [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") and [Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States "Vice President of the United States") [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence "Mike Pence"), with their wives, were at Kennedy Space Center in [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida") to see the launch attempt on May 27, 2020,[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-54) and returned for launch on May 30, 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SFN=20200527-1)[\[48\]](#refnote-SFN=20200527)[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-55) The launch live stream was watched online by 3 million people on NASA feeds,[&#8202;](#ref-back-SpaceX_2020-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-SpaceX_2020) and the SpaceX feed peaked at 4.1 million viewers. NASA estimated roughly 10 million people watched on various online platforms, approximately 150,000 people gathered on Florida's [space coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_coast "Space coast")[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-57) in addition to an unknown number watching on television.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SpaceX_2020-1)[\[55\]](#refnote-SpaceX_2020)

Hurley and Behnken described Falcon 9 as a "very pure flying machine", and the ride on [SpaceX Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Merlin "SpaceX Merlin") engines as much smoother than with [Space Shuttle Solid Rocket Boosters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Solid_Rocket_Booster "Space Shuttle Solid Rocket Booster"). Hurley said that the [Merlin Vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_Vacuum "Merlin Vacuum") upper-stage engine was rougher than [Space Shuttle Main Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Main_Engine "Space Shuttle Main Engine"), "kind of like driving fast on a dirt road".[&#8202;](#ref-back-clark20200612-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-clark20200612) They revealed the name of their [Crew Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 "SpaceX Dragon 2") capsule 206, _[Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour "Crew Dragon Endeavour")_, shortly after launch, reviving the tradition from the [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Mercury "Project Mercury"), [Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Gemini "Project Gemini"), and [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program "Apollo program") programs where astronauts would name their spacecraft.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-59) It was the third U.S. spacecraft named _Endeavour_, after [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") [_Endeavour_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Endeavour "Space Shuttle Endeavour"), built in 1991 to replace [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") [_Challenger_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger "Space Shuttle Challenger"), which was [destroyed in 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-60) and the [Apollo command and service module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_command_and_service_module "Apollo command and service module") used for the [Apollo 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_15 "Apollo 15") mission in 1971.[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-61) Hurley said that they chose _Endeavour_ as both his and Behnken's first flights to space were on the Shuttle _Endeavour_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-verge_docked-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-verge_docked)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/The_SpaceX_Crew_Dragon_approaches_the_International_Space_Station_%28iss063e021463%29.jpg/250px-The_SpaceX_Crew_Dragon_approaches_the_International_Space_Station_%28iss063e021463%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_SpaceX_Crew_Dragon_approaches_the_International_Space_Station_(iss063e021463).jpg)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Crew_Dragon_Demo_2_entering_ISS.png/250px-Crew_Dragon_Demo_2_entering_ISS.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Crew_Dragon_Demo_2_entering_ISS.png)

Nineteen hours into the mission, _Endeavour_ arrived at the ISS. Behnken and Hurley greeted the crew of [Expedition 63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_63 "Expedition 63") shortly after the hatch was opened three hours later.

Each crew member brought along a toy from their family; an _[Apatosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apatosaurus "Apatosaurus")_ dinosaur named "Tremor" and a [Ty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_(company) "Ty (company)") plush toy. As in past space missions, the plush toy was used as an indication of [zero gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlessness "Weightlessness") for the strapped-in astronauts. Behnken and Hurley said, "That was a super cool thing for us to get a chance to do for both of our sons, who I hope are super excited to see their toys floating around with us on board".[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-62)[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-63)

The crew were awakened on the second day of the flight with [Black Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sabbath "Black Sabbath")'s " [Planet Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_Caravan "Planet Caravan")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-64) NASA began a tradition of playing music to astronauts during the Gemini program, and first used music to wake up a flight crew during [Apollo 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_15 "Apollo 15"). Each track is specially chosen, often by the astronauts' families, and usually has a special meaning to an individual member of the crew, or is applicable to their daily activities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-chronology-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-chronology)

### Approach, docking, and activities aboard the ISS

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Approach, docking, and activities aboard the ISS")\]

Nineteen hours later, _Endeavour_ approached the ISS. Hurley demonstrated the ability to pilot the spacecraft via its touchscreen controls until it reached a distance of 220 metres (720ft), after which _Endeavour_ autonomously soft-docked to the pressurized mating adapter [PMA-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMA-2 "PMA-2") on the [Harmony module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony_(ISS_module) "Harmony (ISS module)") of the [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") at 14:16UTC on May 31, 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-verge_docked-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-verge_docked)[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-66) Following soft capture, 12 hooks were closed to complete a hard capture at 14:27UTC.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hard_docking-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-Hard_docking) Approximately two hours after docking, the last of three hatches between _Endeavour_ and ISS was opened, and Hurley and Behnken boarded the ISS at 17:22UTC, welcomed by and joining the [Expedition 63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_63 "Expedition 63") crew, consisting of NASA astronaut and station commander [Chris Cassidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Cassidy "Christopher Cassidy") and Russian cosmonauts [Anatoly Ivanishin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoli_Ivanishin "Anatoli Ivanishin") and [Ivan Vagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Vagner "Ivan Vagner").[&#8202;](#ref-back-verge_docked-3)[\[6\]](#refnote-verge_docked)[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapo_boarding-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-wapo_boarding)[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn_boarding-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-cnn_boarding)[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-69)

Over their time aboard the ISS, Hurley and Behnken spent over 100 hours completing science experiments, while traveling 27 million miles over their 1024 orbits of the Earth. Behnken also completed 4 spacewalks with Chris Cassidy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-70)

### Undocking and return

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Crew_Dragon_Demo-2&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Undocking and return")\]

NASA mission managers had evaluated additional sites off [Panama City, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City,_Florida "Panama City, Florida"), [Tallahassee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallahassee,_Florida "Tallahassee, Florida"), [Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida "Tampa, Florida"), and [Daytona Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_Beach,_Florida "Daytona Beach, Florida") to provide more options in the event of stormy tropical weather.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SFN20200624-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-SFN20200624) The [United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard "United States Coast Guard") advised against entering a 10nmi (19km; 12mi) square defined in its [Notices to Mariners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notice_to_mariners "Notice to mariners") for the hours leading up to splashdown noting "hazardous space operations" and the [Federal Aviation Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Administration "Federal Aviation Administration") (FAA) restricted air traffic in the area in a [NOTAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOTAM "NOTAM").[&#8202;](#ref-back-WRAL_2020-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-WRAL_2020)

_[Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour "Crew Dragon Endeavour")_ stayed docked to the ISS for 62 days, 9 hours and 8 minutes, undocking at 23:35UTC on August 1, 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-72) At the time of undocking, _Endeavour_ weighed approximately 12,520kg (27,600lb).[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-73) The capsule completed four departure burns to move away from the ISS, followed by a phasing burn lasting over six minutes to place the capsule on a trajectory to return to Earth. The crew was awakened on the final day of the flight with prerecorded voice messages from their sons. A yaw maneuver occurred at 17:51 to separate the "claw" umbilical mechanism from the trunk.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bogage_2020-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-Bogage_2020)

The deorbit burn of 11 minutes and 22 seconds occurred shortly after 17:56UTC. Just before reentry at 18:11UTC, the nose cone was closed. Drogue chutes deployed at 18:44UTC for approximately one minute, drawing out main parachute deployment approximately one minute later. A maximum of 4 [g](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-force "G-force") was experienced by Dragon capsule and its crew during the re-entry period.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bogage_2020-1)[\[73\]](#refnote-Bogage_2020) _[Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour "Crew Dragon Endeavour")_ returned to Earth with 150kg (330lb) of cargo including 90kg (200lb) of science cargo, mostly kept in freezers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-75)

Sixty-three days, 23 hours, 25 minutes and 21 seconds after launch, at 18:48:06UTC on August 2, 2020, _[Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour "Crew Dragon Endeavour")_ [splashed down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splashdown "Splashdown") off the coast of [Pensacola, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensacola,_Florida "Pensacola, Florida"), marking the first splashdown in 45 years for NASA astronauts since the [Apollo–Soyuz Test Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo%E2%80%93Soyuz_Test_Project "Apollo–Soyuz Test Project"), as well as the first splashdown of a crewed spacecraft in the [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico").[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-76)[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-77)[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-78)[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-79)[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-80) After splashdown, a crew in one fast boat approached the capsule, checking the air quality for any traces of highly toxic [hypergolic propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergolic_propellant "Hypergolic propellant") which could indicate a leak, while a crew in another fast boat collected the four parachutes which had disconnected from the capsule. While crews worked, many private boats entered the hazardous area defined by the Coast Guard despite earlier warnings. Some moving close to the capsule even passing between the capsule placing themselves and recovery crews themselves in danger. After radioed warnings, the boats dispersed to form a more distant ring around the capsule. The [United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard "United States Coast Guard") said in a statement later that evening that previous warnings were advisory in nature; the three patrol boats in the area were stationed within their jurisdiction and did not provide sufficient resources to board vessels interfering with the recovery. The statement added that they would be reviewing events with [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") and [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX").[&#8202;](#ref-back-WRAL_2020-1)[\[70\]](#refnote-WRAL_2020)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bogage_2020-2)[\[73\]](#refnote-Bogage_2020) Later SpaceX and NASA confirmed that their next mission will have a 16km (9.9mi) enforceable keep-out zone patrolled by the U.S. Coast Guard. There will be more boats to assist in enforcing the keep-out zone.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Foust29092020-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-Foust29092020)[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-82)

The [MV _GO Navigator_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_(2009_ship) "Shannon (2009 ship)"), with the assistance of a fast boat crew, attached to the capsule using a retracting A-frame device over the back to place the capsule on the "nest". The "nest" was then pulled further on board the ship where a 30-minute purge of the service section took place, as there were abnormally high levels of [dinitrogen tetroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinitrogen_tetroxide "Dinitrogen tetroxide") detected around the capsule.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FoustSplashdownCoverage-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-FoustSplashdownCoverage)[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-83) Hurley and Behnken were then greeted by crew aboard _GO Navigator_ and were subsequently helped out of the capsule and onto stretchers where they were taken into the onboard medical facility, before taking a helicopter back to land.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bogage_2020-3)[\[73\]](#refnote-Bogage_2020)

Post-flight analysis of the Dragon's [heat shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_shield "Heat shield") revealed higher-than-expected erosion at four points where the capsule bolts to the trunk of the vehicle using tension ties. SpaceX stated that the erosion was likely caused by airflow phenomena that were not expected to occur around the ties. The heat shield design was changed to include more erosion-resistant materials at the ties.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Foust29092020-1)[\[80\]](#refnote-Foust29092020) One of four ties was noted to have deep erosion. Though it could not be determined why this was not seen during SpaceX Demo-1, [Hans Koenigsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Koenigsmann "Hans Koenigsmann") speculated that it may be related to different masses and trajectories flown by the spacecraft.[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-84) In addition, SpaceX noted that the parachutes were deployed lower than expected, but still within the allowable range. To address the issue, the instrument used to measure barometric pressure which determines altitude was replaced.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Foust29092020-2)[\[80\]](#refnote-Foust29092020)

### Follow up mission of the capsule

Megan McArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Megan_McArthur "K. Megan McArthur") in Crew-2 mission.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RefurbishedSeat-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-RefurbishedSeat) The intervening Crew Dragon mission, [SpaceX Crew-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Crew-1 "SpaceX Crew-1") SpaceX's first operational astronaut flight launched four [Expedition 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_64 "Expedition 64") astronauts aboard [Crew Dragon _Resilience_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Resilience "Crew Dragon Resilience") on November 16, 2020, at 00:27:17UTC.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SFN20201116-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-SFN20201116)
