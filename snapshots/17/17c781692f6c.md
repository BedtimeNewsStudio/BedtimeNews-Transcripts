_Ruhnama_

The _**Ruhnama**_, or _**Rukhnama**_, translated into English as _**Book of the Soul**_ or _**Book of the Spirit**_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-:16) is a two-volume work written by [Saparmurat Niyazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saparmurat_Niyazov "Saparmurat Niyazov"), the [president of Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkmenistan "President of Turkmenistan") from 1990 to 2006. The book explores the philosophical relationship between ethics and the success of states, using [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan "Turkmenistan") as a case study. Turkmenistan is presented as a modern continuation of the historical [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire "Seljuk Empire"), [Oghuz Yabgu State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oghuz_Yabgu_State "Oghuz Yabgu State"), and other [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmens "Turkmens")-founded states. It offers an overview of [Turkmen history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Turkmenistan "History of Turkmenistan"), religion, and culture. The book was designed to serve as a form of [state propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_propaganda "State propaganda"), emphasizing the foundations of Turkmen identity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

The _Ruhnama_ was introduced to [Turkmen culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_culture "Turkmen culture") gradually but eventually pervasively. Niyazov first placed copies in the nation's schools and libraries but eventually went as far as to make an exam on its teachings an element of the [driving test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_test "Driving test"). It was mandatory to read _Ruhnama_ in schools, universities and governmental organisations. New governmental employees were tested on the book at job interviews.

After Niyazov's death in December 2006, its popularity remained high.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] However, in the following years, its ubiquity had waned as President [Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurbanguly_Berdimuhamedow "Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow") removed it from the public school curriculum and halted the practice of testing university applicants on their knowledge of the book.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Background

[Epics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_(genre) "Epic (genre)") had played multiple important roles in the social life of Central Asia across centuries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) Pre-modern rulers of these regions usually appropriated the text and invented a connection between themselves and the epic-cast, to seek legitimacy for their new order.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

[Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin") had considered these epics to be "politically suspicious" and capable of inciting nationalist feelings among the masses; almost all significant Turkmen epics were condemned and banned by 1951–52.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) These epics would be rehabilitated back into public (and academic) discourse only with the onset of [Glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost "Glasnost").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-4)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-4) _Ruhnama_ built on this rehabilitation phase.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-5)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

### Production

[Niyazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saparmurat_Niyazov "Saparmurat Niyazov") claimed to have received a [prophetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophecy "Prophecy") vision where Turkmen ancestors of eminence urged him to lead Turkmens to the "golden path of life".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-6)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) The first version was released in the 1990s but soon withdrawn because it did not fulfill Niyazov's expectations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13) Preparations for the revised book were underway as early as April 1999, when Niyazov declared that _Mukkadesh Ruhnama_ (The Holy Ruhnama) would be the second landmark text of Turkmens, after the Quran.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-7)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-6)

The first volume was finally published in December 2001.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15) On 18 February 2001, it was accepted at the 10th joint meeting of the State Assembly of Elders of Turkmenistan and the National Assembly.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-9) In September 2004, Niyazov issued a second volume.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-8)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-:5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[b\]](#refnote-11) An edited volume of the _Ruhnama_, published a year later, quotes his overall purpose to have lain in highlighting the nation's significant contributions to fields of art and science.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-12)

Victoria Clement and Riccardo Nicolosi suspect that the work was [ghost-written](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghostwriter "Ghostwriter").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-:16)

## Genre

Scholars note Ruhnama to be a "mosaic" of different literary genres, the text combines spiritual and political advice, legends, autobiography, short stories, poems, and (fabricated) Turkmen history.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-9)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-14) Written to "recover real Turkmen history without Soviet distortions", Niyazov promised the return of an atavist past from the times of [Oghuz Khagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oghuz_Khagan "Oghuz Khagan"), but only if the conduct of ancient Turkmens were emulated in accordance to his sage guidance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-10)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

> The Ruhnama brings the national perception into a system and organisation. I have written the Ruhnama to enable my nation to perceive our past and to envision our own dignity.

—Niyazov, c.2002,Clement (2014)

According to Amieke Bouma, a scholar of post-socialist historiography, it is best treated as an epic in its own right: the [Oğuzname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%C4%9Fuzname "Oğuzname") of the third millennium.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-11)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) Tanya L. Shields reads it as an anti-colonial autobiography, which gets "almost comic in its grandiosity".[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-15) It has been compared to [Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk "Mustafa Kemal Atatürk")'s _[Nutuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutuk "Nutuk")_, and [Leonid Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev "Leonid Brezhnev")'s _[Trilogiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brezhnev's_trilogy "Brezhnev's trilogy")_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3)

## Contents

### Volume 1

Stories and proverbs are borrowed from existing Turkmen [epics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_(genre) "Epic (genre)")— _[Oğuzname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%C4%9Fuzname "Oğuzname")_, _[Book of Dede Korkut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Dede_Korkut "Book of Dede Korkut")_, and _[Epic of Koroghlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_of_Koroghlu "Epic of Koroghlu")_—in preaching of morals and promotion of a model [code of conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_conduct "Code of conduct").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-12)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) These are often supplemented with Niyazov's explanatory annotations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-13)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[c\]](#refnote-17) Virtues like generosity, unity, humility, hospitality, patience, honesty, defence of fatherland, protection of female dignity, and caring for horses (something that is placed into utmost importance by [Niyazov's successor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurbanguly_Berdimuhamedow "Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow")) are emphasised.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-14)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) Some of his own poems singing [paeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paean "Paean") of the Turkmen are present, too.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-15)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

The _[Shajara-i Tarākima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shajara-i_Tar%C4%81kima "Shajara-i Tarākima")_ (unattributed) and writings of [Ahmad ibn Fadlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Fadlan "Ahmad ibn Fadlan") are relied upon for a reconstruction of national history.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3) Some seventy states are alleged to have been established by them—the [Anau culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anau_culture "Anau culture"), [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire "Parthian Empire"), [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire"), [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire "Seljuk Empire"), and [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") among others—till the eighteenth century; this entire span was simultaneously periodised into four epic-epochs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-16)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-2)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[d\]](#refnote-18) Distinct since the inception of humans,[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[e\]](#refnote-19) the Turkmen were the same as the Turks and descended from the venerable Noah via [Oghuz Khagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oghuz_Khagan "Oghuz Khagan"), who had set up the first polity c.5000 years ago.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-17)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-3)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:11-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-:11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[f\]](#refnote-21)[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[g\]](#refnote-22) Warfare was apparently rare, and the state always preferred peace. Despite vague references to archaeo-historical evidence to support this range of pioneer assertions, there is a total lack of source material, as understood in an academic sense.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-18)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

The Persian sphere of influence on Turkmen polity across the medieval era was neatly purged; though, [Zoroaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroaster "Zoroaster") was appropriated as a Turkmen hero who had advised people not to abide by [Mazdak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazdak "Mazdak")'s path of fire-worship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-4)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3) Both 18th and 19th centuries—integral to the foundation of modern Turkmenistan—are skipped except for the mention of [Magtymguly Pyragy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magtymguly_Pyragy "Magtymguly Pyragy") and the [Battle of Geok Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Geok_Tepe "Battle of Geok Tepe").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-19)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:11-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-:11) Soviet Turkmenistan is described in a single page, where it is blamed for colonisation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-23)[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-24) The narrative resumes with Niyazov ushering in independence of the state. Several of his specific policies find a mention.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-20)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

Niyazov's life is described in great detail throughout the text—loss of parents in childhood, attachment to land, and his patriotic zeal for attaining sovereignty from Soviet imperialism. These descriptions form an embedded stratum of the volume.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-21)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

### Volume 2

Drafted with explicit parallels to the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran "Quran"), this volume asked that the _Ruhnama_ be recited as a regular prayer after purifying oneself; it was also to never lie in an improper place.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-22)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)

Thematically, the volume is concerned with morals and ethics. A total of 21 chapters deal with optimum manners and [decorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decorum "Decorum") for different situations and target audiences.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-23)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) Niyazov's own narration gains a position of authority; he does not always seek support from the epics to support his positions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-24)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-:16)

## Society

### Niyazov

Niccolosi notes _Ruhnama_ to have transformed Turkmenistan into an " [epideictic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epideictic "Epideictic") space", which was in "permanent, unanimous exultation of the person of [Niyazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saparmurat_Niyazov "Saparmurat Niyazov")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-:16) Bouma found the _Ruhnama_ to rebirth Turkmenistan under Niyazov's responsible leadership.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-25)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2) The most significant component of ideological propaganda during the later phase of [Niyazov's personality cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saparmurat_Niyazov#Cult_of_personality "Saparmurat Niyazov"), the text was a marker of politico-cultural literacy and key to survival in post-Soviet Turkmenistan.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-:12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-26)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-:9)[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-28)[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-2)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15) Niyazov claimed those who read it thrice were destined for heaven.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[11\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[h\]](#refnote-33)

In 2002, Niyazov's government adopted a law to [rename the months of the year and days of the week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaming_of_Turkmen_months_and_days_of_week "Renaming of Turkmen months and days of week") in the [Turkmen language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_language "Turkmen language"), with the new names being references to parts of _Ruhnama_, including naming September after the book itself.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lenta-calendar-2008-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-lenta-calendar-2008-07)

After the publication of the second volume, Niyazov had mosques and churches display the Ruhnama as prominently as the Quran and Bible, and cite its passages during sermons.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-27)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-3)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-:8) The [Türkmenbaşy Ruhy Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrkmenba%C5%9Fy_Ruhy_Mosque "Türkmenbaşy Ruhy Mosque"), which was commissioned in 2002 at his birthplace, is the largest mosque in Central Asia and features engravings from the _Ruhnama_ as well as the Quran across its wall and minarets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-28)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-4)[\[1\]](#refnote-:16)[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-35)[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[i\]](#refnote-36) A twenty foot tall neon Ruhnama was erected at an [Ashgabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashgabat "Ashgabat") park in 2003.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:17-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-:17)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-2)[\[19\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[j\]](#refnote-38) A mural of Niyazov drafting the Ruhnama has been put in place, too.[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-39) In August 2005, the first volume was [launched into orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_Memorial_Capsule "Turkmenistan Memorial Capsule") so that it could "conquer space".[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-40)

A photo-journalistic essay in 2006 noted the nation to be filled with advertisements of Ruhnama – each at a cost of two dollars.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-41)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-3)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15) Government offices featured the Ruhnama prominently on their desk (often devoting a separate room), and state media regularly broadcast their content, with religious reverence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-3)[\[19\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-:12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-:6)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-4)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[k\]](#refnote-43) Official ceremonies featured hundreds of Turkmens singing from the book.[&#8202;](#ref-back-newyorker-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-newyorker) 12 September was declared a national holiday.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-4)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)

#### Education

_Ruhnama_ was the most integral aspect of the national educational curriculum across multiple domains.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-5)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-1)[\[20\]](#refnote-:9)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-5)[\[1\]](#refnote-:16) This emphasis on Ruhnama obviously ran in parallel to a rapid deterioration in overall standards of education.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-6)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-46)

A course in _Ruhnama_ was mandated for all students in school as part of [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science "Social science").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-29)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-4)[\[19\]](#refnote-:8) It was also made a required reading across all universities, and knowledge of the text was necessary for holding state employment; this perpetuated discrimination on minorities who were not proficient in Turkmen.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-7)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-5)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[11\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-47)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:10-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-:10)[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-49)[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[l\]](#refnote-51)[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[m\]](#refnote-52) [Turkmen State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_State_University "Turkmen State University") even had a "Department of the Holy Ruhnama of Türkmenbaşy the Great", and _Ruhnama_ Studies were pursued as a major research agenda in the country, often at the cost of academic disciplines.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-8)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-53)

The text also doubled as the sole government-approved version of history across all Turkmen schools until Niyazov's demise, and had a substantial negative effect on academic scholarship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-30)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-9)[\[4\]](#refnote-:13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-5)[\[19\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[n\]](#refnote-54) Several conferences on _Ruhnama_ itself were organised by historical and cultural institutes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-31)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-2)[\[18\]](#refnote-:12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[o\]](#refnote-55) Other common topics were Turkmen epics, ancient Turkmen culture, and men of eminence – all deriving from _Ruhnama_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-32)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[p\]](#refnote-56) The only books which were allowed to be published were those whose views were in service of Ruhnama; Turkmenistan does not have a significant record of public debates surrounding history, unlike other post-Soviet states.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-33)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-6)[\[19\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-6)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[q\]](#refnote-57)

In 2004, primary and secondary schools were assigned between two and four hours a week to _Ruhnama_ while universities were assigned from four to eight hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-7)[\[7\]](#refnote-:15)[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[r\]](#refnote-58) 26 of the 57 examination cards for the 2006 Turkmenistan University Entrance Examination revolved around themes set in _Ruhnama_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[34\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[s\]](#refnote-59) In the words of [Laura E. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_E._Kennedy "Laura E. Kennedy"), Ruhnama was taught with a [theological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology "Theology") zeal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-60)

### Berdimuhamedow

In his early days, _Ruhnama_ was led away from its earlier spot-of-prominence though it continued to be a part of educational curricula.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-34)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:14-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-:14) In Spring of 2007, official references to _Ruhnama_ were trimmed and around 2009–10, television broadcasts of Ruhnama stopped.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:14-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-:14) Scholars have noted these incremental changes to fit [Berdimuhamedow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurbanguly_Berdimuhamedow "Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow")'s posturing as a would-be harbinger of Turkmen renaissance, which necessitated partial critique of his predecessor's tenure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-35)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:14-2)[\[42\]](#refnote-:14)

In 2011, the requirement to pass a secondary-school examination on the _Ruhnama_ was rescinded.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-:7) And in 2014, it was finally declared that Turkmen universities would no longer test applicants on their knowledge of the book, in what Slavomir Horak interpreted as the total purge of _Ruhnama_ from Turkmen educational curricula.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-64)

It has been noted that books written by Berdimuhamedow, Niyazov's successor, had begun to be included in coursework following his inauguration as leader. Luca Anceschi, an expert on the region and [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow") professor, saw this as a transfer of Niyazov's cult of personality to Berdimuhamedow.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[43\]](#refnote-:7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-65)

## Translations

The _Ruhnama_ has been translated to over 50 languages.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-5)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3) These translations were primarily designed by foreign corporations to gain a cordial relationship with Niyazov, and were not meant for international consumption.[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-66)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:10-1)[\[37\]](#refnote-:10)[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[t\]](#refnote-67)

These state-authorized translations vary substantially from one to another, leading Dan Shapira to conclude that the text remains in flux.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-6)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-68) The English version was translated from a Turkish translation of _Ruhnama_; it does not correspond to the Turkmen version in many places, but is generally more accurate and bulky than the Russian translation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-7)[\[14\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-36)[\[3\]](#refnote-:2)
