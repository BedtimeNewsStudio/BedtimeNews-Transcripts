Socotra

**Socotra**,[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-1) locally known as **Saqatri**,[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[b\]](#refnote-2) is a [Yemeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen "Yemen") island in the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"). It is situated between the [Gulf of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aden "Gulf of Aden"), the [Guardafui Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardafui_Channel "Guardafui Channel") and the [Arabian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Sea "Arabian Sea"), and lies near major shipping routes. The largest of the six islands in the [Socotra archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate "Socotra Governorate"), it comprises around 95% of the landmass of the archipelago. It lies 380 kilometres (205 nautical miles) south of the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula") and 232km (125nmi) east of the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBrownMies20126-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBrownMies20126) The island is culturally and administratively a part of Yemen, but geographically it belongs to Africa, for it represents a continental fragment that is geologically linked to the [Somali plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_plate "Somali plate"). The inhabitants of the island are called [Soqotrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soqotrans "Soqotrans"), and they speak [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic") and [Soqotri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soqotri_language "Soqotri language").

Socotra is unusual in hosting a large number of species that are [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism "Endemism"); a third of its plant life is found nowhere else on Earth. Due to the island's unusual geography, it has been described as "the most alien-looking place on Earth".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Huntingford-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-Huntingford) The island measures 132km (82mi) in length and 42km (26mi) across at its widest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-5) In 2008, Socotra was recognised as a [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site").[&#8202;](#ref-back-autogenerated1-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-autogenerated1)

## Etymology

Scholars' views vary regarding the origin of the island's name. One theory is that the term _Socotra_ may derive from a Greek name that is derived from the name of a [South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia "South Arabia") tribe mentioned in [Sabaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaic "Sabaic") and [Ḥaḍramitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadramautic_language "Hadramautic language") inscriptions as _Dhū-Śakūrid_ (s³krd).[&#8202;](#ref-back-name-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-name) Another theory is that the Arabic term _Suqutra_ breaks down as follows: _suq_ means 'market', and _qutra_ is a vulgar form of _qatir_, which refers to [dragon's blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon's_blood "Dragon's blood"). The capital city of Socotra was [Suq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suq,_Yemen "Suq, Yemen") as reported by the Portuguese in the 16th century, which they referred to as _market place_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-8)

## History

Main article: [History of Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Socotra "History of Socotra")

### Prehistory

There was initially an [Oldowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldowan "Oldowan") lithic culture in Socotra. Oldowan stone tools were found in the area around [Hadibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadibu "Hadibu") in 2008.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-9)

### Antiquity

Socotra played an important role in ancient international trade and appears as _Dioskouridou_ (Διοσκουρίδου νῆσος), meaning "the island of [Dioscurides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioscurides "Dioscurides")" in the [Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea "Periplus of the Erythraean Sea"), a first-century AD Greek navigation aid.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-10)

The [Hoq Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoq_Cave "Hoq Cave") contains a large number of inscriptions, drawings and archaeological objects. Further investigation showed that these had been left by sailors who visited the island between the first century BC and the sixth century AD. The texts are written in the [South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_South_Arabian_script "Ancient South Arabian script"), [Ethiopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge'ez_script "Ge'ez script"), Indian [Brāhmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script "Brahmi script"), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet "Greek alphabet"), [Palmyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmyrene_script "Palmyrene script") and [Bactrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactrian_language "Bactrian language") languages. This corpus of nearly 250 texts and drawings constitutes one of the main sources for the investigation of [Indian Ocean trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade "Indian Ocean trade") networks in that time period.[&#8202;](#ref-back-strauch-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-strauch)

### Medieval

In 880, an [Aksumite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum "Kingdom of Aksum") expeditionary force conquered the island, and an [Oriental Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches "Oriental Orthodox Churches") bishop was consecrated. The Ethiopians were later dislodged by a large armada sent by Imam [Al-Salt bin Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Salt_bin_Malik "Al-Salt bin Malik") of [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Oman "Sultanate of Oman").[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-12) According to the Persian geographer [Ibn al-Mujawir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_al-Mujawir "Ibn al-Mujawir"), who testifies having arrived in Socotra from India in 1222, there were two groups of people on the island, the indigenous mountain dwellers and the foreign coastal dwellers. There were large settlements of Indian traders from [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh") and [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan,_Pakistan "Balochistan, Pakistan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-13)

[Ibn Hawqal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Hawqal "Ibn Hawqal"), a 10th-century history chronicler, mentions Socotra in his book _The Renaissance of Islam_. He mentioned it as a prominent source of [piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy "Piracy"), stating: "the island Socotra in particular was regarded as a dangerous nest of pirates, at which people trembled as they passed it. It was the [point d'appui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_d'appui "Point d'appui") of the Indian pirates who ambushed the Believers there."[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-14)

### Early modern

In 1507, a [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal "Kingdom of Portugal") fleet commanded by [Tristão da Cunha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trist%C3%A3o_da_Cunha "Tristão da Cunha") with [Afonso de Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_de_Albuquerque "Afonso de Albuquerque") landed at [Suq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suq,_Yemen "Suq, Yemen") and [captured the port after a stiff battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Socotra "Battle of Socotra") against the [Mahra Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahra_Sultanate "Mahra Sultanate"). Their objective was to set a base in a strategic place on the route to India. The lack of a proper harbor and the infertility of the land led to famine and sickness in the garrison, and the Portuguese abandoned the island in 1511.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-15) The Mahra sultans took back control of the island, and the inhabitants were [converted to Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization "Islamization").[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-16)

### Modern

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Soqotri_people%2C_1918.jpg/500px-Soqotri_people%2C_1918.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Soqotri_people,_1918.jpg) Local men from Socotra taken by Charles K. Moser (1918)[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-17)

In 1834, the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company "East India Company") stationed a garrison on Socotra, in the expectation that the Mahra sultan of [Qishn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qishn "Qishn") and Socotra would accept an offer to sell the island. The lack of good anchorages proved to be as much a problem for the British as the Portuguese. The sultan refused to sell, and the British left in 1835. After the [capture of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Expedition "Aden Expedition") by the British in 1839, they lost interest in acquiring Socotra. In 1886, the British government decided to conclude a [protectorate treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Protectorate "Aden Protectorate") with the sultan in which he promised this time to "refrain from entering into any correspondence, agreement, or treaty with any foreign nation or power, except with the knowledge and sanction of the British Government".[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-18) In October 1967, in the wake of the departure of the British from Aden and southern Arabia, the Mahra Sultanate was abolished.

On 30 November 1967, Socotra became part of [South Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yemen "South Yemen"). Between 1976 and 1979, the island served as a base for the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy "Soviet Navy"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-20) although the South Yemeni government and president, [Ali Nasir Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Nasir_Muhammad "Ali Nasir Muhammad"), denied their existence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-21) [Slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery "Slavery") in the island was abolished under the rule of the [Yemeni Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Socialist_Party "Yemeni Socialist Party").[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-22)

Since [Yemeni unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_unification "Yemeni unification") in 1990, Socotra has been a part of the Republic of Yemen, affiliated first to [Aden Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Governorate "Aden Governorate"). In 2004, it was moved to be a part of the [Hadhramaut Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadhramaut_Governorate "Hadhramaut Governorate"). Later, in 2013, it became a [governorate of its own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate "Socotra Governorate").

Socotra was impacted by the [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami "2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami"), which damaged 40 fishing boats, even though the island was 4,600km (2,858mi) away from the tsunami epicentre off the west coast of [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh "Aceh"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-23) In 2015, the cyclones [Chapala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Chapala "Cyclone Chapala") and [Megh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Megh "Cyclone Megh") struck the island, causing severe damage to its infrastructure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-f11-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-f11)

Beginning in 2015, the UAE began increasing its presence on Socotra, first with humanitarian aid in the wake of tropical cyclones Chapala and Megh, and it eventually establishing a military presence on the island. On 30 April 2018, the UAE, as part of the ongoing [Saudi Arabian–led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian%E2%80%93led_intervention_in_Yemen "Saudi Arabian–led intervention in Yemen"), landed troops on the island and [took control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_occupation_of_Socotra "United Arab Emirates occupation of Socotra") of [Socotra Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Airport "Socotra Airport") and [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Socotra "Port of Socotra").[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-25) On 14 May 2018, Saudi troops were also deployed on the island, and a deal was brokered between the UAE and Yemen for a joint military training exercise and the return of administrative control of the airport and seaport to Yemen.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-26)[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-27) In June 2020, the UAE-backed [Southern Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Transitional_Council "Southern Transitional Council") (STC) seized control of the island [in a coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Transitional_Council_takeover_of_Socotra "Southern Transitional Council takeover of Socotra"), ousting the local authorities and establishing its own government. However, in 2023, the [Presidential Leadership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Leadership_Council "Presidential Leadership Council"), Yemen's internationally recognized government, integrated the STC into the government and recognized the STC's rule of the archipelago. Under its rule, peaceful protests against the coup and the UAE's interference in Socotra were violently dispersed, and journalists that criticized the coup were arrested and beaten.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-:2)

## Geography

See also: [Geology of Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Socotra "Geology of Socotra")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Socotra_Island.PNG/500px-Socotra_Island.PNG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Socotra_Island.PNG) [Locator map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locator_map "Locator map") of Socotra

Socotra is one of the most isolated landforms on Earth of [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent "Continent") origin (i.e., not of [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic "Volcanic") origin). The archipelago was once part of the supercontinent of [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana "Gondwana"). It detached during the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene "Miocene") epoch, in the same set of [rifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rift "Rift") events that opened the [Gulf of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aden "Gulf of Aden") to its northwest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-29) The island is culturally and administratively a part of Yemen, but geographically it belongs to [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa"), for it represents a continental fragment that is geologically linked to the [Somali plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_plate "Somali plate"). Socotra is known for its exceptional level of endemism, with about 37% of its plant species, 90% of its reptile species, and 95% of its land snail species found nowhere else on Earth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-31)

The archipelago consists of the main island of Socotra (3,665km2 or 1,415sqmi), three smaller islands, [Abd al Kuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al_Kuri "Abd al Kuri"), [Samhah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhah "Samhah") and [Darsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darsa "Darsa"), and two rocky islets, Ka'l Fir'awn and Sābūnīyah, both uninhabitable by humans but important for [seabirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabird "Seabird").[&#8202;](#ref-back-GEF-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-GEF) The island is about 125 kilometres (78mi) long and 45 kilometres (28mi) north to south.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-33) The distance from Socotra to [Cape Guardafui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Guardafui "Cape Guardafui"), its nearest point in the African mainland, is 232km (125nmi), and about 351km (190nmi) from [Ras Fartaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Fartaq?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ras Fartaq") in mainland [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula").[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEBrownMies20126-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEBrownMies20126) It has three major physical regions:

- The narrow coastal [plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain "Plain") with its characteristic dunes, formed by monsoon winds blowing during three summer months. The wind takes up the coast sand in a spiral and, as a result, forms the snow-white Socotran sand dunes.
- The [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone "Limestone") [plateaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau "Plateau") of [Momi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momi_Plateau?action=edit&redlink=1 "Momi Plateau"), [Homhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homhil_Plateau?action=edit&redlink=1 "Homhil Plateau") and [Diksam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diksam_Plateau "Diksam Plateau") with its characteristic [karst topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst_topography "Karst topography") based on limestone rock areas intersected with inter-hill plains. For centuries, and until recently, Socotra's main economic activity was subsistence [transhumant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transhumance "Transhumance") animal husbandry, predominantly goats and sheep on these plateaus. The outcome is a unique and still active [cultural landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_landscape "Cultural landscape") of agro- [pastoralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism "Pastoralism") with its characteristic [rainwater harvesting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainwater_harvesting "Rainwater harvesting") systems.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-34)
- A central massif, the [Hajhir Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajhir_Mountains "Hajhir Mountains"), composed of granite and [metamorphic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock "Metamorphic rock").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Flora-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-Flora) rising to 1,503 metres (4,931ft).[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-36)

## Climate

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Chapala_2015-11-01_0915Z.png/500px-Chapala_2015-11-01_0915Z.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chapala_2015-11-01_0915Z.png) Tropical Cyclone Chapala over Socotra in 2015

The climate of Socotra is classified in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification") as _BWh_ and _BSh_, meaning a transitional [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate "Hot desert climate") and a [semi-desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate "Semi-arid climate") with a mean annual temperature over 25°C (77°F). Yearly rainfall is light but is roughly spread throughout the year. [Orographic lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orographic_lift "Orographic lift") provided by the interior mountains, especially during the northeast monsoon from October to December, results in the highest inland areas averaging as much as 800 millimetres (31.50in) per year and receiving over 250 millimetres (9.84in) per month during November and December.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-37) The southwest [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon") season from June to September brings strong winds and high seas.

In an extremely unusual occurrence, the normally [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aridity "Aridity") western side of Socotra received more than 410 millimetres (16.14in) of rain from [Cyclone Chapala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Chapala "Cyclone Chapala") in November 2015.[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-38) Cyclones rarely affect the island, but in 2015 [Cyclone Megh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Megh "Cyclone Megh") became the strongest, and only, major cyclone to strike the island directly.

## Endemism

The [Socotra archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate#Ecology "Socotra Governorate") has been dubbed the Galapagos of the Indian Ocean due to its ecological diversity and high rates of [endemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism "Endemism").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) It has 835 vascular plant species, of which 37% are endemic. Six bird species, 95% of its terrestrial molluscs, and 90% of its reptiles are endemic to the archipelago. A 2016 genetic survey of all Socotran reptiles revealed unexpectedly high levels of cryptic diversity, indicating that the island's actual reptile diversity may be greater than currently recognized.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-:7) Socotra is home to several native mammals, none of which are endemic.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4)

### Endemic species

Due to the dry conditions and small size of the island, its fauna tend to be small.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-2)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4)

The island has several native bats and shrews, including the [Etruscan shrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_shrew "Etruscan shrew"), which is the smallest mammal in the world by mass.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-42) Socotra is of particular interest to ornithologists. The island has been designated an endemic bird area.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-43)

Some of the island's most striking species are its endemic plants and trees. Socotra has many native [drought resistant plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought_tolerance "Drought tolerance") which have adapted to the island's arid environment by developing large, bulbous stems in which they store their water.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-3)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) One notable example is [_Dendrosicyos socotranus_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrosicyos "Dendrosicyos") (cucumber tree) which is the only tree in the _[Cucurbitaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucurbitaceae "Cucurbitaceae")_ family. This species, which can grow over six meters high, has specialized cells which expand to hold water during wet periods.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-4)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/Dragons_Blood_Tree%2C_Socotra_Island_%2810941931846%29.jpg/500px-Dragons_Blood_Tree%2C_Socotra_Island_%2810941931846%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dragons_Blood_Tree,_Socotra_Island_(10941931846).jpg) [Dragon Blood Trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracaena_cinnabari "Dracaena cinnabari") in Socotra

Due to the island's long arid periods, several endemic flora have developed an evolutionary strategy of longevity, prioritizing long individual life span over reproduction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-5)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) These endemic flora are found in dry, low-lying areas of the island, and they grow slowly and rarely fruit or flower.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-6)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) These slow-growing endemic species are particularly vulnerable to [climate warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), as increasing dry periods may prevent them from reproducing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-7)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) One example is the Dragon's Blood Tree, or _[Dracaena cinnabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracaena_cinnabari "Dracaena cinnabari")_, which may be the most well known example of Socotran endemism. The tree contains a bright red sap which is used commercially.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-44) _D. cinnabari_ has a very distinct appearance: its trunk branches out into an umbrella-like crown with tightly packed leaves and widespread branches. This plant structure is observed in arid climates which have atmospheric moisture.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-8)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) Tightly packed leaves and wide spread branches facilitate the condensation of water from moisture in the air, and the shade provided by the umbrella crown may prevent evaporation of moisture from the soil.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-9)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) One dragon's blood forest remains on the island.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-:5) The average age of these trees is approximately 300 years. This suggests that the forest is over-mature, and indicates a decline in new growth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[\[43\]](#refnote-:5)

### Evolution

Socotra's high rates of species diversity and endemism can be attributed to a long period of isolation, as well as extreme climatic conditions which vary spatially and depending on altitude.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-10)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) These conditions have created a variety of [ecological niches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_niche#:~:text=In_ecology,_a_niche_is,and_a_consumer_of_prey). "Ecological niche").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-11)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) Researchers have attempted to date the origins of endemic species in convergence with the geological timeline of the island, which may help discern instances of [vicariant speciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopatric_speciation "Allopatric speciation") after the island split off from the mainland.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-12)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4)

Plant life on Socotra shares floristic similarities with northeast Africa and the Indian subcontinent. The genus _[Dirachma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirachma "Dirachma")_ has two species – one is native to Socotra and the other is native to [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia "Somalia"), over 1000km away. These species share unique traits, including eight-part flowers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-13)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) Several of the island's endemic species (members of the _[Echidnopsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echidnopsis "Echidnopsis")_ genus, for example) are thought to have evolved from a single colonizing ancestor.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-14)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) Once the colonizing ancestor was cut off from its original gene pool its descendents began to [diversify rapidly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_radiation "Adaptive radiation").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-15)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4)

The most ecologically diverse parts of Socotra are its wet [refugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugium_(population_biology) "Refugium (population biology)"), such as its cliffs and escarpments.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-16)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) These areas receive significantly more rainfall and mist than the rest of the island. These isolated areas in numerous separate areas on the island have allowed for speciation to occur.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-17)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) Species in the refugia are twice as likely to be endemic as species on the rest of the island.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-18)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) The refugia are particularly sensitive to changes in the climate, and have probably expanded and retracted many times over the evolutionary history of the island.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-19)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) The largest wet refugium on the island is the [Hajhir massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajhir_Mountains "Hajhir Mountains"). Fifty-five of the endemic species on the mountains are endemic to that particular refugium.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-20)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4)

### Current ecological threats

Socotra has experienced a steady decline of plants diversity over the past several decades.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-:6) Its native flora are vital for the survival of endemic fauna. Several factors, such as erosion, extreme weather events, and overgrazing may be responsible for this decline.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-1)[\[44\]](#refnote-:6) Intensified winds due to rising global temperatures threaten Socotra's old growth forests.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-2)[\[43\]](#refnote-:5) Traditional land management which has been practiced by indigenous people on Socotra for thousands of years, is becoming less widespread as the island becomes more influenced by the outside world.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-21)[\[38\]](#refnote-:4) Increasing populations of invasive species, such as rats and cats, also threaten the endemic flora and fauna.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-2)[\[44\]](#refnote-:6) Species on Socotra have evolved in very particular climatic conditions, and their ranges are very restricted, making them highly vulnerable to climate change.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-47) Without intervention, many of the island's endemic species may face extinction,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-3)[\[44\]](#refnote-:6) including potentially numerous cryptic species that have yet to be formally described.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[39\]](#refnote-:7)

## Demographics

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Qalansyah_-_men_at_the_market_square.jpg/500px-Qalansyah_-_men_at_the_market_square.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Qalansyah_-_men_at_the_market_square.jpg) Men at the market square in [Qulensya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qulensya "Qulensya")[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Socotri_Children%2C_Socotra_Is_%2810942706844%29.jpg/500px-Socotri_Children%2C_Socotra_Is_%2810942706844%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Socotri_Children,_Socotra_Is_(10942706844).jpg) Socotran children

Most of the inhabitants are indigenous [Soqotri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soqotri_people "Soqotri people") from [Al-Mahrah tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mahrah_tribe "Al-Mahrah tribe"), who are of Southern Arabian descent from [Al Mahrah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mahrah_Governorate "Al Mahrah Governorate"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-Xavier-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-Xavier) and are said to be especially closely related with the Qara and [Mahra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehri_people "Mehri people") groups of Southern [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia "Arabia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Natvoltn-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-Natvoltn) Some of the inhabitants are African, descending from former slaves who settled on the island.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-50) The majority of male residents on Socotra are reported to be in the J\* subclade of [Y-DNA haplogroup J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J_(Y-DNA) "Haplogroup J (Y-DNA)"). Several of the female lineages, notably those in [mtDNA haplogroup N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_N_(mtDNA) "Haplogroup N (mtDNA)"), are unique to the island.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-51)

Almost all inhabitants of Socotra, numbering about 50,000, live on the main island of the archipelago.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-Reuters) The principal city, [Hadibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadibu "Hadibu") (with a population of 8,545 at the [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census "Census") of 2004); the second largest town, [Qalansiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalansiyah "Qalansiyah") (population 3,862); and [Qād̨ub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q%C4%81d%CC%A8ub "Qād̨ub") (population 929) are all located on the north coast of the island of Socotra.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-53) Only about 450 people live on 'Abd-al-Kūrī and 100 on Samha; the island of Darsa and the [islets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet "Islet") of the archipelago are uninhabited.[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-54)

### Language

Main article: [Soqotri language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soqotri_language "Soqotri language")

The island is home to the [Semitic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages "Semitic languages") [Soqotri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soqotri_language "Soqotri language"), which is related to such other [Modern South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_South_Arabian "Modern South Arabian") languages on the Arabian mainland as [Mehri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehri_language "Mehri language"), [Harsusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsusi_language "Harsusi language"), [Bathari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathari_language "Bathari language"), [Shehri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehri_language "Shehri language"), and [Hobyot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobyot_language "Hobyot language"), which became the subject of European academic study in the nineteenth century.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-55)[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-56)

There is an ancient tradition of poetry and a poetry competition is held annually on the island.[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-57) The first attested Socotran poet is believed to be the ninth-century [Fatima al-Suqutriyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatima_al-Suqutriyya "Fatima al-Suqutriyya"), a popular figure in Socotran culture.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-:3) [Socotra Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Swahili "Socotra Swahili") is extinct.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guthrie-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-Guthrie)

### Religion

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Mosque%2C_Hadibo%2C_Socotra_Island_%2811007460353%29.jpg/500px-Mosque%2C_Hadibo%2C_Socotra_Island_%2811007460353%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mosque,_Hadibo,_Socotra_Island_(11007460353).jpg) A [mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mosques_in_Yemen "List of mosques in Yemen") in [Hadibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadibu "Hadibu") showcasing the local architecture

The earliest account concerning the presence of Christians in Socotra stems from the early-medieval 6th century AD Greek merchant [Cosmas Indicopleustes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmas_Indicopleustes "Cosmas Indicopleustes").[&#8202;](#ref-back-caves-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-caves) Later the Socotrans joined the [Assyrian church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East "Assyrian Church of the East").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-:0) During the 10th century, Arab geographer [Abu Muhammad al-Hasan al-Hamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Muhammad_al-Hasan_al-Hamdani "Abu Muhammad al-Hasan al-Hamdani") recorded during his visits that most of the islanders were Christian.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Christianity went into decline when the [Mahra Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahra_Sultanate "Mahra Sultanate") took power in the 16th century, and the populace had become mostly Muslim by the time the Portuguese arrived later that century.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-:1) An 1884 edition of [_Nature_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)") writes that the disappearance of Christian churches and monuments can be accounted for by a [Wahhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism "Wahhabism") excursion to the island in 1800.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-63) Today the only remnants of Christianity are some cross engravings from the first century CE, a few Christian tombs, and some church ruins.[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-64)

## Transport

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Socotra_-_Stop_for_a_stretch_%286407189003%29.jpg/500px-Socotra_-_Stop_for_a_stretch_%286407189003%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Socotra_-_Stop_for_a_stretch_(6407189003).jpg) [Toyota Land Cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Land_Cruiser "Toyota Land Cruiser") parked next to [Dragon's blood trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracaena_cinnabari "Dracaena cinnabari")

Public transport on Socotra is limited to a few [minibuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minibus "Minibus"); car hire usually means hiring a 4WD car and a driver.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-65)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Time-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-Time) Transport is a delicate matter on Socotra as road construction is considered locally to be detrimental to the island and its ecosystem. In particular, damage has occurred via chemical pollution from road construction while new roads have resulted in [habitat fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_fragmentation "Habitat fragmentation").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Socotra_eco_impact-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-Socotra_eco_impact)

The only [port on Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Socotra "Port of Socotra") is 5 kilometres (3 miles) east of Hadibu. Ships connect the port with the Yemeni coastal city of [Mukalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukalla "Mukalla"). The journey takes two to three days, and the service is used mostly for cargo. The UAE funded the modernization of the port on Socotra.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-68)

[Yemenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemenia "Yemenia") and [Felix Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Airways "Felix Airways") flew from [Socotra Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Airport "Socotra Airport") to [Sanaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanaa "Sanaa") and [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden "Aden") via [Riyan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyan_Airport "Riyan Airport"). After March 2015, due to [ongoing civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_(2015%E2%80%93present) "Yemeni Civil War (2015–present)") involving [Saudi Arabia's Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Saudi_Air_Force "Royal Saudi Air Force"), all flights to and from Socotra were cancelled.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-69) During the deployment of Emirati troops and aid to the island, multiple flight connections were made between [Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi "Abu Dhabi") and Socotra as part of Emirati efforts to provide Socotra residents with access to free healthcare and provide work opportunities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-70) Currently, Yemenia has scheduled flights twice a week from Jeddah to Socotra.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-71)

## Tourism

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Socotra_-_Local_men_%286407165515%29.jpg/500px-Socotra_-_Local_men_%286407165515%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Socotra_-_Local_men_(6407165515).jpg) Tourists posing for a picture with locals

Among 19th-century visitors to the island came British celebrity explorers [Theodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Theodore_Bent "James Theodore Bent") and [Mabel Bent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabel_Bent "Mabel Bent"), and their party, from mid December 1896 to mid February 1897.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-72)

Prior to the construction of the Socotra airport, the island could only be reached by a cargo ship. The ideal time to visit Socotra is from October to April; the remaining months usually have heavy monsoon rainfall, making it difficult for tourists; flights also usually get cancelled.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-73) The island lacks any well-established hotels, although there are a few guesthouses for travelers to stay during their short visits.[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-74) The island received over 1,000 tourists each year until 2014,[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-75) but has since been affected by the civil war.

Tourism to the island has increased over the years as many operators have begun offering trips to the island, which _[Gulf Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Today "Gulf Today")_ claimed "will become a dream destination despite the country's conflict". In May 2021, the Ministry of Information stated that the UAE is violating the island and has been planning to control it for years, running illegal trips there for foreign tourists without obtaining any permission from the Yemeni government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-76) In early 2026, hundreds of European and American tourists were stranded on the island, as a state of emergency was declared causing the closure of all ports.[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-77) Later, 609 tourists were flown to [Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah "Jeddah").[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-78)
