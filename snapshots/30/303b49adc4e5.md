Typhoon Hagibis

Reiwa 1 East Japan Typhoon

**Typhoon Hagibis**,[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-2) known in Japan as **Typhoon No.19** or the **Reiwa 1 East Japan Typhoon** (令和元年東日本台風, _Reiwa Gannen Higashi-Nihon Taifū_),[&#8202;](#ref-back-jma-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-jma) was an extremely large, powerful and costly [tropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone "Tropical cyclone") that caused widespread destruction in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") and is one of the costliest typhoons on record. The nineteenth [named storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone_naming "Tropical cyclone naming"), ninth [typhoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon "Typhoon"), and third [super typhoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_typhoon "Super typhoon") of the [2019 Pacific typhoon season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pacific_typhoon_season "2019 Pacific typhoon season"), it was the strongest typhoon to strike mainland Japan in decades, and one of the largest typhoons ever recorded, with a peak gale-force diameter of 825 nautical miles (949mi; 1,528km). The typhoon raised global media attention, as it greatly affected the [2019 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup "2019 Rugby World Cup") being hosted by Japan.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-4) With a death toll of 139, Hagibis was also the deadliest typhoon to strike Japan since [Typhoon Fran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Fran "Typhoon Fran") in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Pacific_typhoon_season "1976 Pacific typhoon season").

Hagibis caused severe flooding and extreme weather that have caused serious problems.
Hagibis developed from a tropical disturbance first monitored a couple hundred miles north of the [Marshall Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Islands "Marshall Islands") on October 2, 2019. The [Joint Typhoon Warning Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Typhoon_Warning_Center "Joint Typhoon Warning Center") (JTWC) and the [Japan Meteorological Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Meteorological_Agency "Japan Meteorological Agency") (JMA) began issuing advisories on Tropical Depression _20W_. Little change occurred as it travelled west toward the Mariana Islands over the next day, but on October 5, it began quickly organizing and early that day, the system was issued with the name _Hagibis_ by the JMA. Environmental conditions became extremely favourable for further development, and Hagibis underwent a period of [explosive intensification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_intensification "Rapid intensification") on October 6, and became a [Category 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffir%E2%80%93Simpson_scale#Category_5 "Saffir–Simpson scale")-equivalent super typhoon in under 12 hours, the second such system of the 2019 season, before passing through the Northern Mariana Islands at peak intensity, with 10-minute sustained winds of 105kn (195km/h; 120mph) and a central pressure of 915 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_(unit) "Pascal (unit)") (27.02 [inHg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch_of_mercury "Inch of mercury")).[&#8202;](#ref-back-DT-BT-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-DT-BT) Shortly afterwards, it underwent an [eyewall replacement cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyewall_replacement_cycle "Eyewall replacement cycle"), weakening before reaching a second peak intensity on October 8. Traveling toward Japan, Hagibis encountered unfavorable conditions and steady weakening commenced. On October 12, Hagibis made landfall on Japan on the [Izu Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_Peninsula "Izu Peninsula") near [Shizuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizuoka,_Shizuoka "Shizuoka, Shizuoka") and the [Greater Tokyo Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Tokyo_Area "Greater Tokyo Area"). By the next day, Hagibis became an [extratropical low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone "Extratropical cyclone") and the JMA and JTWC issued their final advisories on the system, dissipating on October 14 as it was absorbed into a low to its northeast.

Hagibis caused catastrophic destruction across much of eastern Japan. Hagibis spawned a large [tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado "Tornado") on October 12, which struck the [Ichihara area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichihara,_Chiba "Ichihara, Chiba") of [Chiba Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiba_Prefecture "Chiba Prefecture") during the onset of Hagibis; the tornado, along with a 5.7 magnitude earthquake off the coast, caused additional damage to those areas that were damaged by Hagibis.[&#8202;](#ref-back-earthquake_nyt-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-earthquake_nyt)[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-cnn) Hagibis caused more than $17 billion (2019 [USD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")) in damages, making it, at the time, the costliest typhoon on record until it was beaten by [Typhoon Doksuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Doksuri "Typhoon Doksuri") of 2023 (when not adjusted for inflation).[&#8202;](#ref-back-MILT_report-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-MILT_report)

In early October, a poorly organized and broad area of [thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorm "Thunderstorm") persisted over 1,500km (930mi) east of [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam"). With favorable atmospheric conditions and warm [sea surface temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature "Sea surface temperature") prevailing, the [Joint Typhoon Warning Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Typhoon_Warning_Center "Joint Typhoon Warning Center") (JTWC) began noting the possibility of [tropical cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis "Tropical cyclogenesis") on October4,[&#8202;](#ref-back-ABPW10_PGTW_040600-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-ABPW10_PGTW_040600) eventually issuing a [Tropical Cyclone Formation Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Cyclone_Formation_Alert "Tropical Cyclone Formation Alert") the next day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ABPW10_PGTW_050230-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-ABPW10_PGTW_050230) The system initially remained stationary,[&#8202;](#ref-back-WWJP25_RJTD_041800-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-WWJP25_RJTD_041800) consolidating a center of circulation in the lower levels of the atmosphere.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ABPW10_PGTW_050230-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-ABPW10_PGTW_050230) The [Japan Meteorological Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Meteorological_Agency "Japan Meteorological Agency") (JMA) declared the disturbance a [tropical depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_depression "Tropical depression") at 00:00 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time "Coordinated Universal Time") on October5.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DT-BT-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-DT-BT) At the time, the system was 1,030km (640mi) northeast of [Pohnpei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pohnpei "Pohnpei"), quickly developing [cumulonimbus clouds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulonimbus_cloud "Cumulonimbus cloud") around its center and establishing conducive [outflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outflow_(meteorology) "Outflow (meteorology)") as it tracked west around the periphery of an [area of high pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-pressure_area "High-pressure area").[&#8202;](#ref-back-WTPQ30_RJTD_050000-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-WTPQ30_RJTD_050000)[&#8202;](#ref-back-WTPQ30_RJTD_050600-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-WTPQ30_RJTD_050600) The tropical depression strengthened into a tropical storm by 18:00UTC on October5 while 1,560km (970mi) east of Guam,[&#8202;](#ref-back-DT-BT-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-DT-BT) gaining the name _Hagibis_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WTJP21_RJTD_051800-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-WTJP21_RJTD_051800) A dominant curved [rainband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainband "Rainband") had begun to wrap around the center of Hagibis, signifying further organization.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WTPQ30_RJTD_051800-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-WTPQ30_RJTD_051800) On October6, the storm made a slight turn towards the west-southwest and began an accelerated period of intensification within an environment with low [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear "Wind shear") and atop warm waters,[&#8202;](#ref-back-WTPQ31_PGUM_060653-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-WTPQ31_PGUM_060653)[&#8202;](#ref-back-DT-BT-3)[\[4\]](#refnote-DT-BT) reaching severe tropical storm intensity at 12:00UTC and typhoon strength six hours later as it developed a small [eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_(cyclone) "Eye (cyclone)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-DT-BT-4)[\[4\]](#refnote-DT-BT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-WDPN31_PGTW_061500-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-WDPN31_PGTW_061500)

Typhoon Hagibis entered a period of [explosive intensification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_intensification "Rapid intensification") on October7,[&#8202;](#ref-back-WDPN31_PGTW_070300-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-WDPN31_PGTW_070300) with its [central pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barometric_pressure "Barometric pressure") falling 55hPa (mbar; 1.62inHg) in 12hours according to the JMA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DT-BT-5)[\[4\]](#refnote-DT-BT) Estimates from the JTWC suggested a 185km/h (115mph) increase in the storm's maximum winds in 22hours. During this phase, Hagibis maintained a pinhole [eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_(cyclone) "Eye (cyclone)") 9km (5.6mi) across, encircled by a highly compact and sharply defined eyewall.[&#8202;](#ref-back-WDPN31_PGTW_070300-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-WDPN31_PGTW_070300) The rate of intensification was among the fastest observed in the Western Pacific.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FastestRates-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-FastestRates) According to the JMA, Hagibis reached its peak intensity at 09:00UTC on October7 with a minimum pressure of 915hPa (mbar; 27.02inHg) and 10-minute [sustained winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_sustained_wind "Maximum sustained wind") of 195km/h (121mph); Hagibis would maintain this intensity for 72hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DT-BT-6)[\[4\]](#refnote-DT-BT) The JTWC classified Hagibis as a [Category 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffir%E2%80%93Simpson_scale#Category_5 "Saffir–Simpson scale")-equivalent super typhoon[&#8202;](#ref-back-STY-0)[\[b\]](#refnote-STY) early on October7,[&#8202;](#ref-back-WTPQ31_PGUM_070655-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-WTPQ31_PGUM_070655) and later assessed peak 1-minute sustained winds of 295km/h (185mph), while also estimating a pressure of 890hPa (mbar; 26.28inHg), as Hagibis passed just south of [Anatahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatahan "Anatahan") in the [Northern Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands "Northern Mariana Islands").[&#8202;](#ref-back-WTPQ31_PGUM_071550-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-WTPQ31_PGUM_071550) Hagibis was unusually rapid in its trek through the Mariana Islands, traveling with a forward motion of 27–34km/h (17–21mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-NWSTiyan_PTCR-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-NWSTiyan_PTCR)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Hagibis_2019_both_landfalls.gif/500px-Hagibis_2019_both_landfalls.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hagibis_2019_both_landfalls.gif) Typhoon Hagibis making landfall in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") on October 12

After passing the Mariana Islands, Hagibis began an [eyewall replacement cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyewall_replacement_cycle "Eyewall replacement cycle"), which halted further strengthening. As the primary eyewall began to erode,[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-25) the JTWC downgraded the typhoon to a Category4-equivalent super typhoon at 00:00 UTC on October8\. Several hours later, Hagibis completed the eyewall replacement cycle and reintensified to Category5-equivalent intensity, attaining a secondary peak intensity with 1-minute sustained winds of 280km/h (175mph). Steady weakening began to ensue after as Hagibis began encountering increasingly unfavourable conditions and its outer rainbands began to erode. After gradual weakening, Hagibis made landfall on [Shizuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizuoka_Prefecture "Shizuoka Prefecture") as a Category 2-equivalent typhoon, with 1-minute sustained winds of 155km/h (95mph), at around 08:30UTC on October12\. While over Japan, Hagibis became disorganized from high wind shear and eventually became extratropical on October13.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-26) The remnants accelerated and moved over the Northern Pacific Ocean before being absorbed by a separate low to its northeast the following day over the [Gulf of Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Alaska "Gulf of Alaska").

### Mariana Islands

On October7, [U.S. president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") approved an emergency declaration and evacuation orders for [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam "Guam") and the [Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Islands "Mariana Islands") ahead of Hagibis, with typhoon warnings issued on the islands of [Saipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saipan "Saipan"), [Tinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinian "Tinian"), [Alamagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamagan "Alamagan"), and [Pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_(island) "Pagan (island)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-30)

### Japan

Forecasts across eastern, western, and northern Japan called for strong winds and torrential rain that would likely cause flooding and mudslides.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rugby-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-Rugby) [JR Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Railways_Group "Japan Railways Group"), [Japan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Airlines "Japan Airlines"), and [All Nippon Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Nippon_Airways "All Nippon Airways") suspended services.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-nyt) [JMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Meteorological_Agency "Japan Meteorological Agency") weather forecaster, Yasushi Kajiwara, said, "It is a level 5 situation; some sort of disaster may have already taken place. People are strongly advised to act to protect their lives right away."[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-cnn) Evacuation orders have been issued to more than 800,000 households across 11 prefectures.[&#8202;](#ref-back-guardian-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-guardian) Over 230,000 people took the advice to head to evacuation shelters.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn2-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-cnn2)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Empty_shelves_in_Tokyo_area_as_people_get_ready_for_typhoon_Hagibis_-_Oct_11_2019_330pm.jpeg/500px-Empty_shelves_in_Tokyo_area_as_people_get_ready_for_typhoon_Hagibis_-_Oct_11_2019_330pm.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Empty_shelves_in_Tokyo_area_as_people_get_ready_for_typhoon_Hagibis_-_Oct_11_2019_330pm.jpeg) Shelves in shops around [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo") were quickly cleared, as people bought supplies ahead of Hagibis making landfall.

The typhoon had effects on several major sporting events occurring in Japan. Three matches of the [2019 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup "2019 Rugby World Cup") were cancelled due to Hagibis, including the [Pool B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup_Pool_B "2019 Rugby World Cup Pool B") matches between [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_rugby_union_team "New Zealand national rugby union team") and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team "Italy national rugby union team"), and [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_national_rugby_union_team "Canada national rugby union team") and [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_rugby_union_team "Namibia national rugby union team"), and the [Pool C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup_Pool_C "2019 Rugby World Cup Pool C") match between [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_rugby_union_team "England national rugby union team") and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_rugby_union_team "France national rugby union team"). This marked the first time that matches have been cancelled in the history of the Rugby World Cup.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt-1)[\[30\]](#refnote-nyt)[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-35) All cancelled matches were counted as draws: the cancelled fixture effectively eliminated Italy from the tournament, as they had a chance to potentially qualify for the knockout stage with a sufficient margin of victory against New Zealand.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-36)

On October11, it was announced that the Saturday practice session for the [2019 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Japanese_Grand_Prix "2019 Japanese Grand Prix") at [Suzuka Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_Circuit "Suzuka Circuit") would be cancelled, and the Saturday qualifying session was postponed to Sunday morning prior to the race.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC_-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-BBC_) The [F4 Japanese Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F4_Japanese_Championship "F4 Japanese Championship") cancelled its round at the circuit as well.[&#8202;](#ref-back-F4_Cancellation-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-F4_Cancellation) [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball "Nippon Professional Baseball") postponed both Game 4 Climax Series games in the [2019 Pacific League Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pacific_League_Climax_Series "2019 Pacific League Climax Series") and the [2019 Central League Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Central_League_Climax_Series "2019 Central League Climax Series"), despite the games being played indoors in domed stadiums.[&#8202;](#ref-back-belluna-0)[\[c\]](#refnote-belluna) Both games were planned to take place on Saturday, October12, one in [Tokorozawa, Saitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokorozawa,_Saitama "Tokorozawa, Saitama"), and the other in [Bunkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunky%C5%8D "Bunkyō"), Tokyo. The games were instead played the next day on Sunday, October13.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-40)

### Mariana Islands

The [Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Islands "Mariana Islands") were glanced by Typhoon Hagibis. Acting Governor [Arnold Palacios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Palacios "Arnold Palacios") began giving "all-clear" signals based on information from the [National Weather Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Weather_Service "National Weather Service") and CNMI Emergency Operations Center. Communities have been cleaning up debris and all evacuation centers are now closed. On October12, most utilities were restored and had started reopening.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-41)

### Japan

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Aerial_photograph_of_2019_Typhoon_Hagibis_flood_in_Nagano_prefecture_-_GSI_5676.jpg/500px-Aerial_photograph_of_2019_Typhoon_Hagibis_flood_in_Nagano_prefecture_-_GSI_5676.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Aerial_photograph_of_2019_Typhoon_Hagibis_flood_in_Nagano_prefecture_-_GSI_5676.jpg) An aerial view of flooding in [Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_(city) "Nagano (city)"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan").

Early on October12, a [tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado "Tornado") struck [Ichihara City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichihara_City "Ichihara City"), which killed one person and left two people injured.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tornado-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-tornado) In the afternoon, some areas of [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") suffered heavy flooding, with tens of thousands of homes without power. The [Japan Meteorological Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Meteorological_Agency "Japan Meteorological Agency") warned that high winds could cause further flooding and landslides. The agency issued evacuation advisories in high-risk areas.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-bbc) Over 76 centimetres (30in) of rain fell in parts of Japan. Japan's [Fire and Disaster Management Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_and_Disaster_Management_Agency "Fire and Disaster Management Agency") stated that at 139 have been confirmed dead of which 118 were directly caused and 21 post-disaster and 3 people missing, with 388 people injured by the storm, 48 of which seriously.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-44) More than 270,000 households lost power across the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-bbc) Ten trains of the [Hokuriku Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuriku_Shinkansen "Hokuriku Shinkansen") Line in [Nagano City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_City "Nagano City") were inundated by flood waters, leading to the scrapping of the trains and a loss of [¥](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_yen "Japanese yen") 32.8billion (US$300million).[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-45) Total economic losses across the nation were huge, which stood at ¥1.88trillion (US$17.3billion).[&#8202;](#ref-back-MILT_report-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-MILT_report)

At around 18:22 [JST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Standard_Time "Japan Standard Time") (09:22UTC) on October12, a [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale "Moment magnitude scale") 5.7 earthquake occurred off the coast of [Chiba Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiba_Prefecture "Chiba Prefecture"), worsening the dangerous conditions already created by Hagibis.[&#8202;](#ref-back-earthquake_independent-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-earthquake_independent)

Hagibis also led to the cancellation of several sporting events, such as three [Rugby World Cup 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup "2019 Rugby World Cup") matches; involving [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_rugby_union_team "Namibia national rugby union team") versus [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_national_rugby_union_team "Canada national rugby union team"), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_rugby_union_team "New Zealand national rugby union team") versus [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team "Italy national rugby union team"), and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_rugby_union_team "England national rugby union team") versus [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_rugby_union_team "France national rugby union team"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-47)[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-48) and the third practice and qualifying for the [Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Japanese_Grand_Prix "2019 Japanese Grand Prix").[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC_-1)[\[35\]](#refnote-BBC_) Qualifying for the Grand Prix was rescheduled to the Sunday morning before the race.

### Retirement

Due to the severe impacts of Typhoon Hagibis in Japan, the name _Hagibis_ was officially retired during the 52nd Annual Session, organized by the ESCAP/WMO Typhoon Committee in February 2020. In February 2021, the Typhoon Committee subsequently chose _[Ragasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ragasa "Typhoon Ragasa")_ as its replacement name.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-49)

**Historical comparisons to Hagibis:**

- [Typhoon Ida (1958)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ida_(1958) "Typhoon Ida (1958)") – An intense, but deadlier typhoon that also affected similar areas; known as the Kanogawa Typhoon in Japan.
- [Typhoon Tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Tip "Typhoon Tip") (1979) – The largest and most intense tropical cyclone on record, which took a similar path to Hagibis.
- [Typhoon Mireille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Mireille "Typhoon Mireille") (1991) – A powerful typhoon that hit Japan and became the costliest typhoon on record, adjusted for inflation.
- [Typhoon Higos (2002)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Higos_(2002) "Typhoon Higos (2002)") – A strong typhoon which was slightly weaker than Hagibis but had a comparable track and also affected Japan.
- [Typhoon Ma-on (2004)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ma-on_(2004) "Typhoon Ma-on (2004)") – Another intense typhoon which also had a similar track to Hagibis and also affected the Japanese Grand Prix.
- [Typhoon Phanfone (2014)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Phanfone_(2014) "Typhoon Phanfone (2014)") – Another strong typhoon that made a comparable trajectory; also affected the Japanese Grand Prix.
- [Typhoon Jebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Jebi "Typhoon Jebi") (2018) – The costliest typhoon on record in Japan in terms of insured losses.
- [Typhoon Faxai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Faxai "Typhoon Faxai") (2019) – Another powerful typhoon which struck Japan a few weeks before Hagibis.
