**Abid Hasan Safrani**, IFS, born **Zain-al-Abdin Hasan**, was an officer of the [Indian National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Army "Indian National Army") (INA) and later, after 1947, an Indian [diplomat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomat "Diplomat"). He famously introduced the slogan '[Jai Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Hind "Jai Hind")', coined by [Chempakaraman Pillai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chempakaraman_Pillai "Chempakaraman Pillai"), which translates to 'victory belongs to India' that is used for official and semi-official purposes including army salutes, moral upliftment and in pop culture.[[2]](#cite_note-2)

Born to an anti-colonialist family in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad_State "Hyderabad State"), Abid Hasan was brought up in India and later went to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") to train as an engineer. While he was a student in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Abid Hasan met [Subhas Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhas_Chandra_Bose "Subhas Chandra Bose") and decided to join the [Indische Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indische_Legion "Indische Legion"). Hasan would later serve as Bose's personal secretary and interpreter while Bose was [in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indische_Legion "Indische Legion"). Hasan also sailed with Bose in the German [U-boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat "U-boat") [U-180](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-180 "U-180") in 1943 on Bose's voyage to South East Asia. Over the course of the reformation of the INA and its campaigns in the [south east Asian theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_Asian_theater_of_World_War_II "South-East Asian theater of World War II"), Hasan rose to be a [Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_(rank) "Major (rank)") in the [Azad Hind Fauj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Hind_Fauj "Azad Hind Fauj"). It was also during this that he adopted "*Safrani*", after the holy Hindu colour of [Saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron_(color) "Saffron (color)"), to his name as a mark of communal harmony.

After repatriation to India at the end of the war, Abid Hasan was released following the end of the [INA trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INA_trials "INA trials") in 1946 and joined the [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress") briefly. After partition, Hasan chose to settle in Hyderabad and joined the nascent [Indian Foreign Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Foreign_Service "Indian Foreign Service"). Over a long diplomatic career, Hasan served as the Indian [Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador "Ambassador") to a number of countries including [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") and [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark") before retiring in 1969 and settling back in Hyderabad. Abid Hasan Saffrani died in 1984.

On suggestion of Abid Hasan, the INA adopted "[Jai Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Hind "Jai Hind")" as its slogan;[[3]](#cite_note-Singh1986-3) The term "[Jai Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Hind "Jai Hind")" had been coined by **[Chempaka Raman Pillai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chempakaraman_Pillai#References "Chempakaraman Pillai")** .[[4]](#cite_note-4) In the 1940s, the INA leaders decided to devise a religion-agnostic greeting for its soldiers: Thakur Yashwant Singh suggested "Hindustan ki Jai", but Hasan felt this was too long and suggested "Jai Hind" as an alternative, which was unanimously accepted.[[3]](#cite_note-Singh1986-3) The term emerged as a national slogan after India's independence.[[5]](#cite_note-5)

Netaji Bose's nephew Aurobindo Bose later married Safrani's niece. Her name is [Suraiya Hasan Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suraiya_Hasan_Bose "Suraiya Hasan Bose"). She was the daughter of elder brother of Abid Hasan safrini by name[*[clarification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*] Badrul Hasan, who worked with Gandhiji. Mr Abid Hasan died on April 9, 1984.

## Scholarly activities

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Abid_Hasan&action=edit§ion=1 "Edit section: Scholarly activities")]

Hasan was also a scholar who spent long hours with his Persian and Urdu poetry.[[6]](#cite_note-6) His [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi")-[Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu "Urdu") translation of *[Jana Gana Mana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jana_Gana_Mana "Jana Gana Mana")* with the music from [Ram Singh Thakuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Singh_Thakuri "Ram Singh Thakuri"), became *[Shubh Sukh Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shubh_Sukh_Chain "Shubh Sukh Chain")*, the anthem of the [Provisional Government of Free India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arzi_Hukumat-e-Azad_Hind "Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind").[[7]](#cite_note-tribune2002-7) Abid Hasan Safrani died on 9 April 1984 in Hyderabad.
