# Zarqawi died 52 minutes after air strike, US says

Abu Musab al-Zarqawi took nearly an hour to die after a US air strike destroyed his safe house, the results of a US autopsy showed today.

The spokesman said Zarqawi died 52 minutes after the first of two 500lb bombs hit the building and 24 minutes after US forces arrived at the scene.

The leader of al-Qaida in Iraq had been meeting a spiritual leader at the house near Baquba, north-east of Baghdad.

A web statement from the group today said a militant named Abu Hamza al-Muhajer had been appointed its new leader. The name al-Muhajer, Arabic for "immigrant," suggests he is not Iraqi but little else is known about him.

The US autopsy on Zarqwai revealed there was "no evidence of beating or any firearm injuries", despite claims he had been beaten by US troops after being pulled from the rubble.

A US doctor who arrived at the scene 28 minutes after Thursday's air strike found his cardiac pulse "was such that he was not going to live", army spokesman Major General William Caldwell said.

"It was very evident that he had extremely massive internal injuries," he added.

He confirmed that Zarqawi - believed to have killed and beheaded the British hostage Ken Bigley - died 52 minutes after the two bombs were dropped by an F-16 aircraft.

Zarqawi is the most prominent Islamist militant to have been killed by US forces during the insurgency in Iraq.

Despite boasts that the terror leader always wore an explosive bomb belt in case of capture by US-led forces, Maj Gen Caldwell said he had been wearing "some black outfit".

A separate FBI examination confirmed the body was Zarqawi's. The spiritual advisor, Sheik Abd al-Rahman, was killed instantly in the air strike.
