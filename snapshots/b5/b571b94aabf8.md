## Current Cigarette Smoking Among U.S. Adults Aged 18 Years and Older

[By Sex](#by_sex) \| [By Age Group](#age_group) \| [By Race/Ethnicity](#by_race) \| [By U.S. Census Region](#by_census) \| [By Education](#by_education) \| [By Marital Status](#by_marital) \| [By Annual Household Income](#by_income) \| [By Sexual Orientation](#by_orientation) \| [By Health Insurance Coverage](#by_insurance) \| [By Disability](#by_disability) \| [By Generalized Anxiety Disorder](#by_generalized_anxiety)

Cigarette smoking remains the leading cause of preventable disease and death in the United States.

Cigarette smoking kills more than 480,000 Americans each year.1 Cigarette smoking [cost the United States more than $600 billion](https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/php/data-statistics/economic-trends/index.html#cdc_research_or_data_summary_what_cdc_is_doing-economic-cost-of-cigarette-smoking) in 2018, including more than $240 billion in healthcare spending and nearly $372 billion in lost productivity.1,2,3,4

In 2022, an estimated 11.6% (28.8 million) of U.S. adults currently smoked cigarettes. Current cigarette smoking was defined as smoking ≥100 cigarettes during a lifetime and now smoking cigarettes either every day or some days.5
