**Barbara Radding Morgan** (born November 28, 1951) is an American [teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher "Teacher") and a former [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") [astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut "Astronaut"). She participated in the [Teacher in Space Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher_in_Space_Project "Teacher in Space Project") as backup to [Christa McAuliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christa_McAuliffe "Christa McAuliffe") for the 1986 [ill-fated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster") [STS-51-L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-51-L "STS-51-L") mission of the [Space Shuttle _Challenger_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger "Space Shuttle Challenger"). In 1998, eight years after the Teacher in Space Project had ended, she was selected by NASA as an astronaut candidate, training as a [mission specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_specialist "Mission specialist"); astronaut Morgan flew on [STS-118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-118 "STS-118") in August 2007. As such, she became the first teacher (by original career) to go into space.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)

## Early life and education

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")\]

Morgan was born to Dr. and Mrs. Jerry Radding in 1951 and raised in [Fresno, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresno,_California "Fresno, California"), where she attended [Herbert Hoover High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover_High_School_(Fresno) "Herbert Hoover High School (Fresno)"). Following graduation in 1969, she was accepted to [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California "Palo Alto, California"), where she graduated with distinction in 1973 with a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in [Human Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_biology "Human biology"). She obtained her teaching credential from [Notre Dame de Namur University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_de_Namur_University "Notre Dame de Namur University") in nearby [Belmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont,_California "Belmont, California") in 1974.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio1-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-bio1)

## Teaching career since 1974

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Teaching career since 1974")\]

Morgan began her teaching career in 1974 on the [Flathead Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flathead_Indian_Reservation "Flathead Indian Reservation") at Arlee Elementary School in [Arlee, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlee,_Montana "Arlee, Montana"), where she taught remedial reading and math. From 1975 to 1978, she taught remedial reading/math and second grade at McCall-Donnelly Elementary School in [McCall, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCall,_Idaho "McCall, Idaho"). From 1978 to 1979, Morgan taught English and science to third graders at [Colegio Americano de Quito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colegio_Americano_de_Quito "Colegio Americano de Quito") in [Quito, Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quito,_Ecuador "Quito, Ecuador"), for a year. From 1979 to 1998, Morgan taught second, third, and fourth grades at McCall-Donnelly Elementary School.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ellis-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-ellis)

## Teacher in Space Project

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Teacher in Space Project")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Teacher_in_Space_Project_McAuliffe_and_Morgan.jpg/250px-Teacher_in_Space_Project_McAuliffe_and_Morgan.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Teacher_in_Space_Project_McAuliffe_and_Morgan.jpg) [Christa McAuliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christa_McAuliffe "Christa McAuliffe") and Morgan in December 1985

Morgan was selected as the backup candidate for the NASA [Teacher in Space Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher_in_Space_Project "Teacher in Space Project") on July 19, 1985. From September 1985 to January 1986, Morgan trained with [Christa McAuliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christa_McAuliffe "Christa McAuliffe") and the [Space Shuttle _Challenger_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger "Space Shuttle Challenger") crew at NASA's [Johnson Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Space_Center "Johnson Space Center"), [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_Texas "Houston, Texas"). Following McAuliffe's death in the [_Challenger_ disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster"), Morgan assumed the duties of Teacher in Space Designee. From March 1986 to July 1986, she worked with NASA, speaking to educational organizations throughout the country.

In the fall of 1986, Morgan returned to Idaho to resume her teaching career. She taught second and third grades at McCall-Donnelly Elementary and continued to work with NASA's Education Division, Office of Human Resources and Education. Her duties as Teacher in Space Designee included public speaking, educational consulting, curriculum design, and serving on the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation")'s Federal Task Force for Women and Minorities in Science and Engineering.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)

## NASA career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: NASA career")\]

In January 1998, 12 years after McAuliffe's death, Morgan was selected by NASA as an astronaut candidate ( [mission specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_specialist "Mission specialist")) and reported to the Johnson Space Center in August 1998 to begin training to become a full-time astronaut. Following the completion of two years of training and evaluation, she was assigned technical duties in the Astronaut Office Space Station Operations Branch. She served in the Astronaut Office [CAPCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsule_communicator "Capsule communicator") Branch, working in [Mission Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Control "Mission Control") as prime communicator with on-orbit crews.[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)

Like many other astronauts and [cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmonaut "Cosmonaut"), Morgan had been a licensed [amateur radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio "Amateur radio") operator, KD5VNP, having passed the technician class license exam in 2003 (the license expired in 2013).[&#8202;](#ref-back-qrz-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-qrz) This qualified her to use the facilities of the [Amateur Radio on the International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_Radio_on_the_International_Space_Station "Amateur Radio on the International Space Station") (ARISS) project.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-qrz-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-qrz)

Morgan was initially scheduled to fly on the [STS-118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-118 "STS-118") mission on [Space Shuttle _Columbia_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia "Space Shuttle Columbia") in November 2004. During the [disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia_disaster "Space Shuttle Columbia disaster") that destroyed _Columbia_ in February 2003, she was aboard a training [chase plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_plane "Chase plane") that was following the shuttle as it prepared to land.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) As a result of the disaster, STS-118 was delayed until 2007 and was moved to [_Endeavour_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Endeavour "Space Shuttle Endeavour").

Morgan's duties as a mission specialist were no different than those of other crew members. While NASA press releases and media briefings often referred to her as a "mission specialist educator" or "educator astronaut", Morgan did not train in the [Educator Astronaut Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educator_Astronaut_Project "Educator Astronaut Project"). [NASA Administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Administrator "NASA Administrator") [Michael D. Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Griffin "Michael D. Griffin") clarified at a press conference after STS-118 that Morgan was not considered a mission specialist educator, but rather was a standard mission specialist, who had been a teacher.[&#8202;](#ref-back-press1-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-press1)

Prior to her flight on STS-118, NASA seemed to limit Morgan's exposure to the press, but she did a series of interviews shortly before the start of the mission about what the crew of STS-118 would be doing to help build the International Space Station,[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13) commenting, "You know, there's a great sense of pride to be able to be involved in a human endeavor that takes us all a little bit farther. When you look down and see our Earth, and you realize what we are trying to do as a human race, it's pretty profound."[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

Three weeks after Morgan's mission ended, she conducted her first space education assignment at [Walt Disney World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_World "Walt Disney World") in Florida. Morgan's words from that day were etched into a plaque on a wall of Mission: Space. The "Wall of Honor" contains quotes from notable people, such as [Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong "Neil Armstrong"), [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy"), [Charles Lindbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lindbergh "Charles Lindbergh"), [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking"), [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan "Carl Sagan"), [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo "Galileo"), and Christa McAuliffe. Morgan's plaque is placed beside McAuliffe's, which says: "Space is for everybody ... That's our new frontier out there."[&#8202;](#ref-back-post-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-post) This event was one of a series of lectures Morgan would complete.

### Spaceflight experience

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Spaceflight experience")\]

[STS-118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-118 "STS-118"), an assembly mission to the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station"), successfully launched from Florida's [Kennedy Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center "Kennedy Space Center") at 6:36:42p.m. EDT, 8 August 2007.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) Morgan served as [robotic arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Manipulator_System "Remote Manipulator System") operator and transfer coordinator, coordinating the transfer of over 5,000 pounds (2,300kg) of cargo to the International Space Station, and bringing home over 3,000 pounds (1,400kg).[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) In addition to her other duties, Morgan participated in twenty-minute amateur radio question-and-answer sessions with young people at the Discovery Center of Idaho and other centers, and joined Mission Specialist [Alvin Drew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Drew "Alvin Drew") in an education event with young people at the [Challenger Center for Space Science Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_Center_for_Space_Science_Education "Challenger Center for Space Science Education") in [Alexandria, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Virginia "Alexandria, Virginia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-ham1-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-ham1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) The event was hosted by June Scobee, widow of Space Shuttle _Challenger_'s commander, [Richard "Dick" Scobee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Scobee "Dick Scobee"). The center honored Morgan with the _President George H.W. Bush Leadership Award_. STS-118 landed successfully at Kennedy Space Center on August 21, a day ahead of schedule due to concerns about [Hurricane Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dean "Hurricane Dean").[&#8202;](#ref-back-spacecomshort-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-spacecomshort)[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

## Post-NASA career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Post-NASA career")\]

On June 28, 2008, Morgan announced that she would leave [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") for a teaching job at [Boise State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_State_University "Boise State University"). In August 2008, Morgan took a full-time position as a distinguished educator in residence; a dual appointment to BSU's colleges of engineering and education. There she advises, leads and represents the university in policy development, advocacy and fund-raising in science, technology, engineering and math.[&#8202;](#ref-back-releaseh08161-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-releaseh08161)

On July 4, 2008, Morgan received the "Friend of Education" award from the [National Education Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Education_Association "National Education Association"). The following month, Barbara R. Morgan Elementary School opened in McCall, Idaho.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

In 2008 she appeared in the [Discovery Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Channel "Discovery Channel") documentary miniseries [When We Left Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_We_Left_Earth:_The_NASA_Missions "When We Left Earth: The NASA Missions").[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)

She appeared on the 2020 [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") documentary miniseries _[Challenger: The Final Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger:_The_Final_Flight "Challenger: The Final Flight")_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

## Awards and honors

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Awards and honors")\]

Morgan received the [Adler Planetarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adler_Planetarium "Adler Planetarium") Women in Space Science Award in 2008.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27) She was awarded the [Frank J. Malina Astronautics Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_J._Malina_Astronautics_Medal "Frank J. Malina Astronautics Medal") by the [International Astronautical Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronautical_Federation "International Astronautical Federation") for her contributions to space science education in 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28)

Actress [Mary Chris Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Chris_Wall "Mary Chris Wall") portrayed Morgan in the 1990 television film _[Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_(1990_film) "Challenger (1990 film)")_.

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Barbara_Morgan&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Morgan is married to writer Clay Morgan of McCall, Idaho;[&#8202;](#ref-back-mcmwfc-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-mcmwfc) they have two sons. She is a classical [flutist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flutist "Flutist") who also enjoys [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), literature, hiking, swimming, and [cross-country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing "Cross-country skiing").[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio1-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-bio1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-edu-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-edu)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bio2-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-bio2)
