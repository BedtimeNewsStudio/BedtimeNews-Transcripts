The next pair of Starship vehicles are nearly ready for flight, with both bringing new design changes to the table. While SpaceX readies Ship 29 and Booster 11 for flight 4 of Starship, let's go over the changes and compare them to the previous flight vehicles: Ship 28 and Booster 10.

## Starship Flight Test 4

On Thursday, June 6th, 2024, Ship 29 and Booster 11 lifted off from the Orbital Launch Pad in Starbase, Texas on the fourth flight test of Starship. The milestones achieved during the flight test can be found in SpaceX's post-flight press release [here](https://www.spacex.com/launches/mission/?missionId=starship-flight-4).

![GPaXWoga0AccBpr.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/52e7a883-0499-4be1-8c2b-fd544d3819ff-large.jpg)Ship 29 reentering Earth's atmosphere on the fourth Starship flight test. \| Photo courtesy of [SpaceX](https://twitter.com/SpaceX)

## Starship 29

Similar to how Ship 25 was Ship 24's "twin" Ship, with smaller revisions and primarily post-flight upgrades, Ship 29 is the twin of Ship 28. As a result of Ship 28's failure to achieve all mission objectives on the third flight, SpaceX has made many modifications to the Starship upper stage, so let's take a look at them.

![S29FullStack.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/9a0996df-3e42-4993-a1e0-9e8741a48750-large.jpg)Ship 29 conducting a second wet dress rehearsal.  \|  Photo courtesy of [SpaceX](https://twitter.com/SpaceX)

#### Ship - Attitude Control System

##### Redundant Roll Thrusters Added

In order to resolve the attitude control issues discovered on Ship 28 during the third flight test of Starship, which ultimately resulted in a partial loss of attitude control, new roll thrusters have been added directly below the PEZ dispenser door on Ship 28.

> SpaceX has since added additional roll control thrusters on upcoming Starships to improve attitude control redundancy and upgraded hardware for improved resilience to blockage.

\-\- " [Lessons Learned from Flight 3](https://www.spacex.com/updates/#flight-3-report)", SpaceX Press Release, 24 May 2024

The comparison photo below uses an image of Ship 28 and Ship 30, as they are from the same angle. The thrusters on Ship 29 are the same and are pictured in the second photo below.

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/7192b061-67a5-4834-9b3a-97e89ce28f9c-medium.jpg)New roll thrusters on Ship 29.  \|  Photo courtesy of [LabPadre](https://twitter.com/LabPadre) [Rover](https://www.youtube.com/@LabPadre)

#### Ship - Gaseous Raceway

##### New Pipe Added

An additional pipe has been added to the gaseous raceway on the center of the Ship, which is noticeably smaller in diameter compared to the other pipes. This pipe runs from the forward end of the vehicle to the aft end, although it is unclear what it contains.

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/8e4a1a2c-a18f-4ade-bb03-16f7004ade55-medium.jpg)The new pipe, as seen at the forward end of Ship 29.  \|  Photo courtesy of [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics/) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang)

#### Ship - Thermal Protection System

##### Tile Adhesive Changed in Specific Areas

SpaceX has used a blue tile adhesive since Ship 24, and it seems that it isn't holding up to the company's expectations. As a result, many areas on Ship 29 have had their tiles removed and a new red glue applied when installing the replacement tiles.

Additionally, the steel surface is being scuffed up, likely to allow better adhesion compared to what happens on the plain steel's smooth surface. It seems that SpaceX is having issues getting good adhesion, but they are changing their processes to accommodate their learnings.

In advance of Flight 3, this was done to Ship 28 on some of the section seams in an effort to resolve many of the tile issues that were experienced with Ship 25 on Flight 2.

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/87e97b8b-16fc-4c4e-9fb9-d4ca02bdb873-medium.jpg)New red adhesive on areas on Ship 29's heat shield.  \|  Photo courtesy of [BocaChicaGal](https://twitter.com/BocaChicaGal) for [NASASpaceflight](https://twitter.com/NASASpaceflight) (Left) and [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics/) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang) (Right)

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/240e172b-4e2d-467a-8c6a-210f53614fa5-medium.jpg)Ship 25 on Starship Flight 2, as seen from the WB-57 plane.  \|  Photo courtesy of [NASA](https://twitter.com/NASA)

##### Nose Tip Tiles Replaced

One of the areas that had the adhesive replaced was the tip of the nosecone, which had all of the tiles removed before being reinstalled with the red glue. We never saw the tip due to the position of the onboard cameras on Starship, but SpaceX may have seen issues with this area.

![29TileTip.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/675ceb17-849e-4b10-91fc-68a4f88c19d3-large.jpg)Tiles replaced on Ship 29's nose tip.  \|  Photo courtesy of [BocaChicaGal](https://twitter.com/BocaChicaGal) for [NASASpaceflight](https://twitter.com/NASASpaceflight)

##### External Stringer Tiles Replaced

Additionally, the two bands of external stringers with adhered tiles over them have had their tiles replaced and the red adhesive applied. These areas make up a fairly large amount of the adhered tiles, so this is a notable change.

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/9277fa7f-fd32-4c90-8327-c48fd2378a08-medium.png)Tiles replaced on Ship 29's forward dome external stringers.  \|  Photo courtesy of [BocaChicaGal](https://twitter.com/BocaChicaGal) for [NASASpaceflight](https://twitter.com/NASASpaceflight)

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/33142acd-9d98-4f1d-9477-dd1204496af4-medium.png)Tiles replaced on Ship 29's aft dome external stringers.  \|  Photo courtesy of [BocaChicaGal](https://twitter.com/BocaChicaGal) for [NASASpaceflight](https://twitter.com/NASASpaceflight)

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/057570bc-cb39-4b35-bc0a-fd7f5ed17211-medium.jpg)Untiled external stringer bands on Ship 27.  \|  Photo courtesy of [Kevin Randolph](https://twitter.com/CosmicalChief) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang)

##### Flap and Body Tiles Inspected

In addition to the adhesive work, SpaceX has put a lot of effort into ensuring that Ship 29's tiles are seated correctly, with a lot of focus on the flap surfaces. These were specifically observed shedding tiles on Ship 28 during Flight 3, so it makes sense why they received a lot of attention.

![FlapTileReplace.png](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/dba832df-745e-4d2d-9488-3fae3dadcc67-large.png)Tiles replaced on one of Ship 29's forward flaps.  \|  Photo courtesy of [BocaChicaGal](https://twitter.com/BocaChicaGal) for [NASASpaceflight](https://twitter.com/NASASpaceflight)

##### Horizontal Seam Removed

In their effort to reduce the number of horizontal seams in the heatshield, the transition to smaller tiles on the hull has been moved to the existing section weld on the aft section, removing a seam on the mid-LOX barrel.

![TileSeam.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/d29e20ac-eeca-464f-a951-9d0681b37167-large.jpg)Tile seam removed.  \|  Ship 28 (Left) and Ship 29 (Right)  \|  Photos courtesy of [RGV Aerial Photography](https://twitter.com/RGVaerialphotos)

## Super Heavy Booster 11

Super Heavy Booster 11 has some more noticeable changes on it from the outside, but the list isn't super large, or rather, the publically known list isn't super large.

![B11OLM.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/606dee86-da91-4561-9382-632e74357e7a-large.jpg)Booster 11 on the Orbital Launch Mount.  \|  Photo courtesy of [SpaceX](https://twitter.com/SpaceX)

#### Booster - Vented Interstage

##### Fourth Alignment Wedge Added

You may be aware that the three clamps holding the Ship and Booster together have a wedge-shaped housing around them to guide the Ship. and

On Booster 11, a fourth one of these has been added. However, this additional wedge does not have a clamp inside of it and therefore is not related to holding the stages together.

It's likely that this is for alignment of the stages during stacking operations, as SpaceX has seemingly been having continued issues with integration on the launch pad.

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/b8f6809d-3c8b-4ce5-8b09-44e1b6f2e90a-medium.jpg)An extra alignment wedge on Booster 11's hot staging ring.  \|  Photo courtesy of [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics/) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang)

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/ffd27862-be4e-452d-88cf-31ee714184c6-medium.jpg)Additional wedge **with no** clamp on the inside.  \|  Photo courtesy of [RGV Aerial Photography](https://twitter.com/RGVaerialphotos)

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/49e8079a-1471-499d-b0f7-ab6718431926-medium.jpg)Reference image of a wedge **with** aclamp on the inside.  \|  Photo courtesy of [BocaChicaGal](https://twitter.com/BocaChicaGal) for [NASASpaceflight](https://twitter.com/NASASpaceflight)

##### Thing Beside Alignment Wedge

A... thing... has been added beside the additional alignment wedge mentioned above. It seems to have a dome-shaped component on the top of it, but it really isn't clear what it is.

This may be a camera facing upwards or some type of sensor, but these are really just unfounded guesses. It is unclear if this is related to SpaceX's decision to jettison the hot staging ring on the flight test.

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/7d2e23e8-88fc-4ae5-8091-3f23dbe31d4f-medium.jpg)A new addition on Booster 11's hot staging ring.  \|  Photo courtesy of [BocaChicaGal](https://twitter.com/BocaChicaGal) for [NASASpaceflight](https://twitter.com/NASASpaceflight)

![](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/ffd27862-be4e-452d-88cf-31ee714184c6-medium.jpg)The thing, as seen on the side of the Booster.  \|  Photo courtesy of [RGV Aerial Photography](https://twitter.com/RGVaerialphotos)

##### Engine Chill Pipe Shortened

The engine chill downpipes from the Ship stage have been shortened significantly compared to previous Boosters. It's unclear why this change was made, but it should function the same as before.

![ECPipe11.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/f9c54c72-7645-4056-b158-07fb630d0e01-large.jpg)Shortened engine chill downpipe.  \|  Booster 10 (Left) and Booster 11 (Right)  \|  Photos courtesy of [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang) (Left) and [RGV Aerial Photography](https://twitter.com/RGVaerialphotos) (Right)

#### Booster - Vehicle Structure

##### Grid Fin Socket Stringer Doublers

Another change noted between Booster 10 and Booster 11 is the implementation of new supports beneath the grid fin sockets. The stringers underneath each round cutout have had doublers added over the existing sets, effectively doubling the material thickness below them.

![StringerDoublersGridsB.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/158ced79-9477-486d-ae7c-8c9d5952d53b-large.jpg)Stinger doublers added near the grid fin sockets.  \|  Booster 10 (Left) and Booster 11 (Right)  \|  Photos courtesy of [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics/) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang)

##### Grid Fin Socket Plate Doublers

On the upper portion of the grid fin socket frame, they've similarly added two small "fingers", increasing the thickness of that portion and likely alleviating stress in that region.

![DoublersGrids.webp](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/2ffef542-2cb2-4fe8-8fb5-0702e7028a4d-large.webp)The upper portion of the surrounding frame.  \|  Booster 10 (Left) and Booster 11 (Right)  \|  Photos courtesy of [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics/) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang)

##### Vent Doublers Revised for Methane Tank

A revised doubler plate over the vent openings was introduced, which features a reduced amount of material and likely an easier installation. Currently, this design is only on the liquid methane tank.

![VentDoubler.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/ddad385a-6fcf-418a-8da8-4a8afbddd7fd-large.jpg)Revised doubler design.  \|  Booster 8 (Left) and Booster 11 (Right)  \|  Photos courtesy of [RGV Aerial Photography](https://twitter.com/RGVaerialphotos) (Left) and [Kevin Randolph](https://twitter.com/CosmicalChief) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang) (Right)

##### Methane Tank Internal Stringers Increased

Booster 11 saw an increase in stringers in the methane tank with about 72 columns of internal stringers, which is an increase from 24 on previous boosters. This will give the vehicle more rigidity while depressurized.

![InternalStringers.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/3ddd4a62-ec93-4d30-9121-7ffc8e909e17-large.jpg)Updated stringer layout.  \|   Booster 10 (Left) and Booster 11 (Right)  \|   Photos courtesy of [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang) (Left) and [RGV Aerial Photography](https://twitter.com/RGVaerialphotos) (Right)

##### Cowbell Diverters Reinforced

The cowbell deflectors over four of the liquid oxygen tank vents have been reinforced, with additional plating along the length of them connected to the hull of the Booster.

![CowbellChange.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/af177b84-1b94-4478-b42d-36e47cebe83a-large.jpg)Updated cowbell deflector.  \|  Booster 10 (Left) and Booster 11 (Right)  \|  Photos courtesy of [John Cargile](https://twitter.com/GroundTruthPics/) for [WAI Media](https://twitter.com/FelixSchlang)

##### New LOX Tank Stringer Doublers

At the top of the aft tank #5 section, Booster 11 features numerous large doublers over the internal stringers, which are not in other areas and were not on previous vehicles.

![InternalStringersDoublers.jpg](https://api.ringwatchers.com/images/2a74d645-89b6-4654-b67b-4599e26f688e-large.jpg)New stringer doublers.  \|  Booster 10 (Left) and Booster 11 (Right)  \|  Photos courtesy of [RGV Aerial Photography](https://twitter.com/RGVaerialphotos)

## Maybe This Time?

The boxes for Starship's flight objectives are steadily being ticked off, and SpaceX's hardware-rich approach results in a lot of vehicles being produced. While this rocket might not be the most upgraded we've seen, there certainly are more in the pipeline, and we look forward to taking a closer look at those.

> Upgrades derived from the \[third\] flight test will debut on the next launch from Starbase on Flight 4, as we turn our focus from achieving orbit to demonstrating the ability to return and reuse Starship and Super Heavy.

\-\- " [Lessons Learned from Flight 3](https://www.spacex.com/updates/#flight-3-report)", SpaceX Press Release, 24 May 2024
