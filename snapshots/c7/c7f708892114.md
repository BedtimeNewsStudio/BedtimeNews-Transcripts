**Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology** (also known as **TJHSST, Thomas Jefferson, or TJ**) is a magnet high school in [Fairfax County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County,_Virginia "Fairfax County, Virginia"), United States, operated by [Fairfax County Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Public_Schools "Fairfax County Public Schools"). The school occupies the building of the previous [Thomas Jefferson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_High_School_(1964%E2%80%931987) "Thomas Jefferson High School (1964–1987)"), constructed in 1964. A [selective admissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_school "Selective school") program was initiated in 1985 through the cooperation of state and county governments and corporate sponsorship from the defense and technology industries. It is one of 18 Virginia [Governor's Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor's_Schools_(Virginia) "Governor's Schools (Virginia)"), and a founding member of the National Consortium for Specialized Secondary Schools of Mathematics, Science and Technology.

Attendance at the school is open to students in six local jurisdictions based on academic achievement described in the Student Portrait Sheet—a compilation of 4 essays, problem-solving skills—assessed by the Problem Solving Essay, an unweighted grade-point average consisting of 7th grade final grades—8th grade first quarter grades—and summer grades, and socio-economic background.[&#8202;](#ref-back-admissions-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-admissions) Before the 2020–21 school year, the admissions process also involved a mathematics, reading, and science exam.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Natanson-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-Natanson)

## Administration

The current principal of Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology is Michael Mukai.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)

## History

### 20th century

[Thomas Jefferson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_High_School_(1964%E2%80%931987) "Thomas Jefferson High School (1964–1987)") was constructed and opened in 1964.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Baye-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-Baye)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) Fairfax County Public Schools' superintendent William J. Burkholder and his staff began working on the idea of a science high school in 1983 with advice from the superintendent's business/advisory council.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henderson-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-Henderson) Burkholder announced the plans for the magnet school in January 1984.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Latimer-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-Latimer) The school board chose Thomas Jefferson High School as the location for the new magnet school in June 1984[&#8202;](#ref-back-Painton-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-Painton) and approved the funding in February 1985.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Carton-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-Carton)

The school was originally intended to only serve [Fairfax County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County,_Virginia "Fairfax County, Virginia") students, but after Virginia governor [Charles S. Robb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_S._Robb "Charles S. Robb") chose Fairfax County as the location of a regional science and technology school, the school board voted to accept the funding from the state and allow students from [Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia "Arlington County, Virginia"), [Loudoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudoun_County,_Virginia "Loudoun County, Virginia"), and [Prince William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_County,_Virginia "Prince William County, Virginia") counties and from the Cities of [Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia "Fairfax, Virginia") and [Falls Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church,_Virginia "Falls Church, Virginia") to attend as well.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Painton2-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-Painton2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Painton3-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-Painton3) The business community played a significant role in the creation of the school, providing around $3 million in contributions and advice on the school's curriculum.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sugawara-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-Sugawara) Hazleton Laboratories (now [Fortrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortrea "Fortrea")), [Honeywell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell "Honeywell"), [AT&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T "AT&T"), [Dominion Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Energy "Dominion Energy"), [Sony Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Corporation "Sony Corporation"), [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard "Hewlett-Packard"), [Xerox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerox "Xerox"), and other companies made contributions in equipment or finances to the school before it opened.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Blechman-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-Blechman)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henderson-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-Henderson) Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology opened in fall 1985 with 400 ninth-graders and 125 seniors who were selected from 1,200 applicants.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Blechman-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-Blechman)

### 21st century

From 2013 to 2017, the school underwent renovations, adding additional research labs, internet cafes, three-dimensional art galleries, a black box theater, and a dome reminiscent of President [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson")'s [Monticello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monticello "Monticello").[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) The renovations cost $90 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)

Admission statistics

The school is part of the [Fairfax County Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Public_Schools "Fairfax County Public Schools") system of [Fairfax County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County,_Virginia "Fairfax County, Virginia"). Students from Fairfax, [Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia "Arlington County, Virginia"), [Loudoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudoun_County,_Virginia "Loudoun County, Virginia"), and [Prince William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_County,_Virginia "Prince William County, Virginia") counties and from the Cities of [Fairfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia "Fairfax, Virginia") and [Falls Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_Church,_Virginia "Falls Church, Virginia") are eligible for admission.[&#8202;](#ref-back-eligible-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-eligible) Students must be enrolled in Algebra 1 or a higher level mathematics class in 8th grade and have a minimum GPA of 3.5 to be eligible.[&#8202;](#ref-back-eligible-1)[\[36\]](#refnote-eligible)

The admissions process is based on grade point average, a mathematics or science related problem solving essay, a student portrait sheet demonstrating skills and character, and details about a student's socioeconomic background including whether they are economically disadvantaged, a special education student, or an English language learner.[&#8202;](#ref-back-admissions-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-admissions) Each public school is allocated a number of seats equal to 1.5% of that school's 8th grade student population; the remaining seats are not allocated and offered to the highest evaluated remaining students.[&#8202;](#ref-back-admissions-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-admissions) During the admissions process, students are identified only by a number; admissions officers do not know their race, ethnicity, sex, or name.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)

Before the 2020–21 school year, the admissions process also included a mathematics, reading, and science exam.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Natanson-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-Natanson)

## Controversies

In the 2020s, Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology (TJHSST) underwent three major controversies regarding its admissions process,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Natanson-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-Natanson) intentionally delayed distribution of National Merit awards,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-:0) and its relationship with Chinese Communist Party-affiliated entities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-:1) Amidst these controversies, Principal Ann Bonitatibus resigned in October 2024;[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41) she stated in an email to parents that she had "pursued and accepted" a "promotion" to the [Fairfax County Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfax_County_Public_Schools "Fairfax County Public Schools")' Human Resources department, which drew widespread skepticism.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42) She was replaced by Michael Mukai, a TJHSST alumnus, on January 9, 2025.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43)

The admissions process and the demographics of the student body it produces, in particular the relative under-representation of [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") and [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans "Hispanic and Latino Americans") students, as well as attempts to diversify the student body, have been a source of controversy throughout the school's recent history.

#### Affirmative action program

After the school's early graduating classes included relatively few black and Hispanic students, FCPS created a race-based [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action "Affirmative action") program to admit more black and Hispanic students.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kahn-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-Kahn) The program was in effect for the admissions process for the graduating classes of 1997 through 2002; the county ended it because of legal challenges to similar programs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kahn-1)[\[46\]](#refnote-Kahn) Following the end of this program, the share of black and Hispanic students at the school decreased from 9.4 percent in 1997–98 to 3.5 percent in 2003–04.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kahn-2)[\[46\]](#refnote-Kahn) Black and Hispanic students remained significantly under-represented at the school through the 2000s and 2010s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47)[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)

#### Department of Education complaint

In 2012, a civil rights complaint against the school was filed with the [U.S. Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Education "United States Department of Education") Office of Civil Rights by _Coalition of the Silence,_ an advocacy group led by former county School Board member Tina Hone, and the Fairfax chapter of the [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Colored_People "National Association for the Advancement of Colored People"), alleging that it discriminated against black, Hispanic, and [disabled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability "Disability") students.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49)[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50) In response, the Office of Civil Rights, in September 2012, opened an investigation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51)[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-52)

#### 2020 Admissions policy changes

In 2020, the school board made a number of significant changes to the admissions process meant to increase the ratio of black and Hispanic students admitted. These included the elimination of the $100 application fee; the increase of the number of admitted students from around 480 to 550; the elimination of an entrance exam; the allocation of seats to each middle school equal to 1.5% of their 8th grade student population; and the addition of "experience factors" including whether students are economically disadvantaged, English language learners, or special education students.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-:2) Following these changes, the proportion of black and Hispanic students admitted increased from 4.52% to 18.36% while the proportion of [Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans "Asian Americans") decreased from 73.05% to 54.36%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-admissions2021-1)[\[30\]](#refnote-admissions2021) The proportion of female students admitted also increased, from 41.80% to 46.00%,[&#8202;](#ref-back-admissions2021-2)[\[30\]](#refnote-admissions2021) and to 55.45% the next year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-admissions2022-1)[\[31\]](#refnote-admissions2022)

#### Coalition for TJ v. Fairfax County School Board

In March 2021, the Coalition for TJ, an advocacy group opposed to the changes and represented by the [Pacific Legal Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Legal_Foundation "Pacific Legal Foundation"), sued the Fairfax County school board, alleging that the changes to the admissions process discriminated against Asian Americans.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-65)

In February 2022, judge [Claude M. Hilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_M._Hilton "Claude M. Hilton") of the [United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Virginia "United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia") ruled in _[Coalition for TJ v. Fairfax County School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_TJ_v._Fairfax_County_School_Board "Coalition for TJ v. Fairfax County School Board")_ in the Coalition for TJ's favor and ordered the school to return to the previous admissions process.[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-66) The school board appealed the decision to the [United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Fourth_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit") and in March 2022 that court issued a stay on the order that allowed the school to continue the new admissions process while the case was pending.[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67) The [Supreme Court of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") rejected a request to vacate the stay in April 2022.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Howe-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-Howe) The case was heard in the court of appeals on September 16, 2022, and decided on May 23, 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4th-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-4th) The Fourth Circuit, by a 2 to 1 vote, reversed the district court and restored the new admission plan. The Fourth Circuit's decision was appealed to the Supreme Court, but the Supreme Court rejected to review the case on February 20, 2024 with Justice [Clarence Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Thomas "Clarence Thomas") and Justice [Samuel Alito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Alito "Samuel Alito") dissenting from the denial.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-70)

#### Virginia Attorney General investigation

An investigation from the office of then-Virginia Attorney General, [Jason Miyares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Miyares "Jason Miyares"), found that the school had violated the rights of, and discriminated against Asian American students. It published internal messages from the Fairfax County School Board such as:[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-:5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-:6)

- “there has been an anti asian feel underlying some of this, hate to say it lol,”
- "Asians hate us."
- that Asian students were “discriminated against in this process,”

The ultimate determination was that "\[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[t\]he revised admissions policy was a deliberate, race-based system designed to help some and disadvantage others. The otherwise qualified students not admitted to TJ lost out because they were Asian American...the Office of the Attorney General’s Office of Civil Rights has found reasonable cause to believe that FCPS discriminated against Asian American students on the basis of race in violation of the Virginia Human Rights Act and Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964."[\[71\]](#refnote-:5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-1)[\[72\]](#refnote-:6)

### Merit award controversy

In December 2022, it was reported that during the previous five years,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-:0) some students at the school who had been named [National Merit Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Merit_Scholarship "National Merit Scholarship") Commended Scholars had not been notified of their achievement until several months after the school was given the awards to distribute to the students. This meant that it was too late for the students to include the award with their college applications. A lawyer named Shawnna Yashar, whose son was one of the students at the school whose commendation information had not been reported by the school, said, "Keeping these certificates from students is theft by the state."[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-73)[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-74) School officials stated that the issue was a "one-time human error."[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-75) It prompted then-Governor [Glenn Youngkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Youngkin "Glenn Youngkin") to propose legislation mandating immediate notification of such opportunities to parents and students.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[40\]](#refnote-:3)

### Relationship with the Chinese government

In March 2023, the conservative group Parents Defending Education published the findings of an investigation showing that a non-profit that fundraises for TJ had received over $1 million in donations since 2014 from sources affiliated with the Chinese government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-76) Further investigation revealed that over $3.6 million had been donated.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-:7) In return, TJ staff handed over significant amounts of intellectual property, including blueprints floor plans of the school, curricula, lab photographs, and thumb drives with Senior Student Research projects. According to [The National Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Review "National Review"), "Senior research projects are a TJ student’s crowning achievement...Sometimes, the projects are done in conjunction with defense contractors or 'mentors' in the \[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[D.C.-Maryland-Virginia\] area...TJ says on its website that students have partnered with George Washington University, the National Institutes of Health, and K2M. Chinese officials requested, and gained access to, examples of past student research projects, without student consent. In 2017, one administrator asked TJ to 'put \[senior research projects\] on a thumb drive,' as 'Dropbox is not accessible in China.'" At least twenty "Thomas schools", replicas of TJ, have been opened in China.[\[77\]](#refnote-:7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-78)

## Curriculum

TJ's curriculum is focused on college preparation, with an emphasis on science and technology.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Profile-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-Profile)

All 9th graders participate in IBET, a program that integrates biology, English, and technology. It is designed to introduce students to the academic rigor of TJ while fostering connection.The program was formerly named IBEST, and included statistics, until its recent removal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-80)

Additionally, all seniors are required to participate in TJ's senior research program. Students typically work in one of nine specialized labs: Astronomy, Biotechnology, Chemical Analysis, Computer Systems, Engineering, Mobile & Web Application Development, Neuroscience, Oceanography, and Quantum Physics & Optics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-:4) A mentorship program is also available, where students can work with industry professionals to conduct research outside of the typical school environment. This program culminates in tjSTAR, a symposium where students present their final research.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[81\]](#refnote-:4)

### TJ3Sat and TJREVERB projects

The Systems Engineering Course designed and built a [CubeSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat "CubeSat") which was launched on November 19, 2013, from [Wallops Flight Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallops_Flight_Facility "Wallops Flight Facility") in Virginia. [Orbital Sciences Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_Sciences_Corporation "Orbital Sciences Corporation") donated the CubeSat Kit to the school on December 6, 2006, and provided the launch for the satellite. After a successful launch at 8:15PM EST, TJ3SAT became the first satellite launched into space that was built by high school students.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-82) The launched satellite contained a 4-watt transmitter operating on [amateur radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio "Amateur radio") frequencies, and a [text-to-speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-speech "Text-to-speech") module to allow it to broadcast [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII "ASCII")-encoded messages sent to it from Jefferson.

TJREVERB, a 2U CubeSat, is the school's second CubeSat mission. After a 6-year period of planning, building, and testing from 2016 to 2022 that was interrupted by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Virginia "COVID-19 pandemic in Virginia"), REVERB was launched aboard [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX")'s [CRS-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRS-26 "CRS-26") on November 26, 2022, at 2:20PM EST.[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-83)[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-84) REVERB was deployed from the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") on December 29, 2022, via [Nanoracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoracks_CubeSat_Deployer "Nanoracks CubeSat Deployer").[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-85)[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-86) The satellite is being located through crowdsourcing, with help from [AMSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMSAT "AMSAT").[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-87) It will test the [Iridium satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iridium_satellite "Iridium satellite") radio[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-88) and connect to the students' ground station through email.

### Computer Systems Lab

The school's computer systems lab is one of the few high school computing facilities with a [supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer "Supercomputer"). In 1988, a team from the school won an [ETA-10P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA10 "ETA10") supercomputer in the SuperQuest competition, a national science competition for high school students.[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-89) The ETA-10P was damaged by a roof leak in the 1990s. [Cray Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cray_Inc. "Cray Inc.") donated a new [SV1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cray_SV1 "Cray SV1") supercomputer, known as Seymour, to the school on December 4, 2002, which is on display as of 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-90)

The lab also supported a number of [Sun Microsystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Microsystems "Sun Microsystems") thin clients for use by students enrolled in [AP Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Computer_Science "AP Computer Science"). In 2008, the school received a grant from Sun Microsystems for $388,048, which was student-written.[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-91) The Syslab was given 7 Sun workstations, 12 Sun servers, and 145 Sun Rays for distribution throughout the school. These were placed in the existing AP Computer Science Lab and the science classrooms, support backend services, and serve as kiosks placed around the school for guests, students, and faculty. However, the Sun Rays were taken out of the AP Computer Science Lab due to teachers' objections. By 2014, the Sun Ray clients were decommissioned, and replaced with Linux-based thin clients running [LTSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_Terminal_Server_Project "Linux Terminal Server Project").[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-92)[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-93)[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-94)[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-95)

## Awards and recognition

In 2021 and 2022, the _[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report "U.S. News & World Report")_ ranked TJ as the best overall high school in the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-usnews2021-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-usnews2021)[&#8202;](#ref-back-usnews-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-usnews) It was previously ranked fourth in 2020,[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-97) tenth in 2019,[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-98) and sixth in 2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-99) In 2016, the school placed first in _[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek "Newsweek")_'s annual "America's Top High Schools" rankings for the third consecutive year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-100)[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-101)[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-102) The average [SAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT "SAT") score for the graduating class of 2020 was 1528 and the average [ACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_(test) "ACT (test)") score was 34.5.[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-103)

The school had 14 [Intel Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Science_Talent_Search "Regeneron Science Talent Search") Semifinalists in 2007,[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-104) 15 in 2009,[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-105) and 13 in 2010.[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-106) In 2024, 7 were named.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-107)

In 2007, for schools with more than 800 students in grades 10–12, TJ was cited as having the highest-performing [Calculus BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Calculus "AP Calculus"), [AP Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Chemistry "AP Chemistry"), [AP French Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_French_Language_and_Culture "AP French Language and Culture"), [AP U.S. Government and Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_United_States_Government_and_Politics "AP United States Government and Politics"), and [AP U.S. History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_United_States_History "AP United States History") courses among all schools worldwide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-108) In 2014, 3864 [AP Exams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Placement_exams "Advanced Placement exams") were taken by students; over 97% earned a score of 3, 4, or 5.

President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") signed the [America Invents Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leahy-Smith_America_Invents_Act "Leahy-Smith America Invents Act") into law on September 16, 2011, at the school. The law was made to reform [U.S. patent laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_patent_law "United States patent law").[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-109)

In 1997, 2000, 2013, 2017, and 2025, the [wind ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_ensemble "Wind ensemble") of the school was among fifteen high-school bands invited to the Music for All National Concert Band Festival in Indianapolis.[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-110)
