# arrest warrant

An [arrest](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/arrest) warrant is a document issued by a [judge](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/judge) or [magistrate](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/magistrate) that authorizes the police to take someone accused of a crime into [custody](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/custody). An arrest [warrant](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/warrant) is issued by the competent authority upon a showing of [probable cause](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/probable_cause), which means a warrant may be issued if a [reasonable person](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/reasonable_person) would believe the information at hand is sufficient to suggest [criminal](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/criminal) activities.

Arrest warrants serve the purpose of protecting people from [unlawful](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/unlawful) arrests under the [Fourth Amendment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fourth_amendment). The warrant also gives an [actual notice](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/actual_notice) to the person or persons being [arrested](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/arrest) about the [charges](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/charge) pressed against them.

An arrest warrant is preferred, but not required, to make a lawful arrest.

In [*City of Saint Paul v. Adolph Toblera*](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_case?case=11561345231837123894&q=CITY+OF+ST.+PAUL,+Respondent,+v.+Adolph+T.+TOBLER&hl=en&as_sdt=6,33), the Supreme Court of Minnesota defined an arrest warrant as “a judicial process by which, in the name of the state, a defendant is brought before the court to answer a criminal charge made against him.”

In [*Bienenstock v. McCoy et. al.*](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5587666/bienenstock-v-mccoy/?q=Bienenstock+v.+McCoy&type=o&order_by=score+desc&stat_Published=on), the Supreme Court of New York stated that an arrest warrant “is a judicial order or command, in writing, in the name of the people, signed by an authorized judicial officer commanding the arrest of the defendant named for the crime charged therein.”
