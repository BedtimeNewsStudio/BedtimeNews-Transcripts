# NASA Authorization Act of 2010

NASA Authorization Act of 2010

U.S. law

The **NASA Authorization Act of 2010** is a U.S. law authorizing [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") appropriations for [fiscal years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year "Fiscal year") 2011, 2012, 2013 with the same top-line budget values as requested by [US President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama"). It resulted from the [Augustine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_of_United_States_Human_Space_Flight_Plans_Committee "Review of United States Human Space Flight Plans Committee")'s review of then-current crewed space flight plans.

The law supports an overall growth in [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science"), [aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics "Aeronautics"), and [space technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_technology "Space technology") and defines a long-term goal for [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight") to expand a permanent human presence [beyond low Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible_path "Flexible path"). Key objectives of this goal include full utilization of the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") (ISS), determining the ability of humans to live in [outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_space "Outer space") for extended periods of time, maximizing the role of [space exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_exploration "Space exploration") and [space technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_technology "Space technology") in current and future missions, advancing knowledge and inspiring young people into [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education "Higher education"), and building upon international partnerships.

The act was signed by President Obama on October11, 2010. A total of $58 billion in funding is called for, spread across three years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-spacenews-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-spacenews)

## Human spaceflight

In regard to [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight"), the law is intended to:

- Couple human spaceflight efforts to national and global needs and challenges.
- Provide a sustainable [space exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_exploration "Space exploration") program to incorporate new technologies and in-space capabilities.
- Require immediate development of the [Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System "Space Launch System") (SLS) as a follow-on launch vehicle to the Space Shuttle and continued development of a [crew exploration vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_exploration_vehicle "Crew exploration vehicle") to be capable of supporting missions beyond [low Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit "Low Earth orbit") starting in 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-senatebill-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-senatebill)
- Support a sound performance and cost framework by maximizing use, where possible, of the [workforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workforce "Workforce"), assets, and capabilities of the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle"), [Constellation program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation_program "Constellation program"), and other NASA programs.

## Space technology

The law will invest in space technologies and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics "Robotics") capabilities that are tied to the overall [space exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_exploration "Space exploration") framework and support U.S. innovation and competitiveness.

## Commercial cargo and crew

In regard to commercial cargo and crew, the law will:

- Continue to support commercial cargo development and provide additional funds to meet launch infrastructure requirements and accelerate development activity.
- Expand the [Commercial Crew Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Crew_Development "Commercial Crew Development") Program in 2011 for concept development and supporting activities, while requiring a number of studies to ensure effective oversight of the potential initiation of a commercial crew capability procurement program no earlier than 2012.

## International Space Station

In regard to the International Space Station (ISS), the law will:

- Extend the ISS to at least 2020 to support international and commercial collaboration and growth, research, and technology development to maximize the scientific return on the significant investment in the ISS.
- Establish an independent [non-profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit "Non-profit") to work with NASA to fully develop the ISS U.S. segment as a National Laboratory.
- Require an assessment of ISS requirements for parts and equipment needed to ensure its full functionality through 2020.

## Additional "launch on need" Shuttle mission

The law authorizes an additional Space Shuttle flight ( [STS-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-135 "STS-135")), contingent on a safety review, to provide necessary support for the extension of the ISS.

## Science and aeronautics

The law protects a balanced portfolio for NASA, including full funding of [aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics "Aeronautics"), [Earth science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_science "Earth science") and [space science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_science "Space science").

## Education

In regard to education, the law will:

- Support new education initiatives, such as teacher training programs, to reinforce NASA's role in developing a [workforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workforce "Workforce") with strong [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science"), [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology "Technology"), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering "Engineering"), and [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") skills.
- Increase the investment in [NASA EPSCoR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_EPSCoR?action=edit&redlink=1 "NASA EPSCoR") (Experimental Program to Stimulate Competitive Research) and NASA Space Grant program.

## Rescoping and revitalizing institutional capabilities

The law requires NASA to examine alternative [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management "Management") models for NASA's [workforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workforce "Workforce"), centers, and capabilities, while enforcing short-term prohibitions on major center displacements and reductions-in-force until the study is completed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-senate-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-senate)
