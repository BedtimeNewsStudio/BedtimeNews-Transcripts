**KEY FIGURES**

- 3 Central American countries declared El Niño-linked drought emergencies
- 4 named storms so far in the 2026 Atlantic Hurricane Season
- 364K people assisted by UN and partners following June 2026 earthquakes in Venezuela

**CENTRAL AMERICA: DROUGHT**

El Salvador and Panama both declared nationwide states of emergency on 25 August to accelerate drought response through 2027 amid forecasts of a record-strength El Niño by then. Honduras expanded its emergency red alert from 75 to 103 municipalities this week, building on a drought emergency it declared on 19 August. El Salvador logged a 41 per cent rainfall deficit in May and 19 consecutive dry days since 7 August, threatening food security for subsistence farmers in the Dry Corridor. The Panama Canal Authority plans to cut daily vessel transits from 36 to 32 starting 15 September due to lower water levels, adding pressure to global shipping already strained by reduced vessel traffic through the Strait of Hormuz and likely causing cost of living increases with direct and indirect effects on agricultural production and food insecurity.

**COSTA RICA: EARTHQUAKE**

The National Emergency Commission (CNE) activated damage assessment protocols after two moderate earthquakes struck the Central Valley in the early hours of 24 August, with no fatalities or injuries reported. A magnitude 5.4 quake hit at 1:37 a.m. local time, followed two minutes later by a magnitude 4.8 aftershock, both centered in Coronado, northeast of the capital, San José, at shallow depths of 7 and 10 kilometres respectively. The shallow depth amplified the shaking across the Central Valley, causing power outages and disrupted cell service in San José. Damage included a collapsed wall on a home, a fallen utility pole and a landslide on Route 32. The National Seismological Network attributed the activity to a local fault.

**CARIBBEAN: HURRICANE SEASON**

Tropical Storm Dolly formed over the central Atlantic on 27 August, becoming the fourth named storm of the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season after Arthur, Bertha and Cristobal. The storm carried maximum sustained winds of 40 mph and moved westward, with increasing wind shear and dry air forecast to weaken it as it nears the Leeward Islands. No coastal watches or warnings were in effect. Heavy rainfall remains the primary concern rather than wind impacts, particularly in higher terrain prone to flash flooding. The United States’ National Hurricane Center forecasts rain beginning in the Leeward Islands late 29 August into 30 August, before extending to the Virgin Islands, Puerto Rico and Hispaniola on 30 and 31 August.

**VENEZUELA: EARTHQUAKE**

Two months after the 24 June earthquakes, the UN and partners continue scaling up the response alongside authorities, having reached nearly 364,000 people, 54 per cent of them women and girls, with essential services and assistance toward a target of 1.3 million. More than 90 per cent of those reached are concentrated in the seven hardest-hit states, with 107 organizations addressing urgent needs in food security, water and sanitation, health, and shelter. Recent flooding from tropical waves and heavy rainfall has since struck several areas, including the Capital District, leaving six people dead and damaging homes and infrastructure and disrupting transport, with impacts on communities already hosting earthquake-displaced families. Authorities have activated emergency protocols and the National Civil Protection System in response.
