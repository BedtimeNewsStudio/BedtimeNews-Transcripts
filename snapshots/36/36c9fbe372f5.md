_**Mulan**_ is a 2020 American [fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_film "Fantasy film") [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_film "Action film") [drama film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_(film_and_television) "Drama (film and television)") produced by [Walt Disney Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures "Walt Disney Pictures"). Directed by [Niki Caro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Caro "Niki Caro") from a screenplay by [Rick Jaffa, Amanda Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Jaffa_and_Amanda_Silver "Rick Jaffa and Amanda Silver"), Lauren Hynek, and Elizabeth Martin, it is a [live-action adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_remakes_and_adaptations_of_Disney_animated_films "List of remakes and adaptations of Disney animated films") of Disney's 1998 animated film _[Mulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(1998_film) "Mulan (1998 film)")_, itself based on the Chinese folklore story _[Ballad of Mulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hua_Mulan "Hua Mulan")_. The film stars [Liu Yifei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Yifei "Liu Yifei") in the title role, alongside [Donnie Yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Yen "Donnie Yen"), [Tzi Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzi_Ma "Tzi Ma"), [Jason Scott Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Scott_Lee "Jason Scott Lee"), [Yoson An](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoson_An "Yoson An"), [Ron Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Yuan "Ron Yuan"), [Gong Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_Li "Gong Li"), and [Jet Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Li "Jet Li") in supporting roles. In the film, Hua Mulan, the eldest daughter of an honored warrior, masquerades as a man to take her ailing father's place during a general conscription to counter the [Rouran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouran_Khaganate "Rouran Khaganate") army in Imperial China.

Plans for a live-action remake began in 2010 but the project stalled in [development hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_hell "Development hell") for most of the decade. Filming took place in New Zealand and China from August to November 2018. Over the course of production, the film was the subject of several controversies, including changes to the source material and filming in Xinjiang in light of the [persecution of Uyghurs in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Uyghurs_in_China "Persecution of Uyghurs in China") and [Xinjiang internment camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_internment_camps "Xinjiang internment camps"). The film also received criticism for its production team largely consisting of people of non-Chinese descent. This included the hiring of Caro, rather than an Asian director.

_Mulan_ held its world premiere at the [Dolby Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Theatre "Dolby Theatre") in Hollywood on March 9, 2020. The film was originally scheduled for a [wide theatrical release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_release "Wide release") later that month, but was cancelled in the United States after being delayed several times [due to the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_cinema "Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema"). Subsequently, the film was released on September 4, 2020, by [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+ "Disney+") for a [premium fee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_on_demand#Premium "Video on demand") known as [Premier Access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+_Premier_Access "Disney+ Premier Access") in countries where the service had launched.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-6) The film had a traditional theatrical release in countries without Disney+ where theaters had re-opened.

Due to its overall limited theatrical release as a result of the pandemic, the film was a [box-office bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-office_bomb "Box-office bomb"), grossing $69.9 million at the box office against a production budget of $200 million. The film received generally positive reviews from Western, non-Asian critics, who praised the action sequences, costumes, and performances, but criticized the screenplay and editing. It received unfavorable reviews from fans of the original animated film, Chinese diaspora, and Chinese critics, who criticized the character development, its cultural and historical inaccuracies, and its depiction of Chinese people. At the [93rd Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/93rd_Academy_Awards "93rd Academy Awards") and [26th Critics' Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_Critics'_Choice_Awards "26th Critics' Choice Awards"), the film received nominations for its costume design and visual effects, as well as a [Best Special Visual Effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Special_Visual_Effects "BAFTA Award for Best Special Visual Effects") nod at the [74th British Academy Film Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_British_Academy_Film_Awards "74th British Academy Film Awards").

## Plot

In [Imperial China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_China "Imperial China"), [Hua Mulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(Disney_character) "Mulan (Disney character)") is an adventurous and active girl, much to the disappointment of her parents Zhou and Li, who hope that one day she will be wed to a good husband. As a young woman, Mulan is arranged to meet with a [matchmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matchmaking "Matchmaking") to demonstrate her fitness as a future wife, but the matchmaker deems her a disgrace in front of her family after a mishap.

To the north, an imperial outpost is invaded by [Rouran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouran_Khaganate "Rouran Khaganate") warriors, under the leadership of Böri Khan. They are assisted by the witch Xianniang, who uses her magic to pose as a surviving soldier and report the attack to the Emperor of China; he then issues a [conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription") decree ordering every family to contribute one man to fight Khan's forces. Imperial soldiers arrive in Mulan's village to enlist recruits and Zhou is forced to pledge his service as he has no sons, immediately falling over in front of the soldiers due to his crippled leg. Realizing that her father has no chance of survival, Mulan flees with his armor, horse, and sword to join in his place. Mulan arrives at the training camp, which is run by Commander Tung, an old comrade of Zhou. Alongside dozens of other inexperienced recruits, she ultimately becomes a trained soldier under his tutelage without exposing her true identity.

Böri Khan's army continues to advance, forcing Tung to end training early and send his battalion to fight. Mulan, separated from the battalion, is confronted by Xianniang, who mocks her for pretending to be a man. She attempts to kill Mulan, but her attacks are stopped by the leather with which Mulan's chest had been bound to hide her identity. Mulan removes her male disguise, returning to the battle just as the Rourans begin attacking her fellow troops with a [trebuchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebuchet "Trebuchet"). Mulan uses discarded helmets and her archery skills to maneuver the trebuchet into firing on a snowy mountain, triggering an avalanche that buries the Rourans.

Mulan rides back to camp and rescues Chen Honghui, a soldier she befriended in camp. Mulan decides to reveal her gender to Commander Tung, who expels her from the army. Later, she is confronted by Xianniang, who reveals that she was also shunned by her people and fights for Böri Khan only because he would treat her as an equal. She also reveals that the attacks on the outposts have been a diversion, as Böri Khan's true plan is to capture and execute the Emperor for having his father killed. Risking execution, Mulan returns to her battalion to warn them of the impending capture. The soldiers she befriended stand up for her, and Tung decides to believe her, and allows her to lead a unit to the Emperor's palace.

Xianniang uses her magic to take the image of the Imperial Chancellor, and persuades the Emperor to accept Böri Khan's challenge to single combat, while removing the city guards from their posts. The guards are murdered, and the Rourans prepare to burn the Emperor alive. Mulan's unit distracts the Rourans while Mulan goes to save the Emperor. Böri Khan tries to snipe her with an arrow, but Xianniang, sympathetic to Mulan and disenchanted with the Khan, transforms into a hawk and sacrifices herself by intercepting the arrow. Mulan kills Böri Khan, but not before he disarms her and destroys her father's sword. She frees the Emperor, who offers to let her join his personal guard. She declines the offer to return to her village.

Mulan is reunited with her family. An emissary from the Emperor, under the leadership of Commander Tung, arrives to present Mulan with a new sword, while making a personal request that she join the Imperial Army as an officer.

## Production

### Development

As early as 2010, [Walt Disney Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures "Walt Disney Pictures") had expressed interest in a [live-action adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_remakes_and_adaptations_of_Disney_animated_films "List of remakes and adaptations of Disney animated films") of the 1998 animated film _[Mulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(1998_film) "Mulan (1998 film)")_ with international Chinese star [Zhang Ziyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Ziyi "Zhang Ziyi"), and [Chuck Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Russell "Chuck Russell") chosen as the director. That version of the project never came to fruition.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-26)

On March 30, 2015, _[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ reported that Disney had restarted development of the live-action adaptation with [Chris Bender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Bender_(film_producer) "Chris Bender (film producer)") and [J. C. Spink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._C._Spink "J. C. Spink") producing, while Elizabeth Martin and Lauren Hynek would be writing the screenplay.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-DevelopingMulan-27) On October 4, 2016, it was announced that [Rick Jaffa and Amanda Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Jaffa_and_Amanda_Silver "Rick Jaffa and Amanda Silver") would rewrite the script, combining the Chinese ballad and the 1998 animated film, while Jason T. Reed would be producing the film along with Bender and Jake Weiner.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-28) On February 27, 2020, Reed said that Mulan's sidekick from the original film, [Mushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushu "Mushu"), was removed due to the character's negative reception in China.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-MulanOnSet-12) Reed also said that in addition to drawing from the animated film and the original text, the filmmakers viewed adaptations of _Ballad of Mulan_ produced in China for cinema and television. He stated that since "\[t\]he traditional Disney audience and the diaspora Asian audience viewed the movie in one way, and the traditional Chinese in China audience viewed a slightly different way," the filmmakers "dug in to try and make sure that \[they are\] addressing both of those audiences in a thoughtful way."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-MulanOnSet-12)

### Casting

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Liu_Yifei_at_the_2016_BAZAAR_Stars%E2%80%99_Charity_Night.jpg/500px-Liu_Yifei_at_the_2016_BAZAAR_Stars%E2%80%99_Charity_Night.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Liu_Yifei_at_the_2016_BAZAAR_Stars%E2%80%99_Charity_Night.jpg) Yifei Liu plays Mulan in the remake.

Several 2010s Hollywood films were accused of [whitewashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewashing_in_film "Whitewashing in film"), and as soon as _The Hollywood Reporter_ wrote that Disney was making a live-action adaptation of the 1998 film, _Mulan_ came under intense scrutiny. An [online petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_petition "Online petition") titled "Tell Disney You Don't Want A Whitewashed Mulan!" received more than 100,000 signatures.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-BBC-7) On October 4, 2016, Disney announced that a global search for a Chinese actress to portray the title role was underway.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-FindingMulan-29) A team of casting directors visited five continents and saw nearly 1,000 candidates for the role with criteria that required credible martial arts skills, the ability to speak clear English, and star quality.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-YifeiCast-30)

On November 29, 2017, Chinese-American actress [Liu Yifei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Yifei "Liu Yifei") was cast in the film to portray the titular role of Mulan. Many celebrated this as a win for diversity in Disney films.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-BBC-7) Further cast announcements for [Donnie Yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Yen "Donnie Yen"), [Gong Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_Li "Gong Li"), [Jet Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Li "Jet Li"), and Xana Tang were made in April 2018,[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Deadline1-11)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-HollywoodReporter3-14) [Utkarsh Ambudkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utkarsh_Ambudkar "Utkarsh Ambudkar") and [Ron Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Yuan "Ron Yuan") in May,[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-AmbudkarYuanCast-18) [Yoson An](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoson_An "Yoson An") and [Chum Ehelepola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chum_Ehelepola "Chum Ehelepola") in June,[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-HollywoodReporter2-15) [Jason Scott Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Scott_Lee "Jason Scott Lee") in July,[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-HollywoodReporter1-13) [Tzi Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzi_Ma "Tzi Ma"), [Rosalind Chao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalind_Chao "Rosalind Chao"), [Cheng Pei-Pei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_Pei-Pei "Cheng Pei-Pei"), [Nelson Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Lee "Nelson Lee"), [Jimmy Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wong "Jimmy Wong") and [Doua Moua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doua_Moua "Doua Moua") in August,[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Deadline3-23)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Deadline4-20) and [Chen Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Tang_(actor) "Chen Tang (actor)") in September.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Deadline5-21)

### Director

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/NikiCaro_%28cropped%29.jpg/330px-NikiCaro_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NikiCaro_(cropped).jpg) [Niki Caro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Caro "Niki Caro"), the director of _Mulan_

Disney originally sought an Asian director. They first considered [Ang Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Lee "Ang Lee"), the Taiwanese-born director and two-time [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") winner for [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director "Academy Award for Best Director"). _The Hollywood Reporter_ mentioned that Lee was approached but declined, on October 12, 2016.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-LeePass-31) According to the report, published on November 22, 2016, he said that he would like to see an Asian director leading the film, but he had to decline because he was still obliged to promote his film _[Billy Lynn's Long Halftime Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Lynn's_Long_Halftime_Walk_(film) "Billy Lynn's Long Halftime Walk (film)")_.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-LeeSaid-32) Next, Disney met with [Jiang Wen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiang_Wen "Jiang Wen") for the position; finally on February 14, 2017, New Zealander [Niki Caro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Caro "Niki Caro") was hired as the director of the film, which made _Mulan_ the second Disney film with a female director and a budget above $100 million, following _[A Wrinkle in Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Wrinkle_in_Time_(2018_film) "A Wrinkle in Time (2018 film)")_.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-NikiHelm-33)

### Filming

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") began on August 13, 2018, at locations in New Zealand and China and [wrapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrap_(filmmaking) "Wrap (filmmaking)") on November 25, 2018.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-34)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Deadline3-23) According to actress [Gong Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_Li "Gong Li"), the film's production budget was $290–300 million due to the "massive and... unprecedented scale" of the sets,[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-GongLiBudget-35)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-GongLiBudget2-36) although the actual figure was later reported to be $200 million.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-CNNbudget-3)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Ford-37) The budget makes _Mulan_ the most expensive film ever directed by a woman.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-38)

### Post-production

The visual effects were provided by [Sony Pictures Imageworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Imageworks "Sony Pictures Imageworks"), [Weta Digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weta_Digital "Weta Digital"), [Framestore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framestore "Framestore"), and [Image Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_Engine "Image Engine"). Seth Maury and [Anders Langlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Langlands "Anders Langlands") served as visual effects supervisors and [Sean Andrew Faden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Andrew_Faden "Sean Andrew Faden") served as production supervisor.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-39)

## Marketing

On July 7, 2019, the official teaser trailer and the official teaser poster were released during the broadcast of the [2019 FIFA Women's World Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Final "2019 FIFA Women's World Cup Final").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Liptak-19) The teaser trailer was viewed 175.1 million times in its first 24 hours, including 52 million from China, making it the seventh most viewed trailer in that time period.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-48) On September 30, an exclusive photo featuring Liu as a soldier in the Imperial Army was released on _[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine) "Empire (film magazine)")_'s upcoming _[Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Rise_of_Skywalker "Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker")_ issue.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-49) On October 24, a prequel novel written by writer [Grace Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Lin "Grace Lin") was announced along with a preview being made available online. Entitled _Mulan: Before the Sword_, the novel was released on February 11, 2020.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-50) On November 4, a video clip filmed during a Toho cinema preview was leaked online, featuring new shots of Mulan as a warrior battling fiercely and taking off her hair band.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-51) On November 6, Liu was featured as one of the 25 stars of Next Gen Talent 2019 nominated by _[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ with two new studio stills released concurrently.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-52) As a result of delaying its original March 2020 release, film executives estimated that each time the film was delayed it cost Disney between $200,000 and $400,000 in marketing fees, a figure that could reach $5 million if it was moved out of the summer season altogether.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-53) _Variety_ estimated that Disney spent around $50 million on global advertisement for the film, with _Deadline Hollywood_ attributing $35 million of the marketing budget to TV ads.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Rubin-Aug2020-54)[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-55)

A tie-in novelization of the film written by Elizabeth Rudnick was published by [Disney Publishing Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Publishing_Worldwide "Disney Publishing Worldwide") on February 11, 2020.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-56)

### Theatrical and streaming

_Mulan_'s world premiere was held at the [Dolby Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolby_Theatre "Dolby Theatre") in Hollywood on March 9, 2020.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-57) The film was originally scheduled for a theatrical release of November 2, 2018, but was delayed, with _[The Nutcracker and the Four Realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nutcracker_and_the_Four_Realms "The Nutcracker and the Four Realms")_ taking its slot.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-FindingMulan-29) It was rescheduled for March 27, 2020,[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-58)[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-NewRelease-59) before being removed from the release calendar due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-60) The film later rescheduled for July 24, 2020, taking the former slot of _[Jungle Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Cruise_(film) "Jungle Cruise (film)")_,[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-61) and then was delayed again to August 21, 2020.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-62) On July 23, the film was removed from the release calendar once again.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-63)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-64)[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-65)

On August 4, 2020, Disney announced that it was cancelling the film's theatrical release in the United States, and would instead premiere the film on [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+ "Disney+") with [Premier Access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+_Premier_Access "Disney+ Premier Access") for a premium fee on September 4, 2020.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Disney+Deadline-66) _Mulan_ was available for the purchase until November 2, 2020, and was made available for free to all subscribers on December 4.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-67) The film was not offered in France as a premium offering, instead releasing to Disney+ subscribers in the country for free at a later date.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-InternationalPremiumCost-68) Speaking about the US$29.99 price point for the film in the United States, Disney CEO [Bob Chapek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Chapek "Bob Chapek") said, "We're trying to establish a new premiere access window to capture \[the\] investment we got \[in the film\]... From our research under a premiere access offering, not only does it get us revenue from our original transaction of PVOD, but it's a fairly large stimulus to sign up for Disney+."[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Disney+Deadline-66) The premium fee was approximately US$26.00 in other countries when considering conversion rates for their native currencies.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-InternationalPremiumCost-68) Unlike other premium VOD releases that feature a 48-hour viewing window, _Mulan_ remains available to renters for as long as they stay subscribed to Disney+.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-69)

The film was still released theatrically in countries where theaters had re-opened, and do not have Disney+.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Disney+Deadline-66) In China, the film was released on September 11, 2020.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-70)

_Mulan_ received a [PG-13 rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Association_film_rating_system#Ratings "Motion Picture Association film rating system") from the [Motion Picture Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Association_of_America "Motion Picture Association of America"), the first [Disney live-action remake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_remakes_and_adaptations_of_Disney_animated_films "List of remakes and adaptations of Disney animated films") to be given such a rating.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-71)

### Home media

_Mulan_ was released by [Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_Home_Entertainment "Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment") on Digital HD on October 6, 2020, followed by a [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD"), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray"), and [Ultra HD Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray "Ultra HD Blu-ray") release on November 10, 2020.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-72)

## Controversies

The film was marked by several controversies, relating to both production decisions as well as comments by individuals involved in the film. Disney CFO [Christine McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_McCarthy "Christine McCarthy") has stated that the controversies the film has caused have created "a lot of issues for \[Disney\]".[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-73)

### Filming in Xinjiang

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Mulan_Protest_NYC_14_April_2021.jpg/500px-Mulan_Protest_NYC_14_April_2021.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mulan_Protest_NYC_14_April_2021.jpg) Tibetans protesting the film in [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square "Times Square"). Protestors held placards criticizing the film's Oscar nominations and accusing Disney of ignoring [human rights violations in Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Uyghurs_in_China "Persecution of Uyghurs in China").

Criticism has been directed at the fact that filming took place in the [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_region "Autonomous region") of [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang "Xinjiang"), where [human rights abuses are taking place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_internment_camps "Xinjiang internment camps"). At the end of the credits, the film gives special thanks to several government entities in Xinjiang, including the now-sanctioned Xinjiang [Public Security Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_security_bureau_(China) "Public security bureau (China)") in [Turpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turpan "Turpan"), and several local committees of the [Publicity Department of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicity_Department_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "Publicity Department of the Chinese Communist Party").[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-74)[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-75) The public security bureau in Turpan was added to the U.S. [Bureau of Industry and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Industry_and_Security "Bureau of Industry and Security")'s [Entity List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity_List "Entity List") in October 2019.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-76)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-77)

According to [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters"), after the controversy about the film's links with Xinjiang erupted overseas, the Chinese government ordered media outlets not to cover the release of _Mulan_.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-78) At a September 10 conference, Disney CFO Christine McCarthy said in response to the controversy that "almost the entirety" of the film had been shot in New Zealand, but that 20 Chinese locations were used to "accurately depict some of the unique landscape and geography of the country".[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Palmeri-79)[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-80) US Senator [Josh Hawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hawley "Josh Hawley") [sent a letter](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Letter%20to%20Disney%20CEO%20Mr.%20Bob%20Chapek%20About%20Mulan%20and%20Xinjiang "wikisource:Letter to Disney CEO Mr. Bob Chapek About Mulan and Xinjiang") to Disney CEO [Bob Chapek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Chapek "Bob Chapek") asking, among other things, for clarification on Chinese government involvement in the film.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Bond-81) The [Human Rights Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Foundation "Human Rights Foundation") also sent a letter to Chapek asking Disney to condemn human rights violations and consider donating some of the film's earnings to promote human rights in Xinjiang.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Bond-81)[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Davis-82)[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-83)

In September 2020, 135 [British MPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") signed a letter condemning China's human rights abuses, addressed to [Liu Xiaoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Xiaoming "Liu Xiaoming"), Chinese ambassador to the U.K. The petition was written in response to a [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") video detailing the production of _Mulan_.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-84)[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-85) On October 7, 2020, British politician [Iain Duncan Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iain_Duncan_Smith "Iain Duncan Smith") sent an email to Disney, criticizing the filming in Xinjiang. Disney defended it, saying, "it is standard practice across the film industry worldwide to acknowledge in a film's credits the cooperation, approvals, and assistance provided by various entities and individuals over the course of a film's production."[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Davis-82)

### Lack of East Asians in production team

Disney was criticized because _Mulan_'s production team was largely composed of people who were neither of Chinese descent nor otherwise Asian. This included the costume designer and screenwriter,[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-86) but Disney received particular criticism for not hiring an Asian director. In a February 2020 interview with _[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_, Caro responded, "Although it's a critically important Chinese story and it's set in Chinese culture and history, there is another culture at play here, which is the culture of Disney, and that the director, whoever they were, needed to be able to handle both—and here I am."[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Ford-37) In an August 2020 interview with _[Film School Rejects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_School_Rejects "Film School Rejects")_, Caro added, "Firstly, I resist the idea that you tell somebody who can tell what story. That sounds a little bit like censorship to me. An artist will express themselves, and the burden of responsibility is on the art. That will be judged—and should be judged. The other side of it is that more diverse people need to be allowed to tell stories. That's what it comes down to. The people who are hired for all kinds of stories need to be more diverse. It can't just be white people being hired to make movies, no matter what the subject matter is. Our culture is going to be richer for the more diversity there is, and the art, the movies, the television, it will be better. The more this conversation is being had, the more that diverse artists are given opportunities."[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-87)

### Li Shang

In a February 27, 2020, interview, film producer Jason T. Reed said that Mulan's love interest from the original film, [Captain Li Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Disney's_Mulan_characters#Li_Shang "List of Disney's Mulan characters"), was dropped in response to the [MeToo movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeToo_movement "MeToo movement"). In his statement, he explained that "having a commanding officer that is also the sexual love interest was very uncomfortable and we didn't think it was appropriate".[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-88) The reasoning for not including the character was met with social media backlash from fans of the original film and members of the [LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ "LGBTQ") community, who deemed Shang's relationship with Mulan's male [alter ego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter_ego "Alter ego") Ping to be [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexual "Bisexual").[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Forbes-89)[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-90) Reed was initially surprised by criticism of Shang's removal, but acknowledged that the character had become an " [LGBTQ icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_icon "LGBTQ icon")",[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Vogue-91) and clarified that Shang's role would be served by two new characters, Commander Tung and Chen Honghui.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Forbes-89)[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Vogue-91)

In September 2020, Cynthia Vinney of _[CBR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_Book_Resources "Comic Book Resources")_ wrote that Honghui's interactions with Mulan were "more homoerotic" than Shang's in the animated version and likewise "can be read as bisexual".[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-92) Lauren Puckett of _[Harper's Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper's_Bazaar "Harper's Bazaar")_ wrote "Some fans understood and agreed with the #MeToo argument. Others found it offensive, arguing that Shang would never use his commanding position to coerce a woman into romance. He waited until after she was no longer under his command to pursue any sort of romantic relationship. And anyway, it was Mulan who had the crush on _him_! \[...\] Despite \[Honghui's\] attraction, he hardly has the same respect for her that brave and beautiful Li Shang did."[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-93)

### Liu's comments on Hong Kong

A call to boycott the film began when Liu reshared an image posted by the _[People's Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Daily "People's Daily")_, an official newspaper of the [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party "Chinese Communist Party"). The image included a quote from Chinese reporter [Fu Guohao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_August_2019_Hong_Kong_protests#12%E2%80%9314_August_police_brutality_protests "List of August 2019 Hong Kong protests"), who worked for the state-owned [tabloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_tabloid "Chinese tabloid") _[Global Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Times "Global Times")_ and was assaulted by protesters during the [2019–2020 Hong Kong protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests "2019–2020 Hong Kong protests"): "I support Hong Kong police. You can beat me now." The post added in English: "What a shame for Hong Kong." This sparked international controversy, with Liu being accused of supporting [police brutality in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_misconduct_allegations_during_the_2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests "Police misconduct allegations during the 2019–2020 Hong Kong protests"). The hashtag #BoycottMulan started trending.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-94)[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-95)[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-96) As a result of the controversy, Liu did not appear at the 2019 [D23 Expo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D23_Expo "D23 Expo"), leaving only Caro to present the film at the panel.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-97)

## Reception

### Premium video on demand

_[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")_ estimated _Mulan_ would need to be rented by about 8.4 million subscribers (13.8%) in order to [break even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_even "Break even").[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-Rubin-Aug2020-54) On the weekend of the film's release, the Disney+ app was downloaded 68% more than the past weeks' total, similar to the 72% spike upon _[Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_(2020_film) "Hamilton (2020 film)")_'s release that July.[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-98) _[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood "Deadline Hollywood")_ reported that industry sources with knowledge of the situation "are figuring that Disney may not have earned great presales" and that they could put _Mulan_ on a third-party service before the December release.[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-D'Alessandro-99) However, following the film's opening weekend, _[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider "Business Insider")_ reported it had been viewed by 1.12 million households (only including users renting with TV-connected devices), which would result in $33.5 million in sales for Disney.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-100) [Yahoo! Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Finance "Yahoo! Finance") then reported that, according to analytics research firm 7Park Data, nearly 29% of U.S. Disney+ subscribers that accessed the app from September 1 to 12 purchased _Mulan_, which in the best-case scenario would result in $261 million for Disney.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-101) 7Park Data co-founder Brian Lichtenberger stated Yahoo! Finance overestimated _Mulan_'s revenue, as their figures were for accounts active during that period and not the total subscriber base, and clarified that 10.3% of all accounts purchased the film, with U.S. revenue estimated to be $62–93 million; Yahoo! Finance amended their article to reflect this.[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-102)[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-103)

According to [Nielsen ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_ratings "Nielsen ratings"), _Mulan_ was one of the top ten most-watched [streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media "Streaming media") in the United States between August 31 and September 6, 2020. During its opening weekend between September 4–6, _Mulan_ drew 525 million streaming minutes in the United States.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-104)

In its first weekend of being available on other digital platforms, _Mulan_ was the top-rented film on [FandangoNow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandango_Media "Fandango Media"), and third on both [Apple TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV_(device) "Apple TV (device)") and [Google Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Play "Google Play").[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-105) In its second weekend in the open market, the film placed second at Fandango, fourth on Google Play, and fifth at Apple TV.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-106) In October 2020, _[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ said the film was the most popular PVOD title amid the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"),[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-107) and the following month _[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")_ reported the film was the sixth-most watched straight-to-streaming title of 2020 up to that point.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-108)

### Box office

_Mulan_ was a box office bomb.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-109) It grossed $5.9 million from nine countries in its international opening weekend, including $1.2 million in Thailand and $700,000 in Singapore, both of which were the highest debuts of 2020 in the respective countries. It also made $800,000 in the United Arab Emirates, and the same amount in Saudi Arabia.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-110)[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-D'Alessandro-99) In its second weekend, the film made $29.1 million from 17 countries, including $1.8 million in Russia, and finished first in Ukraine, Hungary, Serbia, and South Africa.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-ChinaOpening-111) Through three weekends, the film grossed $57 million internationally.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-ThirdWeekend-112)

The film made $23.2 million on its opening weekend in China, a low figure but a 23% improvement upon _[Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_(2019_film) "Aladdin (2019 film)")_'s debut the year before.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-ChinaOpening-111) Several factors were cited as contributing to the film's muted performance, including the announcement of the official Chinese release date only days before the premiere, giving little chance to build a strong marketing campaign; the prior release on Disney+, resulting in pirated copies being available online; a lack of local press coverage in order to avoid drawing attention to the controversy over the film having been partially shot in the Xinjiang region; and poor fan reception on sites such as [Maoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoyan "Maoyan") and [Douban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douban "Douban").[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-ChinaOpening-111)[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-113)[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-:1-114) The revenue of the film fell 72% in its second weekend of release in the country, to $6.5 million.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-ThirdWeekend-112)

### Critical response

[Review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes") reports that 72% of 329 critic reviews were positive, with an average rating of 6.7/10. The site's critical consensus read, "It could have told its classic story with greater depth, but the live-action _Mulan_ is a visual marvel that serves as a stirring update to its animated predecessor."[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-tomatoes-115) On [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), the film has a [weighted average score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean") of 66 out of 100 based on 52 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-metacritic-116)

[Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") of the _[Chicago Sun-Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")_ gave the film three and a half out of four stars, praising the "fine cast, exciting action and spectacular visuals" and writing, "This is such a great-looking film, with amazing set pieces and dazzling action and colors so vibrant they would dazzle a [Crayola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crayola "Crayola") factory, it will still play well on your home monitor. There are so many gorgeous shades of orange and magenta, blue and yellow, it's as if we're seeing these colors for the first time."[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-117) Kate Erbland of _[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")_ gave the film a B+, calling it a "remarkable action epic that carves its own path" and writing, " _Mulan_ is perhaps the best example of how to marry the original with something fresh. _The Ballad of Mulan_ has always been an epic-scale story about the power of being yourself in a world not ready to accept that, a tale that will likely always have resonance."[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-118)

Leah Greenblatt of _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ gave the film a B+ and described it as "a classic hero(ine)'s tale, exhilarating in its elaborate set pieces and large-scale ambitions even when the smaller human story within it sometimes falls short."[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-119) Writing for _[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")_, Peter Debruge said, "On one hand, the result isn't immediately recognizable as 'a Disney movie,' but neither does it establish its own narrative or visual signature, the way [Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino") did when remixing Asian influences for _[Kill Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Bill:_Volume_1 "Kill Bill: Volume 1")_. This is pure pastiche, as Caro and her crew shamelessly pilfer from kung fu, [Fifth Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_China#Rise_of_the_fifth_generation "Cinema of China") and [Hong Kong action movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_action_cinema "Hong Kong action cinema"), incorporating [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime "Anime") and [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood "Bollywood") touches as well."[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-120)

Mick LaSalle of the _[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")_ wrote, " _Mulan_ is a spirit lifter, and though it doesn't arrive as planned, it could not arrive at a better time", saying, "Throughout _Mulan_, there are shots of such visual splendor that viewers will catch their breath. In one, the morning mist clears and reveals an army in the distance, with its flags and colors, a frightening yet strangely beautiful sight."[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-121) Justin Chang of the _[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")_ wrote, " _Mulan_ is a heroic muddle, one that elicits both a disappointed sigh and an appreciative nod. It lays down a marker of progress achieved and progress to come."[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-122)

Aja Romano of _[Vox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vox_(website) "Vox (website)")_ wrote, "The _Mulan_ remake jettisons everything great about Disney's animated classic and delivers nothing new" and " _Mulan_'s few bright spots can't save it from clunky writing".[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-123)

Walter Chaw of Film Freak Central gave the film half a star out of four, writing, "\[E\]verything about this _Mulan_ is ironic accidentally. Whatever its intentions, its execution and the circumstances of its creation are in opposition to them. It's a feminist tract that enforces male notions of value; a call to arms that fights for the wrong side of our current history; and a proud statement of national identity that celebrates the Nation of Disney as opposed to China. It's majestically painful as a representation of how white people view Asians and, yes, it would be different had an Asian person been allowed to direct the film."[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-124)

Grace Wong of the _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ wrote, "The animated _Mulan_ meant so much to me, and the character I grew up loving taught me that being loyal, brave and true means standing up for what you believe in, no matter the cost. Today, I believe Mulan would be on the side of the Hong Kong protesters, who also fight to have independence, their voices heard and their lives valued. I hoped to see these threads in the new iteration. Instead we got an almost unrecognizable, twisted reflection of the original."[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-125)

[Hamilton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_College "Hamilton College") professor Zhuoyi Wang argues that the film "is not, as many believe, just another Disney film suffering from simple artistic inability, cultural insensitivity, or political injustice, but a window into the tension-ridden intersectionality of the gender, sexual, racial, cultural, and political issues that shape the production and reception of today's cross-cultural films. \[...\] The film made significant compromises between its goals of cultural appropriateness, progressive feminism, and monetary success. Although it eventually failed to satisfactorily resolve these at times conflicting missions, it still achieved important progress in addressing some serious gender and cultural problems in Mulan's contemporary intertextual metamorphosis, especially those introduced by the Disney animation."[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-126)

#### In China

_Mulan_ was poorly received by Chinese critics and audiences. The film scored 4.7 out of 10 on the rating site [Douban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douban "Douban") after its debut.[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-127) Reviewers on Douban contended that the film had flat characters and a bland story with details that did not make sense. Many people also appeared unsatisfied with how the film handles certain Chinese cultural elements. One of the more common complaints about the film is its treatment of _[qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi "Qi")_. _Qi_ is a traditional idea in martial arts and Chinese medicine concerning a person's energy flow. But in _Mulan_, _qi_ becomes a magical power that the eponymous heroine possesses. That power is limited by dishonesty, keeping Mulan from realizing her full potential until she strips away her disguise as a man. Other Chinese viewers complained about some of the characters' makeup, saying that it reflects Western stereotypes of China, rather than being a reflection of actual Chinese culture.[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-128)[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-129)

Yanni Chow and Carol Mang of [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters") wrote that the film had "a lacklustre reception in Hong Kong" amidst calls from pro-democracy activists for a boycott.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-:1-114)

## Sequel

In April 2020, months before the film's eventual September 2020 release date, it was reported that a _Mulan_ sequel is in development with Chris Bender, Jason T. Reed, and Jake Weiner returning as producers.[\[155\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulan_(2020_film)#cite_note-155)\[ _[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\] However, development has stalled throughout the years and no reports regarding the sequel has surfaced, as of 2026.
