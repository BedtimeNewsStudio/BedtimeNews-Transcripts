**Reliance Industries Limited** is an Indian [multinational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation "Multinational corporation") [conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_(company) "Conglomerate (company)") headquartered in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"). Its businesses include [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry "Energy industry"), [petrochemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrochemicals "Petrochemicals"), [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas"), [retail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retail_industry "Retail industry"), [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_industry "Entertainment industry"), [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_industry "Telecommunications industry"), [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media "Mass media"), and [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry "Textile industry"). Reliance is the largest public company in India by [market capitalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalisation "Market capitalisation")[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-:6)[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6) and revenue,[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) and the 88th largest company worldwide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) It is India's largest private tax payer[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) and largest exporter, accounting for 7% of India's total merchandise exports.[&#8202;](#ref-back-financial1-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-financial1)

The company has attracted controversy for reports of [political corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_corruption "Political corruption"), [cronyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronyism "Cronyism"), [fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraud "Fraud"), [financial manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_manipulation "Market manipulation"), and exploitation of its customers, Indian citizens, and natural resources.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-auto)[&#8202;](#ref-back-OutlookControversies-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-OutlookControversies)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto2-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-auto2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-auto1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto3-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-auto3)

## History

### 1958–1985

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a2/Dhirubhai_Ambani_2002_stamp_of_India.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dhirubhai_Ambani_2002_stamp_of_India.jpg) Stamp released in 2002 to honor company founder [Dhirubhai Ambani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhirubhai_Ambani "Dhirubhai Ambani")

Reliance Commercial Corporation was set up in 1958 by [Dhirubhai Ambani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhirubhai_Ambani "Dhirubhai Ambani") as a small venture firm trading commodities, especially spices and polyester yarn.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RIL_history-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-RIL_history) In 1965, the partnership ended and Dhirubhai continued the [polyester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyester "Polyester") business of the firm.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) In 1966, Reliance Textiles Industries Pvt. Ltd. was incorporated in [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra "Maharashtra"). It established a [synthetic fabrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fabrics "Synthetic fabrics") mill in the same year at [Naroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naroda "Naroda") in [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat").[&#8202;](#ref-back-ETH-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-ETH) On 8 May 1973, it became Reliance Textiles Industries Limited. In 1975, the company expanded its business into textiles, with "Vimal" becoming its major brand in later years. The company held its [initial public offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") (IPO) in 1977.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Milestones-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-Milestones) The issue was over-subscribed by seven times.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) In 1979, a textiles company Sidhpur Mills was amalgamated with the company.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) In 1980, the company expanded its polyester yarn business by setting up a Polyester Filament Yarn Plant in Patalganga, [Raigad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raigad_district "Raigad district"), Maharashtra with financial and technical collaboration with [E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._I._du_Pont_de_Nemours_&_Co. "E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co."), U.S.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ETH-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-ETH)

### 1981–2000

In 1985, the name of the company was changed from _Reliance Textiles Industries Ltd._ to _Reliance Industries Ltd._[&#8202;](#ref-back-ETH-2)[\[17\]](#refnote-ETH) During 1985 to 1992, the company expanded its installed capacity for producing [polyester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyester "Polyester") [yarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn "Yarn") by over 145,000 tonnes per annum.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ETH-3)[\[17\]](#refnote-ETH)

The [Hazira petrochemical plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Hazira_Manufacturing_Division_(HMD) "Reliance Hazira Manufacturing Division (HMD)") was commissioned in 1991–92.[&#8202;](#ref-back-HDFCH-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-HDFCH)

In 1993, Reliance turned to the overseas capital markets for funds through a [global depository](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_depository_receipt "Global depository receipt") issue of Reliance Petroleum. In 1996, it became the first private sector company in India to be rated by international [credit rating agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_rating_agencies "Credit rating agencies"). [S&P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S&P "S&P") rated Reliance "BB+, stable outlook, constrained by the sovereign ceiling". [Moody's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's "Moody's") rated "Baa3, Investment grade, constrained by the sovereign ceiling".[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

In 1995/96, the company entered the [telecommunications industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_industry "Telecommunications industry") through a joint venture with [NYNEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYNEX "NYNEX"), USA, and promoted Reliance Telecom Private Limited in India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-HDFCH-1)[\[21\]](#refnote-HDFCH)

In 1998, Reliance took over [Indian Petrochemicals Corporation Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Petrochemicals_Corporation_Limited "Indian Petrochemicals Corporation Limited") during privatization of public sector enterprises.

In 1998/99, RIL introduced packaged [LPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_petroleum_gas "Liquefied petroleum gas") cylinders under the brand name _Reliance Gas_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-HDFCH-2)[\[21\]](#refnote-HDFCH)

The years 1998–2000 saw the construction of the [integrated petrochemical complex at Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar_Refinery "Jamnagar Refinery") in [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-HDFCH-3)[\[21\]](#refnote-HDFCH) the largest refinery in the world.

### 2001 onwards

In 2001, Reliance Industries Ltd. and Reliance Petroleum Ltd. became India's two largest companies in terms of all major financial parameters.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23) In 2001–02, Reliance Petroleum was merged with Reliance Industries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Milestones-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-Milestones)

In 2002, Reliance announced India's biggest gas discovery (at the [Krishna Godavari basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Godavari_basin "Krishna Godavari basin")) in nearly three decades and one of the largest gas discoveries in the world during 2002. The in-place volume of natural gas was more than 7 trillion cubic feet, equivalent to about 120 crore (1.2 billion) barrels of crude oil. This was the first-ever discovery by an Indian private sector company.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Milestones-2)[\[18\]](#refnote-Milestones)[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

In 2002–03, RIL purchased a majority stake in Indian Petrochemicals Corporation Ltd. (IPCL), India's second largest petrochemicals company, from the government of India,[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25) RIL took over IPCL's Vadodara Plants and renamed it as Vadodara Manufacturing Division (VMD).[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27) IPCL's Nagothane and [Dahej manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Dahej_Manufacturing_Division "Reliance Dahej Manufacturing Division") complexes came under RIL when IPCL was merged with RIL in 2008.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28)[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)

In 2005 and 2006, the company reorganised its business by demerging its investments in power generation and distribution, financial services and telecommunication services into four separate entities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-demerger-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-demerger)\[ _[non-primary source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources "Wikipedia:No original research")_\]

In 2006, Reliance entered the organised retail market in India[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31) with the launch of its retail store format under the brand name of ' [Reliance Fresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Fresh "Reliance Fresh")'.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33) By the end of 2008, Reliance Retail had close to 600 stores across 57 cities in India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34)

In November 2009, Reliance Industries issued 1:1 bonus shares to its shareholders.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)

In 2010, Reliance entered the broadband services market with acquisition of Infotel Broadband Services Limited, which was the only successful bidder for pan-India fourth-generation (4G) spectrum auction held by the government of India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)
In the same year, Reliance and [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP "BP") announced a partnership in the oil and gas business. BP took a 30 per cent stake in 23 oil and gas production sharing contracts that Reliance operates in India, including the KG-D6 block for $7.2 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto4-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-auto4) Reliance also formed a 50:50 joint venture with BP for sourcing and marketing of gas in India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto5-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-auto5)

In 2017, RIL set up a [joint venture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_venture "Joint venture") with Russian Company [Sibur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibur "Sibur") for setting up a [Butyl rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butyl_rubber "Butyl rubber") plant in [Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar "Jamnagar"), [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat"), to be operational by 2018.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41)

In 2018, the [Commercial Court (England and Wales)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Court_(England_and_Wales) "Commercial Court (England and Wales)"), hearing a legal case involving Reliance Industries and the [Union of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_India_(government) "Union of India (government)"), ruled for the first time that the [foreign act of state doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_state_doctrine "Act of state doctrine") applied when issues were being addressed through [arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration "Arbitration"), as well as when they are addressed through [litigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litigation "Litigation").[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42)

In August 2019, Reliance added Fynd[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43) primarily for its consumer businesses and mobile phone services in the [e-commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-commerce "E-commerce") space.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45)

In December 2022, Reliance Industries Market cap stood at ₹17.59 lakh crore (US$223.78 billion).[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46)

In the field of [generative pre-trained transformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_pre-trained_transformer "Generative pre-trained transformer") (GPT) technology, Reliance Industries Ltd. and The BharatGPT Group announced in February 2024 that they would launch a [large language model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model") (LLM), Hanuman's [AI system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI"), in March 2024. The model will work in 11 local languages in four major areas: [health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health "Health"), [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance "Governance"), [financial services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_services "Financial services") and [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education "Education").[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47)

In February 2024, Reliance and [The Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") announced a deal to merge their streaming and television assets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49) The deal was closed in November 2024, with the [Disney Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Star "Disney Star") joint venture having a reported value of $8.5 billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50)

In October 2024, Reliance entered into an agreement with [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia") to [procure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement "Procurement") [Blackwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwell_(microarchitecture) "Blackwell (microarchitecture)") chips for its planned data centre in Jamnagar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51)

On 14 August 2026, Reliance Industries and [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Holdings "Rolls-Royce Holdings") announced their strategic intent for a partnership to develop and manufacture an indigenous aero engine for the [Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Medium_Combat_Aircraft "Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft") (AMCA). A dedicated **Aero Gas Turbine Complex** will be established in India. The facility will support the programme by serving as a [centre of excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_of_excellence "Centre of excellence") for power and propulsion technology where the design, development, manufacturing, testing, production and through-life support of the programme will be undertaken. This is a parallel proposal to the [Safran–GTRE programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Medium_Combat_Aircraft#Safran%E2%80%93GTRE_engine_co-development "Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft").[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-52)[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-53)[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-54)

## Shareholding

The number of shares of RIL are approx. 644.51 crore (6.44 billion).[&#8202;](#ref-back-shareownership-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-shareownership) The promoter group, the Ambani family, holds 50.39% of the total shares whereas the remaining 49.61% shares are held by public shareholders, including FII and corporate bodies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-shareownership-1)[\[55\]](#refnote-shareownership) [Life Insurance Corporation of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Insurance_Corporation_of_India "Life Insurance Corporation of India"), public sector company, is the largest non-promoter investor in the company, with 6.49% shareholding.[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-56)

In January 2012, the company announced a buyback program to buy a maximum of 12 crore (120 million) shares for ₹10,400 crore (US$1.95 billion). By the end of January 2013, the company had bought back 4.62 crore (46.2 million) shares for ₹3,366 crore (US$574.42 million).[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-57)

### Listing

The company's equity shares are listed on the [National Stock Exchange of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stock_Exchange_of_India "National Stock Exchange of India") Limited (NSE) and the [BSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Stock_Exchange "Bombay Stock Exchange") Limited. The [Global Depository Receipts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Depository_Receipt "Global Depository Receipt") (GDRs) issued by the company are listed on [London Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stock_Exchange "London Stock Exchange").[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-58)[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-59) It has issued approx. 5.6 crore (56 million) GDRs wherein each GDR is equivalent to two equity shares of the company. ### Cronyism

_Seminar_ magazine (2003) detailed Reliance founder Dhirubhai Ambani's proximity to politicians, his enmity with [Bombay Dyeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Dyeing "Bombay Dyeing")'s [Nusli Wadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusli_Wadia "Nusli Wadia"), the exposes by the [Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Express "Indian Express") and Arun Shourie about illegal imports by the company and overseas share transactions by shell companies, and the botched attempt to acquire [Larsen & Toubro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larsen_&_Toubro "Larsen & Toubro").[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-126)

As early as 1996, _Outlook_ magazine addressed other controversies related to fake and switched shares; insider trading; and a nexus with the state-owned [Unit Trust of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_Trust_of_India "Unit Trust of India"). Five main allegations concerning Reliance, which have plunged the Indian capital markets into a period of uncertainty unsurpassed since the days of the securities scam were:

- Reliance issued fake shares.
- It switched shares sent for transfer by buyers to make illegal profits.
- It indulged in insider trading in shares.
- It established a nexus with the Unit Trust of India to raise huge sums of money to the detriment of UTI subscribers.
- It attempted to monopolize the private telecom services market through front companies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-2)[\[11\]](#refnote-auto)

### 2005 Criminal investigation

The [Central Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation "Central Bureau of Investigation") (CBI) filed a charge sheet in a Mumbai court against Reliance Industries Limited (RIL) and four retired employees of [National Insurance Company Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Insurance_Company_Limited "National Insurance Company Limited") (NICL), including a former CMD, under provisions of the [Prevention of Corruption Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_Corruption_Act "Prevention of Corruption Act") for [criminal conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_conspiracy "Criminal conspiracy") and other charges. Acting on a reference from CVC in March 2005, the CBI started probing the conspiracy that led to the filing of the charge sheet on 9 December 2011. The 2005 complaint had alleged irregularities in the issuance of insurance policies — for coverage of default payments — by NICL to RIL. The charge sheet also mentioned criminal offenses with dishonest intention and causing wrongful loss totaling ₹147.41 crores to NICL and wrongful gain to the private telecom provider.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto2-2)[\[13\]](#refnote-auto2)

Two retired senior officials of National Insurance Company Limited and 11 others were awarded varying jail terms by a Delhi court in Jan 2014.[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-127)

### RIL plane grounded

A business jet owned by Reliance Industries (RIL) was grounded by The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) on 22 March 2014 during a surprise inspection, for carrying expired safety equipment on board; its pilot was also suspended for flying without a license.[&#8202;](#ref-back-128-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-128)

### Krishna Godavari (KG) Basin gas controversy

Reliance Industries Limited (RIL) was supposed to relinquish 25% of the total area outside the discoveries in 2004 and 2005, as per the Production Sharing Contract (PSC). However, the entire block was declared as a discovery area and RIL was allowed to retain it. In 2011, the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) criticized the Oil Ministry for this decision. The CAG also faulted RIL for limiting the competition in contracts, stating that RIL awarded a $1.1 billion contract to Aker on a single-bid basis.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-2)[\[14\]](#refnote-auto1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-:1)

### Petition against Reliance Jio

A PIL filed in the Supreme Court by an NGO Centre for Public Interest Litigation, through Prashant Bhushan, challenged the grant of a pan-India license to RJIL by the Government of India. The PIL alleged that RJIL was allowed to provide voice telephony along with its 4G data service, by paying an additional fee of just INR 16,580 million (US$280 million) which was arbitrary and unreasonable and contributed to a loss of INR 228,420 million (US$3.8 billion) to the exchequer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-130)[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-131)

The CAG in its draft report alleged rigging of the auction mechanism, whereby an unknown ISP, Infotech Broadband Services Pvt Ltd, acquired the spectrum by bidding 5000 times its net worth, after which the company was sold to Reliance Industries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-132-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-132)

### Future Retail deal and possession

In February 2022, Reliance terminated the leases of hundreds of Future Retail locations, the next largest retail chain in India, and took possession of those brick-and-mortar shops. Future Retail had a deal to sell its assets to Reliance, but that deal was contested by [Amazon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com "Amazon.com"), which in 2019 acquired a stake in a subunit of Future Retail along with certain rights with respect to the transfer of the retailer's assets. Reliance's possessions came after rounds of legal wrangling, including a 2020 arbitration in Singapore and an antitrust review by the [Competition Commission of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_Commission_of_India "Competition Commission of India").[&#8202;](#ref-back-133-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-133)[&#8202;](#ref-back-134-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-134)[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-135)[&#8202;](#ref-back-136-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-136)

### Stock manipulation and penalty

For [manipulating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_manipulation "Stock manipulation") shares of [Reliance Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Petroleum "Reliance Petroleum") Limited (RPL), Reliance Industries was fined Rs. 950 crore (about 447 crore in retracted gains and 500 crore in interest) in 2007.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto3-2)[\[15\]](#refnote-auto3) In April 2006, RPL went public as a Reliance subsidiary at a price of Rs. 60 per share. The market crashed by 30% after it floated at roughly Rs. 100, and RPL was back at 60. In accordance with [Securities and Exchange Board of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Board_of_India "Securities and Exchange Board of India") directive, RIL carried out an organised operation with the help of its agents in order to obtain unauthorised profits from the trading of its formerly listed unit, RPL, which was combined with the former in 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-137-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-137)[&#8202;](#ref-back-138-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-138)[&#8202;](#ref-back-139-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-139)[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[140\]](#refnote-140)[&#8202;](#ref-back-141-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-141)

### Reliance's business ties with Russia

Reliance Industries has faced criticism for maintaining business relations with Russia despite international sanctions imposed following [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia's_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russia's invasion of Ukraine") in 2022. The company has engaged in energy deals with Russian oil giant [Rosneft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosneft "Rosneft"), raising concerns about its alignment with global efforts to reduce economic ties with Russia. Reliance has been listed on [Leave Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_Russia "Leave Russia"), a platform tracking companies still active in the Russian market, further intensifying scrutiny over its role in facilitating [Russian energy exports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy_of_Russia "Energy policy of Russia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-142-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-142)[&#8202;](#ref-back-143-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-143) In December 2024, Reliance Industries entered into a contract with [Rosneft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosneft "Rosneft") to procure 500,000 barrels of oil per day for 10 years. This corresponds to around US$13 billion per year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-144)

## Reliance demerger and family feud

The Ambani family holds around 45% of the shares in RIL.[&#8202;](#ref-back-145-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-145) Since its inception, the company was managed by its founder and chairman [Dhirubhai Ambani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhirubhai_Ambani "Dhirubhai Ambani"). After suffering a stroke in 1986, he handed over the daily operations of the company to his sons [Mukesh Ambani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukesh_Ambani "Mukesh Ambani") and [Anil Ambani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Ambani "Anil Ambani"). After the death of Dhirubhai Ambani in 2002, the management of the company was taken up by both the brothers. In November 2004, Mukesh Ambani, in an interview, admitted to having differences with his brother Anil over 'ownership issues'.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-:0) He also said that the differences "are in the private domain". The share prices [&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-147) of RIL were impacted by some margin when this news broke out. In 2005, after a bitter public feud between the brothers over the control of the Reliance empire, mother Kokilaben intervened to broker a deal splitting the RIL group business into two parts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-148) In October 2005, the split of Reliance Group was formalised. Mukesh Ambani got Reliance Industries and IPCL. Younger brother Anil Ambani received telecom, power, entertainment, and financial services business of the group. The [Anil Dhirubhai Ambani Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Dhirubhai_Ambani_Group "Anil Dhirubhai Ambani Group") includes Reliance Communications, Reliance Infrastructure, Reliance Capital, Reliance Natural Resources and Reliance Power.[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-149)[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-150)

The division of Reliance group business between the two brothers also resulted in de-merger of 4 businesses from RIL.[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-151)[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-152) These businesses immediately became part of Anil Dhirubhai Ambani Group. The existing shareholders in RIL, both the promoter group and non-promoters, received shares in the de-merged companies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-demerger-1)[\[30\]](#refnote-demerger)
