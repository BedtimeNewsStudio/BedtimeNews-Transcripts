The reform seeks to expand pension coverage, strengthen the long-term sustainability of the system and adjust the interaction between the public pension scheme and the private pension system. The implementing regulations were subsequently approved and published in September 2025.

**Background and policy rationale**

The reform responds to long-standing structural challenges in Peru’s pension system, including low effective coverage, especially among self-employed and informal workers, as well as fragmented contribution histories linked to labour market informality. These issues have been highlighted repeatedly in public policy discussions in recent years, together with broader concerns regarding access to adequate pensions and the long-term sustainability of the system.

Pension reform has been under discussion for several years within government and Parliament, forming part of wider debates on social protection, labour market participation and formalisation. The proposal advanced through parliamentary committees and plenary debates, alongside consultations and public hearings with social partners and stakeholders, reflecting the systemic importance of pension policy for employment relations.

These discussions have also taken place in a context shaped by repeated extraordinary withdrawals from pension funds, which have influenced the public debate on long-term savings, pension adequacy and the overall sustainability of the system. In that context, the reform has generally been presented as an effort to improve coverage, strengthen retirement protection and restore a clearer long-term pension framework.

Employer organisations, including the Confederación Nacional de Instituciones Empresariales Privadas (**CONFIEP**), have publicly underscored the importance of advancing structural pension reform while cautioning against further extraordinary withdrawals. In its policy document Propuestas para impulsar el crecimiento económico y bienestar del Perú (“Proposals to Promote Economic Growth and Well-Being in Peru”), **CONFIEP** highlighted the need to prioritise pension reform, ensure at least a minimum pension, incorporate informal and independent workers into the system, and promote complementary pension savings. In public statements, **CONFIEP** likewise warned that further withdrawals should not displace the broader reform agenda.

**Main elements of the reform**

Law No. 32123 introduces a series of structural changes, including:

* the creation of the Integrated Pension System of Peru, bringing together the public and private pension schemes within a single multi-pillar framework under public and private administration;
* mechanisms aimed at guaranteeing access to at least a minimum pension;
* mandatory affiliation to the pension system from the age of 18, together with rules on choice and default allocation between schemes;
* new mechanisms aimed at expanding coverage for workers with intermittent, informal or non-standard contribution trajectories, as well as complementary savings mechanisms; and
* updated rules for the private pension system, including greater competition in pension fund management and new commission arrangements linked to results.

Together, these measures are intended to broaden participation in the pension system while adapting the framework to Peru’s labour market realities and reinforcing the long-term pension function of the system. Private-sector commentary has generally viewed the reform as a step in the right direction, while noting that its ultimate effectiveness will depend on implementation and on preserving the long-term purpose of pension savings.

**Implementation and transition**

Following the adoption of the law, the Government of Peru approved and published the implementing regulations on 5 September 2025, setting out the institutional arrangements and operational mechanisms for the modernised pension system and initiating its phased implementation. Under these regulations, individuals reaching the age of 18 from June 2027 will be required to affiliate through the Secure and Transparent Affiliation Platform (PAST) and will have 12 months to choose between the public and private pension schemes, with default allocation applying in the absence of an active choice.

It should also be noted that Law No. 32445, adopted later in September 2025, is a separate law authorising an extraordinary withdrawal from private pension funds and amending certain provisions of Law No. 32123. In particular, it repealed Article 9 of Law No. 32123. For that reason, earlier references to phased mandatory contributions for self-employed workers should no longer be treated as part of the framework currently in force.
