Presidential elections were held in Sri Lanka on 21 September 2024.[[1]](#cite_note-AJ-26.07.2024-7)[[2]](#cite_note-8) Incumbent [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Sri_Lanka "President of Sri Lanka") [Ranil Wickremesinghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranil_Wickremesinghe "Ranil Wickremesinghe") ran for re-election as an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_politician "Independent politician") candidate, making him the first sitting president to run for re-election since [Mahinda Rajapaksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahinda_Rajapaksa "Mahinda Rajapaksa") in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election "2015 Sri Lankan presidential election").[[3]](#cite_note-9)[[4]](#cite_note-AD-26.07.2024-10)[[5]](#cite_note-DH-27.07.2024-11) Other prominent candidates included [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Sri_Lanka) "Leader of the Opposition (Sri Lanka)") [Sajith Premadasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajith_Premadasa "Sajith Premadasa"), [Anura Kumara Dissanayake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anura_Kumara_Dissanayake "Anura Kumara Dissanayake") of the [NPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Power "National People's Power"), and [Namal Rajapaksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namal_Rajapaksa "Namal Rajapaksa"), son of former President Mahinda Rajapaksa.[[6]](#cite_note-12)

The elections were a three-way contest between Wickremesinghe, Premadasa and Dissanayake. For the first time in a Sri Lankan presidential election, no candidate received a majority of the vote. Dissanayake was in first place with 42%, followed by Premadasa with 33%. Incumbent president Wickremesinghe finished third, receiving only 17% of the vote. As no candidate received a majority, second preference votes were counted.[[7]](#cite_note-13) The following day, Dissanayake was declared the winner with 56% of the vote after second preferences had been taken into account.[[8]](#cite_note-14)[[9]](#cite_note-15) He was inaugurated on 23 September.

The elections signified a major [political realignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_realignment "Political realignment") in Sri Lanka.[[10]](#cite_note-16) Dissanayake's victory was the first time a [third-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_party_(politics) "Third party (politics)") candidate was elected president. This was also the first election where neither of the top two candidates were endorsed by the [United National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_National_Party "United National Party") or the [Sri Lanka Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Freedom_Party "Sri Lanka Freedom Party").

## Background

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")]

The last direct presidential elections held in Sri Lanka were in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election "2019 Sri Lankan presidential election"), where [SLPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Podujana_Peramuna "Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna") candidate [Gotabaya Rajapaksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotabaya_Rajapaksa "Gotabaya Rajapaksa") won the election in a landslide victory, defeating his main opponent [Sajith Premadasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajith_Premadasa "Sajith Premadasa").[[11]](#cite_note-17)[[12]](#cite_note-18)

Rajapaksa [resigned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_of_Gotabaya_Rajapaksa "Resignation of Gotabaya Rajapaksa") on 14 July 2022 during the [2022 Sri Lankan protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sri_Lankan_protests "2022 Sri Lankan protests").[[13]](#cite_note-19) This triggered an [indirect presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election "2022 Sri Lankan presidential election") via Parliament a week later, to elect a successor according to the Article 40 of the Constitution.[[14]](#cite_note-20) Ranil Wickremesinghe, who had been appointed as [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Sri_Lanka "Prime Minister of Sri Lanka") by Rajapaksa just two months earlier, received the most votes and was sworn in as the 9th President of Sri Lanka on 21 July 2022.[[15]](#cite_note-21)[[16]](#cite_note-22)

Article 40 of the Constitution of Sri Lanka states that "Any person so succeeding to the office of President shall hold office only for the unexpired period of the term of office of the President vacating office.[[17]](#cite_note-23) President Wickremesinghe's term expired on 17 November 2024.

Discussion surrounding the 2024 election had been a major topic since the beginning of the year. The [Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_Sri_Lanka "Election Commission of Sri Lanka") stated that the election had to be held between 17 September and 16 October, as required by the Constitution. On 26 July, the Election Commission issued a [Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sri_Lanka_Gazette "The Sri Lanka Gazette") notification declaring that the election would be held on 21 September 2024, with nominations for candidates to be submitted by 15 August.[[18]](#cite_note-24) The commission chose to hold the election on a Saturday, stating that this would help ensure a high [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout "Voter turnout"). On the same day, Ranil Wickremesinghe announced his candidacy for a second term as president, running as an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_politician "Independent politician") candidate.[[1]](#cite_note-AJ-26.07.2024-7)[[5]](#cite_note-DH-27.07.2024-11)

Approximately 17 million people were eligible to vote in this election.[[19]](#cite_note-IFES-25)[[20]](#cite_note-AP-06.09.2024-26)[[21]](#cite_note-27)

## Electoral system

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Electoral system")]

Further information: [List of presidential elections in Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidential_elections_in_Sri_Lanka "List of presidential elections in Sri Lanka")

### Type of electoral system

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Type of electoral system")]

The President of Sri Lanka is elected through a system of limited [ranked voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_voting "Ranked voting"). Voters can express up to three ranked preferences for the presidency. If no candidate receives more than 50% of all valid votes in the first count, all candidates except the two who received the highest number of votes are eliminated. The second and third preference votes of the eliminated candidates are then redistributed to the remaining two candidates until one of them secures an outright majority.[[22]](#cite_note-28)

In practice, this system had seen no use. Every presidential election since the first in [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election "1982 Sri Lankan presidential election") had resulted in a candidate from one of the major parties or alliances winning an outright majority in the first count. Consequently, most voters choose to mark only one candidate on their ballots, and many are either unaware that they can rank multiple candidates or choose not to exercise that prerogative.[[23]](#cite_note-29)[[24]](#cite_note-30)

### Voting procedure

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Voting procedure")]

The Election Commission outlined the voting procedure on 30 August 2024, detailing the ranked voting system due to the multiple candidates contesting the presidential election, ensuring voters were informed on how to cast and submit their ballots.[[25]](#cite_note-31)[[26]](#cite_note-32) Voting began at 13,134 polling stations nationwide at 7:00 AM [SLST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Standard_Time "Sri Lanka Standard Time") on 21 September and closed at 4:00 PM SLST.[[27]](#cite_note-ajstake-33)

### Postal voting

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Postal voting")]

The Election Commission announced the dates and locations for postal voting in a media release on 29 August 2024. This facility is available only to pre-approved officials engaged in election duties.[[28]](#cite_note-34)[[29]](#cite_note-35) Postal voting was conducted on 4, 5, 6, 11, and 12 September, strictly according to the provided schedule.[[30]](#cite_note-36)[[31]](#cite_note-37)[[27]](#cite_note-ajstake-33)

### Ballot boxes and security systems

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Ballot boxes and security systems")]

Due to the larger size of the ballot papers, the Election Commission has opted to use cardboard ballot boxes instead of traditional wooden ones. These boxes will be available in three sizes. A combination of wooden and cardboard ballot boxes was used in the [2019 Sri Lankan presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election "2019 Sri Lankan presidential election") and the [2020 Sri Lankan parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Sri_Lankan_parliamentary_election "2020 Sri Lankan parliamentary election").[[32]](#cite_note-38)[[33]](#cite_note-39)

To further ensure security, the government imposed a liquor ban on the weekend of 21 to 22 September and deployed more than 63,000 police officers to secure polling stations and counting centres. It also banned the holding of victory rallies or celebrations until a week after the results were announced.[[34]](#cite_note-40) A nighttime curfew was also declared after the end of voting, which was lifted at noon on 22 September.[[35]](#cite_note-41)

## Campaign

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Campaign")]

### Platforms

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Platforms")]

Ranil Wickremesinghe highlighted his administration's success in resolving shortages of basic goods. Anura Kumara Dissanayake campaigned on a platform promoting economic freedoms and welfare protections for the working class. Sajith Premadasa vowed to mitigate the impact of the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund")'s restructuring programme on the poor and promised devolved political powers to the [Tamil minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils "Sri Lankan Tamils"). Namal Rajapaksa pledged to reduce tax burdens, attributing the recent [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_economic_crisis_(2019–present) "Sri Lankan economic crisis (2019–present)") and [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sri_Lankan_political_crisis "2022 Sri Lankan political crisis") crises to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").[[20]](#cite_note-AP-06.09.2024-26)

### Abolition of executive presidency

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Abolition of executive presidency")]

During the election campaign, both Dissanayake and Premadasa promised that, if elected, they would abolish the executive presidential system and return the country to a parliamentary democracy. This familiar pre-election pledge had been declared by numerous candidates in the past, yet hopes have been met with doubt.[[112]](#cite_note-TH-20A-120)

On 23 September 2024, National People's Power member [Sunil Handunnetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Handunnetti "Sunil Handunnetti") informed the media that the abolition of the executive presidency remains a priority for the government led by newly elected President Anura Kumara Dissanayake.[[113]](#cite_note-DM-23S-121)[[114]](#cite_note-EN-24S-122)

### Debates

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Debates")]

The first presidential debate was scheduled for 7 September 2024, hosted by the March 12 Movement. Four candidates, Sajith Premadasa, Namal Rajapaksa, Dilith Jayaweera, and P. Ariyanethiran, confirmed their participation. The debate took place at the [Bandaranaike Memorial International Conference Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandaranaike_Memorial_International_Conference_Hall "Bandaranaike Memorial International Conference Hall") (BMICH) and was broadcast live on mainstream television and social media platforms.[[115]](#cite_note-123) However, despite initial confirmations, only Dilith Jayaweera attended the debate.[[116]](#cite_note-124)[[117]](#cite_note-125)

### Election monitors

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Election monitors")]

Following an invitation from the Election Commission of Sri Lanka, the [European External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service "European External Action Service") (EEAS) of the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") (EU) will deploy an Election Observation Mission (EOM) to observe the Presidential Election scheduled for 21 September.[[118]](#cite_note-126)[[119]](#cite_note-127)

The Election Commission further announced that representatives from 12 countries have been invited to observe the upcoming election. Among those participating are South Asian countries such as India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, and the Maldives, as well as a [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations "Commonwealth of Nations") [observer group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Observer_Groups "Commonwealth Observer Groups") (COG)[[120]](#cite_note-128) and representatives from the [Asian Election Observation Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Network_for_Free_Elections "Asian Network for Free Elections").[[121]](#cite_note-129)

### Campaign finances

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Campaign finances")]

#### Expenditure cap and regulations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Expenditure cap and regulations")]

The Election Commission, through a Gazette notification (Gazette Extraordinary – No. 2397/66 on 16 August 2024), set an [expenditure cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_finance "Campaign finance") of Rs. 109 per voter for the 2024 presidential election. As a result, each candidate is now permitted to spend a maximum of Rs. 1.8 billion (Rs. 1,868,298,586).[[122]](#cite_note-130) These regulations have been enforced under the Regulation of Election Expenditure Act, No. 3 of 2023, which was certified on 24 January 2023.[[123]](#cite_note-131)

### Favourability ratings

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Favourability ratings")]

Note

* *Negative scores, which denote a net favourability rating below zero, indicate that the individual or institution is considered unpopular. In contrast, positive scores, where the net favourability is above zero, suggest that the individual or institution is generally regarded as popular.*

January 2024

In the Institute for Health Policy (IHP) Sri Lanka Opinion Tracker Survey (SLOTS) polling, all major party candidates continued to have negative favourability ratings. The net favourability rating of Anura Kumara Dissanayake increased by 12 points to −10, while the favourability ratings of Sajith Premadasa and incumbent President Ranil Wickremesinghe each decreased by 9 points, to −53 and −77, respectively.[[142]](#cite_note-150)

March 2024

In IHP SLOTS polling, the net favourability rating of Sajith Premadasa increased by 30 points to −30 in March compared to the previous month, while the favourability ratings of Anura Kumara Dissanayake and President Ranil Wickremesinghe remained relatively unchanged at −24 (down 2 points) and −78 (up 1 point), respectively.[[143]](#cite_note-151)

July 2024

In the IHP SLOTS polling conducted in July 2024, the net favourability rating of Anura Kumara Dissanayake rose by 29 points from June, reaching +3. President Ranil Wickremesinghe's favourability improved by 40 points to −24, surpassing that of Sajith Premadasa. Sajith Premadasa's favourability rating changed little, shifting from −42 in June to −44 in July.[[144]](#cite_note-152)

August 2024

In the IHP SLOTS polling for August 2024, the net favourability rating of Sajith Premadasa improved to −32, a 10-point increase compared to July. Meanwhile, Anura Kumara Dissanayake and President Ranil Wickremesinghe saw their favourability ratings drop to −21 (a decrease of 16 points) and −33 (a decrease of 4 points), respectively. The favourability rating of Namal Rajapaksa, stood at −89.[[145]](#cite_note-153)

## Aftermath

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2024_Sri_Lankan_presidential_election&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: Aftermath")]

While counting was still underway, foreign minister [Ali Sabry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Sabry_(Sri_Lankan_politician) "Ali Sabry (Sri Lankan politician)") congratulated Dissanayake, citing his strong showing in the results.[[147]](#cite_note-155) The test match between [Sri Lanka and New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_cricket_team_in_Sri_Lanka_in_2024–25#1st_Test "New Zealand cricket team in Sri Lanka in 2024–25") featured a rest day due to the election. It was the first time in sixteen years since a test match had a rest day.[[148]](#cite_note-156)

SJB MP Harsha de Silva congratulated Dissanayake. The [Tamil National Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_National_Alliance "Tamil National Alliance"), which had endorsed Premadasa, congratulated Dissanayake for his "impressive win" without relying on "racial or religious chauvinism".[[149]](#cite_note-157) Ranil Wickremesinghe also congratulated Dissanayake.[[150]](#cite_note-inauguaral-158)

The [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") congratulated Dissanayake and said it was ready to discuss the future of the economic recovery plan.[[151]](#cite_note-159)

Dissanayake attributed his victory to the "collective effort" of voters.[[152]](#cite_note-160) He was sworn in as president on 23 September.[[150]](#cite_note-inauguaral-158) Parliament was dissolved by Dissanayake the next day, and [early parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Sri_Lankan_parliamentary_election "2024 Sri Lankan parliamentary election") were called on 14 November.[[153]](#cite_note-161)
