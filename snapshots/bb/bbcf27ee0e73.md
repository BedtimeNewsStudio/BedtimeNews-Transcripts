# 37.2m people, a 7.5% increase, cruised globally in 2025

Intent to cruise again is at all-time high 90%, and 2024 worldwide economic impact was $198b, according to CLIA figures.

[![Image 9: Picture of Anne Kalosh](https://eu-images.contentstack.com/v3/assets/blt57a7a4fed12d2120/blt9af12223c7c5cfd4/6a2dee8a8b42b2864039a248/AK.JPG?width=100&auto=webp&quality=80&disable=upscale)](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/author/anne-kalosh)

Some 37.2m people took an oceangoing cruise in 2025, 7.5% more than the 34.6m in 2024, with nearly 90% of cruisers indicating they intend to sail again, the highest level ever recorded by [Cruise Lines International Association](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/keyword/cruise-lines-international-association).

The figures, from CLIA's 2026 State of the Cruise Industry report, were announced by CLIA President/CEO Bud Darr at [Seatrade Cruise Global](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/events/seatrade-cruise-global). He opened his remarks on the main stage by calling the state of the industry "excellent."

“Cruising is sailing toward the future with exceptional momentum and strength,” Darr said. “Record demand, growing interest from new cruisers and sustained confidence in the cruise experience are being matched by innovation, technological advancements and destination partnerships. At the same time, we’re seeing a meaningful shift in recognition that cruise is a leader in sustainable operations.”

#### Cruise counts by region

Passengers from North America numbered 22.1m in 2025, 7.5% more than the 20.5m in 2024. Of those, 20.6m, a 7.5% increase, were from the [United States,](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/events/united-states) which comprised 55% of global cruise demand.

Related:[Cruise industry leaders keep to resilience and growth in uncertain times](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/people-opinions/cruise-industry-leaders-keep-to-resilence-and-growth-in-uncertain-times)

Some 8.9m Europeans cruised, up 5.3% from the 8.4m the year before. [Germany](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/events/germany) led, with 2.8m, up 10%

[Asia's](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/keyword/asia) total reached 3m, 15% more than the 2.6m in 2024. China led, with 1.1m, up 15.8%.

Australasia's tally was 1.5m, 7.4% higher than the 1.4m previously.

And South America's total was 1.22m, 4.4% above the 1.17m in 2024.

#### 40m cruisers by 2030

Darr said there is still "a lot of room" to grow. CLIA forecasts 40m cruisers by the decade's end.

#### Top destinations

The [Caribbean](https://seatrade-cruise.com/keyword/caribbean), Bahamas and Bermuda together handled 16.27m passengers, an 8.4% increase, and accounting for a 44% global destination share.

This was followed by the Mediterranean, with one in six passengers — 3.22m total, up 3.4%. And non-Mediterranean Europe handled 3.22m cruisers, a 5.8% increase. Asia/China had 3.11m, up 19.6%.

Global demand is supported by a broadening source market and a wide range of cruise experiences across regions and ship types, Darr noted.

#### 325 CLIA oceangoing ships

This year CLIA tallies 325 member line oceangoing ships, representing around 690,000 lower berths globally.

Darr said the fleet is divided into about one-third small ships, one-third medium-sized ships and one-third large. Among the trends are growth in luxury, expedition and exploration travel.

#### Younger, more diverse cruisers

Cruising continues to attract a younger and varied audience, according to the CLIA report. About one-third of cruisers are under 40, and approximately one-third of cruise trips are multigenerational.

Related:[Australia holds its own as world's fourth largest cruise source market](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/people-opinions/australia-holds-its-own-as-world-s-fourth-largest-cruise-source-market)

#### $198b economic impact

Cruise tourism generated $198b in global [economic impact](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/events/economic-impact) in 2024, supporting 1.8m jobs worldwide and contributing $60b in wages, according to CLIA’s recently released 2024 Global Economic Impact study.

In the US alone, cruising contributed $75b to the economy, supporting 333,000 jobs and over $25b in wages, with $41.4b in gross domestic product.

Most of this impact comes from shoreside spending, reflecting cruise lines’ investment in ports, destinations and communities. Cruise tourism supports a wide network of businesses, including transportation providers, hotels, restaurants and suppliers.

#### Strides in energy efficiency

New ships are increasingly more energy efficient than previous generations and nearly all are now designed with fuel-flexible engines. Many are equipped to use shore power, and lines are focused on energy efficiency and innovative environmental technologies.

[International Maritime Organization](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/events/imo) regulations require ships to achieve net-zero emissions by or around 2050, a deadline CLIA member lines are committed to.

Cruise industry strides in [sustainabiility](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/events/sustainability) are real, Darr and "Getting the facts out is making a difference. These are not just words; they're underpinned by actions."

#### Overtourism

"Unmanaged tourism is a genuine problem," Darr told the Seatrade audience. But he argued that "cruise tourism is managed tourism" since itineraries are planned far in advance so numbers and flow are predictable.

According to Darr, the industry continues to work with [governments](https://www.seatrade-cruise.com/events/government) and local stakeholders to help with planning and tourism management.

CLIA's 2026 State of the Cruise industry report, including 2024 economic impact data, is available at [https://cruising.org/resources/state-cruise-industry-report-2026](https://cruising.org/resources/state-cruise-industry-report-2026).
