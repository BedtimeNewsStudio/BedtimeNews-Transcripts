Multi-millionaire [castle owner Ann Kaplan Mulholland](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/money/tax/ann-kaplan-mulholland-lympne-castle-avoid-non-dom-tax/) is reluctantly packing her bags for Milan before the Treasury snatches an “astronomical amount” of her £500m fortune.

“I seriously don’t want to leave but we have no choice because of ‘Reckless’ Reeves,” she says, coining a new name for the Chancellor. “It upsets me as we love England, it’s our home.”

The Canadian businesswoman is yet another ultra-wealthy [foreign resident who is planning their getaway](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/money/tax/staying-in-britain-under-labour-will-cost-me-10m/) ahead of Labour’s non-dom tax raid.

Currently, Mulholland and her cosmetic surgeon husband, Stephen, avoid paying UK tax on money they make overseas. But the relief offered to around 70,000 fellow millionaires is coming to an end.

Mulholland, who resides in Lympne Castle, Kent, says the “ill-thought-through and amateur” crackdown will “choke England for years to come” as wealthy investors shun the country.

“‘Reckless’ needs to listen to people who are saying ‘no’, not just everyone who’s saying ‘yes’ and patting her on the back,” she says. “This is a serious thing as the economy is really struggling.”

Her warning comes despite the Chancellor’s plans to [water down the non-dom tax raid](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2025/01/23/rachel-reeves-to-water-down-non-dom-tax-raid/) by granting new arrivals a four-year reprieve in paying domestic taxes on foreign wealth.

It’s a move which has failed to impress Mulholland. “Having just four years will not encourage enterprise, it will attract zero investors,” she predicts. “It’s a complete walk-around, it’s like changing the fork when the cake tastes bad.

“Like everything that ‘Reckless’ has done, she promises results based on assumptions without a single forethought to the implications of her actions.

“Record numbers of non-doms are leaving the country and taking their billions with them.”

The UK lost 10,800 millionaires to overseas countries in 2024, which is equivalent to the revenue from half a million average taxpayers **,** and more than double the number who left in 2023.

Italy is the prime destination many are heading to, with wealthy international people paying a flat tax rate of €200,000 (£166,000) a year regardless of how much they earn overseas.

Prime rental prices in Milan, the country’s business capital, have [jumped by around 10pc in the last year](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2024/12/28/milan-mansion-rents-surge-as-wealthy-flee-britains-non-dom/) as demand from London movers doubled, according to Sotheby’s International Realty Italy.

“I’d much rather stay in England but it would cost an astronomical amount to do that,” Mulholland says. “We have until April 2026 until this impacts us, as that would be our fourth year. We’ll get out before then. We’ve already started the application process to enter Italy as residents.

“If ‘Reckless’ had a brain, she would take a page out of Italy’s offer and realise she could stop the bleed by offering non-doms the option to pay a fee of £200,000 per year. If she did that, I’d be first in line to say I’m staying and here’s your cheque.

“But that’s not happening. Wealthy individuals are preparing to walk down the runways of Italy, Switzerland and Dubai where government concierges will greet them with open arms and wallets.”

Under Labour’s new non-dom regime, foreign earnings will also be brought into the inheritance tax system.

Last week Reeves told the BBC that “if you make Britain your home, you should pay your taxes”. The Government hopes the crackdown will raise £5.2bn for the public purse by 2028-29.

Lympne Castle will remain in Ms Mulholland’s hands, saving local jobs

As for the future of Lympne Castle, which the couple bought in 2023 for £5.5m, Mulholland says the estate will remain in her hands. “I’m still going to keep the castle and I will come to England for the maximum number of days I can,” she says. “I love it and there’s a community of people that are job-dependent on us.”

Major renovations at the castle, including the opening of a hotel and farmers’ market, will therefore continue.

In an effort to avoid paying taxes on foreign wealth, Mulholland has previously suggested she will [create her own non-dom kingdom](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/money/tax/ann-kaplan-mulholland-lympne-castle-avoid-non-dom-tax/) in Lympne. She was poised to request permission from King Charles to become ruler of both the castle and the nearby Romney Marsh, but decided against it after realising such a move wouldn’t be legal.

Having refrained from setting up her own country, the 64-year-old isn’t backing out from conjuring further grand ideas before her impending departure to Italy. “I’ll send my resume to the Prime Minister,” she says. “He can hire me as Chancellor and I’d work for a dollar a year. I looked it up and I don’t have to be born in England to take the job.”
