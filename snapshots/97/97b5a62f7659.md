# Billionaire On Brink Of Chilean Presidency

By [Luisa Kroll](https://www.forbes.com/sites/luisakroll/ "https://www.forbes.com/sites/luisakroll/"),

Executive Editor.

Executive Editor

Follow Author

Dec 13, 2009, 09:42pm ESTJun 19, 2013, 04:41pm EDT

[Add Forbes on Google](https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=forbes.com)

This article is more than10years old.

One of Chile's most successful entrepreneurs took a step closer to winning the country's presidency on Sunday.

Right-wing billionaire [Sebastian Pinera](http://www.forbes.com/lists/2009/10/billionaires-2009-richest-people_Sebastian-Pinera_YLRC.html) was on track to beat out his three leftist opponents by a solid margin in the country's presidential election. With 60% of the vote counted Sunday night, the billionaire was leading with 44% of the vote. But he failed to win a majority, which means Pinera, 60, now faces a run-off on Jan. 17 against former President Eduardo Frei, who had 30% of the vote.

If Pinera wins in the second round, as some predict, it would be the first time Chile has been ruled by a right-leaning government since the fall of Augusto Pinochet’s military dictatorship in 1990 and the first time the conservatives have assumed power in public elections since 1958.

It would also be the first time a billionaire has run the South American country. Pinera, who was a senator for eight years and ran unsuccessfully for president in 2005, [ranked 701st](http://www.forbes.com/lists/2009/10/billionaires-2009-richest-people_Sebastian-Pinera_YLRC.html) among the world's billionaires in March with a net worth of $1 billion. He is likely worth much more than that now thanks to the soaring value of his most valuable holding, a 26% stake in Chile's national airline LAN, which was recently worth $1.5 billion.

Victory is no sure thing, though, as the ruling left is already trying to rally support around Frei.

Pinera, who has a doctorate in economics from Harvard, made his first mark in business by founding Chile's credit card giant Bancard. He now has a variety of investments, including interests in one of the country's most popular football teams, TV channel Chilevision and music company Blanco Y Negro.

Questioned about his conflicts of interest, the wealthy businessman announced in April that he was giving up administration of his holdings to focus on his campaign. At the time, he placed $400 million worth of investments in a blind trust to be administered by four Chilean investment firms. He still controls his stakes in LAN and Chilevision but has said that if he wins, he will sell his stake in LAN by March 11, 2010, the official inaugural date for the presidency.

Sometimes called Chile's Berlusconi, a reference to Italy's billionaire prime minister, who has also been accused of conflict of interests, Pinera is a somewhat controversial candidate for other reasons. He quit the board of LAN in 2007 after being fined $680,000 for buying stock ahead of the company's 2006 midyear earnings announcement.

He also is believed to have the backing of ex-military figures and reportedly said he would try to speed up the trials of soldiers still being held for human rights violations under the Pinochet regime, something he later denied. Still, Pinera is unusual among Chile's right in that he apparently voted to end Pinochet's dictatorship in 1988.

Chileans themselves don't seem overly concerned, perceiving him as a fairly moderate candidate and a fiscal conservative who would continue the prudent economic policies of the popular outgoing president, [Michelle Bachelet](http://www.forbes.com/lists/2009/11/power-women-09_Michelle-Bachelet_Z2QJ.html).
