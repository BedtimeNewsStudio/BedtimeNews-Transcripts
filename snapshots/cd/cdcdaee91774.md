This is a **list of countries by number of military and paramilitary personnel**. It includes any government-sponsored soldiers used to further the domestic and foreign policies of their respective government. The term " [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country "Country")" is used in its most common use, in the sense of [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_(polity) "State (polity)") which exercises [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty "Sovereignty") or has limited [recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_recognition "Diplomatic recognition").

## Guide to the list

The list consists of columns that can be sorted by clicking on the appropriate title:

- The names of the states, accompanied by their respective [national flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_flag "National flag").
- The number of [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military "Military") personnel on [active duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_duty "Active duty") that are currently serving full-time in their military capacity.
- The number of military personnel in the [reserve forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_forces "Reserve forces") that are not normally kept [under arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_arms "Under arms"), whose role is to be available to [mobilize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilization "Mobilization") when necessary.
- The number of personnel in [paramilitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramilitary "Paramilitary") forces: armed units that are not considered part of a nation's formal military forces.
- The total number of active, reserve, and paramilitary personnel.
- The ratio per thousand inhabitants of total military (active, reserve, and paramilitary).
- The ratio per thousand inhabitants of active military only.

As military forces around the world are constantly changing in size, no definitive list can ever be compiled.

All of the 178 countries listed here, especially those with the highest number of total soldiers such as the two [Koreas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea") and [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), include a large number of paramilitaries, civilians and policemen in their reserve personnel. Some countries, such as [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy") and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), have only volunteers in their armed forces. Other countries, such as [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius "Mauritius") and [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama"), have no national armies, but only a paramilitary force.

### Tooth-to-tail ratio

The numbers of military personnel listed include both support personnel (supplies, construction, and contracting) and actual combat personnel. For a typical country, the proportion of this total that comprises actual combat forces is about 26%[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1) (so, for every soldier there will be around three support personnel). This proportion is referred to as the "tooth-to-tail ratio".

Some countries have a considerably smaller [tooth-to-tail ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooth-to-tail_ratio "Tooth-to-tail ratio"): For example, the [United States Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces") has a tooth-to-tail ratio of 17%, meaning that for every combat unit there are around five support units.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)
