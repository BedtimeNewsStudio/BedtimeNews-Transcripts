2011 Singaporean general election

General elections were held in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") on 7 May 2011 to elect members of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Singapore "Parliament of Singapore"). They were the thirteenth general elections since the introduction of [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance_of_Singapore "Self-governance of Singapore") in 1959 and the eleventh since [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Singapore_Agreement_1965 "Independence of Singapore Agreement 1965") in 1965. [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Singapore "President of Singapore") [S.R. Nathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.R._Nathan "S.R. Nathan") dissolved parliament on 19 April on the advice of [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong") three weeks before the election. The number of elected seats was increased from 84 to 87 from the previous election. This was the second election contested by Lee as prime minister.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-2)

Key issues in the elections included immigration, the cost of living and housing, as well as ministerial salaries, opposition representation, the management of the [2010 Summer Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Summer_Youth_Olympics "2010 Summer Youth Olympics"), and the national security including the [Mas Selamat Kastari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mas_Selamat_Kastari "Mas Selamat Kastari")'s breakout in 2008. As a result, the election is described by analysts and pundits as a "watershed" election, whereas it saw the most number of seats being contested at the time, and the electorate surpassed two million, making it the most actively contested in Singapore's history at the time since its first post-independence election in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Singaporean_general_election "1968 Singaporean general election"). The only constituency with a multi-cornered fight was [Punggol East SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punggol_East_SMC "Punggol East SMC"), the first such multi-cornered contest since [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Singaporean_general_election "2001 Singaporean general election"), while the only constituency with a [walkover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkover "Walkover") was the five-seat [Tanjong Pagar GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjong_Pagar_GRC "Tanjong Pagar GRC"); it was the last general election to have a [walkover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkover "Walkover") in at least one constituency until [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Singaporean_general_election "2025 Singaporean general election"). It also marked the last general election to feature [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") prior to his [death in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Lee_Kuan_Yew "Death and state funeral of Lee Kuan Yew").[&#8202;](#ref-back-GE2011-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-GE2011)

While the ruling [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party "People's Action Party") (PAP) retained its [supermajority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajority "Supermajority"), their popular vote fell further to a record-worst score in post-independence with only 60.14%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ft-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-ft) Nevertheless, the election saw historic gains for the opposition in Singapore's political landscape, with the [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "Workers' Party (Singapore)") (WP) clinching the first [Group representation constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representation_constituency "Group representation constituency") since its creation in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Singaporean_general_election "1988 Singaporean general election"), with the team defeating the incumbent PAP in the [Aljunied GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljunied_GRC "Aljunied GRC") to expand their representation to eight seats, including [Hougang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_SMC "Hougang SMC") and the two [non-constituency seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-constituency_Member_of_Parliament "Non-constituency Member of Parliament") (NCMP).[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-nyt) With amendments to the constitution on increasing the required minimum opposition candidates to nine, three NCMPs were awarded with one going to [Singapore People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_People's_Party "Singapore People's Party") (SPP) after a narrow defeat in [Potong Pasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_SMC "Potong Pasir SMC"), returning that SMC to PAP's hands for the first time since [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Singaporean_general_election "1984 Singaporean general election"); for the first time since [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Singaporean_general_election "1988 Singaporean general election"), only one opposition party (the WP) won elected MPs, as the SPP's [Chiam See Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiam_See_Tong "Chiam See Tong"), who led his team in [Bishan–Toa Payoh GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishan%E2%80%93Toa_Payoh_GRC "Bishan–Toa Payoh GRC"), was defeated while his wife, [Lina Loh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Loh "Lina Loh"), lost in his [safe seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_seat "Safe seat") of Potong Pasir SMC.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-8)

## Background

The 2011 general election was the thirteenth held since the introduction of [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance_of_Singapore "Self-governance of Singapore") in 1959 and the eleventh since Singapore's independence in 1965. The governing [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party "People's Action Party") (PAP) aimed to secure its thirteenth consecutive term in office since its first electoral victory in [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Singaporean_general_election "1959 Singaporean general election"). It was the second general election led by [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong") as both [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") and Secretary-General of the PAP.

The [returning officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Returning_officer "Returning officer") is the Chief Executive Director of the [People's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Association "People's Association") (PA) Yam Ah Mee, who replaced his predecessor Tan Boon Huat after serving as returning officer for three general elections from 1997 to 2006.[&#8202;](#ref-back-APPOINTMENT_OF_RETURNING_OFFICER-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-APPOINTMENT_OF_RETURNING_OFFICER)

### Parliamentary reforms

On 11 March 2010, the Government tabled three bills in the parliament to amend the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies_of_Singapore "Constituencies of Singapore"), the Presidential Elections Act and the Parliamentary Elections Act,[&#8202;](#ref-back-3_bills-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-3_bills) all of which were later passed by its legislature on 26 April by a 74–1 vote following a three-hour debate on the bill.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-11)

These amendments include the reduced average size of [Group representation constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representation_constituencies "Group representation constituencies") (GRC) and the increase of the minimum opposition legislators and [Nominated Members of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominated_Member_of_Parliament "Nominated Member of Parliament") (NMP), from three and six respectively to nine each. As such, this was the first election to increase the [Non-Constituency Members of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-constituency_Member_of_Parliament "Non-constituency Member of Parliament") (NCMPs) since the implementation of the scheme in 1984. Another new addition was "cooling-off" day, a one-day period of [election silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_silence "Election silence") held on the eve of polling day, which prohibit regular campaigning with some exceptions such as the party political broadcasts. Internet campaigning was also formally legalised as a legitimate method of political campaigning.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3_bills-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-3_bills)

A month before the dissolution, the [Elections Department Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_Department_Singapore "Elections Department Singapore") announced a raise of the maximum campaigning expenses in this election, which raised from the previous amount per each voter of $3 up to $3.50.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-12)

### Political parties

Main article: [List of political parties in Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Singapore "List of political parties in Singapore")

The governing [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party "People's Action Party") (PAP) has been in power since Singapore's independence in 1965, and is currently led by the [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong"), marking its second election under Lee's premiership.

The last parliament besides PAP, comprises two other opposition parties, namely [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "Workers' Party (Singapore)") (WP) and [Singapore Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Alliance "Singapore Democratic Alliance") (SDA). The WP, led by [Low Thia Khiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Thia_Khiang "Low Thia Khiang") since 2001, become the main opposition party per the results of the 2006 election, as well as its first NCMP [Sylvia Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Lim "Sylvia Lim"), who was also the party's chairwoman. The SDA was left with [Singapore Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Justice_Party "Singapore Justice Party") (SJP) and [Pertubuhan Kebangsaan Melayu Singapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertubuhan_Kebangsaan_Melayu_Singapura "Pertubuhan Kebangsaan Melayu Singapura") (Singapore Malay National Organization/PKMS) as its membership parties after two parties, [Chiam See Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiam_See_Tong "Chiam See Tong")'s [Singapore People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_People's_Party "Singapore People's Party") (SPP) and the [National Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_Party_(Singapore) "National Solidarity Party (Singapore)") (NSP), both left SDA, and [Desmond Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Lim "Desmond Lim") took over Chiam as its secretary-general. Former WP candidate [Goh Meng Seng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Meng_Seng "Goh Meng Seng") joined NSP in 2008, a year after NSP's departure, and become NSP's secretary-general.

The [Singapore Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Party "Singapore Democratic Party") (SDP) is the only existing party without representation; however, secretary-general [Chee Soon Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chee_Soon_Juan "Chee Soon Juan") was still unable to contest because of the undischarged [bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy "Bankruptcy") at the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sdpjohn-0)[\[b\]](#refnote-sdpjohn)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-14) Debuting in this election is [Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Party_(Singapore) "Reform Party (Singapore)"), which was created on 18 June 2008 by former WP secretary-general [J.B. Jeyaretnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.B._Jeyaretnam "J.B. Jeyaretnam"), who had since cleared of bankruptcy and was reinstated into the law bar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-15) J.B. died three months later on 30 September,[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-16) and his son [Kenneth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Jeyaretnam "Kenneth Jeyaretnam") has since become its secretary-general.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-17)

The Electoral Boundaries Review Committee normally publishes an updated list of electoral divisions just before elections are called. Prior to the latest amendments, there were 14 GRCs, each with five or six seats, and nine [Single Member Constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Member_Constituency "Single Member Constituency") (SMC). There were a total of 84 seats being contested in the [2006 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Singaporean_general_election "2006 Singaporean general election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-cna_feb24-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-cna_feb24)

The new electoral map for 2011 was announced on 24 February 2011. The number of MPs to return has increased by three seats to 87, and four-member GRCs reappeared from the political map for the first time since its last appearance in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Singaporean_general_election "1997 Singaporean general election"). Both [Hong Kah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kah_GRC "Hong Kah GRC") and [Jalan Besar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalan_Besar_GRC "Jalan Besar GRC") GRCs were renamed into [Chua Chu Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chua_Chu_Kang_GRC "Chua Chu Kang GRC") and [Moulmein–Kallang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulmein-Kallang_GRC "Moulmein-Kallang GRC") GRCs respectively, while three of five existing six-member GRCs were reduced to five seats each with [Ang Mo Kio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Mo_Kio_GRC "Ang Mo Kio GRC") and [Pasir Ris–Punggol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasir_Ris-Punggol_GRC "Pasir Ris-Punggol GRC") remained at six (these GRCs would eventually be reduced in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Singaporean_general_election "2020 Singaporean general election")). With the population growth in [Yishun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishun "Yishun") and [Simpang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpang "Simpang") leading to the formation of [Nee Soon GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nee_Soon_GRC "Nee Soon GRC"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-ge_feb24-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-ge_feb24) there are a total of 15 GRCs this election, up by one from the last election. Only two constituencies of [Bishan–Toa Payoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishan-Toa_Payoh_GRC "Bishan-Toa Payoh GRC") and [Tampines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampines_GRC "Tampines GRC") were left untouched.

The number of SMCs have been increased by three to 12 for this election, with the introductions of [Hong Kah North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kah_North_SMC "Hong Kah North SMC"), [Pioneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_SMC "Pioneer SMC"), [Punggol East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punggol_East_SMC "Punggol East SMC") and [Sengkang West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengkang_West_SMC "Sengkang West SMC"), as well as returning SMCs of [Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountbatten_SMC "Mountbatten SMC"), [Radin Mas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radin_Mas_SMC "Radin Mas SMC"), [Whampoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whampoa_SMC "Whampoa SMC") and [Yuhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuhua_SMC "Yuhua SMC") after being part of GRCs in previous elections. In turn, [Chua Chu Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chua_Chu_Kang_SMC "Chua Chu Kang SMC"), [MacPherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacPherson_SMC "MacPherson SMC"), [Nee Soon Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nee_Soon_Central_SMC "Nee Soon Central SMC"), [Nee Soon East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nee_Soon_East_SMC "Nee Soon East SMC") and [Yio Chu Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yio_Chu_Kang_SMC "Yio Chu Kang SMC") were absorbed into neighbouring GRCs.

### New candidates

Main article: [Pre-election day events of the 2011 Singaporean general election §New candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-election_day_events_of_the_2011_Singaporean_general_election#New_candidates "Pre-election day events of the 2011 Singaporean general election")

A total of 78 candidates were brand-new to this election. Notable candidates out of the 24 introduced from the PAP that were part of the "fourth-generation" (4G) cabinet which include the eventual fourth [Prime Minister of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") [Lawrence Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Wong "Lawrence Wong"), a future [Deputy Prime Minister of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Deputy Prime Minister of Singapore") [Heng Swee Keat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heng_Swee_Keat "Heng Swee Keat"), an ex- [SAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Air_Force "Singapore Air Force") Chief and ministers [Chan Chun Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chun_Sing "Chan Chun Sing"), [Tan Chuan-Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Chuan-Jin "Tan Chuan-Jin"), lawyer [Edwin Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Tong "Edwin Tong"), civil servants [Low Yen Ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Yen_Ling "Low Yen Ling") and [Sim Ann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim_Ann "Sim Ann"), as well as [Desmond Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Lee_(Singaporean_politician) "Desmond Lee (Singaporean politician)") and [Ong Ye Kung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Ye_Kung "Ong Ye Kung"), the sons of former MPs [Lee Yock Suan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Yock_Suan "Lee Yock Suan") and Ong Lian Ten respectively.

There were 54 debuting candidates from six opposition parties, which include [Pritam Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pritam_Singh "Pritam Singh") who made another inroad into Parliament and went on to become the WP leader in 2018, as well as [Lina Loh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Loh "Lina Loh") (wife of then- [Potong Pasir SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_SMC "Potong Pasir SMC") MP [Chiam See Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiam_See_Tong "Chiam See Tong")), [Kenneth Andrew Jeyaretnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Jeyaretnam "Kenneth Jeyaretnam") (son of the late [J. B. Jeyaretnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Jeyaretnam "J. B. Jeyaretnam")), [Nicole Seah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Seah "Nicole Seah"), [Tan Jee Say](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Jee_Say "Tan Jee Say"), [Hazel Poa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazel_Poa "Hazel Poa") (who also joined by her spouse [Tony Tan Lay Thiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Tan_Lay_Thiam "Tony Tan Lay Thiam")) and [Benjamin Pwee Yek Guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Pwee "Benjamin Pwee").

### Retiring politicians

Main article: [Pre-election day events of the 2011 Singaporean general election §Outgoing candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-election_day_events_of_the_2011_Singaporean_general_election#Outgoing_candidates "Pre-election day events of the 2011 Singaporean general election")

20 existing PAP members from the [11th Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Parliament_of_Singapore "11th Parliament of Singapore") retired this election, including [speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Parliament_of_Singapore "Speaker of the Parliament of Singapore") [Abdullah Tarmugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Tarmugi "Abdullah Tarmugi"), [Senior Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Minister_of_Singapore "Senior Minister of Singapore") [S Jayakumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S_Jayakumar "S Jayakumar"), [Lim Boon Heng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Boon_Heng "Lim Boon Heng"), [Lee Boon Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Boon_Yang "Lee Boon Yang") and [Yu-Foo Yee Shoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu-Foo_Yee_Shoon "Yu-Foo Yee Shoon") among others;[&#8202;](#ref-back-ret-a-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-ret-a) two members died during their term (Ang Mo Kio GRC's [Balaji Sadasivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaji_Sadasivan "Balaji Sadasivan") and Jurong GRC's [Ong Chit Chung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Chit_Chung "Ong Chit Chung")) but neither by-elections were called since their wards were in the purview in [Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_Representation_Constituency "Group Representation Constituency").

[Eric Low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Low "Eric Low"), another PAP candidate that first entered politics in the [2001 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Singaporean_general_election "2001 Singaporean general election") but lost twice to WP in [Hougang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_SMC "Hougang SMC"), did not seek re-election, making him the second PAP candidate to participate but did not enter parliament (the first being [Pang Kim Hin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pang_Kim_Hin "Pang Kim Hin")).

### Battleground Constituencies

Similar to the previous elections, many media outlets named the following constituencies as "hotspots":[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-31)

- [Aljunied GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljunied_GRC "Aljunied GRC"), in which it was PAP's narrowest victory (of 56.09%-43.91%) in the last election, as [Hougang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_SMC "Hougang SMC") incumbent [Low Thia Khiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Thia_Khiang "Low Thia Khiang") left the ward to lead his WP's A-team to contest the ward; analysts hinted that the addition of [Kaki Bukit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaki_Bukit,_Singapore "Kaki Bukit, Singapore") division into the ward (in exchange of Aljunied-Hougang division to [Ang Mo Kio GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Mo_Kio_GRC "Ang Mo Kio GRC")) could be decisive, as many of the wards consist of parts of the previously defunct [Cheng San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_San_GRC "Cheng San GRC") and [Eunos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunos_GRC "Eunos GRC") GRCs, both of which WP had strong support in during the constituency's existence.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-32)
- Similarly, [Bishan–Toa Payoh GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishan%E2%80%93Toa_Payoh_GRC "Bishan–Toa Payoh GRC"), which faced its first contest since the formation in 1997, was also a hotspot as [Potong Pasir SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_SMC "Potong Pasir SMC") incumbent Chiam See Tong left the ward to also lead his SPP's A-team (and the only GRC team) to contest the ward. Unlike Aljunied, the boundaries for Bishan–Toa Payoh were intact throughout its existence.

## Controversies

### Online video

During the 2011 elections campaigning, [Vivian Balakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Balakrishnan "Vivian Balakrishnan") said the SDP was "suppressing a certain YouTube video, which raises some very awkward questions about the agenda and motivations of the SDP and its candidates".[&#8202;](#ref-back-accusation-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-accusation) He issued the following statement:

I am not sure what \[the SDP\] strategy is...I can't help feeling that part of the reason for their reticence is they have elements of their agenda they are not prepared to disclose and subject to scrutiny. Eventually, they will have to come out of the closet. ( [The Straits Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Straits_Times "The Straits Times"), 20 April 2011)

Vincent Wijeysingha rejected his comments stating, "We've been a very open party and we're very clear."[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-39)

This incident was cited in an article published in [The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist") criticising the ruling party's election strategy[&#8202;](#ref-back-economist-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-economist) [The New Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Paper "The New Paper") released a story next day, with the headline: "Is Singapore ready for a GAY MP?".[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-41) [Kenneth Jeyaretnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Jeyaretnam "Kenneth Jeyaretnam") of the [Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Party_(Singapore) "Reform Party (Singapore)") called Balakrishnan's campaign a "low attack."[&#8202;](#ref-back-accusation-1)[\[36\]](#refnote-accusation)

Balakrishnan received widespread controversy and criticism online for his remark,.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-42) On 28 April, he told the press: "there is "no need" to further discuss \[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[the\] video". He said that his question was a "legitimate".[\[41\]](#refnote-43)

### Cooling-off day

[Nicole Seah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Seah "Nicole Seah"), a team member contesting [Marine Parade GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Parade_GRC "Marine Parade GRC") under the NSP team, filed a complaint to the [Elections Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_Department "Elections Department") on 6 May stating PAP-team member [Tin Pei Ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_Pei_Ling "Tin Pei Ling") had violated the state-mandated cooling-off period 24 hours before polls by posting a Facebook comment "in response to a video \[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[in the state press\] that showed Seah crying after being told about a MacPherson female resident who could not get a refund of her son's $80 tuition fees".[\[42\]](#refnote-44)

The NSP team was advised by the Elections Department to lodge a police report before the Elections Department could investigate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-oohstraits-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-oohstraits)

The day after the election, Seah told reporters that her party had not received any response after making the complaint, and said no decision had been taken on whether or not to pursue the issue. She added that the NSP knew "it is an uphill battle to get any results out of this. I would rather devote my time and resources to the residents".[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-46)

A similar complaint was lodged against Seah alleging that material had been published on her Facebook page during Cooling-Off Day. On 10 August, the [Singapore Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Police_Force "Singapore Police Force") announced that it had concluded its investigations into the two incidents, and that aside from a "stern warning" to Tin's friend, neither action was taken against either Tin or Seah.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-47)

Separately, the NSP also complained that the PAP had been distributing election material to residents in [Tampines GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampines_GRC "Tampines GRC") in violation of cooling-off regulations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-oohstraits-1)[\[43\]](#refnote-oohstraits)

## Returning officer

Yam Ah Mee was appointed by [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong") as Returning Officer for the 2011 presidential election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-48) Yam announced the results of the four nominated candidates for the presidential election at 4:24am (GMT +8). Yam's robotic voice announcing the results for Aljunied GRC had turned into a mix just within a few hours ensuing the elections. Many took clips of his voice and mixed it with various styles of music, from electro to dance, on YouTube. In less than 24 hours after the release of the 2011 presidential election results, a club mix of Yam announcing the results was released on YouTube,[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-49)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Yummy-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-Yummy) by the same user responsible for the earlier club mix of Yam's general elections "performance". Yam has since explained that his "robotic" and "nasal" voice is due to an operation he had, to remove a fishbone in his larynx,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Yummy-1)[\[48\]](#refnote-Yummy) although he has also said, explaining his impassive delivery to _[The Straits Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Straits_Times "The Straits Times")_, that he "felt it was important to announce \[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[the election results\] clearly, concisely and impartially."[\[49\]](#refnote-51)

### Yam Ah Mee

**Yam Ah Mee** ( [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"):杨雅镁; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): _Yáng Yǎměi_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-52)[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-53) born 2 July 1957) is a Singaporean former [brigadier-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier-general "Brigadier-general") and civil servant who served as Chief Executive Director of the [People's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Association "People's Association") between 2010 and 2013. He is best known for his monotone voice and expressionless demeanour as the returning officer for the 2011 election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-APPOINTMENT_OF_RETURNING_OFFICER-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-APPOINTMENT_OF_RETURNING_OFFICER) Yam was also the Returning Officer for the [2012 Hougang by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Singaporean_by-election "2012 Singaporean by-election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-54) The [2013 Punggol East by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Punggol_East_by-election "2013 Punggol East by-election") was Yam's last appearance as Returning Officer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Thanks-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-Thanks)

Yam was educated at [Hwa Chong Junior College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwa_Chong_Junior_College "Hwa Chong Junior College"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-56) before graduating from the [University of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales "University of New South Wales") with a first class honours degree in engineering. He graduated from the [Royal Military College, Duntroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_College,_Duntroon "Royal Military College, Duntroon") in Australia, with The Queens Medal in 1980,[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTEHedges2012152-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTEHedges2012152) under a scholarship conferred by the [Singapore Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Armed_Forces "Singapore Armed Forces") (SAF). He also completed a [Master of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration "Master of Business Administration") degree at the [National University of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Singapore "National University of Singapore") and a [Master of Public Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Public_Administration "Master of Public Administration") degree at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University").[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-58)

Yam had served in the [Republic of Singapore Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Singapore_Air_Force "Republic of Singapore Air Force") (RSAF), rising to the position of Chief of Staff – Air Staff,[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-59) commanding the ground-based, air-defence systems, which include the new [Igla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K38_Igla "9K38 Igla") short range surface to air missiles,[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-60)[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-61) before retiring with the rank [Brigadier-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier-General "Brigadier-General"), and entering the [Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Civil_Service "Singapore Civil Service").

Yam joined the Public Service Division in August 1998 and was appointed the Deputy Secretary (Development), and CEO and Dean of the [Civil Service College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Service_College_Singapore "Civil Service College Singapore"). He was then posted to the [Ministry of Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_(Singapore) "Ministry of Transport (Singapore)") (MOT) as Deputy Secretary (Sea and Air) on 1 June 2004 and was appointed Chief Executive of [Land Transport Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Transport_Authority "Land Transport Authority") on 11 May 2005.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chan-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-Chan) He was also President of the [Singapore Youth Flying Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Youth_Flying_Club "Singapore Youth Flying Club") for 8 years from 1998 to 2006.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-63) In 2008, Yam was awarded the [Pingat Pentadbiran Awam (Emas)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingat_Pentadbiran_Awam "Pingat Pentadbiran Awam") (Public Administration Medal (Gold)).[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-64) On 1 May 2010, Yam was appointed Chief Executive Director-designate of the [People's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Association "People's Association"), and on 1 June 2010 fully assumed the position of Chief Executive Director, taking over from the retiring Tan Boon Huat.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-65)[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-66)

After gaining media attention due to his role as the returning officer for the 2011 general election, Yam did a "spoof" video of himself, in which he announced PAssion Movie Night, held to celebrate a million PAssion Card memberships, in his trademark manner. In May, Yam filmed a video to promote a [K-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-pop "K-pop") dance event on 29 May 2011, to be held at [Ngee Ann City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngee_Ann_City "Ngee Ann City").[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-67)

In January 2013, reports emerged that Yam had resigned from the PA and would leave the organisation by March, to be replaced by Ang Hak Seng, former Chief Executive for the [Health Promotion Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Promotion_Board "Health Promotion Board").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Thanks-1)[\[53\]](#refnote-Thanks)[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-68) In an interview with RazorTV, he announced plans to pursue a career in the private sector after 36 years in the Civil Service.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Thanks-2)[\[53\]](#refnote-Thanks) He held a farewell party on 28 March 2013 and ended his tenure at the PA on 31 March 2013.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-69)

Yam joined Sembcorp Design and Construction as its managing director on 16 April 2013.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Chan-1)[\[60\]](#refnote-Chan) Yam starred in a musical held to celebrate the opening of the first 12 stations of the [Circle line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Line_(Singapore) "Circle Line (Singapore)"), on 2 October.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-70)

## Results

After polls closed at 8pm, vote counting began. Results were announced by the Returning Officer Yam Ah Mee, chief executive director of the [People's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Association "People's Association").[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-71) At 11.58pm (SGT), [Mountbatten SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountbatten_SMC "Mountbatten SMC"), which had re-elected PAP candidate [Lim Biow Chuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Biow_Chuan "Lim Biow Chuan"), was the first result to be called. The final constituency to be called was [Potong Pasir SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_SMC "Potong Pasir SMC"), which was announced at 2.51am, with the PAP's [Sitoh Yih Pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitoh_Yih_Pin "Sitoh Yih Pin") unseated SPP's [Lina Loh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Loh "Lina Loh") at a 0.72% margin (or 114 votes equivalent), following a [recount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_recount "Election recount").

While the PAP retained government control for the 13th consecutive election in addition to its supermajority, its vote majorities were reduced islandwide for a second election in a row, down to its lowest national vote share to 60.17%, slightly surpassing the [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Singaporean_general_election "1991 Singaporean general election")'s share of 60.97%. The constituency with the best performing result for the PAP in this election was at [Hong Kah North SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kah_North_SMC "Hong Kah North SMC") with 70.61%, being the only one to score at least 70% for the election.

PAP won all but two constituencies with 81 out of 87 seats; the WP won the remaining six seats after successfully retaining [Hougang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_SMC "Hougang SMC") and for the first time since GRCs were introduced in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Singaporean_general_election "1988 Singaporean general election"), WP had also won [Aljunied GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljunied_GRC "Aljunied GRC"), the first instance where any opposition party won a GRC.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-72) Its victory also lead to the defeat of cabinet minister [George Yeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Yeo "George Yeo") and [Lim Hwee Hua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Hwee_Hua "Lim Hwee Hua"), who became the first two cabinet ministers to lose re-election since independence,[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-73)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-bbc) with the last time being [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Singaporean_general_election "1963 Singaporean general election") (minister [Kenneth Michael Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Michael_Byrne "Kenneth Michael Byrne") lost his seat of Crawford).[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-75) Furthermore, Hougang SMC clocked in the best performing result for WP at 64.80%, and the second-best performing result for any opposition party in post-independence Singapore's history, only behind 1991's Potong Pasir SMC's record of 69.64%.

The election also saw several records, with the elections of first female opposition MP [Sylvia Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Lim "Sylvia Lim") and first Malay opposition MP [Faisal Manap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_Manap "Faisal Manap") into Parliament. At 27 years of age, [Marine Parade GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Parade_GRC "Marine Parade GRC") MP [Tin Pei Ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_Pei_Ling "Tin Pei Ling") set a record of becoming the youngest MP-elect in post-independence, which was previously held by 29-year old [Ho Kah Leong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Kah_Leong "Ho Kah Leong") back in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Singaporean_by-elections "1966 Singaporean by-elections").[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-76) Her record would later be surpassed by 26-year old MP-elect [Raeesah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raeesah_Khan "Raeesah Khan") in the [2020 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Singaporean_general_election "2020 Singaporean general election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-77) Both Tin and [Alex Yam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Yam "Alex Yam") were also the first two [millennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennials "Millennials") MPs to be elected into parliament, and [Michael Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Palmer_(politician) "Michael Palmer (politician)") become the first ethnic minority MP to manage a SMC since the introduction of the GRC system.

Excluding electors in [Tanjong Pagar GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjong_Pagar_GRC "Tanjong Pagar GRC"), voter turnout for the election was 93.18%, with 2,060,373 votes cast.[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-78)

### By [Community Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Development_Council "Community Development Council")

While the PAP scored its lowest vote share in post-independence Singapore, two opposition parties, the NSP and the WP, who have fielded more than 20 candidates, are the only two opposition parties outside PAP to garner at least 12% of the overall popular vote this election, contributing nearly a quarter of the overall contested vote. NSP didn't field any candidates in the [North West CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_CDC "North West CDC"), and neither WP fielding any in the [South West CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_CDC "South West CDC"). The top performing region this election was the South West CDC (which also include the top scoring [Hong Kah North SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kah_North_SMC "Hong Kah North SMC")), followed by the [Central Singapore CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Singapore_CDC "Central Singapore CDC") (which had PM Lee's [Ang Mo Kio GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Mo_Kio_GRC "Ang Mo Kio GRC") and the sole walkover constituency of [Tanjong Pagar GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjong_Pagar_GRC "Tanjong Pagar GRC")). Along with the aforementioned two CDC, North West CDC also had PAP's contested vote shares polling above national average.

Comparing WP and NSP's contested vote shares that are common in the three CDCs, WP outperformed NSP in two of them, being in Central and [North East CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_CDC "North East CDC"), the latter was PAP's low-scoring CDC in this election in addition to WP electing [Aljunied GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljunied_GRC "Aljunied GRC") and [Hougang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_SMC "Hougang SMC") as the only two opposition-held constituencies this election; the [South East CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_CDC "South East CDC") had NSP prevailing the contested vote despite WP had [East Coast GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_GRC "East Coast GRC") and [Joo Chiat SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joo_Chiat_SMC "Joo Chiat SMC") electing narrowly on both constituencies, as the electorate in [Marine Parade GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Parade_GRC "Marine Parade GRC") is larger than East Coast.

The other four parties contributed a combined 15.00% of the overall popular vote, with the Central CDC scoring 23.99% having the higher vote share among the four participating regions in that election (the exception was the South East CDC), followed by Northwest's 23.66%. The Central CDC also contained the constituency with the narrowest result of [Potong Pasir SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_SMC "Potong Pasir SMC").

## Post-election events

### Ruling party's immediate reactions

The PAP's secretary-general, Prime Minister [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong"), described the results as delivering his party a "clear mandate to form the next government".[&#8202;](#ref-back-83-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-83) In his post-election press conference, Lee said the polls had "heightened (voters') political consciousness and awareness", and admitted that "many of them desire to see more opposition voices in Parliament to check the PAP government".[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-84) He described the PAP's loss of Aljunied GRC, which resulted in George Yeo being voted out of Parliament and losing his position as foreign minister, as a "heavy loss to my Cabinet and my team of MPs", but said that the party would "accept and respect the voters' decision".[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-85) The country's Senior Minister, [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong"), also admitted that "there is a sea change in the political landscape" after his team won [Marine Parade Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Parade_Group_Representation_Constituency "Marine Parade Group Representation Constituency") with just 56.6 percent of the vote.[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-86)

The [third cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Lee_Hsien_Loong_Cabinet "Third Lee Hsien Loong Cabinet") was announced two weeks later where extensive changes were made to the cabinet, and several ministers announcing stepping down from the cabinet, including [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Deputy Prime Minister of Singapore") [Wong Kan Seng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wong_Kan_Seng "Wong Kan Seng"), [Senior Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Minister_of_Singapore "Senior Minister of Singapore") [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong"), and [Minister Mentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_Mentor "Minister Mentor") [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew"). [Finance Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_(Singapore) "Ministry of Finance (Singapore)") [Tharman Shanmugaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharman_Shanmugaratnam "Tharman Shanmugaratnam") succeeded Wong as the new DPM, with existing DPM [Teo Chee Hean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teo_Chee_Hean "Teo Chee Hean") named the new successor to the [Coordinating Minister for National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinating_Minister_for_National_Security "Coordinating Minister for National Security"). Two new MPs were given cabinet portfolios, with [Chan Chun Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chun_Sing "Chan Chun Sing") given the acting [Minister for Community Development, Youth and Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Community_Development,_Youth_and_Sports "Ministry of Community Development, Youth and Sports"), and [Heng Swee Keat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heng_Swee_Keat "Heng Swee Keat") as the [Minister for Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_(Singapore) "Ministry of Education (Singapore)"), making Heng only the second MP to be ascended directly to cabinet minister, after [Richard Hu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hu "Richard Hu").[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-87) It was also announced that [Michael Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Palmer_(politician) "Michael Palmer (politician)") would be named the new [Speaker of the Parliament of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Parliament_of_Singapore "Speaker of the Parliament of Singapore"), replacing outgoing speaker [Abdullah Tarmugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Tarmugi "Abdullah Tarmugi").

### Opposition parties' immediate reactions

The [Workers Party's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "Workers' Party (Singapore)") secretary-general Low Thia Khiang said his team's win in Aljunied was "historic" and meant that voters had "accepted the WP as a rational, responsible and respected party".[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-88) In his victory speech, Low declared his win as a "political landmark in modern Singapore".[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-89)[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-90) He added that it meant the electorate wanted to tell the PAP to be "a more responsive, inclusive, transparent and accountable government”.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ft-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-ft)

In a statement on its website, the [Singapore Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Party "Singapore Democratic Party") thanked its supporters for their support, saying that it was for them that the party "(continues) to labour on in this undemocratic system with all the odds stacked against us."[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-91) Its assistant treasurer [Vincent Wijeysingha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Wijeysingha "Vincent Wijeysingha"), who stood in [Holland–Bukit Timah Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland%E2%80%93Bukit_Timah_Group_Representation_Constituency "Holland–Bukit Timah Group Representation Constituency"), said that the party's positive vote swing in its contested wards of almost 13 percent from the last elections was an "indicator that things are beginning to move up for our party."[&#8202;](#ref-back-opposition_reaction_cna-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-opposition_reaction_cna) In a second statement on its website, the SDP described its results as "disappointing", but promised to "build on the foundation that we have laid" for the next elections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-93) The party's secretary-general [Chee Soon Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chee_Soon_Juan "Chee Soon Juan"), barred from standing in the election, went on to write an opinion piece for [the Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian"), in which he said it "would have been a miracle" had the SDP won any seats, and accused the [media in Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Singapore "Media in Singapore") of suppressing news of the SDP's campaigning.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-94)

Other than the PAP and WP, the only other opposition seat pre-election had been held by the [Singapore People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_People's_Party "Singapore People's Party"), which lost it in the polls by just 114 votes. [Chiam See Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiam_See_Tong "Chiam See Tong"), the SPP's secretary-general, said his party would fight to win back [Potong Pasir Single Member Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_Single_Member_Constituency "Potong Pasir Single Member Constituency"), and said that despite being defeated in [Bishan–Toa Payoh Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishan%E2%80%93Toa_Payoh_Group_Representation_Constituency "Bishan–Toa Payoh Group Representation Constituency"), he would continue in politics, health permitting.[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-95) He also questioned the margin of votes in Potong Pasir, contested by his wife, saying there was "funny business" happening. A petition calling for a by-election in the constituency was started by SPP supporters and Potong Pasir residents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-96)

The [National Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_Party_(Singapore) "National Solidarity Party (Singapore)"), which contested the most seats of all opposition parties, admitted it may have taken on too much, with its leader [Goh Meng Seng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Meng_Seng "Goh Meng Seng") telling reporters that he would be "personally responsible" for the party's failure to win a single seat.[&#8202;](#ref-back-opposition_reaction_cna-1)[\[87\]](#refnote-opposition_reaction_cna) Its star candidate, [Nicole Seah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Seah "Nicole Seah"), said Singaporeans now had to unite as a country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-97) Seah, who contested in Marine Parade, also said there was "so much that needs to be done", and that she would continue her work in the area despite her team's defeat.[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-98) On 26 June, Goh announced his resignation from the party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-99)[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-100)

The leader of the newest opposition party contesting the elections, the [Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Party_(Singapore) "Reform Party (Singapore)")'s [Kenneth Jeyaretnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Jeyaretnam "Kenneth Jeyaretnam"), described his party as having "learnt a lot" and said they had "done very well", as the first new party in over 20 years. He added that the party was "very happy" at its result in [West Coast Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Group_Representation_Constituency "West Coast Group Representation Constituency"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-opposition_reaction_cna-2)[\[87\]](#refnote-opposition_reaction_cna) and that its second team had done "creditably" in [Ang Mo Kio Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Mo_Kio_Group_Representation_Constituency "Ang Mo Kio Group Representation Constituency"). Jeyaretnam also said the team being able to win the votes it did despite being a new party meant that its "core values resonate with the voters".[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-101)

The worst-performing party at the polls was the [Singapore Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Alliance "Singapore Democratic Alliance"), whose secretary-general [Desmond Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Lim "Desmond Lim") polled under 5 percent of votes in [Punggol East Single Member Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punggol_East_Single_Member_Constituency "Punggol East Single Member Constituency") (the sole three-cornered contest), being the only candidate to lost his S$16,000 election deposit this election. He said voters had voted based on brand name, as the other opposition candidate in the ward was from the WP. The SDA also contested [Pasir Ris–Punggol Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasir_Ris%E2%80%93Punggol_Group_Representation_Constituency "Pasir Ris–Punggol Group Representation Constituency"), and Lim said the party was "very happy" at its positive vote swing from 2006 of over 4 percent. However, the SDA's anchorman in the constituency Harminder Pal Singh described the loss as a "time for painful reflection" and said the party would work harder to win more votes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-102)

### Foreign reactions

At an [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") heads-of-state meeting in [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), leaders of ASEAN nations reportedly told [S. Jayakumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Jayakumar_(Singaporean_politician) "S. Jayakumar (Singaporean politician)"), Singapore's representative at the event, that they were "saddened, disappointed and surprised" at the news that foreign minister Yeo had been defeated, according to the state-run Straits Times,[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-103) while at the same meeting the [Prime Minister of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Malaysia "Prime Minister of Malaysia"), [Najib Tun Razak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najib_Razak "Najib Razak"), said the PAP's win would mean a continuity in understanding between the Malaysian and Singaporean governments on bilateral issues.[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-104) The BBC described it as a landmark result.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-105)

### Non-Constituency Member of Parliament offers

Three Non-Constituency Members of Parliament (NCMP) seats were offered after the election to the top three losing opposition candidates, all of which were confirmed on 16 May.[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-106) The Singapore People's Party accepted the seat for [Lina Loh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Loh "Lina Loh"), ensuring that the Chiam family retained representation in Parliament. While Chiam See Tong has said he is opposed to the scheme, the SPP reasoned that it was "critical" to ensure an "alternative voice in Parliament", to allow the party to "remain engaged in national issues", and to be publicly visible until the next election due by 2016. Loh also pointed out that she was "influenced by the wishes of Potong Pasir residents" and she accepted the post as her losing margin was too small.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-107)

The Workers' Party was offered the final two NCMP seats for having the second and third best performing losing candidates, which it accepted despite Low also disputing the scheme. [Yee Jenn Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yee_Jenn_Jong "Yee Jenn Jong") was thus appointed for his performance in Joo Chiat SMC, but as it had to choose one member from the East Coast GRC team, the younger [Gerald Giam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Giam "Gerald Giam") was chosen over team leader and party treasurer Eric Tan as part of its leadership renewal process. Tan resigned from the party, citing his disagreement with the appointment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-108)

The decision for the NCMP later become a flashpoint topic in the [2012 Hougang by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Hougang_by-election "2012 Hougang by-election") took place one year later after information containing details of the CEC's decision was [leaked online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_leak "Data leak"); it was revealed where the CEC conducted a [secret ballot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_ballot "Secret ballot") to decide the NCMP, where Giam was chosen as NCMP with seven votes, while Tan received five, and [Png Eng Huat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Png_Eng_Huat "Png Eng Huat"), the eventual MP-elect for Hougang, received with one despite him being opposed of the NCMP scheme. The WP apologized to the media following the leak and acknowledged the incident, but decide not to proceed further.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NCMP-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-NCMP)

### By-elections

### Exit poll

On 3 September, Joseph Ong Chor Teck was arrested for conducting [exit poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_poll "Exit poll") through Temasek Review, a socio-political website, ahead of the election. ## Reactions

### Rise and use of social media

The election saw wider use of the internet and [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") compared to 2006. Many turned to online platforms to bypass [mass media in Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Singapore "Mass media in Singapore"), which was seen as heavily favouring the ruling PAP. There was a public perception that major state-owned newspapers and broadcasters supported the party's stance and that the electoral system placed opposition parties at a disadvantage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-economist-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-economist) Alternative media was limited until the 2000s, when news sites, blogs and social platforms such as [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") and [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") emerged. These gained some influence by 2006 and became especially prominent after 2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNNGoFang-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-CNNGoFang) According to _[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")_, the PAP's aggressive modernisation of Singapore created "one of the world's most wired societies," leading to new media that "transformed" the electoral scene in Singapore. Characterising the state-run mainstream press as "docile", the British magazine also argued that this also forced significantly more news coverage of the opposition than in previous elections, since the mainstream media feared their readership deserting them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-economist-2)[\[38\]](#refnote-economist) One blogger from [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") claimed, "Thanks to social media, it doesn't matter that the country's largely state-run media leans towards reporting the actions of the PAP, no one's reading anyway."[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNNGoFang-1)[\[115\]](#refnote-CNNGoFang) _The Economist_ however was more cynical in its analysis of the election: "in Singapore, winning 7% of parliamentary seats is tantamount to an opposition triumph".[&#8202;](#ref-back-economist-3)[\[38\]](#refnote-economist)

### Reactions to Aljunied GRC's result

Traditionally regarded as one of the PAP "fortresses", this marked the first instance where the opposition captured a GRC since the scheme was formed in 1988, five elections prior, where the oppositions had unsuccessfully done so due to ostensible requirements of ensuring minority representation in parliament and extensive resources. GRCs comprised about 86% of the seats, but oppositions in previous elections would only contest "less than half the seats". The election saw the most extensive use of co-ordination to avoid "three-cornered fights" and was also notable for seeing "two veteran MPs" making immense risks by choosing to contest in GRCs rather than their traditional SMC safe seats.[&#8202;](#ref-back-economist-4)[\[38\]](#refnote-economist)

With the PAP's team defeated in Aljunied GRC, four of the five members, including Foreign Minister [George Yeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Yeo "George Yeo") and Minister in the Prime Minister's Office [Lim Hwee Hua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Hwee_Hua "Lim Hwee Hua"), announced their retirements from politics in separate news conferences given in the days after the election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-121)[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-122) George Yeo, who remained popular online and continued to have "a flood of support" after the election and had been repeatedly urged to contest the [next election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Singaporean_general_election "2015 Singaporean general election"), or even contest the [2011 Singaporean presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Singaporean_presidential_election "2011 Singaporean presidential election") turned his supporters down, declaring, "I'm a free spirit, and I don't think I'm temperamentally suited for such a job."[&#8202;](#ref-back-123-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-123) A fifth candidate, [Ong Ye Kung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Ye_Kung "Ong Ye Kung"), however, would continue to work in private sector and would later join their team in [Sembawang GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sembawang_GRC "Sembawang GRC") in the next election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-fb_about-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-fb_about)[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-125)[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-126)[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-127)

### Signals to the ruling party

The election results were widely used in national and international discussions that the population was trying to send a message to a ruling party that "can also come across as smug, arrogant and high-handed" despite a win margin of over 20%, which usually counts as a landslide victory for most democratic nations but has been one of the narrowest margins since 1965.[&#8202;](#ref-back-economist-5)[\[38\]](#refnote-economist) The last election with a similarly narrow victory occurred in the [1963 Singapore general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Singaporean_general_election "1963 Singaporean general election"), when the PAP's major opponent was the [Barisan Sosialis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Sosialis "Barisan Sosialis")—which in itself was a splinter group formed from the leftist wing of the PAP, where it had comprised 80% of the PAP grassroots membership, 35 out of the PAP's 51 branch committees and 19 of its 23 organising secretaries. According to the Economist, Singaporeans would prefer not to have an alternative government but a humbler one, as well as a "stronger opposition".

On 14 May, exactly a week after the election, Senior Minister [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong") and Minister Mentor [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") announced in a joint statement that they would be quitting the country's Cabinet, saying it was time for a "team of younger ministers" to "engage with this young generation in shaping the future of Singapore."[&#8202;](#ref-back-128-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-128) In a similar analysis by Bloomberg, the resignations and the ensuing cabinet reshuffle were the actions of a ruling party "seeking to overhaul its image with voters" whose "narrowest election victory on record signaled a shortfall in support among younger voters".[&#8202;](#ref-back-129-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-129) Analysts such as [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup "Citigroup") economist Kit Wei Zheng believed that Lee had somewhat contributed to the PAP's lackluster performance in a local context.[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-130)

Lee Kuan Yew was also quoted as saying that a younger generation was required to "carry Singapore forward in a more difficult and complex situation" while Lee Hsien Loong declared the party "would change the way it governs" and do some "soul-searching". [Singapore Management University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Management_University "Singapore Management University") (SMU) professor [Eugene Tan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Tan "Eugene Tan") said that "\[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[The PAP\] will have to demonstrate that it remains a mass movement, and not \[Lee Kuan Yew\]'s alter ego," noting that younger Singaporeans do not see Lee Kuan Yew with the same "godlike perception" as older Singaporeans born before 1980.[\[126\]](#refnote-131)

With the vacation of the [Senior Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Minister_of_Singapore "Senior Minister of Singapore") roles, the role remained vacant for the next eight years until on 1 May 2019 following [Heng Swee Keat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heng_Swee_Keat "Heng Swee Keat")'s ascension of [Deputy Prime Minister of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Deputy Prime Minister of Singapore"). Goh was also conferred the title of " [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") Senior Minister".[&#8202;](#ref-back-132-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-132)
