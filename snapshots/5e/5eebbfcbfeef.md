## News

12 December 2024

### **Discover the decisive steps we are taking to ensure research integrity**

At _Chemosphere,_ we evaluate many thousands of submissions each year to identify the most original and high quality for publication. But for those of us on the editorial team, it’s equally important that the papers we accept comply with the highest standards of research integrity and publishing ethics.

To help us achieve this, we monitor our publication process, proactively examining articles at every stage. Over the past year, this has resulted in concerns being raised over a number of our published papers, which we have immediately referred to Elsevier's Research Integrity & Publishing Ethics Centre of Expertise. Together with the journal editors, the Centre has conducted a thorough investigation of the flagged papers, in line with Elsevier's policies and the principles of the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE).

As the investigations approach a conclusion, we would like to share with you the steps we have devised to correct the published record and improve our editorial processes.

*   We will retract any articles proven by the investigation to show potential breaches of _Chemosphere_'s publishing policies, including unusual authorship changes, compromised or manipulated peer-review processes, and undisclosed conflicts of interest. And we offer our apologies to our readers that these breaches were not detected during the submission and editorial review process.
*   Where we suspect malpractice but lack conclusive proof, we will publish an Expression of Concern alongside the article.
*   We have moved the journal from single-anonymized to double-anonymized peer review to reduce opportunities for unethical author and reviewer behavior.
*   We have introduced more robust vetting and scrutiny of special issue proposals, guest editors and special issue submissions as they pass through peer review.
*   We are providing editors with extra training and resources, including access to new investigative and diagnostic tools designed to screen submitted manuscripts for integrity concerns.

We believe that these measures will help us regain the standard of research integrity that has always been so important to us. And we won’t hesitate to review and amend this list, if required.

Our sincere thanks to all the Editors and reviewers who always work so hard to uphold the highest standards. However, even though we all do our upmost to minimize errors, it is always possible that some may slip through. If you have any suggested corrections or concerns in relation to a _Chemosphere_ article, please do contact the journal editorial team – we really welcome your input.

And a big thank you too to all our readers and authors. Without your incredible contributions and ongoing support, there would be no _Chemosphere._ We look forward to continuing our relationship as the journal grows and evolves.
