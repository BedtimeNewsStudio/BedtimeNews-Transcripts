The **Toei Asakusa Line** (都営浅草線, *Toei Asakusa-sen*) is a subway line in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo"), Japan, operated by the municipal subway operator [Toei Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toei_Subway "Toei Subway"). The line runs between [Nishi-magome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-magome_Station "Nishi-magome Station") in [Ōta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ōta,_Tokyo "Ōta, Tokyo") and [Oshiage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshiage_Station "Oshiage Station") in [Sumida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumida,_Tokyo "Sumida, Tokyo"). The line is named after the [Asakusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asakusa "Asakusa") district, a cultural center of Tokyo, under which it passes.

The Asakusa Line was the first subway line in Japan to offer [through services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_train "Through train") with a [private railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_railway "Private railway"). Today, it has more through services to other lines than any other subway line in Tokyo. [Keikyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikyu "Keikyu") operates through trains on the [Keikyu Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikyu_Main_Line "Keikyu Main Line") to [Misakiguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misakiguchi_Station "Misakiguchi Station") and the [Keikyu Airport Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikyu_Airport_Line "Keikyu Airport Line") to [Haneda Airport Domestic Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport_Domestic_Terminal_Station "Haneda Airport Domestic Terminal Station"). [Keisei Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Electric_Railway "Keisei Electric Railway") operates through trains on the [Keisei Oshiage Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Oshiage_Line "Keisei Oshiage Line") to [Inba-Nihon-Idai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inba-Nihon-Idai_Station "Inba-Nihon-Idai Station") and the [Keisei Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Main_Line "Keisei Main Line") to [Narita Airport Terminal 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita_Airport_Terminal_1_Station "Narita Airport Terminal 1 Station"), and [Shibayama Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibayama_Railway "Shibayama Railway") runs trains via the Keisei Main Line and the [Shibayama Railway Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibayama_Railway_Line "Shibayama Railway Line") to [Shibayama-Chiyoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibayama-Chiyoda_Station "Shibayama-Chiyoda Station"). Via its through services with Keisei and Keikyu, the Asakusa line is the only train line that offers a direct connection between Tokyo's two main airports.

The Asakusa Line is often split into two routes: Oshiage–Sengakuji and Sengakuji–Nishi-magome; only some trains make all station stops on the line, as many trains travel on the Keikyu Main Line south of Sengakuji.

On maps and signboards, the line is shown in the color rose. Stations carry the letter "A" followed by a two-digit number inside a more reddish vermilion circle.

In fiscal year 2023, the Asakusa Line was Toei's most profitable line, earning 8.67 billion yen in surplus (a 26.4% profit margin). It served 683,003 passengers on average per day, the third highest in the Toei network.[[2]](#cite_note-2)

## Services

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Toei_Asakusa_Line&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Services")]

* Local (普通 *futsū*) trains operate between Nishi-Magome and Sengakuji approximately every ten minutes and are timed to connect to Keikyu through service trains at Sengakuji.
* Rapid service (快速 *kaisoku*) trains operate between Nishi-Magome and [Keisei Sakura Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Sakura_Station "Keisei Sakura Station") approximately every twenty minutes. They make all station stops on the Asakusa Line.
* Limited Express (快特 *kaitoku*) trains operate approximately every twenty minutes. They generally use Keikyu rolling stock and have a southern terminus at [Misakiguchi Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misakiguchi_Station "Misakiguchi Station") or [Keikyu Kurihama Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikyu_Kurihama_Station "Keikyu Kurihama Station"). They operate as Limited Express trains only on the Keikyu Line, and provide local service on the Asakusa Line and local/rapid services on the Keisei Oshiage Line. Their northern terminus is generally either [Aoto Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoto_Station "Aoto Station") or [Keisei Takasago Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Takasago_Station "Keisei Takasago Station"), but select trains operate to [Narita International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita_International_Airport "Narita International Airport").
* Limited Express (快特 *kaitoku*) trains operate approximately every twenty minutes and make all stops (local service) on the Asakusa Line, providing Limited Express service on the Keikyu Line between Sengakuji and [Haneda Airport Domestic Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport_Domestic_Terminal_Station "Haneda Airport Domestic Terminal Station"). Their northern terminus is usually either [Inzai-Makinohara Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inzai-Makinohara_Station "Inzai-Makinohara Station") or [Inba-Nihon-Idai Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inba-Nihon-Idai_Station "Inba-Nihon-Idai Station") on the [Hokuso Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuso_Railway "Hokuso Railway").
* Airport Limited Express (エアポート快特 *eapōto kaitoku*) trains operate approximately every twenty minutes, and skip certain stations while operating on the Asakusa Line. Their northern terminus alternates between "Access Express" (アクセス特急 *akusesu tokkyū*) service to [Narita International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita_International_Airport "Narita International Airport") and Limited Express service to either Aoto or Takasago. The total travel time from Haneda Airport to Narita Airport on this train is approximately one hour and 46 minutes.
* Express (急行 *kyūkō*) Trains operate as Express trains only on the Keikyu Line, and provide local services on the Asakusa Line and the Keisei Line. These trains only operate southwards. Their southern terminus is usually [Haneda Airport Domestic Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport_Domestic_Terminal_Station "Haneda Airport Domestic Terminal Station"). However, there is one train on weekends that operates towards [Zushi-Hayama Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zushi-Hayama_Station "Zushi-Hayama Station") (As of 8 October 2025). Service for this train will start at [Keisei Takasago Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Takasago_Station "Keisei Takasago Station") and will arrive at [Oshiage Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshiage_Station "Oshiage Station") at 22:55.

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Toei_Asakusa_Line&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")]

### Planning (1920–1941)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Toei_Asakusa_Line&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Planning (1920–1941)")]

The original plan for what is now the Asakusa Line was included in a report Tokyo City Notification No. 2 of 1920 ([大正9年東京市告示第2号](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大正9年東京市告示第2号 "ja:大正9年東京市告示第2号")) in 1920. The proposal outlined a route as Line 1 connecting “[Shinagawa Yatsuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitashinagawa_Station "Kitashinagawa Station") – [Shimbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimbashi_Station "Shimbashi Station") – [Tsukiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukiji_Station "Tsukiji Station") – [Ryogoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryōgoku_Station "Ryōgoku Station") West – [Kaminarimon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaminarimon "Kaminarimon") – [Oshiage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshiage_Station "Oshiage Station")”.[[3]](#cite_note-:0-3)

A construction patent for this route was granted to the [Tokyo Underground Light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Underground_Railway "Tokyo Underground Railway") and Tokyo Railway (東京鉄道). However, following the [Great Kantō Earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Kantō_Earthquake "Great Kantō Earthquake") of 1923, the patent was revoked along with those for other planned lines because construction had not yet commenced.

On September 1, 1941, following the establishment of the [Teito Rapid Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teito_Rapid_Transit_Authority "Teito Rapid Transit Authority"), all route licenses held by [Tokyo City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_City "Tokyo City"), the [Tokyo Underground Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Underground_Railway "Tokyo Underground Railway"), the [Tokyo Rapid Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Rapid_Railway "Tokyo Rapid Railway"), and the Keihin Underground Railway were transferred to the Teito Subway in exchange for a fee.[[3]](#cite_note-:0-3)

The routes for which licenses had been granted as of 1941 and which were later taken over for the construction of the Asakusa Line are as follows:[[3]](#cite_note-:0-3)

* Tokyo Underground Railway: [Mita nichome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mita_Station "Mita Station") – [Gotanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotanda_Station "Gotanda Station") – [Magome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magome_Station "Magome Station")
* Keihin Underground Railway: Shimbashi – [Shinagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinagawa_Station "Shinagawa Station")
* Tokyo Rapid Railway: Shimbashi – [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Station "Tokyo Station")

### Construction and opening

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Toei_Asakusa_Line&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Construction and opening")]

The line number is Line 1, because it was technically the first subway line in Tokyo to be planned in the 1920s as an underground route connecting [Keikyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikyu "Keikyu") and [Keisei Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisei_Electric_Railway "Keisei Electric Railway") via [Shinagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinagawa_Station "Shinagawa Station"), eventually allowing for through trains between these two railways. In its original plan form, the line would have actually bypassed [Asakusa Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asakusa_Station_(Tokyo_Metro,_Toei,_Tobu) "Asakusa Station (Tokyo Metro, Toei, Tobu)") entirely. However, the plan was changed to take advantage of the existing [Tobu Isesaki Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobu_Isesaki_Line "Tobu Isesaki Line") (section now named as the [Tobu Skytree Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobu_Skytree_Line "Tobu Skytree Line")) and [Tokyo Metro Ginza Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metro_Ginza_Line "Tokyo Metro Ginza Line") connections at Asakusa.

The Toei Asakusa Line was the first subway line constructed by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government.

Construction of this line began on 27 August 1956 after years of delays, and the initial 3.2 km (2.0 mi) segment between Oshiage and Asakusabashi opened on 4 December 1960. The line then opened in stages from north to south:

* May 1962: Asakusabashi to Higashi-Nihombashi
* September 1962: Higashi-Nihombashi to Ningyōchō
* February 1963: Ningyōchō to Higashi-Ginza
* December 1963: Higashi-Ginza to Shimbashi
* October 1964: Shimbashi to Daimon
* June 1968: Daimon to Sengakuji (Through service with Keikyū begins)
* 15 November 1968: Sengakuji to Nishi-Magome

The line was named Asakusa Line on 1 July 1978.[[4]](#cite_note-4)

From 1998 to 2002, the Asakusa Line was used as part of a rail connection between Tokyo's two major airports, [Haneda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport "Haneda Airport") and [Narita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita_International_Airport "Narita International Airport"). While a few trains still run between the airports, the service has greatly diminished in frequency since 2002.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In 2005, a research group of government, metropolitan and railway company officials proposed that the Asakusa Line be connected to [Tokyo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Station "Tokyo Station") via a spur to the north of [Takarachō Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takarachō_Station "Takarachō Station"). This would provide Tokyo Station's first direct connection to the Toei subway network. It would also make it possible to reach Haneda Airport in 25 minutes (versus 35 minutes today) and Narita Airport in 40 minutes (versus 57 minutes today).[[5]](#cite_note-5) This plan has yet to be finalized or formally adopted. Authorities are re-considering a similar plan as part of the infrastructure improvements for the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics "2020 Summer Olympics"); the proposed line would cut travel time to Haneda from 30 minutes to 18 minutes, and to Narita from 55 minutes to 36 minutes, at a total cost of around 400 billion yen.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

## Rolling stock

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Toei_Asakusa_Line&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Rolling stock")]

A variety of rolling stock is in use due to the large number of through service operators on the line, all of which use [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge "Standard gauge") tracks and [1,500 V DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_railway_electrification_systems#1,500_V_DC "List of railway electrification systems") electrification via [overhead lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_line "Overhead line"). Currently, six operators run trains onto the Asakusa Line, the most of any Tokyo subway line, and the line is unique as the only subway line in Tokyo with through services onto standard gauge railways (all other through services are with narrow gauge lines).
