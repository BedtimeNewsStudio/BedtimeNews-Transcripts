The **Shuttle-C** was a study by [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") to turn the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") launch stack into a dedicated uncrewed cargo launcher.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-:0) The [Space Shuttle external tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_external_tank "Space Shuttle external tank") and [Space Shuttle Solid Rocket Boosters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Solid_Rocket_Booster "Space Shuttle Solid Rocket Booster") (SRBs) would be combined with a cargo module to take the place of the [Shuttle orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_orbiter "Space Shuttle orbiter") and include the [main engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RS-25 "RS-25").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-:0) Various Shuttle-C concepts were investigated between 1984 and 1995.[&#8202;](#ref-back-globalsec-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-globalsec)

The Shuttle-C concept would theoretically cut development costs for a heavy launch vehicle by re-using technology developed for the shuttle program. End-of-life and Space Shuttle hardware would also have been used. One proposal even involved converting _[Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia "Space Shuttle Columbia")_ or _[Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Enterprise "Space Shuttle Enterprise")_ into a single-use cargo launcher. Before the [loss of Space Shuttle _Challenger_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster"), NASA had expected about 24 shuttle flights a year. In the aftermath of the _Challenger_ incident, it became clear that this launch rate was not feasible for a variety of reasons.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rogers-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-rogers) With the Shuttle-C, it was thought that the lower maintenance and safety requirements for the uncrewed vehicle would allow a higher flight rate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-harsh-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-harsh)[&#8202;](#ref-back-eudy-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-eudy)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Shuttle-C_with_ILREC_Piloted_Lander.png/250px-Shuttle-C_with_ILREC_Piloted_Lander.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Shuttle-C_with_ILREC_Piloted_Lander.png) Shuttle-C with ILREC Piloted Lander (launch shroud not visible)

The Shuttle-C would have been the main crew launch vehicle for the ILREC Piloted Lander in the [International Lunar Resources Exploration Program.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Resources_Exploration_Concept "International Lunar Resources Exploration Concept")

In the early 1990s, NASA engineers planning a crewed mission to [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars "Mars") included a Shuttle-C design to launch six non-reusable, 80-ton segments to create two Mars ships in [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth") orbit. After President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") called for the end of the Space Shuttle by 2010, these proposed configurations were put aside.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

As early as the 1970s, some iteration of the Shuttle-C had been studied (Encyclopedia Astronautica mentions it as "Class 1 SDV").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-:0) It was discussed in [Gerard O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_K._O'Neill "Gerard K. O'Neill")'s 1976 book _[The High Frontier: Human Colonies in Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_High_Frontier:_Human_Colonies_in_Space "The High Frontier: Human Colonies in Space")_, with artwork by [Don Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Davis_(artist) "Don Davis (artist)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)
