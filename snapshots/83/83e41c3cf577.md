_**United States v. Nicolás Maduro Moros et al.**_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-fn1-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-fn1) originally filed under _**United States v. Carvajal-Barrios**_, is a federal [criminal case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_procedure "Criminal procedure") filed against former [president of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Venezuela "President of Venezuela") [Nicolás Maduro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro "Nicolás Maduro"), and various senior Venezuelan officials by the [United States Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice "United States Department of Justice") (DOJ) in the [United States District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York "United States District Court for the Southern District of New York") in 2011 and unsealed on March 26, 2020. The indictment alleged that Maduro and his associates conspired with Colombian [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla "Guerrilla") groups to traffic [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine") into the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") as part of what U.S. authorities termed a [narcoterrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcoterrorism "Narcoterrorism") conspiracy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt2020-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-nyt2020)[&#8202;](#ref-back-npr2020-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-npr2020)

The indictment initially included [Hugo Carvajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Carvajal "Hugo Carvajal"), who was arrested in Spain in 2021 and extradited to the United States on a warrant for [drug trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_trafficking "Drug trafficking") in 2014. Carvajal pleaded guilty in June 2025.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-4) [Clíver Alcalá Cordones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%ADver_Alcal%C3%A1_Cordones "Clíver Alcalá Cordones"), who was also indicted, surrendered to U.S. agents in Colombia and, in 2023, pleaded guilty to providing aid to the Colombian [Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Armed_Forces_of_Colombia "Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia") (FARC).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reuters30June23-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-Reuters30June23)

On January 3, 2026, a superseding indictment was unsealed after Maduro and his wife, [Cilia Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilia_Flores "Cilia Flores"), were [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_strikes_in_Venezuela#Abduction_of_Maduro "2026 United States strikes in Venezuela") during a [U.S. military operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_strikes_in_Venezuela "2026 United States strikes in Venezuela") in [Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas "Caracas") supported by the DOJ and [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") (CIA).[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-:2) Maduro and Flores were transported to the United States and taken into federal custody in New York City, and are currently detained at the [Metropolitan Detention Center in Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Detention_Center,_Brooklyn "Metropolitan Detention Center, Brooklyn").[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-auto1) They have pleaded not guilty.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-:1)

Maduro and Flores were set to return to court on June 30, 2026, but a judge delayed their return to court until July 22, 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-10)

On July 22, 2026, Judge [Alvin Hellerstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Hellerstein "Alvin Hellerstein") set the trial date for June 1, 2027, at the request of lawyers on both sides.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-11)

## Background

Venezuela was identified by the [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime "United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime") in 2011 as a major cocaine transit country from Colombia to North America and Europe.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-12):2

On November 18, 2016, Franqui Francisco Flores de Freitas and Efrain Antonio Campo Flores, both nephews of [Cilia Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilia_Flores "Cilia Flores"), were [found guilty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_v._Campo_and_Flores "United States of America v. Campo and Flores") after their arrest in Haiti for cocaine smuggling. They were sentenced on December 14, 2017, to 18 years in a U.S. federal prison.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-14)

## Indictment

On March 26, 2020, [US Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Attorney_General "US Attorney General") [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr "William Barr") announced charges against Nicolas Maduro and several others in the Venezuelan leadership, which he called a "cesspit of corruption".[&#8202;](#ref-back-npr2020-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-npr2020) Barr alleged Maduro was working with the [Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Armed_Forces_of_Colombia "Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia") (FARC) to smuggle cocaine to the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-npr2020-2)[\[2\]](#refnote-npr2020) The charges were filed primarily in the [United States District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York "United States District Court for the Southern District of New York") (SDNY),[&#8202;](#ref-back-LucasNPR050126-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-LucasNPR050126) with related cases in the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia "District of Columbia") and the [Southern District of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_District_of_Florida "Southern District of Florida").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

The only other sitting heads of state to be criminally charged by U.S. prosecutors were Panama's [Manuel Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Noriega "Manuel Noriega") and Libya's [Muammar Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muammar_Gaddafi "Muammar Gaddafi").[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt2020-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-nyt2020)

In a virtual press conference, United States Attorney General [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr "William Barr") described Venezuela under Maduro's rule as a country "plagued by criminality and corruption".[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-16) He accused Maduro and his allies of conspiring with the [FARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FARC "FARC") to ship tons of cocaine into the United States to "wage a health war on American citizens".[&#8202;](#ref-back-npr2020-3)[\[2\]](#refnote-npr2020)

According to prosecutors, Venezuelan leaders and the FARC faction organized an "air bridge" from Venezuelan military airbases to Central America and a parallel maritime route through the Caribbean. These operations allegedly moved hundreds of tons of cocaine annually and generated billions of dollars in illicit revenue for both the guerrilla groups and Venezuela's ruling elite.[&#8202;](#ref-back-npr2020-4)[\[2\]](#refnote-npr2020)

The indictment included charges of narco-terrorism, conspiracy to commit narco-terrorism, drug trafficking, money laundering, and corruption. Prosecutors alleged that Maduro and his associates directed Venezuelan military and intelligence resources to protect cocaine shipments, provide armed escorts for flights, and allow clandestine airstrips to be used for trafficking operations. Some defendants were also accused of arranging weapons transfers to FARC in exchange for drugs, thereby tying the conspiracy to both narcotics and armed conflict.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Hours after the announcement, the [United States Department of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_State "United States Department of State") offered a reward of $15 million for information leading to Maduro's arrest and up to $10 million for several of his top associates.[&#8202;](#ref-back-npr2020-5)[\[2\]](#refnote-npr2020) The indictment was unsealed the same day it was filed, and the DOJ released wanted posters for Maduro and other defendants.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-17) The decision to go public immediately, despite knowing the defendants were beyond U.S. jurisdiction, was interpreted as both a legal maneuver to strengthen U.S. extradition efforts abroad and a political signal of Washington's intent to escalate pressure on the Venezuelan leadership.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

A superseding indictment of January 3, 2026, included the names of Maduro, his wife [Cilia Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilia_Flores "Cilia Flores"), his son [Nicolas Maduro Guerra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro_Guerra "Nicolás Maduro Guerra"), Venezuelan Interior Minister [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello "Diosdado Cabello"), former interior minister [Ramon Rodriguez Chacin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Chac%C3%ADn "Ramón Rodríguez Chacín"), and the alleged leader of [Tren de Aragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tren_de_Aragua "Tren de Aragua"), [Niño Guerrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C3%B1o_Guerrero "Niño Guerrero").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-:2)

## Developments

### Surrender of Clíver Alcalá Cordones in Colombia

One day after the indictment was announced, retired Venezuelan general [Clíver Alcalá Cordones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%ADver_Alcal%C3%A1_Cordones "Clíver Alcalá Cordones") surrendered to Colombian authorities in [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla "Barranquilla") and was transferred to the United States. In 2023, Alcalá pleaded guilty to providing material support to a terrorist organization and to illicit arms transfers, while prosecutors dropped broader narcotics charges. His guilty plea marked the first conviction among those indicted in the case and demonstrated the U.S. willingness to pursue prosecutions even when the top defendants remained outside its jurisdiction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-29)

### Operation Gideon

Main article: [Operation Gideon (2020)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gideon_(2020) "Operation Gideon (2020)")

In early May 2020, a failed armed incursion into Venezuela known as _[Operation Gideon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Gideon_(2020) "Operation Gideon (2020)")_ was launched by a group of Venezuelan dissidents and U.S. private security contractors. The mission aimed to capture Maduro and other senior officials and transport them to the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-30)

Media investigations and testimonies from those involved indicated that the operation was at least partly motivated by the rewards announced by the U.S. government in March 2020, which offered up to $15 million for information leading to Maduro's arrest and lesser sums for several of his associates.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-32)

According to subsequent reports, some participants believed that delivering Maduro to U.S. authorities would secure the bounty and provide international legitimacy for their actions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-33) The Venezuelan government quickly defeated the incursion, killing several participants and arresting dozens more, including two former U.S. special forces soldiers. Maduro cited the incident as proof that the United States was seeking his removal through both legal and covert military means.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-34)

### 2021 arrest of Hugo Carvajal

Former intelligence chief [Hugo Carvajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Carvajal "Hugo Carvajal") initially evaded arrest after fleeing Venezuela in 2019, after breaking ties with Maduro. He was apprehended in Madrid in September 2021 by the [National Police Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Corps_(Spain) "National Police Corps (Spain)") following years in hiding. After a lengthy extradition battle in Spanish courts, Carvajal was extradited to the United States in July 2023 and arraigned before the Southern District of New York. He initially pleaded not guilty but, in June 2025, pleaded guilty to narco-terrorism and drug trafficking charges. His plea was considered a major breakthrough for U.S. prosecutors, who had long sought his testimony regarding ties between Venezuelan officials and Colombian guerrilla groups.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-35)

### 2024 presidential election and aftermath

The [July 2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Venezuelan_presidential_election "2024 Venezuelan presidential election") significantly intensified international pressure on Nicolás Maduro's government. Opposition candidate [Edmundo González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmundo_Gonz%C3%A1lez "Edmundo González") claimed victory based on precinct-level tallies and independent monitoring, while the government-controlled [National Electoral Council (CNE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Electoral_Council_(Venezuela) "National Electoral Council (Venezuela)") declared Maduro the winner. The disputed outcome triggered nationwide protests, which were met with [repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Tun_Tun "Operation Tun Tun") by Venezuelan security forces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-36)

The United States, the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"), and several Latin American governments, including Brazil, Colombia, and Chile, denounced the election as fraudulent and refused to recognize the CNE's declaration of Maduro's victory. They cited reports of voting irregularities, the disqualification of opposition candidates, intimidation of voters, and concerns raised by international observers. Human rights organizations further documented arbitrary arrests and violent repression of protesters in the aftermath.
Despite widespread condemnation, Maduro was [inaugurated on January 10, 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_inauguration_of_Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro "Third inauguration of Nicolás Maduro"), for a third term. His swearing-in deepened Venezuela's [political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Venezuelan_political_crisis "2024 Venezuelan political crisis"), worsened relations with democratic governments across the region, and led to the imposition of additional [international sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_during_the_Venezuelan_crisis "Sanctions during the Venezuelan crisis"). The disputed election also galvanized the opposition, which continued to call for a transitional government and renewed international mediation efforts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-37)

That same day, the U.S. State Department announced that the reward against Maduro was increased from $15 million to $25 million. National Security Council spokesperson [John Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kirby_(admiral) "John Kirby (admiral)") said the decision was part of "a concerted message of solidarity with the Venezuelan people," intended "to further elevate international efforts to maintain pressure on Maduro and his representatives."[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-38) Secretary of State [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken "Antony Blinken") reiterated that the United States "does not recognize Nicolás Maduro as the president of Venezuela." At the same time, U.S. Treasury Under Secretary Bradley Smith added that Washington stood with its "likeminded partners" in "solidarity with the people's vote for new leadership and rejects Maduro's fraudulent claim of victory."[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-39)

In addition to the bounty on Maduro, the U.S. government also increased the reward offers for several other senior Venezuelan officials. The reward for [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello "Diosdado Cabello"), widely regarded as the second-most-powerful figure in the ruling party, was raised from $10 million to $25 million. Similarly, a $15 million reward was announced for Defense Minister [Vladimir Padrino López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Padrino_L%C3%B3pez "Vladimir Padrino López"), reflecting Washington's accusations that he played a central role in enabling drug trafficking and maintaining the regime's grip on power.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-40)[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-41)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\]

### Asset seizures

As part of its enforcement efforts, the United States targeted assets allegedly linked to Maduro. In September 2024, U.S. authorities seized a [Dassault Falcon 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_900 "Dassault Falcon 900") EX jet that prosecutors said had been purchased through shell companies to evade sanctions. The aircraft, intercepted in the Dominican Republic and later flown to [Fort Lauderdale, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale,_Florida "Fort Lauderdale, Florida"), was presented as evidence of how Venezuela's leadership used front companies to conceal illicit financial transactions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-42)

### Capture of Maduro and transfer to U.S. custody

Main article: [2026 United States intervention in Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_intervention_in_Venezuela "2026 United States intervention in Venezuela")

On January 3, 2026, during U.S. military strikes on Venezuelan targets as part of [Operation Absolute Resolve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Absolute_Resolve "Operation Absolute Resolve"), U.S. special forces captured Nicolás Maduro and his wife, [Cilia Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilia_Flores "Cilia Flores"), at a residence in Caracas.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-43)[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-44) The couple was initially transferred to the [USS _Iwo Jima_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Iwo_Jima_(LHD-7) "USS Iwo Jima (LHD-7)") and subsequently flown to the U.S., arriving at [Stewart Air National Guard Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Air_National_Guard_Base "Stewart Air National Guard Base") on the same day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-45)[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-auto1)
Maduro was taken into federal custody to face the 2020 narco-terrorism charges in the Southern District of New York, with an initial court appearance scheduled for the following week.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-46) U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi confirmed that both Maduro and Flores faced charges for narco-terrorism conspiracy and cocaine importation conspiracy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CNN/superseding-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-CNN/superseding)[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-48)[&#8202;](#ref-back-LucasNPR050126-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-LucasNPR050126)

### First court appearance of Nicolás Maduro and Cilia Flores

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Outside_Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro%27s_arraignment_in_NYC_01.jpg/500px-Outside_Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro%27s_arraignment_in_NYC_01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Outside_Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro's_arraignment_in_NYC_01.jpg) Outside of the courthouse where Maduro was arraigned in New York City, January 5, 2026

On January 5, 2026, Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, appeared before U.S. District Judge [Alvin Hellerstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Hellerstein "Alvin Hellerstein").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-:1) They both entered not guilty pleas to numerous drug trafficking charges.[&#8202;](#ref-back-arraignmentandsomeoutcomes-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-arraignmentandsomeoutcomes) This marked the execution of the outstanding arrest warrant from the 2020 indictment and was a rare instance of a sitting head of state being apprehended by U.S. forces to face domestic criminal charges, drawing comparisons to the 1989 capture of Panamanian leader [Manuel Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Noriega "Manuel Noriega").[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-50)

Maduro is represented by lawyer [Barry Pollack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Pollack "Barry Pollack"), and Cilia Flores is represented by lawyer Mark Donnelly. Maduro said: "I'm innocent. I'm not guilty — I'm a decent man". Pollack told the court that his client has health issues that will require attention, while Flores's attorney said she may have fractured or severe bruising on her ribs. The next court proceeding was set for March 17.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-2)[\[7\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-51) Bruce Fein has also been involved as a lawyer in the case.[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-52)

Due to U.S. sanctions on Maduro imposed since 2017, the U.S. government will not allow the Venezuelan government to pay for Maduro's legal representation. Pollack said the [US Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Treasury "US Treasury") granted an exemption to the sanctions on January 9 but revoked it hours later. Pollack said, "The government of Venezuela has an obligation to pay Mr. Maduro's fees, Mr. Maduro has a legitimate expectation that the government of Venezuela would do so, and Mr. Maduro cannot otherwise afford counsel." According to Pollack, Flores may still receive government funds to cover her legal fees.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-53)

### Second court appearance of Maduro and Flores

On March 26, 2026, Maduro and Flores attended another hearing and returned to jail.[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-54) Maduro and Flores were set to return to court on June 30, 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-:4)

### Death of Niño Guerrero

In early June 2026, a military operation was carried out to kill Héctor Rusthenford Guerrero Flores, known as [Niño Guerrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C3%B1o_Guerrero "Niño Guerrero"), the leader of the Venezuelan criminal gang [Tren de Aragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tren_de_Aragua "Tren de Aragua"). Guerrero was one of the co-defendants named in the same indictment as Maduro. U.S. President Donald Trump announced that Guerrero was killed in an airstrike on June 12 in Venezuela conducted in coordination with Venezuelan authorities. Venezuelan officials confirmed their participation in the operation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-guerrero-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-guerrero)

### Third court appearance of Maduro and Flores

The third court appearance for Nicolás Maduro and Cilia Flores ultimately did not take place on June 30, 2026. On June 17, 2026, [U.S. District Judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_judge "United States federal judge") [Alvin K. Hellerstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_K._Hellerstein "Alvin K. Hellerstein") approved a joint request from the prosecution and the defense to postpone the hearing until July 22, 2026. The [U.S. Attorneys' Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Office_for_United_States_Attorneys "Executive Office for United States Attorneys") cited scheduling, transportation, and security logistics as the reasons for the delay, including the heavy demand on law enforcement resources associated with hosting the [2026 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_final "2026 FIFA World Cup final") in nearby [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey").[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-56)

On July 22, 2026 Nicolás Maduro and Cilia Flores finally appeared for their third court appearance, the hearing only lasted around 15 to 20 minutes and focused mainly on setting the timetable for their U.S. criminal case. Judge Alvin Hellerstein agreed to a June 1, 2027 trial date, while Maduro's defence said it plans to argue that he has [sovereign/head-of-state immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunity_from_prosecution_(international_law) "Immunity from prosecution (international law)") and challenge the legality of his capture. Both Maduro and Flores remained silent during the hearing and continue to plead not guilty to the drug-trafficking and narcoterrorism charges.[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-57)

A November 17 hearing has been scheduled.[&#8202;](#ref-back-June2027trial-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-June2027trial) Trial in federal court is scheduled for June 2027.[&#8202;](#ref-back-June2027trial-1)[\[57\]](#refnote-June2027trial)

The case was reassigned to [U.S. District Judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._District_Judge "U.S. District Judge") [Analisa Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analisa_Torres "Analisa Torres").[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-59)

## Indictment in Argentina

In Argentina, a case on Maduro's alleged crimes was filed in 2023. The Argentinian court issued an extradition request warrant for Maduro in 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-:3)

In February 2026, following his capture, Argentina formally requested Maduro's extradition. Argentine federal judge Sebastián Ramos signed a warrant for Maduro for [crimes against humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimes_against_humanity "Crimes against humanity"). Charges include torture, arbitrary detention, and enforced disappearance by the [Bolivarian National Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_National_Intelligence_Service "Bolivarian National Intelligence Service") (SEBIN). Extradition between Argentina and the United States is possible under a 1997 treaty and must be requested by the Argentine foreign minister.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[59\]](#refnote-:3)

## Record reward for Nicolás Maduro

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro_reward_poster_%2850_million%29.jpg/500px-Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro_reward_poster_%2850_million%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro_reward_poster_(50_million).jpg) Updated reward poster of Nicolás Maduro, issued just hours after Attorney General Bondi's announcement

On August 7, 2025, the [U.S. Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice "United States Department of Justice") and the [U.S. Department of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_State "United States Department of State") announced that the reward for information leading to the arrest and/or conviction of Nicolás Maduro had been increased to $50 million. The figure doubled the earlier bounty of $25 million offered in January 2025 and represented the largest reward ever placed by the United States on a foreign head of state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RewardOfferIncrease-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-RewardOfferIncrease)[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-61) The reward was revoked after Maduro's capture by U.S. forces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-auto1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-62)

Former U.S. Attorney General [Pam Bondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pam_Bondi "Pam Bondi") accused Maduro of collaborating with foreign terrorist and criminal organizations, including the [Tren de Aragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tren_de_Aragua "Tren de Aragua"), the [Sinaloa Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinaloa_Cartel "Sinaloa Cartel"), and the [Cartel of the Suns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartel_of_the_Suns "Cartel of the Suns"), to bring deadly violence into the United States. The claim against Tren de Aragua contradicted a U.S. intelligence assessment that found no evidence of widespread coordination between Maduro and TDA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-63)

In a video message, Bondi described Maduro as one of the "world's most notorious narco-traffickers" and a "threat to national security," declaring that under Trump's leadership, "Maduro will not escape justice, and he will be held accountable for his despicable crimes." She also provided the public with a hotline number to report tips to the [Drug Enforcement Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_Enforcement_Administration "Drug Enforcement Administration").[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-64)

On August 14, 2025, Bondi told [Rachel Campos-Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Campos-Duffy "Rachel Campos-Duffy") of [Fox News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News "Fox News") that the DOJ had seized approximately $700 million in assets linked to Maduro. These assets allegedly included multiple luxury homes in Florida, a mansion in the Dominican Republic, private jets, vehicles, a horse farm, jewelry, and large sums of cash. Bondi described Maduro's government as an "organized crime operation" that continued to function despite the seizures.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-65)[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-66)

After the capture of Nicolás Maduro, U.S. Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Rubio "Marco Rubio") added that no one would receive the reward. Rubio said, "He is a fugitive of the American justice with a $50 million reward. I guess we're saving $50 million now," to which Trump replied, "Don't let anybody claim it. Nobody deserves it but us."[&#8202;](#ref-back-arraignmentandsomeoutcomes-1)[\[48\]](#refnote-arraignmentandsomeoutcomes)

### Reactions

#### Venezuela

The Venezuelan government strongly condemned the U.S. decision to raise the bounty on Nicolás Maduro to $50 million. Foreign Minister [Yván Gil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yv%C3%A1n_Gil "Yván Gil") dismissed the announcement as a "crude political propaganda operation," claiming it was intended to distract from U.S. domestic controversies. Vice President [Delcy Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delcy_Rodr%C3%ADguez "Delcy Rodríguez") called Attorney General Pam Bondi's remarks "shameless" and a "ridiculous and cheap show," going further by suggesting that Bondi was "not in her right mind" due to pressure over the [Jeffrey Epstein client list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein_client_list "Jeffrey Epstein client list").[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-67)[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-68)

Maduro responded defiantly in a [Cadena nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadena_nacional "Cadena nacional") broadcast, in which he dared Trump to attempt arrest and warned Venezuela's retaliation could lead to the "end of the American empire." He accused Washington of waging psychological warfare against Venezuela and framed the bounty increase as proof that the country was "winning the global battle against imperialism."[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-69)

The announcement also sparked coordinated [propaganda campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_propaganda "Bolivarian propaganda") across Venezuela. State-controlled media portrayed the bounty increase as proof of U.S. "desperation" and framed it as part of Washington's long-standing efforts to destabilize the country. Pro-government rallies were organized in Caracas and other major cities, where demonstrators carried banners reading "Trump Hands Off Venezuela" and voiced support for Maduro's leadership. In a show of defiance, Maduro ordered military exercises in coastal states, presenting them as a symbolic demonstration of sovereignty and warning foreign adversaries that Venezuela would "defend every inch of its territory."[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-70)

The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Venezuela "National Assembly of Venezuela"), dominated by Maduro loyalists, also issued a formal resolution condemning the U.S. decision. Assembly president [Jorge Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Rodr%C3%ADguez_(Venezuelan_politician) "Jorge Rodríguez (Venezuelan politician)") described the reward increase as an illegal and desperate attempt to undermine Maduro and the Venezuelan people, emphasizing that lawmakers unanimously rejected the measure. Rodríguez, who also serves as the government's chief negotiator in talks with the U.S., argued that neither sanctions nor international pressure would succeed in reversing what he characterized as the popular mandate from the disputed 2024 election that re-elected Maduro.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-71)

#### United States

In the United States, the announcement drew mixed reactions. Supporters of President Trump framed the increase as a bold step to hold Maduro accountable and highlighted the asset seizures as proof of progress. Critics, however, mocked the reward as redundant, pointing out that "he's in Venezuela" and unlikely to be captured. Social media commentators accused the Trump administration of staging a political distraction from domestic controversies, particularly the [Jeffrey Epstein client list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein_client_list "Jeffrey Epstein client list"), which was dominating headlines at the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-72)

The Justice and State Departments defended the decision, stressing that the increased bounty was part of the long-standing [Narcotics Rewards Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotics_Rewards_Program "Narcotics Rewards Program") and consistent with U.S. counter-narcotics and counterterrorism policy. Attorney General [Pam Bondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pam_Bondi "Pam Bondi") argued that Maduro's alleged cooperation with international criminal networks justified extraordinary measures. Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Rubio "Marco Rubio") described the move as part of Washington's broader pressure strategy, intended to further isolate Maduro diplomatically and encourage defections within his government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-73)[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-74)[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-75)

In Congress, Republican lawmakers strongly supported the announcement. Representative [Byron Donalds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Donalds "Byron Donalds") described the reward increase as a necessary step to hold Maduro accountable for "narco-terrorism" and corruption.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-76) Earlier in 2025, Senators [Ted Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz "Ted Cruz"), [Rick Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Scott "Rick Scott"), and [Bill Cassidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Cassidy "Bill Cassidy") had introduced the STOP MADURO Act, which sought to raise the maximum potential reward for Maduro's arrest and conviction to $100 million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-77)

Democratic leaders did not comment directly on the $50 million reward, but had previously emphasized support for Venezuelans during the January 2025 increase to $25 million under former President Biden. In a joint statement, several Democratic foreign policy leaders in Congress condemned Maduro's authoritarianism while urging bipartisan cooperation to support the Venezuelan people.[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-78)

## Reactions to initial indictment in 2020

### Venezuela

In 2020, the Venezuelan government immediately denounced the indictments as an act of "imperialist aggression." [Nicolás Maduro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro "Nicolás Maduro") described the charges as a "fabricated plot" by Washington to justify regime change and destabilize Venezuela. In televised remarks, Maduro accused the Trump administration of using narcotics charges as a political weapon and vowed he would "never surrender to threats."[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto4-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-auto4)

Foreign Minister [Jorge Arreaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Arreaza "Jorge Arreaza") dismissed the indictment in March 2020 as "a new form of coup attempt," arguing that the U.S. was exploiting the global COVID-19 crisis to tighten pressure on Caracas.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-:0) The [Supreme Tribunal of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Tribunal_of_Justice_(Venezuela) "Supreme Tribunal of Justice (Venezuela)") also rejected the legal validity of the charges, declaring that Maduro enjoyed immunity as head of state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto5-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-auto5)\[ _[permanent dead link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Link_rot "Wikipedia:Link rot")_\]

Reactions among the Venezuelan opposition varied. Opposition leader [Juan Guaidó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Guaid%C3%B3 "Juan Guaidó"), who was recognized at the time by the United States and several other governments as the interim president, welcomed the indictments as evidence that "the world is finally holding Maduro and his accomplices accountable."[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-auto)\[ _[permanent dead link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Link_rot "Wikipedia:Link rot")_\] However, some civil society groups and humanitarian organizations warned that the timing of the charges risked complicating cooperation on urgent health and food needs during the pandemic.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[79\]](#refnote-:0)

### United States

In 2020, U.S. officials framed the indictments as part of a broader "maximum pressure" strategy against Maduro. Then-Attorney General [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr "William Barr") accused Venezuelan officials of conspiring with the [FARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FARC "FARC") to "flood the United States with cocaine". He described the Maduro government as "plagued by criminality and corruption."[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt2020-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-nyt2020) The [State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_State "United States Department of State") announced a reward of up to US$15 million for information leading to the arrest of Maduro, under the [Narcotics Rewards Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotics_Rewards_Program "Narcotics Rewards Program").[&#8202;](#ref-back-npr2020-6)[\[2\]](#refnote-npr2020)

Barr and other senior officials compared the case to earlier prosecutions of foreign leaders, notably former Panamanian ruler [Manuel Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Noriega "Manuel Noriega"), who was convicted of drug trafficking in the United States after his overthrow in 1989.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[81\]](#refnote-auto)
