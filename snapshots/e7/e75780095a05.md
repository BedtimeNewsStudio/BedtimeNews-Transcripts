i-Space

**i-Space**[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-1) ( [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters "Simplified Chinese characters"):星际荣耀; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): _xīngjì róngyào_; lit.'Interstellar Glory'; also known as **Space Honor**,[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Jones-2) **Beijing Interstellar Glory Space Technology Ltd.**,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sn20200414-3) **Interstellar Glory**[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-4) or **StarCraft Glory**[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-gbsec-5)) is a Chinese [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_spaceflight "Private spaceflight") space technology [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_product_development "New product development") and [space launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_launch "Space launch") company based in [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing "Beijing"), founded in October 2016.

The company is developing [two-stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-stage-to-orbit "Two-stage-to-orbit") [small satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallsat "Smallsat") [orbital launchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launch_vehicle "Launch vehicle") based on [solid propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_rocket_motor "Solid rocket motor") rocket engines procured from major Chinese government supplier [China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Aerospace_Science_and_Technology_Corporation "China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation") (CAST).[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-gbsec-5)

In July 2019, i-Space successfully launched the Hyperbola-1 and reached [low Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit "Low Earth orbit") on its maiden flight, becoming the first private company from China to achieve orbit.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-qzmaidenflight-6) The company's next three orbital launch attempts (two in 2021 and one in 2022) using the same launch vehicle all ended in failure.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-nsf20210201-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Y5failXinhua-8) But a return to success in orbital launches with the Hyperbola-1 followed in 2023 when the company conducted a successful test launch with no payload in April of that year and continued with a December 2023 launch that placed the DEAR-1 satellite in an [SSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit "Sun-synchronous orbit") orbit.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-spacenews02Nov2023-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-cnstock2023Dec-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Dec23Sohu-11)

## History

The company was founded in 2016.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

By 2019, i-Space had successfully launched the Hyperbola-1S and Hyperbola-1Z single-stage solid-propellant [test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_article_(aerospace) "Test article (aerospace)") rockets into space on [suborbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suborbital_flight "Suborbital flight") [test flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_flight "Test flight"),[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Jones-2)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Deyana-12) and then reached [low Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit "Low Earth orbit") with Hyperbola-1 on its maiden flight on 25 July 2019, becoming the first private company from China to have achieved orbit.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-qzmaidenflight-6)

The company raised US$173 million in private capital in a [series B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_B "Series B") round during 2020.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sn20210803-13)

## Rockets

### Suborbital rockets: Hyperbola-1S and Hyperbola-1Z

The **Hyperbola-1S** (also called **SQX-1S**),[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sfi20180906-14) and the **Hyperbola-1Z** (also called **SQX-1Z**),[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-SpaceWatch2018-15) are single stage, solid-propellant suborbital test rockets. The Hyperbola-1S rocket was 8.4m (28ft) long, with a diameter of 1m (3ft 3in) and weighed 4.6t (5.1 tons). The Hyperbola-1Z rocket has a diameter of about 1.4m (4ft 7in), maximum design speed of 1.6km/s (0.99mi/s) and can reach altitude of 175km (109mi) on a suborbital trajectory.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sfi20180906-14)

The first [sub-orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-orbital_spaceflight "Sub-orbital spaceflight") test flight of Hyperbola-1S took place from [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan "Hainan") island on 5 April 2018 to an altitude of 108km (67mi).[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-16)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Deyana-12)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-gbsec-5)

The second flight of i-Space was a commercial sub-orbital flight launched on 5 September 2018 from the [Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center "Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center") in the [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert "Gobi Desert"), using the Hyperbola-1Z rocket. The sub-orbital flight reached an altitude of 108km (67mi) and a peak velocity of over 1,200m/s (3,900ft/s).[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-spaceflightfansChina20180530-17) It carried payloads from private Chinese satellite companies ZeroG Labs and ADA-space. The rocket delivered three [CubeSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat "CubeSat") satellites one of which subsequently parachuted back to [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth").[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-chinadaily20180905-18)

### Hyperbola-1

Main article: [Hyperbola-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbola-1 "Hyperbola-1")

The **Hyperbola-1** (aka **Shuangquxian-1**, **SQX-1**) (Chinese: **双曲线一号**) rocket is 20.8m (68ft) tall, 1.4m (4ft 7in) in diameter and weighs 31t (34 tons). It consists of four all solid fuel stages, guided by liquid fuel attitude control engines.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Products-19) It can launch 300kg (660lb) into [low Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit "Low Earth orbit") (LEO).[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-spaceflightfansChina20180530-17) The rocket might be based on Chinese military missiles (perhaps [DF-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DF-11 "DF-11") or [DF-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DF-15 "DF-15")).[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-GunterHyperbola1-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sfn-20190725-21) The first stage of the rocket is equipped with four [grid fins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_fin "Grid fin").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Products-19)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-NSF_May_2022-22) The launch price is reported around US$5 million.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-engineering.com-23)

Its successful maiden flight was on 25 July 2019, at 05:00 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time "Coordinated Universal Time") from [Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center "Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-qzmaidenflight-6)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-GunterHyperbola1-20) It launched from a movable supporting platform.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-engineering.com-23) It placed numerous payloads,[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-24) among them the CAS-7B [amateur radio satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio_satellite "Amateur radio satellite"),[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-25) into orbit 300km (190mi) above [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth"). CAS-7B decayed from orbit 6 August 2019.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-26) It was the first Chinese private company to achieve orbit (orbital launches of other private companies before had failed).[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sfn-20190725-21)

A second launch occurred on 1 February 2021, at 08:15 UTC (16:15 Beijing Time) from [Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center "Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center") with 6 unidentified satellites but failed to reach orbit.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-nsf20210201-7) A subsequent investigation revealed that a piece of insulation had broken off and got stuck in the turning mechanism of [grid fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_fin "Grid fin") Number IV. When the piece was blown away, the control system then suddenly overcompensated, resulting in the rocket being ripped apart by excessive aerodynamic forces.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-27)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-NSF_May_2022-22) The rocket was named " [Tianshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Book_from_the_Sky "A Book from the Sky")" because its outer fuselage was covered with the artistic creations (images of compound made-up Chinese characters) of the contemporary artist Xu Bing.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-nsf20210201-7)

iSpace launched a third Hyperbola-1 solid-rocket vehicle on 3 August 2021.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sn20210803-13) [SpaceNews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceNews "SpaceNews") was reporting the same day that the outcome of the launch was unknown, but that amateur video of the launch had been posted, but then deleted from Chinese social media.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sn20210803-13) After most of the day had passed, the Chinese official media [Xinhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhua "Xinhua") reported that the launch was unsuccessful due to off-nominal performance of the rocket which resulted in a failure to achieve orbit.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Y5failXinhua-8) An official statement released by the company itself the following day clarified that the failure was caused by a malfunctioning in the fairing separation process, that precluded the payload from reaching the target orbit.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-28) A fourth launch attempt on 13 May 2022, was unsuccessful as well.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-NSF_May_2022-22)

In April 2023, i-Space performed a fifth launch of the Hyperbola-1 which successfully reached orbit without a payload (or possibly a dummy payload),[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-spacenews02Nov2023-9) and then followed with another launch on 17 December 2023 that placed the DEAR-1 satellite from Chinese company Azspace into a 500 kilometre [SSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit "Sun-synchronous orbit") orbit.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-cnstock2023Dec-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Dec23Sohu-11)

### Hyperbola-2

The **Hyperbola-2** (Chinese: **双曲线二号**) is a two-stage, liquid-fueled, [reusable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reusable_launch_system "Reusable launch system") rocket designed to lift 1.9 tons into LEO. It features the JD-1 engine which employs [methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane "Methane") as fuel and [liquid oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_oxygen "Liquid oxygen") as the oxidizer. The first stage is expected to land propulsively in order to be reused.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-Products-19) The rocket's JD-1 engine had its first hot fire test in May 2020.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-sn-20200605-29) As of July 2022, the first launch was expected to occur in 2023 following a series of first stage hop tests.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-30) In July 2023 i-Space announced that it has decided to suspend further development of the Hyperbola-2 launcher and instead directly proceed with the development of the reusable medium-lift Hyperbola-3 rocket.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-spacenews02Nov2023-9)

### Hyperbola-3

The **Hyperbola-3** is a 69-metre tall, two-stage, liquid-fueled, partially [reusable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reusable_launch_system "Reusable launch system") rocket; it is designed to lift at least 8.5 tons into LEO in reusable mode and 13.4 tons in expendable mode. The rocket uses nine Focus-2[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-31) (JD-2) engines, which use [methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane "Methane") as fuel and [liquid oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_oxygen "Liquid oxygen") as the oxidizer. i-Space is targeting a first flight of the expendable Hyperbola-3 for 2025, while an attempt at first-stage vertical landing and recovery is slated for 2026; a heavy-lift 3-core variant named the Hyperbola-3B is also planned by the company.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-spacenews02Nov2023-9)

On 2 November 2023, i Space performed a VTVL test of Hyperbola 3 and its JD 2 engine. The test article reached a maximum height of 178.42 m during the 51 second long flight.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-spacenews02Nov2023-9)

A second successful test of the Hyperbola-2Y test vehicle took place on 10 December 2023 with the stage reaching a height of about 343 metres and translating a horizontal distance of about 50 metres after its 63 second flight.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-32)

On 5 August 2025, I-Space revealed the completed [drone-ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_spaceport_drone_ship "Autonomous spaceport drone ship") for Hyperbola 3. Similar to SpaceX's drone-ships, this autonomous ship will be the landing barge for I-Space's reusable Hyperbola 3 rocket. The ship launched from [Yangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou "Yangzhou") (扬州市) from where it was built and will eventually be serviced off the shore of [Wenchang Commercial Space Launch Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenchang_Commercial_Space_Launch_Site "Wenchang Commercial Space Launch Site"). The name of the ship is Qinglan (清澜), or calm waves.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-33)

On 29 December 2025, I-Space released footage documenting preparations for a sea-based [VTVL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTVL "VTVL") first-stage recovery of Hyperbola-3 ahead of its first orbital flight planned for 2026. The footage and accompanying description showed ground testing of a full-scale landing leg, conducted at the company's Wencheng facility and subjected to structural load testing intended to simulate landing conditions.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-Space_(Chinese_company)#cite_note-34)

## Other developments
