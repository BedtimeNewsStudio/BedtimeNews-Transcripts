Egyptian-Chinese relations are approaching a significant milestone that reflects the depth of the strategic partnership between Cairo and Beijing. The anticipated visit of Chinese President Xi Jinping to Egypt is expected to provide further momentum to the long-standing and steadily expanding ties between the two countries.

Over the past seven decades, relations between Egypt and China have evolved from traditional friendship and cooperation into a comprehensive strategic partnership, encompassing an increasingly broad range of bilateral cooperation.

The growing political and economic convergence between Egypt and China has contributed to further strengthening bilateral ties and was among the factors supporting Egypt’s accession to the BRICS, reflecting the country’s expanding role in international economic cooperation.

China views Egypt as a key strategic gateway to Africa and the Middle East, given its unique geographic location, its role as a trusted and influential regional and international actor, and the strategic importance of the Suez Canal as a vital artery of global trade.

#### President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi’s op-ed in Al-Ahram

**President El-Sisi: Together for more prosperous future for Egyptian, Chinese peoples, humanity**

​This year we celebrate the 70th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic relations between the Arab Republic of Egypt and the People’s Republic of China. Egypt was the first Arab and African country to establish diplomatic relations with China in 1956, as an extension of the civilizational and historical ties between two nations whose roots extend deep into history.

As we mark this special anniversary between our two friendly countries, we also celebrate the visit of Chinese President Xi Jinping to Egypt as an honored and cherished guest.

Throughout history, Egypt and China have been united by enduring bonds of friendship and a shared legacy of civilizational achievement, embodied by the Nile River, the birthplace of Egyptian civilization and the lifeline of its people, and the mighty Yangtze River, the cradle of Chinese civilization and a timeless source of Chinese wisdom, culture, and philosophy.

Through the knowledge, sciences, and innovations they cultivated, these two ancient civilizations made profound contributions to human progress, creating an enduring arc of achievement stretching from the Pyramids of Egypt to the Great Wall of China.

Egyptian civilization made seminal contributions to fields such as medicine, engineering, and astronomy, while Chinese civilization pioneered transformative innovations that were remarkably advanced for their time.

The knowledge and discoveries generated by these civilizations were transmitted, developed, and built upon by successive generations of scholars and scientists, helping to lay foundations for the Industrial and Technological Revolutions and the unprecedented advances that followed.

Without this cumulative intellectual and scientific heritage, humanity could not have attained its present level of development across such a broad range of fields.

This shared legacy reinforces the legitimate aspiration of Egypt and China to participate fully and equitably in contemporary technological progress and to benefit from its advances, which will advance comprehensive and sustainable development for the peoples of both countries and other nations, and enable Egypt and China to continue their historic contribution to the advancement of human civilization.

The two countries were connected by a historic trade route that contributed to the movement of people and goods and extended from the Chinese Silk Road to the Red Sea and the Egyptian Suez Canal, the lifeline of global trade.

Along this route, missions and trade caravans journeyed from the coasts of China and Asia to Egyptian ports on the Red Sea and the Mediterranean, which continue to serve today as an indispensable link between East and West.

For centuries, Egyptian ports served as major commercial hubs for the exchange of goods among Asia, Europe, and Africa.

These vital routes also enabled the exchange of cultures, knowledge, and ideas between the two peoples, further strengthening the historic bonds between Egypt and China and laying the foundations for centuries of extensive people-to-people, commercial, and cultural interaction.

This long history of engagement ultimately culminated in the establishment of diplomatic relations between the two countries seven decades ago—a landmark moment that opened a new chapter in bilateral relations and paved the way for their continued development and flourishing.

Egypt was the first Arab and African country to establish diplomatic relations with the People’s Republic of China, at a pivotal moment when Egypt affirmed its support for the right of peoples to freely choose their own political and economic systems.

Egypt thereby paved the way for Arab and African relations with China, which have expanded and flourished over the decades, culminating in the establishment of the China-Arab States Cooperation Forum and the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation.

Egypt has consistently adhered to the One-China principle and rejected interference in China’s internal affairs. It supported the restoration of China’s permanent seat on the United Nations Security Council, while China supported Arab rights and the right of the Palestinian people to establish their independent state.

These historic positions remain deeply engraved in the collective memory of both nations as enduring milestones in their relationship.

As the Egyptian and Chinese peoples commemorate this cherished anniversary, they recall this shared history not only with pride, but also as a lasting foundation for solidarity and a source of renewed momentum for deeper cooperation, mutual support, and partnership in the years ahead.

Over the past seven decades, Egypt-China relations have witnessed remarkable development, culminating in the establishment of a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership between the two countries in 2014, during my first official visit to the People’s Republic of China.

At that pivotal juncture, Egypt was determined to broaden and deepen its relations with countries across the world, diversify its foreign policy, and reach out to its friends and partners to forge stronger and more comprehensive partnerships founded on mutual respect, solidarity, and mutual benefit.

Egypt also exerted efforts to advance joint development in infrastructure, investment, trade, manufacturing, and technology transfer. China was among the first countries to demonstrate its keenness to consolidate relations with Egypt on the basis of these firmly established principles, which constituted the solid foundation for enduring and longstanding relations that have succeeded in achieving benefits for the two peoples and safeguarding the interests of the two countries.

Accordingly, the comprehensive strategic partnership established by the two countries has brought about a major transformation in bilateral relations. China has become a major trade and economic partner for Egypt, and Chinese direct investments have increased, including in the Suez Canal Economic Zone SCZONE, which has provided tremendous opportunities for Chinese investors to generate returns and benefit from the significant advantages available to foreign investors in the zone.

The SCZONE contains industrial areas hosting numerous factories of international companies from various countries seeking to benefit from these opportunities, as well as from Egypt’s geographical location at the heart of the world, as an essential link between East and West.

Chinese companies have also contributed to numerous major national projects, such as the construction of the Central Business District in the New Capital, the launch of the electric railway to the Tenth of Ramadan City, the construction of renewable energy power plants, the development of ports, and other projects that formed part of the Egyptian state’s national plans to overhaul its infrastructure, which Egypt undertook with the cooperation with a number of different countries, including China.

This has contributed to Egypt’s globally advanced position in the field of infrastructure, which is pivotal to laying the foundations for an advanced and integrated industrial base, and transforming Egypt into a global logistics hub for industry, trade, and services.

This significant progress in strengthening and modernizing Egypt’s infrastructure has reinforced its position as an attractive destination for major industrial investments from around the world. China has been among the leading countries keen to expand its industrial presence in Egypt and capitalize on the country’s strategic location, advanced infrastructure, and the significant investment opportunities and competitive advantages it offers.

Egypt and China share a common commitment to harnessing the synergies between Egypt’s strategy for industrial localization and China’s strategy for high-quality development, with a view to attracting greater Chinese industrial investment to Egypt. Priority sectors include electric vehicles, energy-storage batteries, renewable energy, electronics, components for seawater desalination plants, shipbuilding, and other advanced industries.

This cooperation also extends to promoting technology transfer in telecommunications, artificial intelligence, and space sciences, which represent key priorities for economic and technological development and promising avenues for expanding Egypt-China cooperation in the next phase of our strategic partnership.

China’s development and its ability to achieve remarkable economic progress and eradicate extreme poverty have been an inspiration to developing countries seeking to realize their aspirations for modernization and advancement.

Egypt views this development positively, while at the same time believing that developing countries have a tremendous opportunity to achieve economic advancement in accordance with a development model suited to their respective circumstances and compatible with their historical and cultural contexts.

In this vein, it is important to underscore the need to create an appropriate international environment that enables these countries to achieve this desired objective.

From this standpoint, Egypt has played its constructive regional and international role over the past decades and adhered to the principles and purposes of the Charter of the United Nations, international law, and the rules-based international order. Egypt has worked to contribute to supporting international organizations in carrying out their essential role in safeguarding international peace and security.

It has made tangible contributions to peacekeeping and peacebuilding operations and participated, alongside all parties of the international community, in efforts to enhance the role of international economic organizations in achieving global sustainable development and advancing the United Nations development goals.

Egypt has firmly maintained its position in support of strengthening the voice of the Global South and promoting its representation in international institutions, in a manner that respects the right of these countries to express their will and takes their concerns into consideration.

Egypt has joined a number of emerging institutions and joined BRICS in 2024, as well as the New Development Bank affiliated with it and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank.

It also joined the Shanghai Cooperation Organization as a dialogue partner, out of its keenness to strengthen South-South dialogue and openness to broad and diverse partnerships with all countries of the world.

I therefore take this opportunity to congratulate China on assuming the BRICS presidency next year, and wish to express that Egypt looks forward to continued coordination and consultation with China in this regard.

Egypt has also adhered to a policy of strategic balance as a guiding principle of Egyptian foreign policy at a time when waves of polarization and competition are intensifying, emphasizing its independent foreign policy and calling for the international community to coordinate its efforts to overcome the global challenges that have come to threaten the entire world.

Egypt and China have become partners contributing together to enhancing the role of the Global South in the international system, in a manner that achieves greater justice and fairness for developing countries.

From the same viewpoint, Egypt has undertaken intensive diplomatic efforts to de-escalate the situation in the Middle East and prevent the escalation and expansion of the conflict, as the region undergoes a dangerous phase of conflicts that threaten regional security and stability and, indeed, threaten international peace and security.

In this context, Egypt and China agree on the necessity of resolving conflicts through peaceful means, taking into consideration the security concerns of all parties, and respecting the sovereignty of states as a basis for the peaceful settlement of conflicts. It is not in the interest of any regional or international party to widen the conflict or exacerbate armed hostilities.

In the same vein, the Nile River has been the foundation of Egyptian civilization and a vital source of its prosperity throughout history. Egypt and its fellow Nile Basin brothers have benefited from the Nile for thousands of years, with its waters serving as a shared resource and a basis for life, development and human well-being.

Throughout its history, Egypt has remained committed to supporting the development of the countries of the Nile Basin, particularly in the areas of water resources development and dam construction, including in the Sudan and Uganda, and, most recently, through its contribution to the mega-project of the Julius Nyerere Hydropower Project in Tanzania.

Egypt will remain steadfast in advocating for transboundary cooperation in accordance with the principles and rules of international law, rather than unilateral approaches that result in harm to downstream States.

Egypt firmly believes that the sustainable management of shared water resources can only be achieved through prior notification, consultations and consensus among riparian States.

In this regard, Egypt highly values China’s position that has affirmed its recognition of the vital importance of the Nile River to Egypt as its principal lifeline, and reiterates the importance of refraining from unilateral measures and strengthening collaboration in good faith to achieve Win-Win cooperation and mutually beneficial outcomes.

Just as Egypt and China have, over the years, written remarkable chapters in the history of cooperation and friendship between our two countries, we look forward to writing together a new chapter in this enduring relationship, whose roots stretch back thousands of years. We aspire to many more decades of progress and prosperity for our two friendly peoples, and to a future in which the world enjoys greater stability, security, peace, and development.

#### Chinese President Xi Jinping’s op-ed in Al-Ahram

**Chinese President Xi Jinping hails 70 years of Egypt-China ties**

Chinese President Xi Jinping marked 70 years of diplomatic relations between Egypt and China in a personally written op-ed published in Al-Ahram on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, using the article to announce a new state visit and call for a "new journey" of cooperation.

The op-ed underscores the importance Beijing places on the relationship: Egypt is the only country on Xi's 2026 itinerary to receive a full state visit, apart from a summit stop in Kyrgyzstan. The visit comes a decade after his last trip to Cairo, at the invitation of President Abdel-Fattah El-Sisi.

In the article, Xi linked the anniversary to a shared history of opposition to colonialism and outlined plans to expand cooperation in industry and education, as well as support for Palestinian statehood.

"Whoever drinks from the Nile's waters returns to it again," Xi wrote, citing an Egyptian proverb as he recalled his first visit to Cairo 10 years ago. He said Egypt and China, heirs to two of the world's oldest civilizations, nourished respectively by the Nile and the Yellow River, have shared mutual respect that has endured "despite changes in the international landscape."

Xi described the relationship as a comprehensive strategic partnership, established in 2014 and built around Egypt's Vision 2030 development strategy and China's Belt and Road Initiative. Bilateral trade between the two countries reached $20.97 billion by the end of 2025, up from $17.37 billion the previous year.

China has invested $1.4 billion in Egypt in recent years, much of it channelled through the Suez Canal Economic Zone, now home to around 200 Chinese companies. Xi cited the zone as a model of industrial cooperation between the two countries.

Chinese contractors also built Africa's tallest building, the 78-storey Iconic Tower in Egypt's New Capital, which Xi cited as a symbol of joint achievement. The tower's builder, China State Construction Engineering Corporation, won a top industry award for its steel structure.

Meanwhile, Xi pointed to an electric rail line linking Cairo to the new capital, financed through a Chinese loan, which he described as the first of its kind on the African continent.

On culture, Xi said dozens of Egyptian secondary schools now teach the Chinese language under a bilateral education protocol, and quoted an old Chinese proverb: "Sincerity to friends brings hearts closer, however great the distance."

He also said the Grand Egyptian Museum has drawn large numbers of Chinese visitors since opening, while an exhibition on ancient Egyptian civilisation was held in Shanghai.

On regional politics, Xi renewed China's call for a two-state solution to the Palestinian question and urged Cairo and Beijing to coordinate on reforming global governance in favour of developing nations.

Egypt recognized the People's Republic of China on 30 May 1956, becoming the first Arab and African country, and the 23rd worldwide, to do so.

The two countries trace their political solidarity to the 1955 Bandung Conference, where Chinese premier Zhou Enlai met Egyptian president Gamal Abdel-Nasser amid a shared struggle against colonialism.

Egypt and China elevated their ties to a comprehensive strategic partnership in December 2014. El-Sisi and Xi marked the Egypt-China Partnership Year in May 2024, celebrating the 10th anniversary of their strategic partnership, when the Egyptian president invited Xi to visit Egypt.

#### Presidential Visits and Meetings

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/818243/11.jpg?width=436&height=327&mode=max)**

**Visits**

**December 2014 — President El-Sisi’s First Visit to China**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi made his first visit to China as President of Egypt on December 22, 2014.

During the visit, the two sides agreed to elevate bilateral relations to a comprehensive strategic partnership. Several economic and technical cooperation agreements were also signed, along with framework agreements for joint development projects.

The visit marked a major turning point in modern Egyptian-Chinese relations, advancing bilateral cooperation beyond its traditional framework and laying the foundation for a long-term strategic partnership.

**September 2015 — President El-Sisi’s Visit to China**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi visited China in September 2015, where he held talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping at the Great Hall of the People.

The discussions focused on following up the implementation of economic agreements, including practical steps to launch projects agreed upon during the President El-Sisi’s previous visit, particularly in the fields of transport, railways, and energy. The two presidents also discussed investment opportunities in the Suez Canal Economic Zone, as a key strategic hub within China’s ambitious Belt and Road Initiative.

**January 2016 — President Xi Jinping’s Visit to Egypt**

Chinese President Xi Jinping paid an official visit to Egypt on January 21, 2016, marking the first visit by a Chinese president to Egypt in more than 12 years. The visit coincided with the 60th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic relations between the two countries.

The two presidents held a summit meeting to explore ways to further strengthen the comprehensive strategic partnership. Their discussions also covered the Belt and Road Initiative, infrastructure projects, and other areas of bilateral cooperation.

**September 2016 — President El-Sisi’s Participation in the G20 Summit in China**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi visited China to participate as a special guest in the G20 Summit in Hangzhou at the invitation of President Xi Jinping, whose country held the G20 presidency at the time.

The summit provided an opportunity for continued coordination between the two presidents following President Xi Jinping’s visit to Egypt. It also underscored Egypt’s importance to China as a leading Arab and African country with a strategic geographic location and significant political and economic weight.

**September 2017 — President El-Sisi’s Participation in the BRICS Summit in China**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi participated in the BRICS Summit and the Dialogue of Emerging Markets and Developing Countries in China.

President El-Sisi met with President Xi Jinping, and the two sides discussed financing infrastructure projects, combating terrorism, strengthening cooperation among developing countries, and other areas of bilateral and international cooperation.

The meeting also contributed to expanding cooperation within the framework of the Belt and Road Initiative.

**September 2018 — President El-Sisi’s Participation in the FOCAC Beijing Summit**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi visited China to participate in the 2018 Beijing Summit of the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation (FOCAC).

During the visit, the two presidents discussed ways to advance the comprehensive strategic partnership, increase Chinese investment, and strengthen cooperation in the Suez Canal Economic Zone. The visit also witnessed the signing of an executive program to strengthen the comprehensive strategic partnership, along with a number of economic cooperation agreements between the two countries.

**April 2019 — President El-Sisi’s Participation in the Belt and Road Forum**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi traveled to Beijing on April 24, 2019, to participate in the Second Belt and Road Forum for International Cooperation at the invitation of President Xi Jinping.

Egypt’s participation highlighted the importance of the Belt and Road Initiative and the need to align it with comprehensive development objectives, infrastructure development, and the promotion of international trade.

**May 2024 — President El-Sisi’s State Visit to China**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/817688/000_34u332c.jpg?width=500&height=375)**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi paid a state visit to China from May 28 to 31, 2024, at the invitation of President Xi Jinping. The visit coincided with the 10th anniversary of the establishment of the comprehensive strategic partnership between the two countries.

The two sides issued a joint statement on deepening the comprehensive strategic partnership and agreed to designate 2024 as the “Year of Egypt-China Partnership,” with a range of political, economic, diplomatic, cultural, and people-to-people activities to be held throughout the year.

**Meetings**

**February 2022 — President El-Sisi’s Meeting with President Xi Jinping in Beijing**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi held a summit meeting with President Xi Jinping at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing on February 5, 2022, during his visit to China to attend the opening ceremony of the Winter Olympic Games.

The meeting took place against the backdrop of the repercussions of the COVID-19 pandemic and disruptions to global supply chains, underscoring the importance of strengthening cooperation in health, the economy, trade, and manufacturing.

**December 2022 — President El-Sisi’s Meeting with President Xi Jinping at the China-Arab Summit**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi met with President Xi Jinping in Riyadh during the first China-Arab States Summit on December 8, 2022.

Although the meeting took place outside China, it represented an important instance of direct presidential engagement within the broader framework of Arab-Chinese cooperation.

**October 2024 — President El-Sisi’s Meeting with President Xi Jinping on the Sidelines of the BRICS Summit**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi met with President Xi Jinping on October 23, 2024, on the sidelines of the BRICS Summit.

The meeting followed Egypt’s accession to BRICS and took place within the broader context of strengthening economic relations with China and expanding cooperation among BRICS member states.

#### Areas of Cooperation between Egypt and China

Egypt-China relations constitute one of China’s most comprehensive partnerships with an Arab or African country.

Since 2014, bilateral relations have been elevated to the level of a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, subsequently expanding to encompass political, economic, and investment cooperation, as well as cooperation in energy, transport, industry, digital technology, space, agriculture, education, culture, tourism, defense, and security.

**Political and Diplomatic Cooperation**

**Regular Political Dialogue**

Relations are characterized by regular communication and coordination between the two countries’ leaderships and foreign ministries through:

**Presidential visits**

- Summit meetings
- Strategic dialogue between the two foreign ministers
- The Joint Governmental Committee
- Consultations on regional and international crises
- Mutual Support on Core Interests

Egypt and China extend mutual support on a number of issues that each side considers to be among its core interests, with an emphasis on:

- Respect for state sovereignty
- Non-interference in internal affairs
- Adherence to the One-China principle
- Support for political settlements to conflicts
- Rejection of the use of force or attempts to impose faits accomplis

China has affirmed its readiness to continue supporting Egypt on issues related to its core interests, while Egypt reaffirms its commitment to the One-China principle and its respect for China’s sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Egypt and China coordinate closely on a range of regional and international issues, including:

**Economic and Investment Cooperation**

**The Belt and Road Initiative**

Egypt is an important partner in the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) due to:

- Its strategic location linking Asia, Africa, and Europe
- The Suez Canal
- The Suez Canal Economic Zone
- Its seaports
- Its extensive road and airport networks
- Its large domestic market
- Its membership in Arab, African, and international organizations and frameworks

Egypt views the BRI as a broad framework for investment and cooperation in transport, energy, industry, and logistics, rather than merely as a maritime connectivity initiative.

**Projects under the Belt and Road Initiative**

Key projects and areas of cooperation developed within this framework include:

- The China-Egypt TEDA Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone in Ain Sokhna
- Chinese industrial investments in the TEDA zone
- The New Capital
- The Central Business District in the New Capital
- The Light Rail Transit (LRT) system serving the New Capital and 10th of Ramadan City

**Energy projects**

**Ports and logistics zones**

**Manufacturing and exports to African markets**

The Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs has underscored the importance of advancing major cooperation projects, including the China-Egypt TEDA Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone, the Central Business District in the New Capital, and the electric rail system serving 10th of Ramadan City.

**Bilateral Trade**

Bilateral trade between Egypt and China reached approximately $20.78 billion in 2025, compared with around $17.37 billion in 2024, representing growth of approximately 19.6%. China is Egypt’s largest trading partner in non-oil commodities.

**2025 Trade Balance Indicators**

- **Egyptian exports to China:** Rose significantly to approximately $819 million.
- **Egyptian imports from China:** Reached nearly $19.9 billion.

**Major Egyptian Exports to China**

- **Agricultural products:** Fresh and frozen fruits, particularly citrus fruits and Egyptian oranges, which enjoy strong demand in the Chinese market.
- **Energy and fuels:** Liquefied natural gas and crude petroleum products.
- **Raw materials and textiles:** Flax fibers, yarn, and cotton textiles.
- **Mineral resources:** Marble, granite, stone, phosphate, and cement.

**Major Egyptian Imports from China**

- **Machinery and equipment:** Machinery, electrical appliances, electronics, and related components.
- **Technology and telecommunications:** Mobile phones, telecommunications equipment, and display screens.
- **Transport and vehicles:** Cars, other means of transport, and automotive spare parts.
- **Textiles and chemicals:** Textiles, synthetic yarns, and chemicals used in local manufacturing.

**Development Financing**

Concessional development financing provided in cooperation with China has amounted to approximately $1.7 billion, according to Egypt’s Ministry of International Cooperation. The financing has supported a range of sectors, including investment, education, scientific research, transport, and agriculture.

In 2023, Egypt and China signed a memorandum of understanding on debt-for-development swaps, which was subsequently activated through development projects. Egypt thus became the first country to enter into this type of financing arrangement with the China International Development Cooperation Agency (CIDCA).

**TEDA-Egypt Chinese Industrial Zone**

The TEDA-Egypt Industrial Zone is one of the most prominent examples of Egyptian-Chinese economic cooperation. It is located within the Sokhna Industrial Zone of the Suez Canal Economic Zone.

In 2024, the Suez Canal Economic Zone indicated that investment in TEDA had reached approximately $2 billion, with around 150 companies operating across various industrial and logistics sectors.

**Technology Research and Development**

**September 2024 — Technology Research and Development Center**

The agreements provided for the establishment of a Chinese research and development center in Egypt specializing in optical networking and green-transformation technologies, alongside professional training and capacity-building programs.

**Automotive Industry**

**October 2024 — Electric Vehicle Plant Agreement with BAIC**

An agreement was signed between Chinese automotive manufacturer BAIC and Alkan Auto, a subsidiary of the Egyptian International Automotive Group, to establish a major plant in Egypt for the local production of electric vehicles with a significant local-content component.

**Electrical Appliances**

**October 2024 — Electrical Appliances Factory in Sadat City**

An agreement was signed to establish a Chinese industrial complex in Sadat City for the production and assembly of electrical and household appliances, supporting local manufacturing and reducing reliance on imports.

**Industrial Development**

**October 2024 — New October Factory**

A land allocation contract under a usufruct arrangement was signed for the establishment of a new Chinese factory in New October City, with estimated investments of $135 million.

**Sustainable Development**

**July 2024 — Three Memoranda of Understanding on Sustainable Development**

Egypt’s Ministry of Planning and International Cooperation signed memoranda of understanding with China’s National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) and the China International Development Cooperation Agency (CIDCA) to promote the exchange of expertise in economic development and advance green and sustainable economic practices.

**Development Cooperation**

**July–August 2025 — Joint Development Cooperation Strategy (2025–2029)**

The first framework document of its kind was signed to define financing priorities and development projects for the 2025–2029 period, in line with Egypt’s national development priorities.

**Development Financing**

**July–August 2025 — First Framework Agreement for Debt-for-Development Swaps**

A framework agreement was signed to govern the implementation of debt-for-development swaps, allowing agreed portions of Egypt’s financial obligations to be redirected toward financing local green and sustainable development projects rather than being repaid through conventional debt-service mechanisms.

**Currency and Banking**

**July–August 2025 — Renewal and Expansion of the Currency Swap Agreement**

An agreement was reached to extend the local-currency swap mechanism between the Central Bank of Egypt and the People’s Bank of China, facilitating bilateral trade and settlement in Egyptian pounds and Chinese yuan while reducing reliance on third-country currencies.

**Public Health**

**2025 — Automated Vaccine Cold Storage Warehouse Project**

An implementation agreement and exchange of letters were signed to finance the Automated Vaccine Cold Storage Warehouse Project in cooperation with China’s Sinovac, supporting Egypt’s role as a regional hub for vaccine storage and distribution in Africa.

**Health and Scientific Research**

**2025 — Biosafety Laboratory Agreement**

An agreement was signed to finance and establish an advanced Biosafety Level 3 (BSL-3) laboratory in Egypt for medical research, disease surveillance, and epidemic testing, funded through a non-reimbursable Chinese grant.

**Human Development**

**2025–2027 — 2,000 Training Opportunities Program**

A cooperation program was established under which China would provide 2,000 training and research opportunities for Egyptian government officials and young professionals in areas including modern public administration, artificial intelligence, and advanced technical education over a two-year period.

**Assistive Technology and Disability Support**

**2025 — Development of Prosthetics and Assistive Devices**

A Chinese grant was provided to support the feasibility study and implementation steps for Egypt’s National Project for the Development of Prosthetics and Assistive Devices, alongside the establishment of a dedicated center of excellence for persons with disabilities.

**Cultural Heritage**

**August 2025 — Egyptian-Chinese Center for Underwater Archaeology**

A formal Memorandum of Understanding was signed to establish a specialized Egyptian-Chinese center for underwater archaeology and submerged cultural heritage in Alexandria.

The center aims to promote joint research and training and facilitate the exchange of Chinese technological expertise in underwater excavation, documentation, and restoration with Egyptian archaeologists.

**Investment and Industrial Development**

**2025 — Package of Nine Investment Memoranda of Understanding**

Nine new memoranda of understanding were signed covering a range of investment and industrial projects, including the local manufacture of electric-vehicle components, solar-panel production in the Suez Canal Economic Zone, the expansion of e-commerce activities, and cooperation in the pharmaceutical and advanced drug-manufacturing sectors.

**Currency and Banking**

**June 2026 — 30 Billion Yuan Currency Swap Agreement**

The Central Bank of Egypt and the People’s Bank of China renewed their bilateral local-currency swap agreement for an additional three years.

The value of the agreement was increased to 30 billion Chinese yuan, equivalent to approximately $4.43 billion, compared with 18 billion yuan under previous arrangements.

The expanded facility aims to facilitate bilateral trade, promote the settlement of transactions in Egyptian pounds and Chinese yuan, and reduce reliance on the US dollar.

**Defense and Military Cooperation**

**August 2026 — “Eagles of Civilization 2026” Joint Exercise**

The Egyptian and Chinese air forces launched the joint “Eagles of Civilization 2026” air exercise at a number of Egyptian air bases.

The exercise involved various types of multirole fighter aircraft from both sides and included training in joint planning and the management of air combat operations, reflecting the growing scope of defense cooperation between the two countries.

**Trade and Exports**

**August 2026 — Package of 17 Export Contracts**

Egyptian companies, in coordination with the Egyptian Commercial Representation, signed 17 export contracts worth approximately $168 million covering agricultural products, textiles, leather goods, and engineering products destined for the Chinese market.

**Trade and Tariffs**

**May 2026 — Activation of Full Tariff Exemption**

Egypt began benefiting from a Chinese initiative launched on May 1, 2026, providing 100% tariff exemption for imports from African countries maintaining diplomatic relations with China.

The initiative offers new opportunities to expand Egyptian exports to the Chinese market and contribute to narrowing the bilateral trade deficit.

**Local Government Cooperation**

**June 2026 — China (Nanjing)–Egypt (Cairo) Economic Cooperation Forum**

Cairo hosted the China (Nanjing)–Egypt (Cairo) Economic Cooperation Forum, which witnessed the signing of cooperation and twinning protocols involving local and economic-development entities, including Giza Governorate and the Suez Canal Economic Zone, aimed at promoting the exchange of development expertise and expanding investment cooperation.

**Industrial Investment**

**2026 — Localization of Heavy and Clean Industries**

Chinese investment in Egypt continued to expand in 2026, with growing interest in localizing heavy and clean industries, including the assembly and manufacture of wind-power turbines and solar panels, alongside ongoing discussions on major industrial projects such as a proposed Chinese aluminum complex in the Suez Canal region.

**Transport and Railways**

**March 2026 — Third Phase of the 10th of Ramadan LRT**

A $200 million concessional government loan agreement with the Export-Import Bank of China, repayable in Chinese yuan, was published in the Official Gazette and entered into force following completion of the required approval procedures.

The financing supports the completion of the third phase of the Light Rail Transit (LRT) line serving Egypt’s new urban communities.
