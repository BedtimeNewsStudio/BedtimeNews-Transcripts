**Charles Frank Bolden Jr.** (born August 19, 1946)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Curr_Bio-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-Curr_Bio) is a former [administrator of NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_NASA "Administrator of NASA"), a retired [United States Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps "United States Marine Corps") [Major General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_General "Major General"), and a former [astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut "Astronaut") who flew on four Space Shuttle missions.

He graduated from the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy "United States Naval Academy") with the class of 1968. Bolden became a [Marine aviator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Aviator "United States Naval Aviator") and [test pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_pilot "Test pilot"). After his service as an astronaut, he became Deputy [Commandant of Midshipmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant_of_Midshipmen "Commandant of Midshipmen") at the Naval Academy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)

On May 23, 2009, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") announced the nomination of Bolden as Administrator of NASA and [Lori Garver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Garver "Lori Garver") as deputy NASA administrator.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Picked-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Picked) Both were confirmed by the Senate by unanimous consent on July 15, 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Space.com-BoldenConfirmed2009-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-Space.com-BoldenConfirmed2009) Bolden was the first [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American "African American") to head the agency on a permanent basis.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Picked-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-Picked)

On January 12, 2017, Bolden announced his retirement from NASA during a [town hall meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_hall_meeting "Town hall meeting") at NASA Headquarters in Washington D.C. His last day would be January 19, and [Robert M. Lightfoot Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Lightfoot_Jr. "Robert M. Lightfoot Jr.") was announced as acting NASA administrator.

In 2020, Bolden was elected a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering "National Academy of Engineering") for leadership and development of U.S. human spaceflight and space operations programs, and for revitalizing fundamental aeronautics research.

## Education

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Charles_Bolden&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Education")\]

Bolden graduated from C. A. Johnson High School in [Columbia, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_South_Carolina "Columbia, South Carolina"), in 1964.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-auto) He earned a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in electrical science from the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy "United States Naval Academy") in 1968, where he was a contemporary of future Marine officers [Oliver North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_North "Oliver North"), [Jim Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Webb "Jim Webb") and [Michael Hagee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hagee "Michael Hagee") and future [Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff "Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff") [Michael Mullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mullen "Michael Mullen"), and Admirals [Dennis C. Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_C._Blair "Dennis C. Blair"), and [Jay L. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_L._Johnson "Jay L. Johnson"). He later earned a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") degree in [systems management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_management "Systems management") from the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California") in 1977. He is a member of [Omega Psi Phi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_Psi_Phi "Omega Psi Phi") fraternity.

## Military career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Charles_Bolden&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Military career")\]

In high school Bolden was turned down for an appointment to the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy "United States Naval Academy") by South Carolina's Congressional delegation, which included then [segregationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segregationist "Segregationist") Senator [Strom Thurmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strom_Thurmond "Strom Thurmond"). Bolden received his appointment after personally writing, as a high school senior, to President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson"). A recruiter came to his house a few weeks later, eventually leading to Bolden receiving an appointment from U.S. Representative [William L. Dawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_L._Dawson_(politician) "William L. Dawson (politician)") from [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois "Chicago, Illinois"). He later received notes of congratulations from Thurmond at various career milestones.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-auto)

Bolden was commissioned a [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant "Second lieutenant") in the [United States Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps "United States Marine Corps") (USMC) following graduation from the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy "United States Naval Academy") in 1968. He was president of his class. He underwent flight training at [Pensacola, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensacola,_Florida "Pensacola, Florida"); [Meridian, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian,_Mississippi "Meridian, Mississippi"); and [Kingsville, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsville,_Texas "Kingsville, Texas"), before being designated a [United States Naval Aviator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Aviator "United States Naval Aviator") in May 1970.

He flew more than 100 sorties into [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam") and [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam"), [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), and [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia") in the [A-6A Intruder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_A-6_Intruder "Grumman A-6 Intruder") while assigned to [VMA(AW)-533](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMFA(AW)-533 "VMFA(AW)-533") at [Royal Thai Air Base Nam Phong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_Air_Base_Nam_Phong "Royal Thai Air Base Nam Phong"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), from June 1972 to June 1973.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-auto1) Upon returning to the United States, Bolden began a two-year tour as a Marine Corps officer selection and [recruiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_recruiting "Military recruiting") officer in [Los Angeles, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California "Los Angeles, California"), followed by three years in various assignments at [Marine Corps Air Station El Toro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_El_Toro "Marine Corps Air Station El Toro"), California.

In June 1979, he graduated from the [United States Naval Test Pilot School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Test_Pilot_School "United States Naval Test Pilot School") at [Naval Air Station Patuxent River, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Patuxent_River,_Maryland "Naval Air Station Patuxent River, Maryland"), and was assigned to the [Naval Air Test Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Test_Center "Naval Air Test Center")'s Systems Engineering and Strike Aircraft Test Directorates. While there, he served as an ordnance test pilot and flew numerous test projects in the [A-6E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_A-6_Intruder "Grumman A-6 Intruder"), [EA-6B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_EA-6B_Prowler "Northrop Grumman EA-6B Prowler"), and [A-7C/E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTV_A-7_Corsair_II "LTV A-7 Corsair II") airplanes. He logged more than 6,000 hours flying time.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Charles_F._Bolden_Jr.%2C_Astronaut%2C_USMC%2C_public_speaking_DM-SN-85-10497.JPEG/250px-Charles_F._Bolden_Jr.%2C_Astronaut%2C_USMC%2C_public_speaking_DM-SN-85-10497.JPEG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Charles_F._Bolden_Jr.,_Astronaut,_USMC,_public_speaking_DM-SN-85-10497.JPEG) Bolden speaking at a USMC recruiting event in 1982

Bolden was selected as an astronaut candidate by NASA in 1980. He was a member of the [NASA Astronaut Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Corps "NASA Astronaut Corps") until 1994 when he returned to assignments in the Marine Corps, first as the Deputy Commandant of Midshipmen at the Naval Academy, effective June 27, 1994. In July 1997, he was assigned as the Deputy Commanding General of [I Marine Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Marine_Expeditionary_Force "I Marine Expeditionary Force"). From February to June 1998, he served as Commanding General, I MEF (Forward) in support of Operation Desert Thunder in [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait "Kuwait"). In July 1998, he was promoted to his final rank of [major general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_general_(United_States) "Major general (United States)") and assumed his duties as the Deputy Commander, [United States Forces Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Japan "United States Forces Japan"). He then served as the Commanding General, [3rd Marine Aircraft Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Marine_Aircraft_Wing "3rd Marine Aircraft Wing"), from August 9, 2000, until August 2002. He retired from the military in August 2004.

## NASA career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Charles_Bolden&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: NASA career")\]

Selected by NASA in May 1980, Bolden [became an astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_9 "NASA Astronaut Group 9") in August 1981. He was one of several astronauts recruited by [Nichelle Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichelle_Nichols "Nichelle Nichols") as part of a NASA effort to increase the number of minority and female astronauts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) His technical assignments included: Astronaut Office Safety Officer; Technical Assistant to the Director of Flight Crew Operations; Special Assistant to the Director of the [Johnson Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Space_Center "Johnson Space Center"); Astronaut Office Liaison to the Safety, Reliability and Quality Assurance Directorates of the [Marshall Space Flight Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Space_Flight_Center "Marshall Space Flight Center") (MSFC) and the [Kennedy Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center "Kennedy Space Center") (KSC); Chief of the Safety Division at JSC; Lead Astronaut for Vehicle Test and Checkout at the Kennedy Space Center; and Assistant Deputy Administrator, NASA Headquarters.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

A veteran of four space flights, he has logged over 680 hours in space. Bolden served as pilot on [STS-61-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-61-C "STS-61-C") (January 12–18, 1986) and [STS-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-31 "STS-31") (April 24–29, 1990), and was the mission commander on [STS-45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-45 "STS-45") (March 24–April 2, 1992), and [STS-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-60 "STS-60") (February 3–11, 1994).

Bolden was the first person to ride the [Launch Complex 39 slidewire baskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Launch_Complex_39 "Kennedy Space Center Launch Complex 39") which enable rapid escape from a [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") on the launch pad. The need for a human test was determined following a launch abort on [STS-41-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-41-D "STS-41-D") where controllers were afraid to order the crew to use the untested escape system.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

A few years before his appointment by President Barack Obama to be administrator of NASA, Bolden auditioned, along with professional actors, for the role of virtual host for NASA's "Shuttle Launch Experience" educational attraction at the [Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Visitor_Complex "Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex") in [Merritt Island, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merritt_Island,_Florida "Merritt Island, Florida").[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

### Spaceflights

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Charles_Bolden&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Spaceflights")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/STS61C-01-007.jpg/250px-STS61C-01-007.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:STS61C-01-007.jpg) Bolden on the flight deck of _Columbia_ during STS-61-C

On [STS-61-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-61-C "STS-61-C"), Bolden piloted [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") [_Columbia_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia "Space Shuttle Columbia"). During the six-day flight, crew members deployed the [SATCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_satellite "Communications satellite") [Ku-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_band "Ku band") satellite and conducted experiments in astrophysics and materials processing. The mission launched from Kennedy Space Center on January 12, 1986, orbited the Earth 96 times, and ended with a successful night landing at [Edwards Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards_Air_Force_Base "Edwards Air Force Base"), California on January 18, 1986. Coincidently, Senator [Bill Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Nelson "Bill Nelson"), who like Bolden would also go on to serve as a NASA Administrator, also flew on STS-61-C as a [payload specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload_specialist "Payload specialist"). STS-61-C was the final mission before the [Space Shuttle _Challenger_ disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster").

Bolden piloted [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") [_Discovery_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Discovery "Space Shuttle Discovery") during [STS-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-31 "STS-31"). Launched on April 24, 1990, from Kennedy Space Center, the crew spent the five-day mission deploying the [Hubble Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope "Hubble Space Telescope") and conducting a variety of mid-deck experiments. They also used a variety of cameras, including both the [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX "IMAX") in cabin and cargo bay cameras, for Earth observations from their record-setting altitude of over 400 miles. Following 75 orbits of Earth in 121 hours, _Discovery_ landed at Edwards Air Force Base on April 29, 1990.

On [STS-45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-45 "STS-45"), Bolden commanded a crew of seven aboard [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") [_Atlantis_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Atlantis "Space Shuttle Atlantis"), launched on March 24, 1992, from Kennedy Space Center. STS-45 was the first [Spacelab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacelab "Spacelab") mission dedicated to NASA's "Mission to Planet Earth". During the nine-day mission, the crew operated the twelve experiments that constituted the ATLAS-1 (Atmospheric Laboratory for Applications and Science) cargo. ATLAS-1 obtained detailed measurements of atmospheric chemical and physical properties. In addition, this was the first time an artificial beam of electrons was used to stimulate an auroral discharge. Following 143 orbits of Earth, _Atlantis_ landed at Kennedy Space Center on April 2, 1992.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Sts060-302-001.jpg/250px-Sts060-302-001.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sts060-302-001.jpg) Bolden on the flight deck of _Discovery_ during STS-60

Bolden commanded [STS-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-60 "STS-60")'s crew of six aboard _Discovery_. This was the historic first joint-American–Russian Space Shuttle mission involving the participation of a Russian cosmonaut, [Sergei Krikalev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Krikalev "Sergei Krikalev"), as a [mission specialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_specialist "Mission specialist"). The flight launched on February 3, 1994, from Kennedy Space Center, and carried the Space Habitation Module-2 ( [SPACEHAB-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrotech_Corporation "Astrotech Corporation")), and the [Wake Shield Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Shield_Facility "Wake Shield Facility"). The crew conducted a series of joint American/Russian science activities. The mission achieved 130 orbits of the Earth, ending with a landing on February 11, 1994, at the Kennedy Space Center.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto1-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-auto1)

### Administrator of NASA

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Charles_Bolden&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Administrator of NASA")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Charles_Bolden_speaks_at_STS-135_wheels_stop_event.jpg/250px-Charles_Bolden_speaks_at_STS-135_wheels_stop_event.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Charles_Bolden_speaks_at_STS-135_wheels_stop_event.jpg) Bolden speaks after landing of the last Space Shuttle mission, [STS-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-135 "STS-135").

In 2009, President Obama appointed Bolden to be administrator of NASA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

In a NASA video published April 28, 2010, titled "NASA's New Era of Innovation and Discovery", Bolden said, "We're going to turn science fiction into science fact".[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

On the same day, at a question and answer session with employees at the Johnson Space Center, Bolden compared the [Constellation Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation_Program "Constellation Program") to a [stillborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillborn "Stillborn") baby calf extracted from a [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel "Camel")'s womb by [U.S. Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marines "U.S. Marines"). Bolden said, "We've got some stillborn calves around, and we have got to figure out ways to help each other bring them back to life".[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)

In a June 2010 interview with _[Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Media_Network "Al Jazeera Media Network")_, Bolden said that the top three goals he was tasked with by President Obama were to help re-inspire children to want to get into science and math, to expand NASA's international relationships, and, "perhaps foremost", "to reach out to the Muslim world ... to help them feel good about their historic contribution to science... and math and engineering".[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17) The White House disagreed with Bolden's statement, with the press secretary saying Bolden probably misspoke and "That was not his task, and that's not the task of NASA".[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)

Bolden said his agency's long-term ambition is landing astronauts on [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars "Mars").[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) He has cited spending cuts as a concern for major NASA projects.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)

On August 28, 2012, he was the first human being to have his voice broadcast on the surface of Mars. Although the rover has no speakers, it received the transmission of his voice and then beamed it back to Earth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

In 2013, he noted the [National Aerospace Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aerospace_Week "National Aerospace Week") as [Administrator of NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_NASA "Administrator of NASA").[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

On October 28, 2015, Bolden presented the next steps for a [human journey to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mission_to_Mars "Human mission to Mars") at the [Center for American Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_American_Progress "Center for American Progress") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.")[&#8202;](#ref-back-CAP-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-CAP)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Video-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-Video)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASA-20151008pdf-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-NASA-20151008pdf)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASA-20151008b-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-NASA-20151008b)

On January 12, 2017, Bolden announced his resignation from NASA during a Town Hall meeting at NASA Headquarters in Washington D.C. His last day would be January 19, and [Robert M. Lightfoot Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Lightfoot_Jr. "Robert M. Lightfoot Jr.") was announced as acting NASA administrator.

After leaving NASA, Bolden has served on the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") Space Advisory Committee.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wapo2022-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-wapo2022)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Charles_Bolden&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Bolden lives in [Alexandria, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Virginia "Alexandria, Virginia"), and is married to Alexis (née Walker); the couple have two children.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29) Bolden is a [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians"), stating in a question and answer session in May 2010:

> You know, the universe is a big place. I'm a practicing Christian, so in my faith, I learn about omnipotent, omnipresent God, which means he's everywhere. He's all-knowing. He does everything. And I just cannot bring my little pea brain to believe that a God like that would pick one planet of one of millions of suns and say that's the only place in the vast universe that I'm going to put any kind of life. And so the problem is I haven't been far enough away.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

In the [2024 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election"), Bolden endorsed [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris").[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)
