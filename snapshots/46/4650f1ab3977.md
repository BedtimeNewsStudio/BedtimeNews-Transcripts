The **People's Action Party** ( **PAP**) is a major political party in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"). Established in 1954 as a conventional [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left "Centre-left") party, following its initial electoral success in [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Singaporean_general_election "1959 Singaporean general election"), [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") sought to reposition the party ideologically toward the [centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism "Centrism"). In pursuit of this objective, he expelled the party's leftist faction, which reorganised as [Barisan Sosialis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Sosialis "Barisan Sosialis"), in 1961, during the period of [Singapore's merger with Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore's_merger_with_Malaysia "Singapore's merger with Malaysia"). Over the course of the 1960s and since then, the PAP continued its ideological shift towards the [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right "Centre-right").[&#8202;](#ref-back-leelieu2-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-leelieu2) After [Singapore's separation from Malaysia and subsequent independence in 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Singapore_Agreement_1965 "Independence of Singapore Agreement 1965"), the majority of opposition parties, excluding the WP, boycotted the [1968 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Singaporean_general_election "1968 Singaporean general election"). Consequently, the PAP secured all parliamentary seats in that election. In the ensuing decades, the PAP consolidated its political dominance through successive electoral victories, and remains the dominant political party in Parliament, followed by the opposition [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "Workers' Party (Singapore)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rodan2-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-Rodan2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-19) It consistently formed the [executive branch of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Singapore "Government of Singapore") and exerted substantial influence over key national institutions, including the country's sole trade union, the [National Trades Union Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trades_Union_Congress "National Trades Union Congress") (NTUC), which is affiliated with the party, as well as the [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Civil_Service "Singapore Civil Service").[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-20)

Between 1965 and 1981, the PAP was the sole political party represented in Parliament. This period of exclusive representation ended with the party's first electoral defeat in a [1981 by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Anson_by-election "1981 Anson by-election") in the [Anson Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anson_Constituency "Anson Constituency"), where the WP secured the seat. Despite this setback, the PAP has retained its political dominance. In every subsequent general election, the party consistently garnered over 60 percent of the popular vote and secured more than 80 percent of parliamentary seats, achieving [landslide victories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_victories "Landslide victories") on each occasion. Having governed continuously for 67 years, the PAP remains the dominant political force in Singapore, effectively operating within the framework of a _[de facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto "De facto")_ one-party state. It has maintained an unbroken two-thirds parliamentary [supermajority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajority "Supermajority") enabling it to amend the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Singapore "Constitution of Singapore") at will. As of 2025[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit), the PAP is the longest-serving uninterrupted ruling party among contemporary multi-party parliamentary democracies and holds the second-longest tenure of any governing party in a multi-party system in modern history, surpassed solely by Mexico's [Institutional Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_Revolutionary_Party "Institutional Revolutionary Party") (PRI), which governed from 1929 to 2000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-21)

Positioned on the centre-right of Singapore's political spectrum, the PAP espouses a combination of [pragmatic conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatic_conservatism "Pragmatic conservatism") and [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism "Economic liberalism").[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-23) The party generally advocates [free-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-market "Free-market") principles, favouring policies such as [low taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Singapore "Taxation in Singapore"), the absence of [tariffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_tariff_rate "List of countries by tariff rate"), [limited government expenditure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_government_spending_as_percentage_of_GDP "List of countries by government spending as percentage of GDP") relative to [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") (GDP), [minimal economic regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_economics "Regulatory economics") and the promotion of [economic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_freedom "Economic freedom").[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-24) Nonetheless, the PAP occasionally engages in strategic [state intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_interventionism "Economic interventionism"). A distinctive feature of its economic approach is the support for the development and expansion of [state-owned enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprise "State-owned enterprise") (SOEs), locally referred to as [government-linked corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_government-owned_companies#Singapore "List of government-owned companies") (GLCs). These entities were initially established in response to the economic disruptions caused by the British military withdrawal from Singapore in 1971.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-25) GLCs played a central role in driving [export-oriented industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export-oriented_industrialisation "Export-oriented industrialisation"), fostering [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development "Economic development") and [generating employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_creation "Job creation") across key sectors of the [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Singapore "Economy of Singapore"). On social matters, the PAP endorses [communitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communitarian "Communitarian") values and [civic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism "Civic nationalism"). A cornerstone of its social policy is the promotion of national cohesion through the integration of the country's major ethnic groups into a unified [Singaporean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporean "Singaporean") identity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-26)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Mr._Lee_Kuan_Yew_Mayoral_reception_1965_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Mr._Lee_Kuan_Yew_Mayoral_reception_1965_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mr._Lee_Kuan_Yew_Mayoral_reception_1965_(cropped).jpg) [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew"), the first [Prime Minister of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") and one of the founders of the People's Action Party

[Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew"), [Toh Chin Chye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toh_Chin_Chye "Toh Chin Chye") and [Goh Keng Swee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Keng_Swee "Goh Keng Swee") were involved in the [Malayan Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Forum "Malayan Forum"), a London-based student activist group that was against [colonial rule in Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya "Federation of Malaya") in the 1940s and early 1950s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-27)[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-28) Upon returning to Singapore, the group met regularly to discuss approaches to attain independence in Malayan territories and started looking for like-minded individuals to start a political party. Journalist [S. Rajaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Rajaratnam "S. Rajaratnam") was introduced to Lee by Goh.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-29) Lee was also introduced to several English-educated left-wing students and Chinese-educated union and student leaders while working on the Fajar sedition trial and the [National Service riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_National_Service_riots "1954 National Service riots") case.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-30)

### Formation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Formation")\]

The PAP was officially registered as a political party on 21 November 1954. Members of the first [Central Executive Committee (CEC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Executive_Committee_(People's_Action_Party) "Central Executive Committee (People's Action Party)") of the party include a group of trade unionists, lawyers and journalists such as Lee Kuan Yew, [Abdul Samad Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Samad_Ismail "Abdul Samad Ismail"), [Toh Chin Chye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toh_Chin_Chye "Toh Chin Chye"), [Devan Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devan_Nair "Devan Nair"), [S. Rajaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Rajaratnam "S. Rajaratnam"), Chan Chiaw Thor, [Fong Swee Suan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fong_Swee_Suan "Fong Swee Suan"), Tann Wee Keng and [Tann Wee Tiong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_&_Tann "Rajah & Tann").[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-31) The political party was led by Lee Kuan Yew as its secretary-general, with Toh Chin Chye as its founding chairman. Other party officers include Tann Wee Tiong, Lee Gek Seng, [Ong Eng Guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Eng_Guan "Ong Eng Guan") and Tann Wee Keng.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-32)

The PAP first contested the [1955 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Singaporean_general_election "1955 Singaporean general election") in which 25 of 32 seats in the legislature were up for election. In this election, the PAP's four candidates gained much support from the trade union members and student groups such as the [University Socialist Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Socialist_Club "University Socialist Club"), who canvassed for them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-:2) The party won three seats, one by its leader [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") for the Tanjong Pagar division and one by PAP co-founder [Lim Chin Siong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Chin_Siong "Lim Chin Siong") for the [Bukit Timah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Timah "Bukit Timah") division.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-34)[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-35) Then 22 years old, Lim was the youngest Assemblyman ever to be elected to office. The election was won by the [Labour Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Front "Labour Front") headed by [David Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Marshall_(Singaporean_politician) "David Marshall (Singaporean politician)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-36)

In April 1956, Lim and Lee represented the PAP at the [London Constitutional Talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka "Merdeka") along with Chief Minister [David Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Marshall_(Singaporean_politician) "David Marshall (Singaporean politician)") which ended in failure as the British declined to grant Singapore [internal self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance_of_Singapore "Self-governance of Singapore"). On 7 June 1956, Marshall, disappointed with the constitutional talks, stepped down as Chief Minister as he had pledged to do so earlier if self-governance was not achieved. He was replaced by [Lim Yew Hock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Yew_Hock "Lim Yew Hock"), another Labour Front member.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-37) Lim pursued a largely [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism "Anti-communism") campaign and managed to convince the British to make a definite plan for self-government. The [Constitution of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Singapore "Constitution of Singapore") was revised accordingly in 1958, replacing the Rendel Constitution with one that granted Singapore self-government and the ability for its own population to fully elect its Legislative Assembly.

PAP and left-wing members who were [communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communists "Communists") were criticised for inciting riots in the mid-1950s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-38)[&#8202;](#ref-back-centre-left-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-centre-left) Lim Chin Siong, [Fong Swee Suan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fong_Swee_Suan "Fong Swee Suan") and [Devan Nair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devan_Nair "Devan Nair") as well as several unionists were detained by the police after the [Chinese middle schools riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_middle_schools_riots "Chinese middle schools riots").[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-40) Lim Chin Siong was placed under solitary confinement for close to a year, away from his other PAP colleagues, as they were placed in the Medium Security Prison (MSP) instead.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-:1) The number of PAP members imprisoned rose in August 1957, when PAP members from the trade unions (viewed as "communist or pro-communist") won half the seats in the Central Executive Committee (CEC). The "moderate" CEC members, including Lee Kuan Yew, Toh Chin Chye and others, refused to take their appointments in the CEC. Yew Hock's government again made a sweeping round of arrests, imprisoning all the "communist" members, before the "moderates" re-assumed their office.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-42)

Following this, the PAP decided to re-assert ties with the labour faction of Singapore in the hope of securing the votes of working-class Chinese Singaporeans, many of whom were supporters of the jailed unionists. Lee Kuan Yew convinced the incarcerated union leaders to sign documents to state their support for the party and its policies, promising to release the jailed members of the PAP when the party came to power in the next elections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-43) Ex- [Barisan Sosialis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Sosialis "Barisan Sosialis") member Tan Jing Quee claims that Lee was secretly in collusion with the British to stop Lim Chin Siong and the labour supporters from attaining power because of their huge popularity. Quee also states that Lim Yew Hock deliberately provoked the students into rioting and then had the labour leaders arrested.[&#8202;](#ref-back-comet-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-comet) Greg Poulgrain of Griffiths University argued that "Lee Kuan Yew was secretly a party with Lim Yew Hock in urging the Colonial Secretary to impose the subversives ban in making it illegal for former political detainees to stand for election".[&#8202;](#ref-back-comet-1)[\[41\]](#refnote-comet) Lee Kuan Yew eventually accused Lim Chin Siong and his supporters of being communists working for the Communist United Front, but evidence of Lim being a communist cadre was a matter of debate as many documents have yet to be declassified.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-45)[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-46)

### First years in government

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: First years in government")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/ST31May1959.jpg/250px-ST31May1959.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ST31May1959.jpg) _[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Straits_Times "The Straits Times")_ the day after the 1959 election, reporting on the results and the PAP's victory

The PAP eventually won the [1959 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Singaporean_general_election "1959 Singaporean general election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-47) The election was also the first one to produce a fully elected parliament and a cabinet wielding powers of full internal [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self_rule "Self rule"). The party has remained in power ever since, winning a majority of seats in every successive general election. Lee, who was chosen to be the first [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") after an [internal election within the PAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_PAP_prime_ministerial_election "1959 PAP prime ministerial election") and would eventually helm this post for the next 31 years,[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-48) requested the British for the release of the left-wing members of the PAP, including the likes of Lim Chin Siong, Fong Swee Suan, [Sandrasegaran Woodhull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandrasegaran_Woodhull "Sandrasegaran Woodhull"), [James Puthucheary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Puthucheary "James Puthucheary") and Devan Nair.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-49)

In 1961, the Singapore Trades Union Congress (STUC), which had backed the PAP back in 1959, split into the pro-PAP [National Trades Union Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trades_Union_Congress "National Trades Union Congress") (NTUC) and the non-affiliated and more [leftist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leftist "Leftist") Singapore Association of Trade Unions (SATU). The SATU collapsed in 1963, following the now PAP-led government's crackdown and detention of its leaders during [Operation Coldstore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Coldstore "Operation Coldstore") and its subsequent official deregistration on 13 November 1963. The NTUC remains as the sole [trade union centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union_centre "Trade union centre") in the country today and continues to have a close relationship with the PAP.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-50)

#### Great Split of 1961

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Great Split of 1961")\]

See also: [1961 Singaporean by-elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Singaporean_by-elections "1961 Singaporean by-elections")

In 1961, disagreements on the proposed merger plan to form [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") and long-standing internal party power struggle led to the split of the left-wing group from the PAP.[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-51)[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-52)[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-53)

Although the "communist" faction had been frozen out of ever taking over the PAP, other problems had begun to arise internally. [Ong Eng Guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Eng_Guan "Ong Eng Guan"), the former Mayor of the City Council after PAP's victory in the [1957 Singapore City Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Singapore_City_Council_election "1957 Singapore City Council election"), presented a set of "16 Resolutions" to revisit some issues previously explored by Chin Siong's faction of the PAP: abolishing the PPSO, revising the Constitution, and changing the method of selecting cadre members.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-:0):82

Although Ong's 16 Resolutions originated from the left-wing faction led by Lim Chin Siong, that faction had only reluctantly asked the PAP leadership to clarify its position on them,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-2)[\[11\]](#refnote-:6) as they still thought that the party with Lee Kuan Yew at the helm was a better alternative than Ong who was regarded as mercurial and a tyrant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-:1) However, Lee took the stance taken by the left-wing PAP members as a lack of confidence in his leadership. This issue caused a rift between the "moderate" PAP members (led by Lee) and the "left-wing" faction (led by Lim).

Ong was then expelled, and he resigned his Assembly seat to challenge the government to a by-election in Hong Lim in April 1961, where he won 73.3% of the vote.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-55) This was despite the fact that Lee Kuan Yew had made a secret alliance with [Fong Chong Pik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fong_Chong_Pik "Fong Chong Pik"), the leader of the [Communist Party of Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Malaya "Communist Party of Malaya") (CPM), to get the CPM cadres to support the PAP in the by-election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-3)[\[11\]](#refnote-:6)

#### Barisan Sosialis

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Barisan Sosialis")\]

The breakaway group of members formed the [Barisan Sosialis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Sosialis "Barisan Sosialis") with Lim Chin Siong as secretary-general.[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-56) Aside from the Chinese union leaders, lawyers Thampoe Thamby Rajah and Tann Wee Tiong,[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-57) several members from the University Socialist Club such as [James Puthucheary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Puthucheary "James Puthucheary") (uncle of [Janil Puthucheary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janil_Puthucheary "Janil Puthucheary")) and [Poh Soo Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poh_Soo_Kai "Poh Soo Kai") joined the party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-58) 35 of 51 branches of the PAP and 19 of 23 branch secretaries defected to Barisan.

### Merger years, 1963–1965

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Merger years, 1963–1965")\]

See also: [PAP–UMNO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAP%E2%80%93UMNO_relations "PAP–UMNO relations")

After gaining independence from Britain, Singapore joined the federation of Malaysia in 1963. Although the PAP was the ruling party in the state of Singapore, the PAP functioned as an opposition party at the federal level in the larger Malaysian political landscape. At that time and until the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malaysian_general_election "2018 Malaysian general election"), the federal government in Kuala Lumpur was controlled by a coalition led by the [United Malays National Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Malays_National_Organisation "United Malays National Organisation") (UMNO). However, the prospect that the PAP might rule Malaysia agitated UMNO. The PAP's decision to contest federal parliamentary seats outside Singapore and the UMNO decision to contest seats within Singapore breached an unspoken agreement to respect each other's spheres of influence and aggravated [PAP–UMNO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAP%E2%80%93UMNO_relations "PAP–UMNO relations"). The [clash of personalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clash_of_personalities "Clash of personalities") between PAP leader Lee Kuan Yew and Malaysian [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Malaysia "Prime Minister of Malaysia") [Tunku Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunku_Abdul_Rahman "Tunku Abdul Rahman") resulted in a crisis and led to Rahman forcing Singapore to leave Malaysia on 9 August 1965. Upon independence, the nascent People's Action Party of Malaya, which had been registered in Malaysia on 10 March 1964, had its registration cancelled on 9 September 1965, just a month after Singapore's exit. Those with the now non-existent party applied to register People's Action Party, Malaya which was again rejected by the Malaysian government, before settling with the [Democratic Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Action_Party_(Malaysia) "Democratic Action Party (Malaysia)") (DAP).

### Post-independence, 1965–present

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Post-independence, 1965–present")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/People%27s_Action_Party_general_election_rally%2C_Tampines_Stadium%2C_Singapore_-_20110505-04.jpg/250px-People%27s_Action_Party_general_election_rally%2C_Tampines_Stadium%2C_Singapore_-_20110505-04.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:People's_Action_Party_general_election_rally,_Tampines_Stadium,_Singapore_-_20110505-04.jpg) A PAP election rally at [Tampines Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampines_Stadium "Tampines Stadium") in 2011

The PAP has held an overwhelming majority of seats in the [Parliament of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Singapore "Parliament of Singapore") since 1966, when the opposition Barisan Sosialis (Socialist Front) resigned from Parliament after winning 13 seats following the [1963 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Singaporean_general_election "1963 Singaporean general election"), which took place months after a number of their leaders had been arrested in [Operation Coldstore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Coldstore "Operation Coldstore") based on accusations of being communists affiliated with the [Communist Party of Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Malaya "Communist Party of Malaya") (CPM).[&#8202;](#ref-back-comet-2)[\[41\]](#refnote-comet)

It subsequently achieved a monopoly in an expanding parliament (winning every parliamentary seat) for the next four elections ( [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Singaporean_general_election "1968 Singaporean general election"), [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Singaporean_general_election "1972 Singaporean general election"), [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Singaporean_general_election "1976 Singaporean general election") and [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Singaporean_general_election "1980 Singaporean general election")). Opposition parties returned to the legislature at a [1981 by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Anson_by-election "1981 Anson by-election"). The [1984 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Singaporean_general_election "1984 Singaporean general election") was the first election in 21 years in which opposition parties won seats. From then until [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Singaporean_general_election "2006 Singaporean general election"), the PAP faced four opposition MPs at most. Opposition parties did not win more than four parliamentary seats from 1984 until [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Singaporean_general_election "2011 Singaporean general election") when the [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "The Workers' Party (Singapore)") (WP) won six seats and took away a [Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_Representation_Constituency "Group Representation Constituency") (GRC) for the first time for any opposition party, as well as until [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Singaporean_general_election "2020 Singaporean general election") by which an opposition party had won more than one GRC, which was also achieved by the WP.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-59)

Even so, the PAP still holds a [supermajority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajority "Supermajority") in the legislature, to the point that Singapore is effectively a [dominant party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_party_system "Dominant party system") similar to Japan's [Liberal Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_(Japan) "Liberal Democratic Party (Japan)") (LDP) rule of the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-60) With its supermajority, the PAP has always had the ability to amend the [Constitution of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Singapore "Constitution of Singapore") at will, including the introduction of multi-member constituencies under the GRC system (in 1988) or the [Nominated Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominated_Member_of_Parliament "Nominated Member of Parliament") (NMPs) scheme (in 1990), which has helped strengthened the government's dominance and control of Parliament.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tan2012-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-Tan2012)

## Leadership transitions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Leadership transitions")\]

The longtime governing party of [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), spans both past and present, but notably occurred in the mid-1980s where the first generation of PAP leaders in the [CEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Executive_Committee_(PAP) "Central Executive Committee (PAP)") and the [Cabinet of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Singapore "Cabinet of Singapore") ceded power to a second generation of leaders.[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-62)

### First to second generation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: First to second generation")\]

By 1984, the "old guard" (first generation of party leaders) had been governing Singapore for approximately a quarter of a century. Aging leadership was a key concern, and then-Prime Minister of Singapore [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") sought to groom younger leaders. In a speech on 29 September 1984, Lee argued that though the first generation of leaders was still "alert and fully in charge", to hang on to power until they had become feeble would allow power to be wrested from them, with no say in who their successors were.[&#8202;](#ref-back-quah-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-quah)

On 30 September, at the Ordinary Party Conference, power was transferred to the second generation of leaders, who were elected to the [Central Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Executive_Committee_(PAP) "Central Executive Committee (PAP)") in place of all the old CEC members; of the 14-member CEC, Lee Kuan Yew remained the only "old guard" leader.[&#8202;](#ref-back-quah-1)[\[60\]](#refnote-quah)

According to a report to the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress"), the old guard were confident in their "rectitude" and discretion in using their extensive political powers for Singapore's common good, but were not as confident in the next generation in doing so. Various limits on executive power were considered, in order to minimise the chances of corruption. These included a [popularly elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_elections_in_Singapore "Presidential elections in Singapore") [President of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Singapore "President of Singapore") with substantial, nonceremonial powers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-locsuccession-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-locsuccession) This particular reform was enacted with a constitutional amendment in 1991.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-65)

The old guard also sought to eschew the use of PAP as a central political institution, seeking to "depoliticise" and disperse power among society, and sought to include low-level community leaders in government. A policy of cross-fertilisation was enacted: exchange of leaders, "elites" and talent would take place between private and government sectors, civilian and military segments of society, and between the party and the [National Trades Union Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trades_Union_Congress "National Trades Union Congress") (NTUC).[&#8202;](#ref-back-locsuccession-1)[\[61\]](#refnote-locsuccession)

### Second to third generation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Second to third generation")\]

The next generation of leaders in the late 1980s was split between the factions of then [Brigadier General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_General "Brigadier General") [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong") and the older, more-experienced [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong"). Lee Hsien Loong was supported by bureaucrats in the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_(Singapore) "Ministry of Defence (Singapore)") and army colleagues in the [Singapore Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Armed_Forces "Singapore Armed Forces");[&#8202;](#ref-back-locsuccession-2)[\[61\]](#refnote-locsuccession) [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong") had more influence in the [Singapore Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Civil_Service "Singapore Civil Service"), the Cabinet and [government-linked companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprise "State-owned enterprise").[&#8202;](#ref-back-rworthington-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-rworthington)

Lee Kuan Yew himself remained Prime Minister and in the CEC until 1990, when he stepped down in favour of Goh Chok Tong as PM. [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong") became PM in 2004.

### Third to fourth generation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Third to fourth generation")\]

On 23 November 2018, two fourth-generation leadership members (then– [Minister for Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_(Singapore) "Ministry of Finance (Singapore)") [Heng Swee Keat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heng_Swee_Keat "Heng Swee Keat") and then– [Minister for Trade and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Trade_and_Industry_(Singapore) "Ministry of Trade and Industry (Singapore)") [Chan Chun Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chun_Sing "Chan Chun Sing")) were elected as the First and Second Assistant Secretaries-General; these were, respectively, the second- and third-highest positions in the party. They replaced [Teo Chee Hean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teo_Chee_Hean "Teo Chee Hean") and [Tharman Shanmugaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharman_Shanmugaratnam "Tharman Shanmugaratnam"). This represented a significant step of the leadership transition from the third generation to the fourth generation.

On 1 May 2019, Heng Swee Keat was appointed the new and sole Deputy Prime Minister, replacing Teo and Tharman. He was then widely seen as the 4th and next [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") and Secretary-General of PAP succeeding incumbent [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong"). However, on 8 April 2021, Heng unexpectedly announced he would step down as the fourth-generation leader and step aside to pave the way for younger and healthier leaders to take over leadership, citing his health and age as reasons. Several Cabinet members were then seen as possible candidates to succeed Heng, including Minister for Finance [Lawrence Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Wong "Lawrence Wong"), Minister for Health [Ong Ye Kung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Ye_Kung "Ong Ye Kung"), and Minister for Education [Chan Chun Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chun_Sing "Chan Chun Sing").[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-67)

On 14 April 2022, Wong was selected as new leader of PAP's fourth-generation (4G) team, succeeding Heng.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-68) Wong received an "overwhelming majority" of support in the consultation process, surpassing that of other nominees.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-69) His candidacy was unanimously endorsed by the cabinet and subsequently by the PAP [MPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_parliament "Member of parliament") at a party caucus on 14 April.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wong-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-Wong) On 4 December 2024, he was elected Secretary-General of People's Action Party, with an endorsement from Lee Hsien Loong.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-71)[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-72)

## Organisation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Organisation")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/People%27s_Action_Party_supporters%2C_Greenridge_Secondary_School%2C_Singapore_-_20110427-04.jpg/250px-People%27s_Action_Party_supporters%2C_Greenridge_Secondary_School%2C_Singapore_-_20110427-04.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:People's_Action_Party_supporters,_Greenridge_Secondary_School,_Singapore_-_20110427-04.jpg) People's Action Party activists during the [2011 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Singaporean_general_election "2011 Singaporean general election")[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/People%27s_Action_Party_headquarters%2C_New_Upper_Changi_Road_310522.jpg/250px-People%27s_Action_Party_headquarters%2C_New_Upper_Changi_Road_310522.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:People's_Action_Party_headquarters,_New_Upper_Changi_Road_310522.jpg) People's Action Party headquarters in New Upper Changi Road

During its initial years, the party had adopted a traditional [Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninism "Leninism") form of party organisation, together with a vanguard cadre from its [labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement "Labour movement")-leaning faction. The PAP Executive later expelled the leftist faction in 1961, bringing the ideological basis of the party into the centre and later in the 1960s moving further to the right.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DianeMilne-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-DianeMilne) Since independence, there have been no publicly known factions within the PAP. While MPs debate policies internally or voice some disagreement in Parliament, everyone must vote along the party line once a decision is made. However, political developments since the late 2010s have challenged the party's cohesion. These include the 2019 defection of former PAP MP and cadre [Tan Cheng Bock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Cheng_Bock "Tan Cheng Bock") to form the [Progress Singapore Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Singapore_Party "Progress Singapore Party") (PSP), alongside Lee Hsien Loong's high-profile family rivalry over [38 Oxley Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38_Oxley_Road "38 Oxley Road") that first erupted publicly in 2017, which involved his brother [Lee Hsien Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Yang "Lee Hsien Yang"). Observers also believe that internal factions do exist but are strictly kept out of the public eye to avoid political instability.[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-74)

In the beginning, there were about 500 so-called temporary [cadres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadre_(politics) "Cadre (politics)") appointed,[&#8202;](#ref-back-DianeMilne-1)[\[70\]](#refnote-DianeMilne) however the current number of cadres is unknown, with the register of cadres being kept confidential. In 1988, [Wong Kan Seng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wong_Kan_Seng "Wong Kan Seng") revealed that there were more than 1,000 cadres.[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-75) Cadre members have the right to attend party conferences and to vote for and elect and to be elected into the [Central Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Executive_Committee_(PAP) "Central Executive Committee (PAP)") (CEC), the pinnacle of party leaders.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DianeMilne-2)[\[70\]](#refnote-DianeMilne)

To become a party cadre, a party member must be first nominated by the MP in their branch. The candidate will then undergo three sessions of interview, each with four to five ministers or MPs and the appointment is then made by the CEC. About 100 candidates are nominated each year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-76) Alternatively, party cadres are recruited based on recommendations by existing PAP cadres, prospective recruits for general election candidates are then internally shortlisted before.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DianeMilne-3)[\[70\]](#refnote-DianeMilne)

### Central Executive Committee and Secretary-General

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Central Executive Committee and Secretary-General")\]

See also: [1959 PAP prime ministerial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_PAP_prime_ministerial_election "1959 PAP prime ministerial election")

Political power in the party is concentrated in the CEC, led by the secretary-general. The secretary-general of the PAP is the leader of the party. Due to PAP's electoral victories in every general election since 1959, the [prime minister of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Singapore "Prime minister of Singapore") has been by convention the secretary-general of the PAP since 1959. Key appointments in the CEC are usually Cabinet members.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DianeMilne-4)[\[70\]](#refnote-DianeMilne)

From 1957 onward, the rules laid down that the outgoing CEC should recommend a list of candidates from which the cadre members can then vote for the next CEC. This has recently changed so that the CEC nominates eight members and the party caucus selects the remaining ten.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DianeMilne-5)[\[70\]](#refnote-DianeMilne)

Historically, the position of Secretary-General was not considered for the office of Prime Minister, but rather the Central Executive Committee held an election to choose the prime minister. There was a contest between PAP Secretary-General [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") and PAP Treasurer [Ong Eng Guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Eng_Guan "Ong Eng Guan"), prior to 1959. Lee subsequently won the leadership and was inaugurated as the first prime minister of Singapore.[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-77)

### Young PAP and internet presence

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Young PAP and internet presence")\]

The Young PAP is the youth-wing of the party, serving as a [youth organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_organisation "Youth organisation") for young adults and students in Singapore who support the PAP and have an interest in [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics "Politics").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:02-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-:02) The incumbent chairman of the youth-wing is [Janil Puthucheary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janil_Puthucheary "Janil Puthucheary").[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-80)[&#8202;](#ref-back-81-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-81)[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-82) The YP's predecessor, the PAP Youth Committee, was established in 1986, under [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong")'s tenure as chairman. All PAP members under the age of 35 had then been grouped under the Youth Committee. In 1993, the Youth Committee was renamed the Young PAP. In an effort to attract members, then Chairman [George Yeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Yeo "George Yeo") said that people joining the YP could take positions different from [central party leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Executive_Committee_(PAP) "Central Executive Committee (PAP)"). The age limit was raised from 35 to 40.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rodan101-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-rodan101) Memberships are issued through the PAP branches under each constituency in Singapore.[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-84) By 2005, the committee had grown to more than 6,000 members.[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-85) In 2010, then Vice-chairman [Zaqy Mohamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaqy_Mohamad "Zaqy Mohamad") said the YP attracts over 1200 new members that year, an increase on the 1000 new members in 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-86)

Since 1995, the youth-wing of the PAP has had an internet presence that aims to "correct 'misinformation' about Singapore politics or culture".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mickey-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-Mickey) Under the urging of then [Minister for Information and the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Information,_Communications_and_the_Arts#Ministers "Minister for Information, Communications and the Arts") George Yeo, Young PAP took charge of running several online websites to create an online presence for the party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-88) After popular forum [Sintercom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintercom "Sintercom") was shut down in 2001, the Young PAP offered their own forum for moderated discussions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-89) They have since set up various blogs and social media accounts with multimedia content to engage the masses.[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-90)[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-91)[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-92)

In February 2007, it was reported by _[The Straits Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Straits_Times "The Straits Times")_ that the PAP's new media committee chaired by Minister [Ng Eng Hen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng_Eng_Hen "Ng Eng Hen"), had initiated an effort to counter critics anonymously on the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet") "as it was necessary for the PAP to have a voice on cyberspace".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-:3) The initiative was divided by two sub-committees, one of which was in charge of strategising the campaigns and is co-headed by Minister [Lui Tuck Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lui_Tuck_Yew "Lui Tuck Yew") and MP Zaqy Mohamad. The other sub-committee—new media capabilities group led by MPs [Baey Yam Keng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baey_Yam_Keng "Baey Yam Keng") and [Josephine Teo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephine_Teo "Josephine Teo") executed the strategies. The initiative was set up after the [2006 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Singaporean_general_election "2006 Singaporean general election") and also included around 20 IT-savvy PAP activists.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[90\]](#refnote-:3)

### Friends of the PAP

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Friends of the PAP")\]

The PAP has a long-running programme, known as 'Friends of the PAP' by which it enlists individuals and organisations to assist in promoting its political goals. In 2002, secretary-general Goh Chok Tong announced an intention to expand this programme, which at the time was primarily limited to "establishment figures" in the public and private sector. He established a related scheme titled "Young Friends of the PAP" to attract Singaporeans below the age of 40 as well and explained that the intention was to "refresh" the PAP and improve the "quality" of PAP's membership. Membership as a 'Young Friend' was by invitation only, and the group was limited to about 500 people.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-94)

According to local media reports, the Friends of the PAP programme had "fallen out of the public consciousness" after 2002. However, in June 2024, the PAP revived the Friends of the PAP programme and expanded it, now with a renewed focus on local [social media influencers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influencer_marketing "Influencer marketing"), with the intention to connect with a wider and younger audience. The PAP did not respond to media queries about whether the Friends of the PAP programme remained invite-only or what the rules of engagement for members of the programme were.[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-95)

## Recruitment and retention

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Recruitment and retention")\]

Unlike many political parties in numerous other countries that rely on [grassroots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassroots "Grassroots") mobilisation and overt rewards for party loyalty, the PAP adopts a highly [centralised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralised "Centralised"), [top-down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom-up_and_top-down_approaches#Corporate_environment_(Performance_management) "Bottom-up and top-down approaches") and [meritocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocratic "Meritocratic") approach to candidate [recruitment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruitment "Recruitment"); this is officially to prevent [nepotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepotism "Nepotism"), [cronyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cronyism "Cronyism"), [branch stacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_stacking "Branch stacking"), [entryism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entryism "Entryism") or any form of [primary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_election "Primary election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-:4) Rather than progressing through local branches or [factional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_faction "Political faction") networks, prospective legislators are identified through "talent spotting" ( [executive search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_search "Executive search")) by senior figures in [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government "Government") and industry.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-:5) This screening mechanism is designed to attract individuals with distinguished records in the [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service "Civil service"), the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military "Military"), [academia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy "Academy"), managerial executives from the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector "Private sector") or [non-profit sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_sector "Voluntary sector"), or professional sectors such as [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering "Engineering"), [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law "Law"), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance "Finance"), [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media "Mass media") and [health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care "Health care"). [Murali Pillai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murali_Pillai "Murali Pillai"), a PAP MP, has claimed that the party's "unique [preselection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preselection "Preselection") invitation system" is meant to "maintain its [technocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocracy "Technocracy"), meritocratic, _[realpolitik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realpolitik "Realpolitik")_ and [pragmatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatism "Pragmatism") character", and to "prevent [personalist dictatorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship#Personalist "Dictatorship"), [populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism "Populism") and [political polarisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_polarisation "Political polarisation")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-98)

Prospective candidates first emerge through recommendations by PAP activists, corporate leaders, MPs and senior civil servants.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-1)[\[93\]](#refnote-:4) Each year, roughly one hundred individuals are invited to "tea sessions", a [euphemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemism "Euphemism") for informal party interviews seeking candidates in upcoming general elections, with 2 to 3 serving [ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Singapore "Cabinet of Singapore"), during which they engage in in-depth discussions lasting up to two and a half hours. Sometimes, the true intent of the interview(s) is initially unbeknownst to attendees.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-:7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-101)[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-102) Those shortlisted then face two formal interviews conducted by a high-level panel at party headquarters; successful interviewees meet again with ministers, though final approval rests with the Central Executive Committee (CEC) before the successful interviewees are "invited to join politics".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[96\]](#refnote-:7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:8-1)[\[97\]](#refnote-:8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-103)

Furthermore, a small number of recruits, typically five or six per electoral cycle, are selected for additional psychological assessment. These candidates undergo a day-and-a-half examination of more than 1,000 questions to evaluate their personality and disposition, and are then earmarked as potential ministerial talent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[\[94\]](#refnote-:5) While the PAP presents this elaborate, multi-stage process as a means of upholding "meritocracy" by choosing the "best man or woman for the job", critics argue that the heavy reliance on elite networks and [professional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional "Professional") pedigrees renders the system fundamentally [elitist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elitism "Elitism") and prone to [groupthink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupthink "Groupthink") rather than genuinely open to wider society, especially the [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class "Working class") who may share a different [socioeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic_status "Socioeconomic status") environment and resulting [worldview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldview "Worldview").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-2)[\[94\]](#refnote-:5)

Although only a fraction of prospective short-listed candidates are selected to represent the PAP at general elections, not everyone invited to the candidate pipeline accepts the offer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-3)[\[94\]](#refnote-:5) Many decline for personal or professional reasons; some encounter employer restrictions, others face familial opposition or believe that they are unsuited for politics. Likewise, when it comes to retaining serving PAP MPs in Singapore, despite the elevated salaries for ministers and MPs in Singapore, a small number also choose not to seek re-election or step aside before any given election, citing similar personal, family, lifestyle or career considerations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-104)[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-105)[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-106)

## Ideology

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Ideology")\]

### Asian democracy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Asian democracy")\]

Professor Hussin Mutalib from the [National University of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Singapore "National University of Singapore") (NUS) opines that the PAP has often set forth the idea of Asian democracy and values, drawing from a notion of [Asian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_culture "Asian culture") and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism") to construct ideological bulwarks against [Western democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_democracy "Western democracy"). He added that for founding prime minister [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew"), "Singapore would be better off without Western-style liberal democracy".[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-107)

Consequently, the governance of the PAP has occasionally been characterised by some observers, especially in the [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world"), as semi-authoritarian by liberal democratic standards or having turned Singapore into a [nanny state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanny_state "Nanny state").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tan2007-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-Tan2007) According to Professor Kenneth Paul Tan from the NUS, the PAP proclaim that many [Singaporeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporeans "Singaporeans") continue to vote for the party as economic considerations, pragmatism and stability triumph over accountability and checks and balances by opposition parties.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tan2007-1)[\[105\]](#refnote-Tan2007)

### Economic policies

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Economic policies")\]

See also: [Temasek (company)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temasek_(company) "Temasek (company)") and [GIC (sovereign wealth fund)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIC_(sovereign_wealth_fund) "GIC (sovereign wealth fund)")

The party [economic ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics "Macroeconomics") has always accepted the need for some [welfare spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare "Social welfare") and for pragmatic [economic interventionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_interventionism "Economic interventionism"). However, [free-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-market "Free-market") policies have been popular since the 1980s as part of the wider implementation of a [meritocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy "Meritocracy") in [civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_society "Civil society"), and Singapore frequently ranks extremely highly on indices of [economic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_freedom "Economic freedom") published by [economically liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_theory_of_economics "Liberal theory of economics") organisations such as the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank") and the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund"). Singapore is also the only Asian country with the top AAA sovereign rating from the "Big Three" [credit rating agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_rating_agencies "Credit rating agencies") of [S&P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S&P "S&P"), [Moody's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moody's "Moody's") and [Fitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitch_Ratings "Fitch Ratings").[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-109)

Lee Kuan Yew said in 1992: "Through [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") watching, I concluded that state welfare and subsidies blunted the individual's drive to succeed. I watched with amazement the ease with which Hong Kong workers adjusted their salaries upwards in boom times and downwards in recessions. I resolved to reverse course on the welfare policies which my party had inherited or copied from [British Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") policies".[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-110)

Notably, since Singapore's independence in 1965, the party has also supported the creation of [state-owned enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprise "State-owned enterprise"), known within Singapore as [Government-linked Corporations (GLCs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_government-owned_companies "List of government-owned companies"), in order to jumpstart [industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation "Industrialisation"), spearhead [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development "Economic development") and lead to [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth "Economic growth") (primarily [job creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_creation "Job creation")) in various sectors of the [Singaporean economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Singapore "Economy of Singapore") as there was a lack of private sector funds and expertise, particularly in the early years of nationhood. Various GLCs were formed to pursue strategic sectors such as in ship building and repair ( [Sembcorp Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sembcorp_Marine "Sembcorp Marine"), [Keppel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keppel_Corporation "Keppel Corporation")), aviation and defence ( [Singapore Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Airlines "Singapore Airlines"), [ST Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ST_Engineering "ST Engineering")), telecommunications ( [Singtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singtel "Singtel")), real estate ( [CapitaLand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CapitaLand "CapitaLand")) and development finance ( [DBS Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DBS_Bank "DBS Bank")) amongst others. In addition, various GLCs were set up as [private-public partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership "Public–private partnership"), notable as joint ventures or strategic alliances with foreign companies or investors with relevant expertise, particularly in the [petrochemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrochemical "Petrochemical") and [oil refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_refinery "Oil refinery") industries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-111)

### Social policies

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Social policies")\]

Since the early years of the PAP's rule, the idea of [survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivalism "Survivalism") as a small and vulnerable country with hostile neighbours has been a central theme of [Singaporean politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Singapore "Politics of Singapore"). According to Diane Mauzy and R. S. Milne, most analysts of Singapore have discerned four major [ideologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideologies "Ideologies") of the PAP, namely [pragmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realpolitik "Realpolitik"), [meritocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy "Meritocracy"), [multiracialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracialism "Multiracialism") and [Asian values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_values "Asian values")/ [communitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communitarianism "Communitarianism").[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-112) The PAP also [advocates nationalism not based on ethnocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism "Civic nationalism"), encouraging a united Singaporean identity while also recognising the [main ethnic groups that make up the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Singapore "Demographics of Singapore").[&#8202;](#ref-back-civnat-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-civnat)

In January 1989, then President [Wee Kim Wee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wee_Kim_Wee "Wee Kim Wee") in his opening address to the [7th Parliament of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Parliament_of_Singapore "7th Parliament of Singapore") stated that Singapore must adopt a set of shared national values. He was of the view that a national ideology was useful to bond Singaporeans together by preserving the cultural heritage of the core communities of Singapore, and upholding certain common values that would capture the essence of being a Singaporean.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SV-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-SV) In response, the government set up a committee as a follow-up to Wee's proposal, and in January 1991, the PAP formally introduced a [white paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_paper "White paper") on "Shared Values" for the country, which consists of five national values to forge a national identity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SV-1)[\[110\]](#refnote-SV) These values were: nation before community and society above self; family as the basic unit of society; regard and community support for the individual; consensus instead of contention, and racial and religious harmony. They were also set as a contrast against the "more Westernised, individualistic, and self-centred outlook on life" and to uphold the "traditional Asian ideas of morality, duty and society".[&#8202;](#ref-back-SV-2)[\[110\]](#refnote-SV)

At an Institute of Policy Studies (IPS) dialogue held on 2 July 2015 and chaired by [Fareed Zakaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fareed_Zakaria "Fareed Zakaria"), Prime Minister [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong") spoke about the need to maintain a [Jeffersonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffersonian_democracy "Jeffersonian democracy") [natural aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy "Meritocracy") in the system to instill a culture of respect and to avoid [anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy "Anarchy").[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-114)[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-115) In November 2019, Lee stated at a party convention that the PAP must not allow the "disconnect between the masses and the elite seen in other countries to take root in Singapore".[&#8202;](#ref-back-116-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-116) During the election campaign in July 2020, Lee's estranged brother over [38 Oxley Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38_Oxley_Road "38 Oxley Road"), [Lee Hsien Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Yang "Lee Hsien Yang"), accused the PAP of [elitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elitism "Elitism") as one of his explanations of joining the [Progress Singapore Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Singapore_Party "Progress Singapore Party") (PSP).[&#8202;](#ref-back-117-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-117) In a 2024 interview, Lee Hsien Loong criticised " [wokeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke "Woke")", claiming that it "causes life to be burdensome" and saying that "he did not want Singapore to go in that direction".[&#8202;](#ref-back-118-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-118)

### Views on other ideologies

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Views on other ideologies")\]

Particularly during the climate of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"), the party was openly hostile to [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist "Communist") or [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing "Left-wing") political ideologies despite a brief joint alliance with the [pro-labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement "Labour movement") co-founders of the PAP during the party's early years, who were eventually [accused of being communists and arrested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Coldstore "Operation Coldstore"). Nevertheless, Singapore formally joined the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement") (NAM) in 1970 and did not openly align with the [Capitalist Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalist_Bloc "Capitalist Bloc"). In 1987, as part of [Operation Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Spectrum "Operation Spectrum"), the PAP government arrested various individuals under accusations of plotting to overthrow the government and to establish a [communist state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state "Communist state"). Since the 2010s, the party while remaining largely conservative was seen by some observers as to have adopted a more [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics "Centre-left politics") tack in certain areas in order to remain electorally dominant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-119)

In 1976, the PAP formally resigned from the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International "Socialist International") (SI) after the [Dutch Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(Netherlands) "Labour Party (Netherlands)") had initially proposed to expel the PAP for the Singapore government's internment of [political prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_prisoners "Political prisoners") without trial, and accused it of human rights violations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-120-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-120)[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-121)

### Symbolism

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=People%27s_Action_Party&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Symbolism")\]

The PAP symbol, which is a [red flash and blue circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_and_circle "Flash and circle") on white, stands for action inside multicultural unity. It also appears on party flags on parades. PAP members at party rallies have customarily worn a uniform of white shirts and white trousers which symbolises incorruptibility and purity of the party's ideologies of the government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-122) [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") acknowledged that the flash and circle was inspired by the [British Union of Fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Union_of_Fascists "British Union of Fascists") (BUF), but that the colors were changed to better represent the PAP.[&#8202;](#ref-back-123-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-123)
