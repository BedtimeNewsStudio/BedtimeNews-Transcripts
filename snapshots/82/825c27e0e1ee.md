The **railway system in [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam")** is owned and operated by the state-owned [Vietnam Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Railways "Vietnam Railways") ( [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language "Vietnamese language"): _Đường sắt Việt Nam_). The principal route, the [single track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_track_(rail) "Single track (rail)") [North-South Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Railway_(Vietnam) "North–South Railway (Vietnam)") running between [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi") and [Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City"), accounts for 1,726 kilometres (1,072mi) of the network's total length of 2,600 kilometres (1,600mi). The national railway network uses mainly [metre gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge "Metre gauge"), although there are several [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge "Standard gauge") and [mixed gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_gauge "Mixed gauge") lines in the north of the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-infra-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-vr-infra)

The first railways in Vietnam were established in the 1880s, with construction beginning in 1888; these included a [tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram "Tram") running between the ports of [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City") and [Cholon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%A3_L%E1%BB%9Bn,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Chợ Lớn, Ho Chi Minh City"), and a [regional rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail "Regional rail") line connecting Saigon with [Mỹ Tho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%E1%BB%B9_Tho_(city) "Mỹ Tho (city)"). Railway construction flourished soon afterwards, during the administration of [Paul Doumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Doumer "Paul Doumer") as [Governor-General of French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_French_Indochina "Governor-General of French Indochina") from 1897 to 1902. It was during this time that construction of the [Yunnan–Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Vietnam_Railway "Yunnan–Vietnam Railway") and North–South railways began. Construction of the north–south line took over thirty years, finally ending in 1936, during which time other branch lines were also completed. Beginning in World War II, the entire rail network became a target of bombing attacks by a number of groups, including both [North Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam") and [South Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam") troops during the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). Although the main lines—particularly the North–South line—were quickly restored and returned to service once conflict ended, many branch lines were abandoned and dismantled at their expense, their infrastructure used to replace damaged sections of the main lines, or sold as [scrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrap_metal "Scrap metal").[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)

With increased economic growth brought on by the [Doi Moi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_Moi "Doi Moi") reforms of the late 1980s, the railway system has entered a renewed phase of development. A number of major projects supported by [official development assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_development_assistance "Official development assistance") have been proposed or are currently underway, including a series of projects to improve bridge and railway safety on the North-South Railway line,[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab) connections to [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia") and [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-aseansec-skrl-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-aseansec-skrl) and the restoration of a number of defunct lines, including the [Đà Lạt–Tháp Chàm railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Lat%E2%80%93Thap_Cham_Railway "Da Lat–Thap Cham Railway") first established in the 1930s. A [high-speed rail link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Express_Railway_(Vietnam) "North–South Express Railway (Vietnam)") between Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City has also been proposed, which would reduce journey length from 30 hours to around 6 hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-afp-jun2010-highspeed-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-afp-jun2010-highspeed) Laos has [held a ceremony but construction remains stalled (2019)](http://www.rfa.org/english/news/laos/railway-01032014153336.html) on a rail line to [Lao Bao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_Bao "Lao Bao") from [Savannakhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannakhet "Savannakhet"), across from the Thai rail head to [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok "Bangkok"). Two high-speed railway projects include the [North-South Railway Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-South_Express_Railway "North-South Express Railway") and the [Ho Chi Minh City–Cần Thơ express railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City%E2%80%93C%E1%BA%A7n_Th%C6%A1_express_railway "Ho Chi Minh City–Cần Thơ express railway").

## Usage

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Usage")\]

Rail transport remains relatively underused as a mode of transport in Vietnam. While road transport dominates the transport sector by far—accounting for 65% of freight moved as of 2006—rail transport accounted for only 4% of freight transportation in 2008, and 5% of passenger transportation, leading it to be considered the "least relevant" of all modes of transport in the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union")'s 2010 [Green Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_paper "Green paper") on Vietnam.[&#8202;](#ref-back-greenbook-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-greenbook) According to reports by the [Asian Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Development_Bank "Asian Development Bank"), however, the rail transport is growing, and carving out a significant role for itself in long-distance bulk cargo transport.[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")\]

### Colonial beginnings

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Colonial beginnings")\]

[![A black-and-white photo of a locomotive carrying and flanked by workers.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Inauguration_de_la_ligne_de_Tramway_Sa%C3%AFgon_%C3%A0_Cholon_le_27_d%C3%A9cembre_1881.jpg/250px-Inauguration_de_la_ligne_de_Tramway_Sa%C3%AFgon_%C3%A0_Cholon_le_27_d%C3%A9cembre_1881.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Inauguration_de_la_ligne_de_Tramway_Sa%C3%AFgon_%C3%A0_Cholon_le_27_d%C3%A9cembre_1881.jpg) Inauguration of the [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon "Saigon")– [Cholon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%E1%BB%A3_L%E1%BB%9Bn,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Chợ Lớn, Ho Chi Minh City") tramway, 1881.

Rail transport was introduced to Vietnam in the 1880s, supported by the French administration of [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina "French Indochina"). The earliest rail project to be completed was the Saigon–Cholon [tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram "Tram"), established in 1881 in what was then [Cochinchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochinchina "Cochinchina"), operated by the Cochinchina Steam Tramway Company (SGTVC). After a month of operation, the Lieutenant Governor of Cochinchina noted that ridership had already reached two thousand passengers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-outremer-U50-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-outremer-U50)[&#8202;](#ref-back-outremer-U50-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-outremer-U50)[&#8202;](#ref-back-outremer-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-outremer) Construction of the first [regional rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail "Regional rail") project, linking [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City") and [Mỹ Tho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%E1%BB%B9_Tho_(city) "Mỹ Tho (city)"), began in the same year, to be completed soon afterwards in 1885. The Saigon–Mỹ Tho line reduced travel time between the two cities from 12 hours to only 3 hours,[&#8202;](#ref-back-hcmmuseum-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-hcmmuseum) and established a connection between Saigon and the shipping lanes of the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta "Mekong Delta").[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-4)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-trainsworld-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-trainsworld)[&#8202;](#ref-back-chambers-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-chambers) The first railway in [Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonkin "Tonkin"), a 600mm (1ft11+5⁄8in) gauge line connecting [Lạng Sơn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%E1%BA%A1ng_S%C6%A1n_(city) "Lạng Sơn (city)") to Phu Lang Thuong (now known as [Bắc Giang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%AFc_Giang "Bắc Giang")), was established in 1895.[&#8202;](#ref-back-chfer-persee2-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-chfer-persee2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-lanessan-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-lanessan)

Railway construction multiplied during the administration of [Paul Doumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Doumer "Paul Doumer") as [Governor-General of French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_French_Indochina "Governor-General of French Indochina") from 1897 to 1902. Doumer's predecessor, [Jean Marie de Lanessan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Marie_Antoine_de_Lanessan "Jean Marie Antoine de Lanessan"), had been convinced of the necessity of building railways to connect the different parts of Indochina, and had identified certain key routes that should be built as a matter of priority; among these, a route connecting [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi") and [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon "Saigon"), and another connecting Hanoi and [Lào Cai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A0o_Cai_province "Lào Cai province").[&#8202;](#ref-back-lanessan-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-lanessan) In 1897, Doumer submitted an overarching proposal for railway development in Indochina, including plans for what would eventually become the [Yunnan–Vietnam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Vietnam_Railway "Yunnan–Vietnam Railway") and the [North–South Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Railway_(Vietnam) "North–South Railway (Vietnam)"). The French government approved the construction of the entire Yunnan line and several sections of the North–South line, approving a loan of 200million francs within the following year. Work began swiftly thereafter, with the Phu Lang Thuong—Lạng Sơn line being upgraded and extended from Hanoi to the Chinese border at Dong Dang. The section between Gia Lâm and Đồng Đăng was inaugurated in July 1900[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) but completion of gauge conversion of the remaining section and the first section of the Yunnan line between [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi") and [Haiphong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiphong "Haiphong") were not completed until 1902.[&#8202;](#ref-back-trainsworld-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-trainsworld)[&#8202;](#ref-back-chfer-persee2-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-chfer-persee2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-haiphong-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-vr-haiphong)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Hano%C3%AF_-_Pont_sur_le_Fleuve_Rouge.jpg/250px-Hano%C3%AF_-_Pont_sur_le_Fleuve_Rouge.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hano%C3%AF_-_Pont_sur_le_Fleuve_Rouge.jpg) [Long Bien Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Bien_Bridge "Long Bien Bridge"), built in 1902.

The Hanoi–Haiphong railway was one leg of the Yunnan–Vietnam Railway, which had begun construction in 1900, and continued northward with the erection of Paul Doumer Bridge (now known as [Long Bien Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Bien_Bridge "Long Bien Bridge")) in 1902, the largest bridge in Southeast Asia at that time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sanbeck-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-sanbeck) Construction on the Yunnan line continued northwestward towards the border town of Lào Cai, opening in segments beginning in 1903; the entire Hanoi–Lào Cai line was finally opened in 1906. Finally, in 1910, the line was extended to its final destination of [Kunming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming "Kunming").[&#8202;](#ref-back-trainsworld-2)[\[15\]](#refnote-trainsworld)[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai2-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-adb-laocai2) Construction of the Yunnan line was an extremely difficult undertaking, incurring not only great expenditures but also a great loss of human life. At least 12,000 labourers died from [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") or accidents, 10,000 of these in the [Nanxi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanxi_River_(Yunnan) "Nanxi River (Yunnan)") Valley alone.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rlee03-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-rlee03)

Construction of the North–South Railway began in 1899, and lasted over thirty years, with individual sections completed serially: from 1899 to 1905, the Hanoi– [Vinh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinh "Vinh") section was laid down, followed by the Nha Trang–Saigon section from 1905 to 1913, then the Vinh– [Huế](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu%E1%BA%BF "Huế") section from 1913 to 1927, and finally, the remaining Huế– [Nha Trang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang "Nha Trang") section from 1930 to 1936. On 2 October 1936, the entire 1,726km (1,072mi) Hanoi–Saigon link was formally put into full operation. The first journeys from end to end of the newly completed line, dubbed the _Transindochinois_, generally took about 60 hours, or two days and three nights.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24) This decreased to about 40 hours by the late 1930s, with trains travelling at an average speed of 43km/h (27mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-5)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)[&#8202;](#ref-back-chfer-persee2-2)[\[17\]](#refnote-chfer-persee2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-lpviet-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-lpviet)

### Wartime

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Wartime")\]

[![A black-and-white photo showing Japanese troops, some on horsebacks, entering Saigon.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Japanese_troops_entering_Saigon_in_1941.jpg/250px-Japanese_troops_entering_Saigon_in_1941.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Japanese_troops_entering_Saigon_in_1941.jpg) The Japanese [Indochina Expeditionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina_Expeditionary_Army "Indochina Expeditionary Army") enters [Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City") in 1941.

From the beginning of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War") in 1937, the railway into [Yunnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan "Yunnan") served as a channel for arms shipments to the Chinese [Kuomintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang "Kuomintang"). French refusal to halt shipments of arms and other war-related goods to China through Tonkin eventually provoked the [Invasion of French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_French_Indochina "Invasion of French Indochina") by Japanese forces in 1940. The Japanese used the railway system extensively during their occupation, inviting sabotage by the [Viet Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Minh "Viet Minh") as well as airborne Allied bombing raids. The railways sustained considerable damage, including the destruction of bridges.[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-6)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-lpviet-1)[\[25\]](#refnote-lpviet)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

Shortly after World War II ended, the [First Indochina War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War "First Indochina War") began, and the Viet Minh's sabotage of the rail system continued, this time against the armies of the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union "French Union"). The French returned several sections of the railway to full operation, allowing trains to circulate freely from Saigon to [Ninh Hoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninh_H%C3%B2a_District "Ninh Hòa District"), Saigon to [Loc Ninh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%E1%BB%99c_Ninh,_B%C3%ACnh_Ph%C6%B0%E1%BB%9Bc "Lộc Ninh, Bình Phước"), Saigon to Mỹ Tho and Huế to Tourane (Da Nang) by the end of 1947. The sections lying between [Nha Trang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang "Nha Trang") and Tourane and north of Huế were judged to be too insecure to be returned to service at the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lpviet-2)[\[25\]](#refnote-lpviet)[&#8202;](#ref-back-catskill-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-catskill)

[![A circular insignia, divided roughly into green and red halves. The phrase "Train Blinde – Aes Triplex Deo Juvante" surrounds the coloured halves.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/77/TRAIN-BLINDE.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:TRAIN-BLINDE.gif) Insignia of _La Rafale_, the [armoured train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_train "Armoured train") used by the French.

Early in the conflict, the Viet Minh's sabotage attempts failed to seriously damage the French railways, and most trains circulated without much protection. Beginning in 1947, however, the Viet Minh began to use mines, with circulation slowing to a crawl as they became more and more powerful.[&#8202;](#ref-back-anai-cdf-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-anai-cdf) In response, the French began using the armed [armoured train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_train "Armoured train") _La Rafale_ as both a cargo-carrier and a mobile surveillance unit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30) In February 1951 the first _Rafale_ was in service on the Saigon-Nha Trang section of the North–South line,[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32) manned by the [2nd Foreign Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Foreign_Infantry_Regiment "2nd Foreign Infantry Regiment") ( [French Foreign Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Foreign_Legion "French Foreign Legion")). Use of the _Rafale_ failed to deter the Viet Minh, however, who continued sabotaging the line, making off with its rails under cover of night and using them to create a 300-kilometre (190mi)-long clandestine rail network between Ninh Hoa and Da Nang, in a Viet Minh-controlled area.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lpviet-3)[\[25\]](#refnote-lpviet) In 1954, following the signature of the [Geneva Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conference_(1954) "Geneva Conference (1954)") and the end of the First Indochina War, Vietnam—along with its railway system—was divided along the [Bến Hải River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%E1%BA%BFn_H%E1%BA%A3i_River "Bến Hải River") in [Quảng Trị Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%E1%BA%A3ng_Tr%E1%BB%8B_Province "Quảng Trị Province").[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-7)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-lpviet-4)[\[25\]](#refnote-lpviet)[&#8202;](#ref-back-catskill-1)[\[27\]](#refnote-catskill)

Throughout the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"), the Vietnamese railway network—especially the North–South Railway—was a target of bombardments and sabotage by both North Vietnamese and South Vietnamese forces. In the South, American aid allowed the [South Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam") government to reconstruct the main line between Saigon and Huế, and several branch lines were also completed. As reconstruction efforts advanced, however, bombardments and sabotage of the railways by the [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong "Viet Cong") and the North Vietnamese intensified, reducing the transportation capacity and effectiveness of the railway, and eventually forcing the abandonment of many large sections of the track. In 1964, Typhoons [Joan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Pacific_typhoon_season#Typhoon_Joan "1964 Pacific typhoon season") and [Iris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Pacific_typhoon_season#Typhoon_Iris "1964 Pacific typhoon season"), the worst to strike Vietnam in sixty-five years, damaged the railway system even further, restricting operations to five separated segments. A second reconstruction effort, assisted again by the U.S. government, began in December 1966, and progressed in those areas where security was re-established. The system reopened 340 kilometers of main line in areas where security was restored.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lpviet-5)[\[25\]](#refnote-lpviet)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vnbasedev-ch8-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-vnbasedev-ch8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vnbasedev-ch1-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-vnbasedev-ch1)

[![A black-and-white photograph showing an explosion on a railway bridge, with an American bomber flying overhead.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/Hai_Duong_bridge_bombing_1972.jpg/250px-Hai_Duong_bridge_bombing_1972.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hai_Duong_bridge_bombing_1972.jpg) An American attack squadron bombs the Hải Dương bridge during [Operation Linebacker I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Linebacker_I "Operation Linebacker I").

In North Vietnam, American bombing of railways was concentrated on key targets such as bridges, both along the North–South Railway and along the lines north of Hanoi, such as the Hanoi–Lào Cai and Hanoi–Dong Dang lines. At several points during the war, American bombing severely hindered transport along both lines, on which the North Vietnamese depended for shipments from their Chinese allies. [Operation Rolling Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rolling_Thunder "Operation Rolling Thunder") was the first large-scale bombing campaign carried out by the [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force "U.S. Air Force"), taking place from March 2, 1965, until November 1, 1968, when US President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson") temporarily called off air raids. Large-scale air raids resumed from May 9 to October 23, 1972, for [Operation Linebacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Linebacker "Operation Linebacker"), and again from December 18–29, 1972, for [Operation Linebacker II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Linebacker_II "Operation Linebacker II"), with fewer target restrictions than _Rolling Thunder_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rolthun-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-rolthun)

Railway engineering troops from the [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_China "People's Republic of China"), deployed in late June 1965, were tasked with repairing the damage caused by the bombing. By late December of that year, reconstruction was complete on 363 kilometers of both the Hanoi–Lào Cai and Hanoi–Dong Dang lines, significantly increasing shipping capacity. A third rail was added to the existing lines, using [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge "Standard gauge") spacing, effectively converting them to [mixed gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_gauge "Mixed gauge") lines. This allowed Chinese trains to connect directly with the Vietnamese railway network, without the need for a break-of-gauge. Many new stations, bridges and tunnels were also built, and an entirely new rail line, the Thái Nguyên–Kép line, was built as a strategic connection between the Hanoi–Dong Dang and Hanoi–Thái Nguyên lines.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tnkepstns-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-tnkepstns)

[![Wide, black and white view of Thanh Hóa Bridge badly damaged. The left support column has collapsed.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1e/ThanhHoaBridge1.jpg/250px-ThanhHoaBridge1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ThanhHoaBridge1.jpg) The [Thanh Hóa Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanh_H%C3%B3a_Bridge "Thanh Hóa Bridge"), damaged by American bombs in 1972.

A particularly difficult target for [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force "U.S. Air Force") bombers was the [Thanh Hóa Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanh_H%C3%B3a_Bridge "Thanh Hóa Bridge"), a well-defended combined road/rail bridge along the main line in [Thanh Hóa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanh_H%C3%B3a_Province "Thanh Hóa Province"). The bridge was attacked several times from 1965 to 1972. Several times, traffic over the bridge was interrupted, but every time, the North Vietnamese were able to repair the damage. The bridge was eventually destroyed by laser-guided [smart bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_bomb "Smart bomb") during separate raids on April 27 and May 13, 1972, as part of Operation Linebacker.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rolthun-1)[\[35\]](#refnote-rolthun)

After the [Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon "Fall of Saigon") on 30 April 1975, the Communist government of the newly unified Vietnam took control of the former South Vietnamese railway. The heavily damaged, war-torn North–South line was restored and returned to service on 31 December 1976, promoted as a symbol of Vietnamese unity. In the short time between the surrender of the South and the reopening of the line, 1334 bridges, 27 tunnels, 158 stations and 1370 switches had been repaired.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lpviet-6)[\[25\]](#refnote-lpviet) Other railway lines that once existed, such as the [Da Lat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Lat "Da Lat")– [Thap Cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phan_Rang%E2%80%93Th%C3%A1p_Ch%C3%A0m "Phan Rang–Tháp Chàm") line, were dismantled during this period to provide materials for the repair of the main line.[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-8)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)[&#8202;](#ref-back-dalathist-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-dalathist) The outbreak of the [Sino–Vietnamese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War "Sino–Vietnamese War") in 1979 resulted in the closing of borders, including railways; particularly, the railway bridge that connected Lào Cai and Hekou on the Yunnan–Vietnam line was destroyed by sabotage during the conflict. Rail traffic between the two countries would eventually resume in 1992.

## Network

In the background are the mountains.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Train_passing_Lang_Co.jpg/250px-Train_passing_Lang_Co.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Train_passing_Lang_Co.jpg) A [diesel locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_locomotive "Diesel locomotive") at [Lang Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lang_Co "Lang Co") Beach.

The Vietnamese railway network has a total length of 2,600 kilometres (1,600mi), dominated by the north–south line running between Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City; as of 2007, 85% of the network's passenger volume and 60% of its cargo volume was transported along this line. There were 278 stations on the Vietnamese railway network as of 2005, 191 of which were located along the north–south line.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-infra-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-vr-infra)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-nsga-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-vr-nsga)

### Operational realities

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Operational realities")\]

The north–south line goes through some congested urban areas, with very little clearance (less than 1 meter on each side), instead they rely on blazing horns. Though the line is single track, speed does pick up outside urban areas, and the rails themselves are of quality to easily outpace any highway traffic, nevertheless this does not allow for schedule flexibility and frequency nor much safety as [grade separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_separation#Railways "Grade separation") is almost nonexistent and level crossings the norm.

### Proposals

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Proposals")\]

Several railway lines have been proposed for construction in Vietnam in recent years. The largest such project is the [high-speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail "High-speed rail") [North–South Express Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Express_Railway_(Vietnam) "North–South Express Railway (Vietnam)") connecting [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi") and [Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City"), (see [High-speed rail](#High-speed_rail), below) valued at approximately US$56billion. Due to its cost, plans for the line are currently on hold pending further study of the project.[&#8202;](#ref-back-na-rejection-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-na-rejection) Other projects involve the restoration of previously existing lines, such as the Da Lat–Tháp Chàm and Ho Chi Minh City–Loc Ninh lines, both of which were originally built in the 1930s, but fell into disuse after decades of war. The proposed [Ho Chi Minh City–Moc Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City%E2%80%93Phnom_Penh_railway "Ho Chi Minh City–Phnom Penh railway") and [Mụ Giạ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%E1%BB%A5_Gi%E1%BA%A1?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mụ Giạ (page does not exist)")– [Vung Ang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vung_Ang?action=edit&redlink=1 "Vung Ang (page does not exist)") lines (both of which had either existed or been considered before World War II) would establish Vietnam's first international railway links to [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia") and [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), respectively.[&#8202;](#ref-back-aseansec-skrl-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-aseansec-skrl)

China

See also: [Yunnan–Vietnam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Vietnam_Railway "Yunnan–Vietnam Railway")

The railway link from [Haiphong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiphong "Haiphong") to the Chinese city of [Kunming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming "Kunming") was originally built by the French administration of [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina "French Indochina") in the early 20th century. The section within Vietnam is 389km long (237km between Hanoi and the border at [Lào Cai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A0o_Cai_province "Lào Cai province")). The railway used 1,000mm (3ft3+3⁄8in) gauge due to the mountainous terrain along the route. Currently, the line is the only main line in China using [metre gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge "Metre gauge"), (or [dual gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_gauge "Dual gauge"), since it can also be [converted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_conversion "Gauge conversion") to [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge "Standard gauge")). Railway service along the Chinese portion of the route is currently suspended. Cross-border service was available until 2002, when floods and landslides, which frequently caused delays along the route,[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-63) caused serious damage to the tracks on the Chinese side.[&#8202;](#ref-back-seat61-vn-2)[\[44\]](#refnote-seat61-vn) The railway will form the Chinese part of the [Kunming–Singapore Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming%E2%80%93Singapore_Railway "Kunming–Singapore Railway"), which has been proposed many times, and which at some point was expected to be completed in 2015.

Railway access to [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning "Nanning") is done through the border at [Đồng Đăng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BB%93ng_%C4%90%C4%83ng "Đồng Đăng"), in [Lạng Sơn Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%E1%BA%A1ng_S%C6%A1n_Province "Lạng Sơn Province"). Regular service generally entails stopping at the border, changing from a Vietnamese metre-gauge train to a Chinese standard-gauge train, and continuing on to Nanning. Since 2009, a daily overnight service is available; the train departs from Hanoi's [Gia Lâm Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_L%C3%A2m_Railway_Station "Gia Lâm Railway Station"), and runs on standard-gauge tracks all the way to Nanning.[&#8202;](#ref-back-seat61-vn-3)[\[44\]](#refnote-seat61-vn)

Cambodia

There is currently no railway connection between Vietnam and Cambodia. The French administration of [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina "French Indochina") originally built [a railway from Saigon to Lộc Ninh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saigon%E2%80%93L%E1%BB%99c_Ninh_Railway "Saigon–Lộc Ninh Railway") in the 1930s, with the intent of extending it further into Cambodia. It was abandoned in 1945, at the start of the [First Indochina War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War "First Indochina War"). A [new line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City%E2%80%93Phnom_Penh_railway "Ho Chi Minh City–Phnom Penh railway") connecting [Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City") to [Phnom Penh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phnom_Penh "Phnom Penh"), Cambodia, was proposed as part of the [Kunming–Singapore Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming%E2%80%93Singapore_Railway "Kunming–Singapore Railway") project, overseen by the ASEAN–Mekong Basin Development Cooperation (AMBDC). The 129km (80mi) Vietnamese portion of the line would begin with a junction of the [North–South Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Railway_(Vietnam) "North–South Railway (Vietnam)") at [Dĩ An Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%A9_An_Railway_Station "Dĩ An Railway Station"), and would end in [Lộc Ninh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%E1%BB%99c_Ninh,_B%C3%ACnh_Ph%C6%B0%E1%BB%9Bc "Lộc Ninh, Bình Phước"), [Bình Phước Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ACnh_Ph%C6%B0%E1%BB%9Bc_Province "Bình Phước Province"), close to the Cambodian border. According to the plan established by ASEAN, this stretch is scheduled for completion by 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-aseansec-skrl-3)[\[4\]](#refnote-aseansec-skrl)[&#8202;](#ref-back-pppost-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-pppost)

Laos

There is currently no railway connection between Vietnam and Laos. In 2007, the Laotian Ministry of Transportation entered into discussion with Vietnam to discuss the possibility of opening a new railway line from [Thakhek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thakhek "Thakhek") in [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos") through the [Mụ Giạ Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%E1%BB%A5_Gi%E1%BA%A1_Pass "Mụ Giạ Pass") to [Tân Ấp Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A2n_%E1%BA%A4p_Railway_Station "Tân Ấp Railway Station") in [Quảng Bình Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%E1%BA%A3ng_B%C3%ACnh_Province "Quảng Bình Province"), connecting to the national railway at [Vung Ang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vung_Ang?action=edit&redlink=1 "Vung Ang (page does not exist)"), a port in [Hà Tĩnh Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A0_T%C4%A9nh_Province "Hà Tĩnh Province") which the Vietnamese Government plans to expand. This would revive, in part, an earlier but aborted scheme, the [Thakhek - Tân Ấp railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thakhek_-_T%C3%A2n_%E1%BA%A4p_railway "Thakhek - Tân Ấp railway"). According to plans established by ASEAN, the line may be extended via Thakhek all the way to the Laotian capital [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane "Vientiane"). Both Laos and Thailand have expressed interest in the project as a shorter export gateway to the Pacific Ocean.[&#8202;](#ref-back-aseansec-skrl-4)[\[4\]](#refnote-aseansec-skrl)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jdi-mar10-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-jdi-mar10)

## High-speed rail

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: High-speed rail")\]

Main article: [North–South Express Railway (Vietnam)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Express_Railway_(Vietnam) "North–South Express Railway (Vietnam)")

[![An 700T type high speed train of Taiwan High Speed Rail, which is a derative of Japan Shinkansen.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Taiwan-HighSpeedRail-700T-testrun-2006-0624.jpg/250px-Taiwan-HighSpeedRail-700T-testrun-2006-0624.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Taiwan-HighSpeedRail-700T-testrun-2006-0624.jpg) Japanese [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen "Shinkansen") technology has been suggested for use in the express railway project. (Photo: [Taiwan High Speed Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_High_Speed_Rail "Taiwan High Speed Rail"))

National railway company [Vietnam Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Railways "Vietnam Railways") has proposed a [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail "High-speed rail") link between [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi") and [Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City "Ho Chi Minh City"), capable of running at speeds of 300km/h (186mph). Funding for the line would come mainly from the Vietnamese government, with Japanese aid in the form of [official development assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_development_assistance "Official development assistance") (ODA). Current technology allows trains travelling on the current, single-track [Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Railway_(Vietnam) "North–South Railway (Vietnam)") to complete the journey in approximately thirty hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vrweb-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-vrweb) Once completed, the high-speed rail line—using Japanese [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen "Shinkansen") technology—would allow trains to complete the Hanoi–Ho Chi Minh City journey in less than six hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-afp-jun2010-highspeed-3)[\[5\]](#refnote-afp-jun2010-highspeed)[&#8202;](#ref-back-na-rejection-1)[\[39\]](#refnote-na-rejection)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt-2007-highspeed-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-nyt-2007-highspeed)

Vietnamese prime minister [Nguyen Tan Dung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguyen_Tan_Dung "Nguyen Tan Dung") had originally set an ambitious target, approving a 1,630km (1,010mi) line to be completed by 2013, with 70 percent of funding (initially estimated at US$33 billion) coming from Japanese ODA, and the remaining 30 percent raised through loans.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nyt-2007-highspeed-1)[\[65\]](#refnote-nyt-2007-highspeed) Later reports raised estimated costs to US$56 billion (almost 60 percent of Vietnam's [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product "Gross domestic product") in 2009) for a completion date in the mid-2030s. On June 19, 2010, after a month of deliberation, Vietnam's National Assembly rejected the high speed rail proposal due to its high cost; National Assembly deputies had asked for further study of the project.[&#8202;](#ref-back-afp-jun2010-highspeed-4)[\[5\]](#refnote-afp-jun2010-highspeed)[&#8202;](#ref-back-na-rejection-2)[\[39\]](#refnote-na-rejection)

However, the project [North-South Express Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_express_railway_(Vietnam) "North–South express railway (Vietnam)") now remains in works, with expectations to begin constructions by 2028-2029.[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-67)[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-68) Furthermore, the proposed [Ho Chi Minh City–Cần Thơ express railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City%E2%80%93C%E1%BA%A7n_Th%C6%A1_express_railway "Ho Chi Minh City–Cần Thơ express railway") is also a project underway.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-69)

### Work begins on High Speed connection to China

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Work begins on High Speed connection to China")\]

Workers began to lay tracks on China's first direct high-speed railway link to Vietnam on 8th August 2023. A track-laying machine put down a 500-meter-long track, domestic media outlet CGTN reported.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-70)

The railway between Fangchenggang and Dongxing, in South China's Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, spans a distance of 46.9 kilometers (km), and has a designed speed of 200 km per hour. Infrastructure is being built with conditions to upgrade the travel speed to 250 km per hour in the future.

### Hanoi Metro

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Hanoi Metro")\]

Main article: [Hanoi Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi_Metro "Hanoi Metro")

In July 2008, the then- [Vietnamese Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Vietnam "Prime Minister of Vietnam") [Nguyễn Tấn Dũng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_T%E1%BA%A5n_D%C5%A9ng "Nguyễn Tấn Dũng") approved an overall transport development plan for Hanoi, which, among other projects, proposed a [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit "Rapid transit") system with five routes (1435mm gauge).[&#8202;](#ref-back-vnnet-feb1809-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-vnnet-feb1809) The project is being carried out by the Hanoi Metropolitan Rail Transport Project Board (HRB).[&#8202;](#ref-back-railwaytech-hanoi-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-railwaytech-hanoi) As of spring 2009, projects for four lines were under consideration: the Nho–Hanoi Railway Station (the "pilot" line), the Nam Thang Long–Tran Hung Dao line, the Cat Linh–Hà Đông line, and the Yen Vien–Ngoc Hoi line. ## Infrastructure

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Infrastructure")\]

Most of Vietnam's railway infrastructure, including bridges, rail trucks, track beds, rolling stocks, signals and communication equipment, and maintenance facilities, has suffered severe deterioration, mainly due to damage inflicted during the Vietnam War and a subsequent lack of capital investment and maintenance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-80)
. More recently\[ _[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\], rehabilitation projects sustained by [official development assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_development_assistance "Official development assistance") have allowed the most critical pieces of infrastructure along the line to be replaced, although much work still remains to be done\[ _[further explanation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\].[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-4)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)

### Tracks

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Tracks")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/My_Son_railway_tracks.jpg/250px-My_Son_railway_tracks.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:My_Son_railway_tracks.jpg) A level crossing near [My Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Son "My Son").

Most existing Vietnamese railway lines use [metre gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge "Metre gauge"), although [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge "Standard gauge") and [mixed gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_gauge "Mixed gauge") are used northeast of Hanoi.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-2)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network) As of 2005, approximately 2,600km (1,600mi) of track was in use throughout Vietnam:[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-infra-2)[\[2\]](#refnote-vr-infra)

- 2,169km (1,348mi) metre gauge (1,000mm (3ft3+3⁄8in));
- 178km (111mi) standard gauge (1,435mm (4ft8+1⁄2in)); and
- 253km (157mi) mixed gauge (1,435mm (4ft8+1⁄2in) and 1,000mm (3ft3+3⁄8in)).

Besides active tracks, around 506km (314mi) of tracks are in use as siding, bringing the total to 3,106km (1,930mi) of track laid nationwide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-3)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network)

### Bridges

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Bridges")\]

Vietnam Railways reports the number of railway bridges along the North–South line to be 1,300, totalling about 28,000m (92,000ft), or about 63% of the national total. Considering both standard rail bridges and combined bridges, the total length along the North–South line is about 36,000m (118,000ft).[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-5)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-4)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network) Many of the railway bridges were severely worn from age and sport damage dating from the Vietnam War, despite their restoration following the war. As of 2007, 278 bridges requiring major rehabilitation remain along the North–South Railway line.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-6)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab) Throughout the entire Vietnamese rail network, Vietnam Railways reports a total of 1,790 railway bridges, with a total length of 45,368m (148,845ft).[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-infra-3)[\[2\]](#refnote-vr-infra)

### Tunnels

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Tunnels")\]

[Play video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Vietnam_Rail_Tunnel.ogv "Play video") Duration: 48 seconds.0:48A Vietnam Railways train passes through a tunnel north of [Quy Nhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quy_Nhon "Quy Nhon").

There are 27 railway tunnels along the North–South line, amounting to a total length of 8,335m (27,346ft). Throughout the entire Vietnamese rail network, Vietnam Railways report a total of 39 tunnels with a combined length of 11,512m (37,769ft).[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-5)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network) Certain tunnels are inadequately drained and suffer from deterioration in the tunnel lining, causing water leaks that necessitate reductions in speed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-10)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)

### Signalling

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Signalling")\]

The North–South Railway line uses a semi-automatic block system, which allows individual signals to work either as [automatic signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_Block_Signal "Automatic Block Signal") or manual signals. These replace the [token](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Token_(railway_signalling) "Token (railway signalling)") method used in earlier years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-infra-4)[\[2\]](#refnote-vr-infra)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-6)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network) According to a joint Japanese-Vietnamese evaluation team, the recent installation of additional auto-signal systems at key crossings along the line has contributed to a decline in railway accidents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-7)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab) [Semaphore signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_semaphore_signal "Railway semaphore signal") were once used throughout the Vietnamese rail network, but these are gradually being replaced with [colour light signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_signal#Colour_light_signals "Railway signal"); most of the railway lines in northern Vietnam have been converted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-infra-5)[\[2\]](#refnote-vr-infra)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-7)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network)

According to Vietnam Railways, automatic warning systems have been installed at 230 level crossings throughout the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-8)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network)

### Communications

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Communications")\]

Since 1998, microband [Asynchronous Transfer Mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asynchronous_Transfer_Mode "Asynchronous Transfer Mode") technology has been used along the North–South Railway line to send television signals; 64kbit/s transmission lines are leased from the Vietnam Post and Telecommunications Corporation (VPTC). Along some sections of the line—for example, from Hanoi to Vinh and from Nha Trang to Ho Chi Minh City—a [fiber-optic network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber-optic_network "Fiber-optic network") has been deployed; Vietnam Railways intends to extend the network along the remaining distance from Vinh to Nha Trang. A switching system featuring digital exchanges is in place, connected via the existing transmission system and the public telephone network. As the modernization of the telecommunication system progresses, manual exchanges are gradually being replaced with digital exchanges.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-infra-6)[\[2\]](#refnote-vr-infra)[&#8202;](#ref-back-vr-network-9)[\[40\]](#refnote-vr-network)

## Safety

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Safety")\]

Main article: [Railway accidents in Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_accidents_in_Vietnam "Railway accidents in Vietnam")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Hanoi_railroad_tracks.jpg/250px-Hanoi_railroad_tracks.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hanoi_railroad_tracks.jpg) Residences directly adjacent to railway tracks in [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi").

The overall condition of railway infrastructure in Vietnam varies from poor to fair; most of the network remains in need of rehabilitation and upgrading, having received only temporary repair from damages suffered during decades of war. A joint Japanese-Vietnamese evaluation team found that the poor state of railway infrastructure was the fundamental cause for most railway accidents, of which the most common types are train crashes against vehicles and persons, especially at illegal [level crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_crossings "Level crossings"); [derailments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derailment "Derailment") caused by failure to decrease speed were also noted as a common cause of accidents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-8)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)

The Vietnamese railway network is crossed by many roads in several crowded areas; as a result, accidents involving vehicles and pedestrians have occurred. In the first 10 months of 2009, 431 railway accidents reportedly took place throughout Vietnam, causing 166 casualties and injuring 319 people.[&#8202;](#ref-back-vnx-railxings-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-vnx-railxings) A researcher from [Villanova University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanova_University "Villanova University") noted "There are numerous safety issues with level crossings...usually, an accident occurs every day."[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-visit-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-jica-visit) Many rail bridges and tunnels have suffered deterioration since the 1970s, requiring trains passing over or through them to reduce speeds as low as 15km/h (9.3mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-11)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai) In addition, the center of the country is subject to violent annual flooding and bridges are often swept away, causing lengthy closures.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-9)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)

Along with recent efforts aimed at infrastructure rehabilitation, the recent adoption of safety measures by Vietnam Railways has led to a decline in railway accidents. These measures include: public awareness campaigns on railway safety in the media; construction of fences and safety barriers at critical level crossings in major cities; mobilization of volunteers for traffic control at train stations and level crossings, especially during holiday seasons; the installation of additional auto-signal systems; and the construction of [flyovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overpass "Overpass") and [underpasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel "Tunnel") to redirect traffic.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jica-bridgerehab-10)[\[3\]](#refnote-jica-bridgerehab)

## Railway management

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Vietnam&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Railway management")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/VNR_Branch_Danang.JPG/250px-VNR_Branch_Danang.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:VNR_Branch_Danang.JPG) The Da Nang branch office of Vietnam Railways.

The Vietnamese railway network is owned and operated by the state-owned enterprise [Vietnam Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Railways "Vietnam Railways") (VNR), which operates a number of different subsidiaries involved in construction, communications, training, and other activities connected to railway maintenance. Following the end of the Vietnam War in 1975, the newly reunified Vietnamese railway network was centrally managed by the precursor of VNR, the government's Department of Railways. Initially, low rail tariffs and the poor state of infrastructure used in other modes of transport led to high usage, but revenue proved to be insufficient to cover the railway's operating costs. In 1986, the Government's implementation of [Doi Moi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_Moi "Doi Moi") economic reforms led to the deregulation of the transport sector and the shift towards a market-led economy, forcing the railways to change in order to maintain a competitive edge. The Department of Railways was reorganized into Vietnam Railways ( [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language "Vietnamese language"): _Đường sắt Việt Nam_) in 1989; since that time, railway reform has passed through a number of stages. Responsibility for rail infrastructure and operations were separated by government decree in 1994. The government of Germany began providing assistance with the restructuring of the railway sector in 2000, allowing VNR to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of its operations, thus increasing its competitiveness. In 2003, VNR was re-organized as a state corporation, the Vietnam Railway Corporation, operating in railway transport and related services;[&#8202;](#ref-back-2003decree-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-2003decree) railway administration and infrastructure management were given to the Vietnam Railway Administration, under the authority of the Ministry of Transport. A Railway Law was passed by the National Assembly on 19 May 2005; although regulations for the law's implementation have yet to be issued, it does provide a strong basis for further sector development; among other things, it proposed that [foreign investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment "Foreign direct investment") be invited to invest in Vietnam Railways.[&#8202;](#ref-back-adb-laocai-12)[\[1\]](#refnote-adb-laocai)
