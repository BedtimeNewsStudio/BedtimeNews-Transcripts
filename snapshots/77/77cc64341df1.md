This is the sprawling campus of Selalu Cinta, an Indonesian factory that makes Nike sneakers and slippers and employs 18,000 people.

Ten years ago, the land was undeveloped, a tangle of palms and underbrush.

The factory is in Central Java, far from high-wage areas near Jakarta.

Over the past decade, employment at Nike’s factories grew dramatically in lower-cost areas where minimum wages are not considered enough to make ends meet.

And employment overall shrank where the minimum wage is considered enough to meet basic needs.

# Nike Is Moving Jobs to Low-Wage Regions of Indonesia

## After moving manufacturing to the developing world to save on labor, Nike and other apparel brands are shifting employment in their Indonesian supply chain away from high-wage parts of the country and into less-developed areas.

by [Rob Davis](https://www.propublica.org/people/rob-davis) and [Nick McMillan](https://www.propublica.org/people/nick-mcmillan), [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org), and [Matthew Kish](https://www.oregonlive.com/staff/matthewkish/posts.html), [The Oregonian/OregonLive](https://www.oregonlive.com/)

### Reporting Highlights

* **Living Wage:** Nike says people who make its products should earn enough to live on with some discretionary money left over — or their employers should have a plan to get them there.
* **Shifting Employment:** The supply chain in Indonesia grew in places where the minimum wage falls short of one leading estimate for the living wage, while it shrank in areas that pay better.
* **Company Response:** Nike says that less-developed regions where it operates shouldn’t be excluded from economic growth, adding in a statement, “Growth and progress go hand in hand.’”
