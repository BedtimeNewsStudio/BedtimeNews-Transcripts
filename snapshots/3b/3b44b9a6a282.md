## Abstract

Consumption of antibiotics is continuing to increase, with China accounting for approximately one quarter of the global intake. As a class of emerging environmental contaminants, antibiotics may pose a potential threat to human health, especially in children. However, the internal antibiotic exposure levels in pregnant women and their determinants remain unclear. Here we investigated the urinary creatinine-corrected concentration of 15 antibiotics in 762 pregnant women from the Shanghai Prenatal Cohort, by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. Logistic regression analysis identified associations between high-antibiotic-level and maternal dietary factors. Results show that cumulatively antibiotics were detected at a frequency of 0.13 – 82.7%, with veterinary antibiotics (VAs) and preferred veterinary antibiotics (PVAs) detected in 76.9% and 98.2% of samples, respectively; PVAs were the most significant contributors to hazard index values > 1. Further, ciprofloxacin was the predominant antibiotic (median: 73.5 μg/mg creatinine), followed by norfloxacin (54.2 μg/mg creatinine); while sulfamethoxazole, enrofloxacin, and ciprofloxacin levels, used as a PVA or VA, were significantly higher in normal-weight and underweight women compared to overweight and obese women. Also, sulfamethoxazole, sulfadiazine, and ciprofloxacin were more frequently detected in mothers with a relatively low education degree. Interestingly, pregnant women with higher milk intake had a 1.96-times (95% CI: 1.10–3.49) greater risk of high-VA-exposure-level than the lower-intake group. The odds of exposure to high PVA, VA, PVA + VA, and all antibiotics levels for mothers with high egg consumption frequency were more than twice that of low-consumption individuals. Collectively, pregnant women in Shanghai are exposed to multiple environmental antibiotics, primarily as PVAs and VAs. Herein, we provide evidence for the association between dietary factors and maternal environmental antibiotic exposure in China. Special attention to antibiotic exposure and confirmation of potential determinants should be taken in the future.

## 1\. Introduction

Global consumption of antibiotics in the clinic, as well as in the animal husbandry and aquaculture industries is gradually increasing. Up to 105,596 tons of antibiotics are expected to be used in food animal production by 2030 ( [Klein et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0070), [Van Boeckel et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0155)). China accounts for a greater share of this increase than developed countries, with an estimated consumption of up to 30% of the globally produced antibiotics by 2030 ( [Bu et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0020), [Liu et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0115), [Van Boeckel et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0155), [Zhang et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0210)). Consequently, 65 antibiotics \[preferred veterinary antibiotics (PVAs), accounting for approximately 60% of all antibiotics\] have been detected in the major Chinese water sources, including the Yangtze River and Taihu Lake, with a maximum observed concentration of up to 1484 ng/L ( [Bu et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0020), [Liu et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0115), [Wu et al., 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0195)). Contaminated drinking water may expose humans to veterinary antibiotics (VAs) and PVAs. Further, animal-derived food products may also serve as a potential source of human antibiotic exposure ( [Bacanli and Basaran, 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0010), [Wang et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0160)), as a substantial level of antibiotic residues have been detected in at least 10% of sampled meat, aquatic products, or milk, including some human antibiotics (HAs) that are not used as veterinary drugs ( [Wang et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0160)). Hence, antibiotics may re-enter the human body via inadvertent intake of antibiotic-contaminated food or drinking water, resulting in continuous exposure to low doses ( [Zhang et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0210)). Also, since Shanghai is located at the intersection of the Yangtze River and the Huangpu River, and has a high population density, its residents may be at a greater risk of exposure to high antibiotic contamination in the [aquatic environment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/aquatic-environment) and common foods ( [Jiang et al., 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0065), [Wang et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0160), [Wu et al., 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0195)). However, the antibiotic [exposure profile](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/exposure-profile) of pregnant women in Shanghai remains unknown.

The antibiotics have been detected in [meconium](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/meconium), implying that antibiotics may cross the [placental barrier](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/blood-placenta-barrier) to the fetus ( [Zhao et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0215)). During critical periods of [embryonic and fetal development](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/prenatal-development), the fetus is notably susceptible to environmental hazards ( [Grandjean et al., 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0045)). Developmental exposure to environmental chemicals can cause important functional deficits and disease across the infant’s lifespan. Further, alterations in the [microbiome](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/microbiome) composition induced by antibiotics can impact a myriad of physiological processes, promoting long-term [disease development](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/disease-course) ( [Francino 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0035)). Recent studies have suggested that exposure to antibiotics during pregnancy may adversely affect the offspring’s health, e.g., resulting in miscarriage, childhood allergic disease and obesity ( [Andersen et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0005), [Lapin et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0090), [Metzler et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0130), [Muanda et al., 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0140), [Mueller et al., 2005](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0145), [Zhang et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0205)). Therefore, the potential effects of continuous exposure to low-dose environmental antibiotics during this period on human’s health have begun to garner attention in the research community. To the best of our knowledge, however, only limited studies have comprehensively investigated the internal antibiotic exposure levels in pregnant women based on biomonitoring ( [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175)). Antibiotic concentrations can be influenced by many socio-demographic and dietary factors, however, which factors are most relevant to antibiotic concentrations during pregnancy, remain unclear. If [prenatal exposure](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/prenatal-exposure) to antibiotics does exert an adverse impact on the infant’s health, reducing the fetal exposure by investigating the source of antibiotics in pregnant women may serve to redress this impact. In the present study, therefore, we sought to investigate antibiotic exposure profiles in pregnant women in Shanghai as well as their primary sources (VAs, HAs, or PVAs); to evaluate the health risk caused by exposure to environmental antibiotics; and to explore the potential determinants of maternal exposure to [urinary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/urinary-system) antibiotics using biomonitoring data.

## 2\. Materials and methods

### 2.1. Study population

The current study included women from the Shanghai Prenatal Cohort, which is an ongoing prospective birth cohort including pregnant women living in all 16 districts in Shanghai (seven urban: Hongkou, Huangpu, Jing’an, Putuo, Xuhui, Yangpu, and Changning; and nine suburban: Baoshan, Chongming, Fengxian, Jiadin, Jinshan, Minhang, Pudong, Qingpu, and Songjiang). For the study, 1,143 pregnant women were recruited during the period of June 2012 to February 2013 from two tertiary-level hospitals in northern and central Shanghai (Xinhua Hospital and the International Peace [Maternity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/obstetrics) and Child Health Hospital). The inclusion criteria were as follows: routine prenatal examination in the study hospital; singleton pregnancy; planning to live in Shanghai for a minimum of two years; and signing an [informed consent](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/informed-consent) form. These pregnant women were recruited in the third-trimester (29–41 gestational weeks) of pregnancy. A total of 762 women with complete data on mother-infant pair information and the analyzed antibiotics were ultimately included in the current study. None of the participants were using the antibiotics selected in the current study during pregnancy, as determined based on the hospital [medical records](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/medical-record).

### 2.2. Questionnaires and medical records

At enrollment, trained interviewers conducted face-to-face questionnaire surveys and gathered information on maternal age at delivery, parental education levels, occupation, family residence zone, household income, living environment information, smoking status including active and passive smoking, as well as dietary and lifestyle-related factors during pregnancy. Medical information, including maternal pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI), gestational age, parity, [pregnancy complications](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/pregnancy-complication) (gestational diabetes mellitus, [gestational hypertension](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/gestational-hypertension), and preeclampsia), medication during pregnancy, type of delivery, infant sex, [Apgar score](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/apgar-score), and birth size (weight and length) were extracted from the hospital medical records with participants’ consent. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committees from all involved research institutions and hospitals (XHEC-C-2012-003).

### 2.3. Sample collection and analysis

Maternal spot urine specimens were collected 1–3 days before delivery. The samples were maintained at 4 °C in a portable incubator packed with ice, transferred to a biobank at Xinhua Hospital, and then stored at −80 °C. The presence of 15 antibiotics from five categories commonly detected in food or [aquatic environments](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/aquatic-environment) ( [Bu et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0020), [Liu et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0115), [Wang et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0160)) were investigated including, four sulfonamides, four [fluoroquinolones](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/quinolone-derivative), three [phenicols](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/chloramphenicol), three [macrolides](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/macrolide), and one [tetracycline](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/tetracycline) ( [Table 2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#t0010)); of which two were VAs, three were HAs, and ten were PVAs. Antibiotics and isotopically-labeled internal standard solutions (sulfamethoxazole- _d_ 4, sulfadiazine-13 _C_ 6, ciprofloxacin- _d_ 8, enrofloxacin- _d_ 5, trimethoprim- _d_ 3, chloramphenicol- _d_ 5, azithromycin- _d_ 3, and tetracycline- _d_ 6) were purchased from Dr. Ehrenstorfer (Augsburg, Germany) and Toronto Research Chemicals (Toronto, ON, Canada), respectively. The purity of all standards was determined to be ≥ 94%.

Total [urinary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/urinary-system) concentrations (free and conjugated) of the 15 antibiotics were determined using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). The urine samples were pretreated using a modified method, as described previously ( [Wang et al., 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0185)). Briefly, an aliquot (200 μL) of urine was spiked with isotope-labeled internal standards and hydrolyzed by β-glucuronidase. Next, the antibiotic mixture was purified using [acetonitrile](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/acetonitrile) and centrifuged at 15000 rpm for 10 min to obtain a supernatant, which was then concentrated in a high-speed vacuum centrifuge to obtain approximately 300 μL of concentrate (Thermo Speedvac SPD 121P Concentrator, Thermos Co., Waltham, MA, USA). Finally, the pretreated samples were analyzed by LC-MS/MS (Agilent 1290-6490, Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). [Quality control](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/quality-control) (QC) samples were interspersed between samples in each batch and were indistinguishable from other samples upon analysis.

Antibiotic levels were determined using an isotope internal calibration curve consisting of six analyte concentrations (0.01–100 ng/mL) prepared for each batch of urine samples. Calibration curves exhibited good linearity, with a correlation coefficient R2 ≥ 0.99 for all compounds. The accuracy (% mean recovery) and precision were determined by replicating sample analysis with low (0.4 ng/mL) and high (80 ng/mL) sample concentrations. The recoveries of 15 antibiotics in spiked urine samples ranged from 85% to 115%. The intra-batch coefficients of variation (CV) and inter-assay CV values were below 15%. The limit of detection (LOD), defined as a signal-to-noise ratio of 3, ranged from 0.1 to 1.0 ng/mL for the selected antibiotics.

The creatinine-corrected [urinary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/urinary-system) antibiotic concentrations (μg/mg creatinine) were used to assess internal exposure levels, and were calculated as volume-based urinary concentration of antibiotics (μg/L) divided by [urinary concentrations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/kidney-concentrating-capacity) of creatinine (mg/L). The urinary creatinine levels were determined using an [enzymatic method](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/enzyme-chemistry) (Roche Cobas 8000 chemistry analyzer, Roche, Basel, Switzerland).

### 2.4. Hazard assessment

The potential health risk associated with individual antibiotics was assessed based on the hazard quotient (HQ), calculated as the ratio of daily exposure dose to the acceptable daily intake (ADI, μg/kg/d). The antibiotic daily exposure dose (DED, μg/kg/d) was determined based on maternal pre-delivery weight, antibiotic concentration, and urine creatinine using a previously published formula ( [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175)). ADI data and urinary antibiotic excretion proportions used in the current study were obtained from previous reports or authority statements ( [Hardman et al., 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0055), [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175), [WHO, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0190)). Since humans may be concurrently exposed to multiple antibiotics, the hazard index (HI) was used to reflect the cumulative health risk of combined antibiotic exposure. HI was defined as a sum of HQ for each antibiotic detected in an individual. Three sulfonamides (sulfamethazine, [sulfamethoxazole](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/sulfamethoxazole) and sulfadiazine) were excluded from the calculation of HI for all antibiotics, PVAs, and sulfonamides due to lack of ADI values based on microbiological effects. HQ or HI indicate a potential health risk if the value is above 1.

### 2.5. Dietary characteristics

During the mother’s hospital delivery, information on dietary patterns throughout the pregnancy was obtained using a standardized [food frequency questionnaire](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/food-frequency-questionnaire) published by the Chinese [Nutrition](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/food-science/nutrition) Society for 32 common food groups (e.g., aquatic products, [livestock](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/farm-animal) and poultry meat, fresh fruit/vegetables, [root vegetables](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/root-vegetable), [cereal crops](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/cereal-crop), soy products/beans, wheat flour products, nuts, etc). With regard to antibiotic exposure, 14 specific food types were selected for analysis based on previous studies examining the dietary predictors of antibiotics in seafood (marine fish, [shrimp](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/shrimp), crab, and shellfish), livestock and poultry meat (beef, mutton, pork, poultry meat, and animal organ), and egg and milk ( [Ji et al., 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0060), [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165)). The frequency of seafood, egg, and milk consumption was classified as ≤ 1–2 times/month or ≥ 1–2 times/week. A portion of the participants also completed the questionnaire on livestock and poultry meat intake quantity, e.g., consumed per day, per week, or per month, as appropriate. The source of drinking water was also included, and was classified into tap water or barreled water.

### 2.6. Statistical analysis

Detection of a corresponding antibiotic category in urine sample was defined as detection of one or more antibiotics in that category. For analysis, maternal antibiotic concentrations below LOD were replaced with those of LOD/2. [Descriptive analyses](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/food-science/descriptive-analysis) were conducted to determine the detection frequencies or selected percentiles of creatinine-corrected or -uncorrected concentrations, DED, HQ, or HI for each antibiotic or category. Non-parametric Mann-Whitney _U_ test or Kruskal-Wallis test was performed to compare the median creatinine-corrected concentrations of an antibiotic across demographic characteristics and food items of interest. [Logistic regression analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/logistic-regression-analysis) examined the associations between demographic factors, drinking water source, common food consumption, and high exposure to VAs, PVAs, VAs + PVAs, or all antibiotics in the urine adjusted for maternal education, maternal age, maternal pre-pregnancy BMI, gestational weeks, and annual family income. Based on PVAs, VAs, PVAs + VAs, high-exposure-level was defined as one or more antibiotics above the 75% percentile. All analyses were performed using Empower(R) (www.empowerstats.com, X&Y Solutions, Inc., Boston, MA, USA) and R software ( [http://www.T-project.org](http://www.t-project.org/)). A _P_-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## 3\. Results

Many of the pregnant women in the current study had a Bachelor degree (90%), and a relatively high annual family income (93%). Women were on average 30-years old. Nearly half of them lived in an urban area. ### 3.2. Cumulative health risk assessment of antibiotics in pregnant women

The daily exposure to antibiotics of most pregnant women was found to be below 1.0 μg/kg/d; while the HI value for VAs in all samples was < 1, save for PVAs and HAs. For fluoroquinolones and macrolides, 0.66% and 0.27% of pregnant women, respectively, had HI > 1. However, when all 12 antibiotics were combined an HI > 1 was detected in 0.93% of samples (the highest HI value was 9.28), with the greatest contribution from ciprofloxacin. Further, the HQ value exceeded 1 in 0.53% of samples ( [Fig. 2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#f0010), [Table S3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#s0095)).

### 3.3. Determinant analysis

Generally, younger maternal age was associated with a significantly higher level and detection frequency of trimethoprim ( [Table 1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#t0005), [Table S1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#s0095)). Furthermore, compared with premature-delivery mothers, mothers with full-term fetuses had significantly higher levels of trimethoprim and [norfloxacin](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/norfloxacin). Maternal normal/underweight status was associated with elevated concentrations of sulfamethoxazole, enrofloxacin, and ciprofloxacin. Of note, higher concentrations and detection frequencies for sulfamethoxazole, sulfadiazine, and ciprofloxacin were generally detected in mothers with low-level education or those who consumed more seafood during pregnancy. Further, elevated concentration of norfloxacin was detected in mothers from suburban areas, with elevated detection rate of ciprofloxacin, [florfenicol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/florfenicol), and azithromycin in mothers from urban areas. Vegetable and nut intake did not affect antibiotic concentrations (data not shown); and no statistically significant differences were observed in VA, PVA, and HA levels between women from suburban and urban areas ( [Fig. S1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#s0095)). The exposure levels for residents from the 16 administrative districts in Shanghai were comparable.

After the adjustment for potential confounders, we found that pregnant women with higher milk intake (i.e., ≥1–2 times/week) had a 1.96-times (95% CI: 1.10–3.49) higher risk of high-VA-exposure than lower-intake group. In addition, the odds of exposure to high levels of PVAs, VAs, PVAs + VAs, as well as to all antibiotics were 215% (OR: 3.15, 95% CI: 1.54–6.47), 107% (OR: 2.07, 95% CI: 1.07–4.21), 245% (OR: 3.45, 95% CI: 1.67–7.12), and 256% (OR: 3.56, 95% CI: 1.69–7.50) higher in the high egg intake group, compared to the low-intake group, respectively. Finally, high intake of seafood, livestock meat, and poultry during pregnancy was not associated with high antibiotic exposure levels ( [Fig. 3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#f0015)).

## 4\. Discussion

In the current study, we investigated the antibiotic [exposure profiles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/exposure-profile) in pregnancy and potential determinants of maternal antibiotic exposure in women from Shanghai. Sulfonamides and fluoroquinolones (used as VAs or PVAs) were found to be the most abundant in the urine of pregnant women. Specifically, ciprofloxacin (PVA) was the predominant antibiotic, followed by norfloxacin. Furthermore, high milk and egg consumption during pregnancy was associated with high PVA and VA exposure levels.

Within this study, nearly all samples (93%) were detected at least two antibiotics, suggesting that pregnant women in Shanghai are widely exposed to multiple environmental antibiotics. Despite the potential impact of antibiotics on [human health](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/public-health), data based on biomonitoring for comprehensive investigation of human antibiotic exposure are limited. In fact, to date, human exposure profiles have only been reported in China and South Korea ( [Ji et al., 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0060), [Liu et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0110), [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165), [Wang et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0170), [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175), [Wang et al., 2018b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0180), [Zhao et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0215)). Among them, two studies assessed the prenatal antibiotic exposure of the fetus, by analyzing maternal urine or [meconium](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/meconium) ( [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175), [Zhao et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0215)). Regarding the exposure level, fluoroquinolone and sulfonamide concentrations detected in the current study were significantly lower than those detected in mothers from two coastal cities in the Zhejiang and Jiangsu provinces ( [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175)). This discrepancy may be associated with the extent of regional antibiotic contamination, level of economic development, and dietary habits and lifestyle of the local people ( [Guo et al., 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0050), [Qian et al., 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0150), [Wang et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0160), [Yan et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0200)). Specifically, aquaculture production in Zhejiang and Jiangsu provinces is nearly 20 times higher than that in Shanghai ( [Liu et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0120)). In general, fluoroquinolone and [sulfonamide antibiotics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/sulfonamide-antibiotic) account for the primary contaminating factors in [surface waters](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/surface-water), due to their extensive use in food animal production and continuous release into different environmental compartments ( [Bu et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0020), [Carvalho and Santos, 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0025)). The concentrations of sulfonamide and fluoroquinolone residues in Shanghai [surface waters](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/surface-water), a city located downstream from the Yangtze River basin, are approximately on the order of part per trillion (maximum concentration: approximately 800 ng/L) ( [Bu et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0020), [Jiang et al., 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0065), [Ma et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0125), [Wu et al., 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0195)), which is similar to the antibiotic level in urine detected in the current study, if not slightly higher. Therefore, contaminated water may be one of the primary VA and PVA exposure sources during pregnancy for mothers in Shanghai.

Furthermore, we also detected HAs that are not commonly used as veterinary drugs (e.g., azithromycin and chloramphenicol) in some of the samples. These HAs have also been detected in pregnant women, adults, and children in other regions of China ( [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165), [Wang et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0170), [Wang et al., 2018b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0180)), with the detection frequency varying from 1.7% to 19.9%. Notably, azithromycin had been detected in certain aquatic products ( [Wang et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0160)). According to previous studies, recent clinical use may constitute the HA exposure source ( [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165), [Wang et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0170), [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175)). However, based on the [medical history](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/medical-history) and face-to-face questionnaire information obtained, none of the participants in the current study were taking the analyzed antibiotics for [therapeutic use](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/therapeutic-use). We, therefore, propose that the detected HAs entered the body via dietary intake of antibiotic-contaminated food or drinking water.

Dietary intake is considered to be one of the potential routes for human antibiotic exposure ( [Ji et al., 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0060), [Li et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0095), [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165)). Consistent with the results of the current study, it has been reported that an increased consumption of milk by children may be associated with increased detection frequencies of VAs, PVAs, and VAs + PVAs in urine ( [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165)). Further, the results of the current study indicated that the odds of maternal exposure to high levels of PVAs, VAs, PVAs + VAs, and all antibiotics are significantly higher in individuals who consume a larger amount of eggs and milk compared to those with low intake. However, studies on the effect of egg consumption on human internal antibiotic exposure levels are limited; while inconsistent findings have been reported on the effect of livestock, poultry, and aquatic product consumption on antibiotic exposure level ( [Ji et al., 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0060), [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165)). No associations were made between high intake of seafood, livestock or poultry meat, with high antibiotic exposure in the current study. However, aquatic products, livestock and poultry meat were shown to be predictors of VA or PVA exposure among school children and the general population ( [Ji et al., 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0060), [Wang et al., 2018a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0165)). This discrepancy may be attributed to the different dietary habits of pregnant women and children. Pregnant women may become more cautious about [food quality](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/food-quality) and safety having learned that they are pregnant, and preferentially select non-polluted meat and wild aquatic products. However, since public awareness concerning the occurrence of antibiotic residues in milk and egg is often much low that for meat products, the level of caution exercised is also correspondingly low. Hence, contaminated egg and milk may constitute another VA and PVA source during pregnancy.

We also observed that the detection frequency and concentrations of specific antibiotics differed with maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, education degree, gestational weeks, and living area. These findings may be related to the dietary habits, pre-pregnancy BMI, physiological status of pregnancy, and different extent of food or aquatic environment contamination. Similar to previous studies involving pregnant women and adults ( [Liu et al., 2017a](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0110), [Wang et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0175)), we found that younger pregnant women who were normal/underweight, with longer gestational age, lower levels of education and living in suburban areas, had higher sulfamethoxazole, trimethoprim, norfloxacin, enrofloxacin, and ciprofloxacin levels. Younger and low-educated adults often consume more meat than older and well-educated adults ( [Fraser et al., 2000](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0040)), which may increase their chance of exposure to animal-derived food contaminated with VAs and PVAs. Moreover, mothers at a longer gestational age may be exposed to relatively higher concentrations of trimethoprim and norfloxacin than mothers at a shorter gestational age since a higher level of food consumption is often associated with [later pregnancy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/third-trimester-pregnancy) ( [Liu et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0105)), which may result in an increased risk of antibiotic exposure. Also, a longer pregnancy may be expected to allow more time for the antibiotics to enter the body. Further, we found that the concentration of norfloxacin was higher in mothers living in suburban areas than those from urban areas. This may be associated with a higher antibiotic contamination in the aquatic environment or with specific food sources in suburban areas compared to urban areas.

In the present study, the estimated DED of most pregnant women was below 1.0 μg/kg/d. However, since water and animal-derived food products are ingested daily, the women may have been exposed to low doses of antibiotics over a long period of time. Further, approximately 1% of pregnant women were at a potential health risk of [adverse effects](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/adverse-event) from antibiotic exposure. The cumulative health risk was determined according to ADI values based on the microbiological effect, suggesting that antibiotics may have disrupted the [gut microbiota](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/intestine-flora) in some pregnant women. Previous studies involving animals suggested that antibiotics may induce development of immune-related diseases and obesity by disrupting the [gut microbiota](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/gut-microbiota) ( [Brestoff and Artis, 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0015), [Kuo et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0085), [Li et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0100)). Of note, according to recent [epidemiological studies](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/epidemiological-study), altering the composition of infant gut microbiota with antibiotics may be associated with obesity in offspring ( [Korpela, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0075), [Zhang et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0205)). Further epidemiological and experimental studies should be conducted to elucidate the underlying mechanism(s).

The current study was a biomonitoring-based prospective study covering all (16) districts in Shanghai, reflecting the internal antibiotic exposure levels of pregnant women living in Shanghai. None of the participants reported use of the antibiotics analyzed in the study during pregnancy, which excluded interference by iatrogenic antibiotics. However, the study has some limitations. First, internal antibiotic exposure levels in pregnant women may have been underestimated because some common antibiotics like β-lactams and other [tetracyclines](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/tetracycline-derivative) were not analyzed in the current study. However, these compounds have been detected in the aquatic environment of China and European countries ( [Bu et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0020), [Kummerer, 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0080)) and children from shanghai ( [Wang et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0170)); the environmental antibiotic exposure level for human may be higher. However, since [cephalosporins](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/cephalosporin-derivative) are widely used clinically, with restricted application in animal husbandry ( [ECDC, 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0030), [MOA, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412019339923#b0135)), β-lactams were not considered in the current study. Second, the [food frequency questionnaire](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/food-frequency-questionnaire) survey was administered during the mother’s hospital delivery stay, which may have resulted in recall bias, increasing the variation of variables. Hence, slight error may be associated with the estimation of true associations between the [food intake](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/food-intake) and antibiotic levels in urine. Nevertheless, since the questionnaire was administered simultaneously in low-exposure and high-exposure groups, the recall bias presumably occurred in both groups, and the impact on the results of the study should, therefore, be minimal.

## 5\. Conclusions

The current study indicated that pregnant women in Shanghai are widely exposed to multiple environmental antibiotics. Fluoroquinolones, as a PVA or VA, were the main contributor to local environmental antibiotic exposure, followed by sulfonamides. Further, egg and milk consumption during pregnancy may serve as the main factor contributing to maternal PVA and VA concentrations. Further studies are warranted to determine prenatal and postnatal exposure sources and pathways for risk management.

Multiple measurements of urinary antibiotic concentrations are essential to more accurate charactering the exposure levels during pregnancy. [Low-dose effects on thyroid disruption in zebrafish by long-term exposure to oxytetracycline](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166445X20303581)

Yu K., …, Huang L.

Aquatic Toxicology • Volume 227 • 2020 • Article 105608


Show abstract




As a feed additive in agriculture, the antibiotic oxytetracycline (OTC) has become widely distributed in the natural environment, leading to the exposure of many organisms to low doses of OTC. Although OTC is clinically contraindicated in children because of its multiple side effects, the effect of exposure to low doses of environmental OTC on children is unknown, particularly during development. In this study, we investigated the effects of OTC on the thyroid endocrine system in zebrafish, through determinations of the whole-body contents of triiodothyronine (T3), thyroxine (T4), and thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and analysis of the mRNA expression of regulatory genes involved in the hypothalamus–pituitary–thyroid (HPT) axis using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction. Zebrafish embryos were exposed to OTC at environmentally relevant concentrations from 2 h to 120 days post-fertilisation. After exposure to OTC at 1,000 and 5,000 ng/L, T3 contents were significantly enhanced (37.8% and 45.1%, respectively) and TSH contents were reduced (16% and 16.3%, respectively) compared with those in the controls. The OTC-driven increase in the transcription of genes involved in thyroid synthesis ( _tpo_ and _nis_) may be responsible for the altered T3 levels. These data indicate that OTC may cause thyroid dysfunction and lead to reduced TSH secretion owing to enhanced negative feedback control of the HPT axis. Meanwhile, a decrease in body length, weight, and BMI and an increase in heart rate were observed with increasing OTC exposure. In conclusion, our results indicate that long-term exposure to low concentrations of OTC may alter the transcription of key genes involved in the HPT axis, as well as T3 and TSH contents, thereby disrupting the thyroid system and affecting the growth and development of zebrafish.
