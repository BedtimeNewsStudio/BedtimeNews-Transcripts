# Narco-submarine

Narco-submarine

Submersible used by drug smugglers

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Peru_Narco-submarine.jpg/960px-Peru_Narco-submarine.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Peru_Narco-submarine.jpg) A narco-submarine captured by the [Peruvian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Navy "Peruvian Navy") in December 2019[&#8202;](#ref-back-PERUsub-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-PERUsub)

A **narco-submarine** (also called a **drug sub** or **narco-sub**) is a type of custom [ocean-going](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seakeeping "Seakeeping"), [self-propelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_propulsion "Marine propulsion"), semi-submersible or fully [submersible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submersible "Submersible") vessel built by (or for) [drug smugglers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_smuggler "Drug smuggler").[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCHMIDT-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-SCHMIDT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tamil-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Tamil)[&#8202;](#ref-back-APF-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-APF)

Newer semi-submersibles are almost fully submersible in order to reduce the likelihood of [detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detection "Detection") by [visual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_light "Visible light"), [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar"), [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar "Sonar"), or [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared "Infrared") systems.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tamil-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-Tamil) [Cargo capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_capacity "Cargo capacity") varies widely with vessel size, although several tons is typical. In 2015, the largest known cargo of 7.7 tonnes (17,000lb) was seized on a semi-submersible craft.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian2015-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-Guardian2015) Some contemporary narco-subs are capable of crossing the [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Popular-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-Popular) Meanwhile, recently captured vessels in the central [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific "Pacific") during the mid 2020s indicate said vessels are increasingly durable enough for their operators to attempt voyages across the Pacific to [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania "Oceania").

## History

During the [Prohibition in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States "Prohibition in the United States"), [bootleggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootlegging_(alcohol) "Bootlegging (alcohol)") used low-profile [riverboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverboat "Riverboat") to evade authorities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)

In the 1980s, [go-fast boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-fast_boat "Go-fast boat") were the [smuggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smuggling "Smuggling") vessel of choice in many parts of the world.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SCHMIDT-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-SCHMIDT) Go-fast boats became more vulnerable to radar detection as radar technology improved, leading to the development of semi-submersibles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-since1993-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-since1993)

In 1988, an uncrewed 6.4-metre (21ft) submarine was found off [Boca Raton, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida "Boca Raton, Florida"). It was designed to be [towed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towed "Towed") by a boat and submerged by [remote control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_control "Remote control"). The sub was empty, but officials and authorities believe it was used for smuggling after it was realized the hatch could be opened only from the outside.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

### 2000s

In 2006 a "submarine" was seized 166km (90nmi; 103mi) southwest of [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica "Costa Rica").[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) The [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard "U.S. Coast Guard") dubbed it _[Bigfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bigfoot "Bigfoot")_ because they heard rumors of their existence, but had never seen any prior to this find.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tamil-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-Tamil) In 2006, the U.S. detected three vessels in total,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-:0) and they estimated between twenty-five and forty semi-subs departed from South America in 2007.[&#8202;](#ref-back-APF2-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-APF2)

In 2006, a 10m (33ft) sub was discovered on the north coast of Spain. While its use is unconfirmed, it is speculated to have been built by, or for, narco-traffickers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

In March 2006, according to a press release,\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] the [Calabrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabria "Calabria") criminal organization ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta "'Ndrangheta") ordered a shipment of nine tons of cocaine to be transported by a narco-submarine from Colombia to [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy"), but according to a countering press release, the vessel was discovered by Colombian and Italian police during construction.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5b/Drugsub-Coast_Guard.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Drugsub-Coast_Guard.jpg) A submarine was seized by the [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard "U.S. Coast Guard") in international waters off [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala "Guatemala") on September 17, 2008.

During the first six months of 2008, the U.S. Coast Guard and [U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Navy "U.S. Navy") detected forty-two subs off the coasts of [Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America "Central America"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) but few seizures resulted. According to various press-releases, the projected eighty-five narco-submarine voyages by the end of 2008 could potentially bring about 544 tons of [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine") to [U.S. customers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugs_in_the_United_States "Drugs in the United States").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NavyTimes-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-NavyTimes) In 2008, the [Mexican Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Navy "Mexican Navy") intercepted a 10m (33ft) submarine in [international waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_waters "International waters") about 200km (110nmi; 120mi) southwest of [Oaxaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaxaca "Oaxaca"). [Mexican Navy Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerzas_Especiales "Fuerzas Especiales") [fast-roped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast-roped "Fast-roped") from a helicopter on to its deck and arrested four smugglers. According to one press-release, the vessel carried 5.3 tons of cocaine; it was towed to [Huatulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huatulco "Huatulco"), Oaxaca, by a Mexican Navy [patrol boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_boat "Patrol boat").[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) Also in 2008, the U.S. Coast Guard captured a semi-submersible vessel in international waters about 563km (304nmi; 350mi) west of [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala "Guatemala"); it was carrying 7.0 tons of cocaine.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23) The 18m (59ft) steel/fiberglass vessel was detected by a U.S. Navy aircraft as part of [Operation Panama Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Panama_Express "Operation Panama Express"), and was intercepted by Coast Guard [Law Enforcement Detachment 402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Enforcement_Detachments "Law Enforcement Detachments") aboard [USS _McInerney_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_McInerney_(FFG-8) "USS McInerney (FFG-8)"). Five days later, an 18m (59ft) semi-submersible was seized in international waters by the [USCGC _Midgett_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Midgett_(WHEC-726) "USCGC Midgett (WHEC-726)") about 322km (174nmi; 200mi) south of [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala "Guatemala").[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25) Several other submarines were intercepted, but not seized because their crews [scuttled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuttling "Scuttling") them upon interception.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[11\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

In 2009, the U.S. detected "as many as sixty" submarine related events, and calculated they were moving as much as a ton of cocaine daily.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JUSTF-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-JUSTF) In the same year, three submarines were seized on the shores of the [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") coast, loaded with 1.5 tons of cocaine.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) The [Colombian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Navy "Colombian Navy") had intercepted or discovered thirty-three submarines by 2009.[&#8202;](#ref-back-since1993-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-since1993)

### 2010s

After the November 5, 2010 arrest of Harold Mauricio Poveda,[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29) a key Mexican–Colombian link, [enhanced interrogation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_interrogation_techniques "Enhanced interrogation techniques") revealed that the FARC ( [Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Armed_Forces_of_Colombia "Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia")) were behind the construction of submarines and were collaborating with the [Sinaloa Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinaloa_Cartel "Sinaloa Cartel") to fund their activities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Southern_Command-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-Southern_Command)

In 2012, United States Coast Guard officials expressed concerns such vessels could potentially be used for [terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism "Terrorism").[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33)

[USCGC _Munro_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Munro_(WMSL-755) "USCGC Munro (WMSL-755)") crew intercepts suspected drug smuggling vessel, June 18, 2019

In 2015, the largest recorded seizure was after a cargo of 7.7 tonnes (17,000lb) was seized on a semi-submersible by [USCGC _Stratton_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Stratton "USCGC Stratton").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guardian2015-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-Guardian2015)[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34)

In 2016, the U.S. Coast Guard seized a semi-submersible in international waters about 300 miles west of [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama"), carrying about 6 tons of cocaine with a street value of about $200 million to U.S. customers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)

In 2017, the U.S. Coast Guard detained a semi-submersible off the coast of [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas") carrying 1.7 tonnes (3,800lb) of cocaine,[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36) and on 13 November the US Coast Guard located and seized another one off the coast of Panama.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Narcosubmarino_en_Ald%C3%A1n%2C_Cangas_2.jpg/500px-Narcosubmarino_en_Ald%C3%A1n%2C_Cangas_2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Narcosubmarino_en_Ald%C3%A1n,_Cangas_2.jpg) Narco-submarine apprehended in Aldán ( [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_(Spain) "Galicia (Spain)")).

In November 2019, a semi-submersible carrying 3 tonnes of cocaine hydrochloride was captured off the Galician coast of Spain after its three crew members—one Spanish and two Ecuadorian nationals—abandoned and attempted to scuttle the vessel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29) The approximately one-month voyage had begun on the Amazon River, entered the Atlantic Ocean through Brazil and continued toward the European mainland.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29) The operation marked the first capture of a semi-submersible drug-smuggling vessel in Europe.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-2)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29)

A narco-submarine was captured by the [Peruvian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Navy "Peruvian Navy") on 8 December 2019 carrying over 2 metric tons (4,400lb) of cocaine off of the coast of the [Department of Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Piura "Department of Piura").[&#8202;](#ref-back-PERUsub-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-PERUsub)[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39)

### 2020s

According to a press-release, on November 5, 2020, a submarine was seized in [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia").[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40) A further interception was made in October 2021 by the [Ecuadorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_Navy "Ecuadorian Navy") naval training ship _[Guayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Guayas_(BE-21) "BAE Guayas (BE-21)")_ in the Pacific Ocean.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41)

The drug trafficker [Laureano Oubiña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laureano_Oubi%C3%B1a "Laureano Oubiña") affirmed the existence of a marine cemetery of narco-submarines near the [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands "Canary Islands") ( [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain")).[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-42)

In February 2021, a European-made semi-submersible under construction was found in a warehouse in Málaga, Spain, during a multinational law-enforcement operation. The vessel was 9 metres long and was estimated to have capacity for carrying up to 2 tonnes of drugs. EMCDDA and Europol cited the case as demonstrating that semi-submersibles could also be constructed in Europe.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-3)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29)

A 22 metres (72ft) long semi-submersible made of [vinyl carbon fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinylon "Vinylon"), christened by its builders _Poseidón_ was found sunk without cargo by the [Guardia Civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Guard_(Spain) "Civil Guard (Spain)") in the [Ría de Arousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADa_de_Arousa "Ría de Arousa") in March 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43) The vessel had a central hold with a load capacity of five tons, a forward [ballast tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballast_tank "Ballast tank") and a [diesel engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine "Diesel engine") at stern.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)

In 2024, the Colombian Navy captured a semi-submersible carrying 5 tonnes of cocaine 1,200 miles southwest of Clipperton Island in the Eastern Pacific in a 62 country operation. Having been captured over 3,000 miles from the Colombian coast, the incident has been cited as proof that drug cartels are now willing to use narco-submersibles for direct, long-distance drug smuggling to Australia and New Zealand via a new route exceeding 4,000 miles rather than conventionally hiding smaller amounts of narcotics in commercial shipping.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45)

Over the next year, at least three narco-submarines were discovered in the [Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands "Solomon Islands") alongside another in [Tonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga "Tonga") after being abandoned by their operators; which has generated concern as the current set of local laws, lack of coast guard coordination, and inexperience within national law enforcement agencies have left Oceanian island nations unprepared to counter the illicit trade.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46)[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47) Besides Oceania, narco-submarines have also begun appearing in West African countries like [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone") due to public institutions being more vulnerable to [state capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capture "State capture") than European counterparts while their locations are ideal relay points to transfer narcotics more easily to European markets via drug mules or hidden amidst local export containers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)

In 2025, the United States military destroyed a semi-submersible during the [2025 United States strikes on Venezuelan boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_strikes_on_Venezuelan_boats "2025 United States strikes on Venezuelan boats") and the [2025 United States naval deployment in the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_naval_deployment_in_the_Caribbean "2025 United States naval deployment in the Caribbean"). It was confirmed that there were some survivors of the attack that were later captured by the U.S.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49)

In January 2026, Australian and Fijian police managed to capture a narco-submarine shipment of over 2 tonnes of cocaine in [Tavua, Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavua,_Fiji "Tavua, Fiji"). Four Ecuadorian nationals and two locals were also arrested amidst a ship-to-shore transfer from a boat after scuttling the submarine.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50)[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51)

In March 2026, as a part of the [Ecuadorian conflict (2024–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_conflict_(2024%E2%80%93present) "Ecuadorian conflict (2024–present)"), Ecuador sank a Narco-submarine located in a [mangrove swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_forest "Mangrove forest") in the Cayapas–Mataje nature reserve near the Northern border, with a drug smuggling camp located nearby.[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-52)[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-53)

### Crewed

#### Semi-submersible

Most vessels commonly described as narco-submarines are purpose-built semi-submersibles rather than conventional submarines. They are designed to travel with most of the hull below the waterline, reducing their visual profile and making detection more difficult.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-4)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29) Vessels seized in the Americas have frequently been constructed in remote areas of the Amazon region, including Colombia, and have been used to transport multi-tonne cocaine shipments.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-5)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29)

Construction sites have historically been selected for concealment, access to river systems through which equipment and materials can be moved, and proximity to the sea.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jaramillo2016p62-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-Jaramillo2016p62) Construction and operation can require specialist skills including engineering, welding, electrical work, fibreglass construction, maritime navigation and experienced seafaring.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jaramillo2016p59-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-Jaramillo2016p59)

A 2016 case study of vessels developed by the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia described three broad phases of technological development. Experimental designs between 1992 and 2004 were comparatively limited in capability; a prototyping period in 2005–2006 introduced more capable self-propelled semi-submersibles; and designs observed from 2007 became more standardised, with greater speed and carrying capacity and, in some cases, steel construction, advanced navigation systems, GPS and measures intended to reduce detection.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jaramillo2016pp6162-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-Jaramillo2016pp6162) The study described a progression from semi-submersibles intended for a single voyage toward more sophisticated craft capable of repeated journeys.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jaramillo2016pp6162-1)[\[56\]](#refnote-Jaramillo2016pp6162)

Conditions for crews can be severe. Accounts examined by Jacome Jaramillo describe cramped interiors, lack of running water, prolonged periods without sleep, engine noise and fumes; vessels have also been deliberately scuttled when interception appeared imminent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jaramillo2016p61-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-Jaramillo2016p61)

Europol reported in 2026 that semi-submersibles built specifically for cocaine trafficking were becoming increasingly sophisticated and capable of travelling longer distances, with improvements in vessel design facilitating Atlantic crossings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Europol2026p6-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-Europol2026p6)

#### Typical characteristics

These are the typical characteristics for semi-submersibles as stated by the U.S. [Joint Interagency Task Force South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Interagency_Task_Force_South "Joint Interagency Task Force South"):[&#8202;](#ref-back-jitfs-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-jitfs)

- Hull material: wood, fiberglass, or steel
- Length 12–24 m
- [Freeboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeboard_(nautical) "Freeboard (nautical)") 0.5 m
- Engines: single or twin [diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine "Diesel engine")
- Fuel capacity: 5.6 [cubic metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_metre "Cubic metre")
- Range: 3200 kilometers
- Speed: 11km/h or more
- Crew: 4
- Capacity 4–12 [metric tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne "Tonne")
- Control: human or [remote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_submarine "Unmanned submarine")

#### True submarines

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Narco_submarine_seized_in_Ecuador_2010-07-02_1.jpg/500px-Narco_submarine_seized_in_Ecuador_2010-07-02_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Narco_submarine_seized_in_Ecuador_2010-07-02_1.jpg) A true submarine seized in Ecuador in July 2010

Narco-submarines were considered by officials to be an oddity until 2000, when [Colombian Anti-Narcotics Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_police "Colombian police") discovered what was reported to be a half-built 36m-long[&#8202;](#ref-back-100_meters-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-100_meters) true submarine in a warehouse outside [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1 "Bogotá").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tamil-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-Tamil)[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-61) The double-hulled steel vessel could have traveled 3,700kilometers, dived 100m,[&#8202;](#ref-back-100_meters-1)[\[60\]](#refnote-100_meters) and could have carried about 15 tonnes of cocaine.[&#8202;](#ref-back-100_meters-2)[\[60\]](#refnote-100_meters)

On July 3, 2010 the Ecuadorian authorities seized a fully functional, completely submersible [diesel-electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel-electric "Diesel-electric") submarine in the jungles bordering [Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador "Ecuador") and Colombia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-APF-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-APF) It had a cylindrical [fiberglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiberglass "Fiberglass") and [kevlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevlar "Kevlar") hull 31m (102ft) long, a 3m (9.8ft) [conning tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conning_tower "Conning tower") with [periscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periscope "Periscope"), and [air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning "Air conditioning"). The vessel had the capacity for about 10 tonnes of cargo, a crew of five or six people, the ability to fully submerge down to 20m (66ft), and the capacity for long-range underwater operation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-APF-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-APF) Ecuadorean authorities seized the vessel before its maiden voyage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-62)[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-63)[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64)

On February 14, 2011 another submarine was seized by the Colombian Navy. The 31m-long fiberglass and Kevlar vessel was found hidden in a jungle area in [Timbiquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbiqu%C3%AD "Timbiquí"), in south-western Colombia. It was capable of travelling 9m (30ft) below water and it could carry four people and up to 8 tonnes of cargo.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-65)

### Uncrewed

In 2022, Spanish police seized three [unmanned underwater vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_underwater_vehicle "Unmanned underwater vehicle") used to smuggle drugs across the Strait of Gibraltar from Morocco, the first known interception of such devices. The drones, carrying up to 200 kg of narcotics each, were linked to French cartels. The operation led to eight arrests and exposed a gang using advanced drones and modified vehicles for trafficking across Europe (BBC, 2022).[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-66)[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67)[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-68)

Europol assessed in 2026 that autonomous semi-submersibles equipped with antennas and a modem were likely already capable of crossing the Atlantic without a crew on board, and that autonomous trafficking methods were likely to undergo further refinement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Europol2026p10-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-Europol2026p10)

### Whale-shaped mini-submarines

In 2025, international drug cartels began using sophisticated whale-shaped mini submarines to smuggle cocaine into Australia via Cyprus. These vessels are transported on cargo ships, dropped in international waters, then retrieved by smaller boats to deliver drugs ashore, posing a significant challenge to law enforcement detection efforts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-70)

### Routes

The western Colombian shore is ideal for smuggling. Of the two tons of cocaine coming out of Colombia daily, about a third leave via the Pacific coast in semi-submersibles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-72) Homeland Security estimate submarines carry one-third of smuggled maritime goods to United States customers, while claiming they "are clueless" about the rest.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-73)

Elsewhere, the U.S. Coast Guard say smugglers are evolving complete logistics: fishing vessels along the way warn the crews against patrols and provide them with refreshments, while [offshore refueling vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underway_replenishment "Underway replenishment") provide unlimited loiter time so smugglers can avoid coastal areas.[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-74) For smugglers, the trips are worth the investment—a nine-ton load earns nearly [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US$ "US$") 200 million wholesale from U.S. customers. Professional fishermen are often at the controls and earn about US$3,000 after completing the excursion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tamil-4)[\[3\]](#refnote-Tamil)[&#8202;](#ref-back-documentary-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-documentary)

Submarine smugglers unload their cargo onto fast-boats for the final leg to shore. According to press-releases, none of the submarines are known to unload at North American ports or beaches.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

[Drug mules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_mule "Drug mule") of the Chipitos branch of the Sinaloa Cartel reportedly hate submarine duty.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-76)

### Atlantic and European routes

Historically, semi-submersibles used for cocaine trafficking were concentrated in the Americas, including Pacific routes from Colombia and Ecuador toward Central America and Mexico.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-6)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29) Their use has since expanded to transatlantic trafficking. The 2019 Galicia case documented a one-month voyage from the Amazon River, through Brazil and across the Atlantic toward Europe.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EMCDDA2022p29-7)[\[38\]](#refnote-EMCDDA2022p29) In 2026, Europol assessed that semi-submersibles and other non-commercial vessels were being used for transatlantic transport and that seizures near the Azores and Galicia demonstrated the expanded range and technical capability of these craft.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Europol2026p3-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-Europol2026p3)

Europol reported that improvements in semi-submersible design had facilitated Atlantic crossings. In March 2025, a semi-submersible carrying approximately 6.5 tonnes of cocaine was intercepted near the Azores; Europol described it as the largest European Union seizure involving a semi-submersible. In mid-September 2025, another semi-submersible was used to traffic more than 3 tonnes of cocaine to the Galician coast of Spain.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Europol2026p6-1)[\[58\]](#refnote-Europol2026p6)

## Countermeasures

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Narco_sub2.jpg/500px-Narco_sub2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Narco_sub2.jpg) A narco-submarine moments before being intercepted by the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy") (August 2007).

### Detection and interdiction

The growing use of semi-submersibles and other non-commercial trafficking vessels has created challenges for conventional surveillance and interdiction. In 2007, the U.S. Coast Guard adjusted their [underwater acoustic sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophone "Hydrophone") to listen for submarines.[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-78) According to a 2019 press-release, the U.S. Coast Guard reports they capture only 11% of submarines on the [East Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pacific "East Pacific") route. The same source acknowledged that the U.S. Coast Guard was overstretched at the time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-79) In 2026, Europol assessed that the shift toward at-sea transfers, semi-submersibles and deep concealment had created new blind spots in maritime surveillance, and that maritime monitoring needed to extend beyond commercial ports to smaller harbours, coastal zones and open-sea corridors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Europol2026p11-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-Europol2026p11)

Maritime domain awareness (MDA) relies on information sharing and analysis across agencies and countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuegerEdmunds2024p173-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-BuegerEdmunds2024p173) In its operational form, MDA is used to direct patrol and interdiction activities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuegerEdmunds2024p174-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-BuegerEdmunds2024p174) MDA systems combine data from satellites, coastal radar, patrolling ships and aircraft with reports from human sources; a continuing challenge is tracking vessels that hide or obfuscate their positions or identities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuegerEdmunds2024pp177178-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-BuegerEdmunds2024pp177178) Bueger and Edmunds identify the Maritime Analysis and Operations Centre–Narcotics (MAOC-N) in Lisbon as an issue-specific information-sharing centre facilitating counternarcotics operations in the Atlantic.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BuegerEdmunds2024p177-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-BuegerEdmunds2024p177)

### Legal

If various militaries attempt to seize the semi-submersibles in international waters, the crews usually [scuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuttling "Scuttling") them. Until 2008, in accordance with [international maritime law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_maritime_law "International maritime law"), the crew was rescued, and, if there was no evidence of wrongdoing, released. To address this [loophole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loophole "Loophole"), the US [Drug Trafficking Vessel Interdiction Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_Trafficking_Vessel_Interdiction_Act "Drug Trafficking Vessel Interdiction Act") in September 2008 made it a "felony for those who knowingly or intentionally operate or embark in a self-propelled semi-submersible (SPSS) without nationality and that is or ever navigated in international waters, with the intent to evade detection".[&#8202;](#ref-back-NavyTimes-1)[\[16\]](#refnote-NavyTimes) The penalty is a prison term of up to twenty years in the U.S.[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-85)[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-86)

The U.S. law does not apply to [flagged vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_flag "Maritime flag") (i.e., [registered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_registration "Ship registration") with some officially recognized government). The bill grants [extraterritorial jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritorial_jurisdiction "Extraterritorial jurisdiction") over international waters and makes it illegal to lack relevant documents. Instead of an anti-narcotics operation turning into a rescue mission after submarines are sunk, the crew can be detained and interrogated using [exigent methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_interrogation_techniques "Enhanced interrogation techniques").[&#8202;](#ref-back-scotsman-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-scotsman)

In 2009, [Congress of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Colombia "Congress of Colombia") passed a law punishing builders of semi-submersible vessels with up to 12 years in prison, or 14 years if they are used to transport drugs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-scotsman-1)[\[87\]](#refnote-scotsman)

Security issues related to "torpedo-style cargo containers", semi-submersible vessels, and submarines were reviewed in an August 2012 article in the U.S. publication Homeland Security Affairs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-88)
