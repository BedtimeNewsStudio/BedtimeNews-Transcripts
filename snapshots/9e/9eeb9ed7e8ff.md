## Quick Summary

The Italo-Ethiopian War of 1895–1896, also known as the First Italo-Ethiopian War, was a conflict where Ethiopia successfully defended its sovereignty against an Italian invasion. This victory was significant as it marked the first time African forces decisively defeated a European power during the colonial era.1

The war stemmed from a dispute over the Treaty of Wichale, signed in 1889. While Italy interpreted the treaty as establishing a protectorate over Ethiopia, Emperor Menilek II understood it differently and later repudiated the treaty entirely. Italy's attempt to assert control militarily began in early 1895.1

Despite initial Italian advances, the Ethiopian army, under Menilek II, was well-armed and strategically prepared. The decisive Battle of Adwa on March 1, 1896, resulted in a crushing defeat for the Italian forces. This victory secured Ethiopia's independence and prevented Italy from building an empire in the region.1
