Military unit

The **Mechanised Infantry Regiment** is an [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry "Infantry") [regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment "Regiment") of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army "Indian Army"), comprising 27 battalions (including 3 tracked and 2 wheeled reconnaissance and support battalions) dispersed under various armoured formations throughout India. Together with the 23 battalions of [Brigade of the Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_of_the_Guards "Brigade of the Guards"), they form part of the _Mechanised Infantry_ arm, which along with the [Armoured Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army_Armoured_Corps "Indian Army Armoured Corps") form the Mechanised Forces.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)

## History

The Mechanised Infantry Regiment is one of the youngest regiments in the army and was the mastermind of [General K Sundarji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnaswamy_Sundarji "Krishnaswamy Sundarji"), who had the foresight to cater to the needs of a modern army.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) After the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1965 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1965"), a need was felt to give infantry battalions greater mobility, especially when operating with armoured formations. In 1969, 1st Madras became the first infantry unit to be equipped with the [armoured personnel carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_personnel_carrier "Armoured personnel carrier") (APC) [TOPAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OT-62_TOPAS "OT-62 TOPAS"). In 1970, nine more of the oldest battalions from various infantry regiments were equipped with APCs, these included TOPAS, [SKOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OT-64_SKOT "OT-64 SKOT") and [BTR-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-60 "BTR-60").[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) These battalions remained affiliated with their erstwhile Infantry Regiments and Regimental Centres and did not form a separate regiment. Eventually, fourteen old infantry battalions were mechanised.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Infantry-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-Infantry)[&#8202;](#ref-back-IA-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-IA)

During the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971 "Indo-Pakistani War of 1971"), some of these battalions saw action as part of combat groupings with [armoured units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army_Armoured_Corps "Indian Army Armoured Corps") for the first time.
The APCs were replaced with [BMP-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-1 "BMP-1") Infantry Combat Vehicles between 1977 and 1978. The need for something more concrete and viable was felt, along with a need to develop a common battle and training philosophy with regards to the type of tactics. This necessitated the raising a totally new arm in the Indian Army, resulting in the various mechanised battalions being brought together under a single [cap badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_badge "Cap badge") as the Mechanised Infantry Regiment, which was raised with fourteen battalions on 2 April 1979 and the affairs of the regiment were transferred from Directorate General of Infantry to Armoured Corps Directorate, which was renamed the Directorate General Mechanised Forces in 1986. Gen K Sundarji was appointed the first Colonel of the Mechanised Infantry Regiment, the post he held till his retirement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Infantry-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-Infantry)[&#8202;](#ref-back-autogenerated1-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-autogenerated1)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Stamp_of_India_-_1988_-_Colnect_165238_-_The_Mechanised_Infantry_Regiment.jpeg/330px-Stamp_of_India_-_1988_-_Colnect_165238_-_The_Mechanised_Infantry_Regiment.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stamp_of_India_-_1988_-_Colnect_165238_-_The_Mechanised_Infantry_Regiment.jpeg) 1988 postal stamp

The regiment saw a rapid expansion in the 1980s. Mechanised infantry battalions numbered 15 to 26 have been raised on all-India mixed class composition, unlike the older units, which were single class ones. The only exception was 20 Rajput, which joined the regiment as the 24th battalion in April 1992.[&#8202;](#ref-back-IA-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-IA)
The Mechanised Infantry Regiment has been involved in [Operation Pawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pawan "Operation Pawan") in Sri Lanka, [Operation Rakshak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rakshak "Operation Rakshak") in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India "Punjab, India") and [Jammu & Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir") and [Operation Vijay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vijay_(1999) "Operation Vijay (1999)") in Jammu & Kashmir. It also took part in UN Peacekeeping Operations in [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia "Somalia"), [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo"), [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola") and [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone "Sierra Leone").The Regiment is affiliated to the Indian Naval Ship [Gharial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Gharial_(L23) "INS Gharial (L23)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Infantry-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-Infantry)[&#8202;](#ref-back-autogenerated1-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-autogenerated1)

## Mechanised Infantry Institutions

### Mechanised Infantry Centre and School

The _Mechanised Infantry Regimental Centre (MIRC)_ was established on 2 April 1979 at Ahmednagar (now Ahilyanagar) and it is spread over 2179 acres. It trains approximately 950 recruits annually.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) It has three training battalions: Infantry Training Battalion, Composite Training Battalion and Driving and Maintenance Battalion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-:0) It was renamed as the _Mechanised Infantry Centre and School (MIC&S)_ on 17 September 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) In addition to the troops from the Mechanised Infantry, those from the Brigade of the Guards undergo their 'mechanised' training at the MIC&S. MIC&S offers eight diploma courses and four post graduate diploma courses under the [Savitribai Phule Pune University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitribai_Phule_Pune_University "Savitribai Phule Pune University").[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

### Quarter Guard

The Quarter Guard of the MIRC was created on 9 August 1979 during the first biennial conference. After the sanction of KLP, the present Quarter Guard was conceptualised in half circular area with front wide platform. The new Quarter Guard was completed in 1986. The Quarter Guard has 18 internal rooms as well as an armour shop and a guard room. To the right and left side of the guard are two guns and two cannons that symbolise the strength of soldiers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-:0)

### Military Band

See also: [Indian military bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_military_bands "Indian military bands")

The MIRC Band is based at [Ahmednagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmednagar "Ahmednagar") and serves all regimental duties related to musical support. The band was formed during April 1982 under the auspices of Brigadier Tilak Raj. The band has held the honour of performing at national events such as the [Beating Retreat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beating_Retreat "Beating Retreat"), [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Day_(India) "Army Day (India)") and [Republic Day Parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Republic_Day_parade "Delhi Republic Day parade"), as well as events at the [Rashtrapati Bhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrapati_Bhavan,_New_Delhi "Rashtrapati Bhavan, New Delhi"). It also has performed at the [2007 Military World Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Military_World_Games "2007 Military World Games"), the [2010 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Commonwealth_Games "2010 Commonwealth Games"), and the 2001 [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai") International Fleet Review among others.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[10\]](#refnote-:0)

### Regimental museum

The MIRC Museum was founded and inaugurated on 9 February 2000 by Lieutenant General Pankaj Joshi, the then Colonel of the regiment. The museum is currently housed at the ground floor of Mechanised Bhavan. The museum houses the regimental colour, as well as the history if the MIRC and mementos from Colonels of the regiment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-3)[\[10\]](#refnote-:0)

## Regimental battalions

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/NAMIS_Republic_day_parade_2023.jpg/250px-NAMIS_Republic_day_parade_2023.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NAMIS_Republic_day_parade_2023.jpg) Nag Missile System, (17th Battalion, Mechanised Infantry Regiment), Republic Day Parade, 2023

The Mechanised Infantry Regiment consists of units designated as _battalions_. Each battalion consists of four [companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_(military_unit) "Company (military unit)") \- Headquarter Company and three Mechanised Companies. In addition, there are [platoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platoon "Platoon") \- Reconnaissance (recce), [Signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army_Corps_of_Signals "Indian Army Corps of Signals"), Adm Medium and [Light Repair Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army_Corps_of_EME "Indian Army Corps of EME"). The sanctioned strength of a battalion is 805 personnel (26 Officers, 52 [JCOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_commissioned_officer "Junior commissioned officer"), 6 technical JCOs/ [CHMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havildar "Havildar") and 721 [ORs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Other_ranks "Other ranks"). The battalions are operational under Armoured Divisions, Armoured Brigades or Independent Mechanised Brigades.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bn-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-Bn)
