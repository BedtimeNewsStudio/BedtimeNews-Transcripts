On 28 August 2025, the [Israeli Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Air_Force "Israeli Air Force") (IAF) conducted [targeted strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_killing_by_Israel "Targeted killing by Israel"), codenamed **Operation Lucky Drop** ( [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): מבצע טיפת מזל)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fleming082825-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-Fleming082825) against several [Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthis "Houthis") ministers in [Bayt Baws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayt_Baws "Bayt Baws"), [Sanaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanaa "Sanaa"), Yemen.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) The group's prime minister [Ahmed al-Rahawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_al-Rahawi "Ahmed al-Rahawi"), defense minister [Mohamed al-Atifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_al-Atifi "Mohamed al-Atifi"), and chief of staff [Muhammad Abd al-Karim al-Ghamari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Abd_al-Karim_al-Ghamari "Muhammad Abd al-Karim al-Ghamari"), who had gathered to listen to a televised speech by Houthi leader [Abdul-Malik al-Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Malik_al-Houthi "Abdul-Malik al-Houthi"), were among the targets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Levaton082825-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-Levaton082825) The deaths of al-Rahawi and several other ministers were later confirmed by the group, and al-Ghamari's death was confirmed in October.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-:4)

## Background

Main articles: [Middle Eastern crisis (2023–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_crisis_(2023%E2%80%93present) "Middle Eastern crisis (2023–present)"), [Red Sea crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_crisis "Red Sea crisis"), and [2025 Israeli attacks in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Israeli_attacks_in_Yemen "2025 Israeli attacks in Yemen")

Following [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas")'s [October 7 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks "October 7 attacks") on Israel in 2023, which led to the [Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war"), the Yemeni [Houthis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthis "Houthis") began launching missiles and drones at Israel and at a number of [international shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_transport "Freight transport") vessels transiting the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea"), actions they say are in solidarity with the [Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinians "Palestinians"). In response, a United States–led coalition launched [a series of missiles and airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US%E2%80%93UK_airstrikes_on_Yemen "US–UK airstrikes on Yemen") against the Houthis, which ended following [a ceasefire deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States%E2%80%93Houthi_ceasefire "2025 United States–Houthi ceasefire") between the US and the Houthis in May 2025. [A deadly Houthi attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Houthi_drone_attack_on_Israel "2024 Houthi drone attack on Israel") in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv "Tel Aviv") in July 2024 led Israel to launch [its first airstrikes on Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_July_2024_Israeli_attack_on_Yemen "20 July 2024 Israeli attack on Yemen"). Since then, Israel began launching occasional strikes against the Houthis, hitting targets such as [Sanaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanaa "Sanaa") and [Hodeidah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodeidah "Hodeidah").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Magdy082825-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-Magdy082825)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Levaton082825-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-Levaton082825)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ghobari082825-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-Ghobari082825)

The 28 August strikes followed a wave of Israeli airstrikes in Sanaa earlier that week on 24 August that killed 10 people and injured 102 others. Among the targets were an oil terminal, a power plant, and a military base near the [presidential palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace_of_Yemen "Presidential Palace of Yemen"). The strikes came after the Houthis fired a [cluster bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_munition "Cluster munition") at Israel for the first time. According to Israeli broadcaster [Channel 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_12_(Israel) "Channel 12 (Israel)"), Israel prepared to target the Houthi leadership during the 24 August strikes but decided against it.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Levaton082825-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-Levaton082825) Earlier on 28 August, the Houthis launched two drones towards Israel, which were intercepted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825)

## Airstrikes

According to [Hezbollah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbollah "Hezbollah")-linked news outlet [Al Mayadeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mayadeen "Al Mayadeen"), on 28 August, at least 10 Israeli airstrikes hit sites in Sanaa, while further attacks took place in the governorates of ['Amran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Amran_Governorate "'Amran Governorate") and [Hajjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjah_Governorate "Hajjah Governorate").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-:1) Saudi news outlet [Al-Hadath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hadath "Al-Hadath") reported that both the [Israeli Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Air_Force "Israeli Air Force") and the [Israeli Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Navy "Israeli Navy") partook in the strikes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9) [Asharq al-Awsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asharq_Al-Awsat "Asharq Al-Awsat") reported that three sites were targeted: a home near a water plant, a site in Jabal Atan, and a site near the Houthi-held [Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace_of_Yemen "Presidential Palace of Yemen") used to hold meetings; with one of the buildings being completely destroyed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) Tribal leaders told the [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") that the strike that killed the Houthi prime minister hit a meeting at a villa in [Bayt Baws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayt_Baws "Bayt Baws"), southern Sanaa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) The meeting was held between government ministers "to evaluate \[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[the government's\] activity and performance over the past year", according to the Houthis.[\[12\]](#refnote-12) [Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English "Al Jazeera English") reported damage on Mount Attan, a hill southwest of Sanaa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

Shortly after the attack, the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces") (IDF) confirmed that it carried out an airstrike on a Houthi military site in Sanaa.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) According to Israeli media, the IAF targeted safe houses where ten top Houthi officials had gathered to watch a televised speech by Houthi leader [Abdul-Malik al-Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Malik_al-Houthi "Abdul-Malik al-Houthi"), launching over ten missiles in under five minutes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fleming082825-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-Fleming082825) Israel's [KAN News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAN_News "KAN News") reported that an attack on the presidential palace killed several Houthi political leaders.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825) The IDF confirmed the following day that it targeted the Houthi defense minister and chief of staff.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)

_[The Times of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_Israel "The Times of Israel")_ reported that Israel had received intelligence at around 1p.m. that 10 Houthi leaders gathered near Sanaa to listen to al-Houthi's speech. Israeli intelligence then provided real-time information about the gathering, enabling the airstrike, despite a high concentration of air defenses.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Nuki082825-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-Nuki082825) The operation was monitored by Israeli defense minister [Israel Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Katz "Israel Katz") and IDF chief of staff [Eyal Zamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyal_Zamir "Eyal Zamir"), while prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu") was briefed via a "red phone".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-4)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825)

According to Israeli estimates, Houthi defense minister [Mohamed al-Atifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_al-Atifi "Mohamed al-Atifi") and chief of the general staff [Muhammad Abd al-Karim al-Ghamari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Abd_al-Karim_al-Ghamari "Muhammad Abd al-Karim al-Ghamari") were likely killed in the attacks near the presidential palace, the latter of whom was previously targeted during the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War "Twelve-Day War") in June 2025.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fleming082825-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-Fleming082825)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-:0) The two made public statements after the attacks, but have not publicly appeared since. It was reported on 29 August that al-Atifi survived with serious injuries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19) Additionally, Houthi Prime Minister [Ahmed al-Rahawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_al-Rahawi "Ahmed al-Rahawi") was reportedly killed alongside several associates in a strike on an apartment in Sanaa's [Bayt Baws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayt_Baws "Bayt Baws") neighborhood, according to [KAN News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Public_Broadcasting_Corporation "Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation") and Yemeni media.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-5)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-:0) The head of the Houthi-controlled [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_(Yemen) "Ministry of Defense (Yemen)"), Asaad al-Sharqabi, was also killed, according to Yemeni government sources cited by [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-:1)

[Ynet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynet "Ynet") reported that Israel had growing confidence that it had killed the "entire military and governmental elite of the Houthis", while [Channel 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_13_(Israel) "Channel 13 (Israel)") said that Israel believed its attack succeeded.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) According to _[The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post "The Jerusalem Post")_, there was an "increasing likelihood" that the defense minister and chief of staff were killed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-6)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825) However, whether al-Ghamari was present in any of the targeted locations or if any senior leaders were killed remained unconfirmed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Nuki082825-1)[\[16\]](#refnote-Nuki082825) Two months later, on 16 October, the Houthis confirmed that al-Ghamari had died.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) A later report by Channel 12 said that the IDF assessed that it killed the entire Houthi cabinet, including the prime minister and 12 other officials, although its findings were not definitive.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

On 30 August, the Houthis published a statement confirming the death of Rahawi as well as an unspecified number of ministers, while saying that others were seriously or moderately injured.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24) It was later confirmed that 12 people, more than half of the 22-member cabinet, were killed, namely the prime minister, 9 other ministers, and 2 cabinet members.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-:3) According to the [Israeli Army Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Army_Radio "Israeli Army Radio"), among the dead were the director of the political bureau, the prime minister's chief of staff, secretary of [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Yemen "Cabinet of Yemen"), [minister of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_(Yemen) "Ministry of Justice (Yemen)"), [minister of economy and trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Industry_and_Trade_(Yemen) "Ministry of Industry and Trade (Yemen)"), [Jamal Amer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Amer "Jamal Amer"), who was [minister of foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_(Yemen) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Yemen)"), [minister of agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Irrigation_(Yemen) "Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation (Yemen)"), and the minister of public relations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[23\]](#refnote-:2) The IDF confirmed killing al-Rahawi along with other senior military and political officials.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

On 16 October, the Houthis announced the death of Muhammad al-Ghamari.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-3)[\[5\]](#refnote-:4) Israeli defense minister [Israel Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Katz "Israel Katz") said that he died of wounds sustained in the attacks,[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27) while the IDF said that he died on 28 August.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) [Saba News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_News_Agency "Saba News Agency") reported that his 13-year-old son and several companions were also killed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29) On 21 October, Yemeni media reported that Houthi Defense Minister Mohamed al-Atifi was reportedly in critical condition and rapidly declining, having been hospitalized since the 28 August airstrike. Israeli intelligence believed that he may have been among the casualties of the strike.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)

Rahawi and other victims of the strike were given a funeral at the [Al-Shaab Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Saleh_Mosque "Al-Saleh Mosque") in Sanaa on 1 September.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[25\]](#refnote-:3) Al-Ghamari's funeral was held on 20 October.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)

## Aftermath

On 31 August 2025, Houthi security forces conducted raids on buildings belonging to [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") agencies in Sanaa and [Hodeidah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodeidah "Hodeidah"), and arrested 11 workers. The workers belonged to various UN agencies including the [World Food Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Food_Programme "World Food Programme"), [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") and the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization").[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33) On 4 September, a Houthi official said the workers were being investigated as spies for Israel and the US, and would be put on trial if the accusations were confirmed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34) On 31 October, the Houthis announced that 43 UN staff held by the group would be tried over their alleged involvement in the attack.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)

## Reactions

Following the strikes, Israeli defense minister [Israel Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Katz "Israel Katz") said: "As we warned the Houthis in Yemen: After the plague of darkness comes the plague of the firstborn," referencing the Biblical [Plagues of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagues_of_Egypt "Plagues of Egypt").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Levaton082825-3)[\[4\]](#refnote-Levaton082825) He added that: "Anyone who raises a hand against Israel – their hand will be severed."[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bob082825-7)[\[3\]](#refnote-Bob082825)

A Houthi defense source initially denied that any senior leaders had been targeted and instead claimed that the attacks targeted Yemeni civilians due to "their positions supporting [Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip "Gaza Strip")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Levaton082825-4)[\[4\]](#refnote-Levaton082825) The Houthis later described the attacks as "criminal and treacherous", and [Supreme Political Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Political_Council "Supreme Political Council") leader [Mahdi al-Mashat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdi_al-Mashat "Mahdi al-Mashat") vowed to avenge the killings, warning international companies to leave Israel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36) Houthi leader Abdul-Malik al-Houthi vowed to continue and to escalate the group's missile and drone attacks on Israel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)

Iran's [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Revolutionary_Guard_Corps "Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps") called the attack a [war crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crime "War crime") that sought to suppress Yemen's support for the Palestinians, and added that the attack "will ignite greater anger and expand the geography of [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_Resistance "Axis of Resistance")." Iranian president [Masoud Pezeshkian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoud_Pezeshkian "Masoud Pezeshkian") urged an international response to Israel, while judiciary chief [Gholam-Hossein Mohseni-Eje'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gholam-Hossein_Mohseni-Eje'i "Gholam-Hossein Mohseni-Eje'i") said the attacks displayed "Israel's failure to defeat Yemen militarily".[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)

[Hezbollah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbollah "Hezbollah") secretary-general [Naim Qassem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naim_Qassem "Naim Qassem") sent condolences to Abdul-Malik al-Houthi.[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39)
