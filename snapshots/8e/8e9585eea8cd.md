# EU-US trade: facts and figures

The European Union and the United States have the **largest bilateral trade and investment relationship** and the most integrated economic relationship in the world.

Together, they represent almost30% of global trade in goods and services and 43% of global GDP.

In 2025, transatlantic trade in goods and services reached over €1.77 trillion (provisional data). Both the EU and US are top trading partners in goods for each other.

![Maps and flags of the United States and of the EU. Two arrows connect these elements.](/media/4qon4kgs/eu-us-maps-visual.png)

## Trade in goods (2025)

In 2025, EU-US trade in goods was worth over €910 billion. This has nearly doubled in the last 10 years.

![Icon representing exports.](/media/sqmfbhay/arrows-import-red.svg)

**€554.6 billion** worth of EU exports to the US

### The US is the main EU trade partner

The US is EU's top trading partner for EU exports and the second (after China) partner for EU imports.

[Text version](#0)

21% of EU exports in goods go to the US, followed by the United Kingdom (13.1%), Switzerland (8.3%), China (7.5%) and Türkiye (4.3%).

22% of the goods imported in the EU come from China, followed by the US (14.2%), the United Kingdom (6.3%), Switzerland (5.7%) and Türkiye (4.1%).

For both imports and exports, the top five most-traded categories of products made up almost half of all trade to the US.

## Trade surplus

The EU had a trade surplus with the US of around €20 billion in 2025, taking into account both goods and services. This represented only around 1% of total EU-US trade.

With regard to trade in goods, the EU had a surplus of €198 billion, and with regard to trade in services, the EU had a deficit of over €178 billion.

In that respect, the **EU and US economies complement each other very well**.

## EU-US joint statement

The EU-US joint statement, agreed on 21 August 2025, set out a framework for reducing tariffs and strengthening the transatlantic trade relationship.

In June 2026, the Council formally adopted two regulations implementing the tariff commitments set out in the Joint Statement:

* a main regulation removing the remaining **customs duties** on US industrial goods and granting p**referential market access** for certain US seafood and non-sensitive agricultural products
* a second regulation extending the **suspension of duties on lobster imports**, including processed lobster, from all countries on a most favoured nation basis

The regulations include reinforced safeguard and suspension mechanisms to protect EU economic interests. The main regulation will cease to apply at the end of 2029, while the lobster regulation will expire on 31 July 2030 unless further action is taken.
