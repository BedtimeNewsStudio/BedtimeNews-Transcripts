# Nike employment drops 5%, CEO John Donahoe takes an 11% pay cut

- Updated: Oct. 06, 2024, 8:54 p.m.
- \|Published: Jul. 25, 2024, 3:24 p.m.

![Nike HQ](https://www.oregonlive.com/resizer/v2/6TAAVKOLXVFR7OHZV3XQNW4LLM.jpg?auth=8140259a461bbab724b23fc77db0eee4aef5a072966487fa42d4bc90aa473a03&width=1280&smart=true)Nike on Thursday disclosed new information about its headcount and executive compensation. By

[Nike](https://www.oregonlive.com/nike/) employs 5% fewer people than it did a year ago, according to an annual report filed Thursday with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

In February, Nike CEO John Donahoe said the company would [cut 2% of its global workforce](https://www.oregonlive.com/business/2024/02/nike-to-lay-off-2-of-global-workforce-ceo-tells-employees.html), or about 1,600 jobs. The annual report shows Nike, as of May 31, 2024, had 79,400 workers, 4,300 fewer than a year earlier.

The difference between the number of reported layoffs and the total job loss is likely attributable to smaller, [earlier rounds of job cuts](https://www.oregonlive.com/business/2023/12/nike-quietly-cuts-employees-in-rolling-layoffs-scope-unclear.html) and employee attrition.

In Oregon, Nike now employs 10,700. That’s down 700 workers from a year ago.

Nike did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The annual report, and a proxy statement also filed Thursday, give the latest information on the company’s headcount and executive compensation, both closely watched metrics in the wake of Nike’s [ongoing struggles](https://www.oregonlive.com/news/2023/10/nikes-shoes-are-setting-records-but-casual-runners-are-wearing-rivals.html) and [falling sales](https://www.oregonlive.com/business/2024/06/nike-shares-fall-as-sales-slow-ceo-donahoe-says-company-taking-challenges-head-on.html).

The proxy statement shows Nike awarded Donahoe $29.1 million in compensation in fiscal 2024 compensation, an 11% decrease.

Nike ties executive compensation to company performance. The company’s stock, which recently had a [20% one-day decline](https://www.oregonlive.com/business/2024/06/nike-stock-has-worst-day-on-record-wiping-out-28-billion-in-value.html), fell 7% in the last fiscal year.

Donahoe’s compensation included $1.56 million in salary, $12.4 million in stock awards, $6.8 million in stock options, $1.95 million in a form of performance bonus and $6.4 million in additional compensation that included $6.28 million in charitable matching contributions.

While he took a pay cut last year, Donahoe is likely still the highest-paid CEO of a public company in Oregon.

For comparison, Intel paid Patrick Gelsinger $16.9 million last year. Intel’s headquarters are in Silicon Valley, where Gelsinger works, but its largest sites are in Oregon.

Nike’s CEO pay ratio, a measure of Donahoe’s pay relative to the pay of the median Nike worker, is now 759 to 1, down from 975 to 1 a year ago.

The company defines its median worker as a retail store employee in Canada who makes $38,462.
