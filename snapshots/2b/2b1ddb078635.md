When news came from Soviet Russia four years ago that Dr. Alexander A. Bogomolets had discovered in ACS (short for “anti-reticular cytotoxic serum") a means of dealing with some baffling diseases and even of prolonging life to 150, the popularizers of science saw their opportunity and wrote rhapsodies about another “miracle" of medicine. It is said that 3,000,000 doses of this ACS serum are distributed every year in Soviet Russia; that Joseph Stalin and the aged former President of the U.S.S.R., Kalinin, get “shots” of it from time to time; that it is good for virtually any human ailment from schizophrenia, an incurable form of insanity, to cancer. Like any other scientific investigator, Bogomolets had his predecessors whose work was an inspiration. Back in 1900 Bordet, a Belgian bacteriologist, who had as much as Wassermann to do with the development of a simple test for syphilis, discovered what are now known as cytotoxic serums, so called because they are poisonous to body cells of various types. He found that when material from the liver of a rabbit was injected into an animal of another species antibodies were marshaled in defense. Bordet took the second animal's blood serum and injected it into the original rabbit. Result: The liver was destroyed. But only the liver. More sensational still, just a little serum from the blood of a second animal proved to be invigorating to the original rabbit. Пlya Metchnikoff, the man who had the world drinking fermented milk a generation ago, because in his opinion it kept at bay the deadlier bacteria in the intestinal tract (a cesspool to him) and thus prolonged life, picked up the clue. He injected red corpuscles, liver or kidney cells into an animal. "After several injections," he wrote, "the blood serum of the animal becomes toxic to the cells which have been introduced. If such a serum is reinjected into the animal from which the original cells have been taken it will kill these specific cells. So, for instance, the injection of sperm cells into a horse produces a serum with specific toxins which lead to sterility if used on the species from which the sperm originally came." Metchnikoff reported that "small portions of cytotoxic serums strengthen specific elements of tissues rather than killing or dissolving them. Something similar occurs with many poisons. In large doses they kill while in small doses they heal or improve."

'The Cement of Life'

Bogomolets carried the reasoning of Bordet and Metchnikoff a step further. He turned to connective or reticular tissue, the stuff that holds the body together, enables wounds to heal, stuff that may be regarded as the cement of life. He produces this serum by inoculating horses with cells of bone marrow and spleen taken from a human corpse and accurately determines the amount of cytotoxins obtained, which is important. The stimulating dose of the diluted horse serum is 0.3-1 cubic centimeter. Bogomolets injected his connective tissue serum under the skin of animals and later of human patients. The defenses of the body against microbes were strengthened astonishingly. Animals that ought to have been killed after inoculation with deadly microbes survived when they received ACS. Bogomolets reported that he even saved the lives of mice to which cancers had been transplanted, that scarlet fever yielded in twenty-four hours, that the condition of a few schizophrenics was improved. Bogomolets admits that he failed to get results in human cancer and insists that when a tumor has appeared it must be cut out. On the other hand, he made wounds and broken bones heal after all other treatment had failed. Similar success is said to have been achieved in infectious arthritis but not in chronic arthritis. In his own clinic Bogomolets treated people over 50 and convinced himself that ACS would prolong life by combating arteriosclerosis. So he wrote: "Man can live more than a hundred years and should try to achieve 150," which, he thinks, "is probably the limit at the present state of human evolution." He would probably be the first to repudiate the claim, widely circulated in this country, that ACS will make it possible for women past 90 “to retain child-bearing function" or that ACS was developed for the primary purpose of prolonging the life of Joseph Stalin. Apparently Bogomolets accepts Metchnikoff's explanation that a serum derived from connective tissue does not kill bacteria or injurious cells directly but that it supplies the body with weapons to wage a fight against invasion. According to him, connective tissue develops from an embryonic "mesochyma" which consists of a network of protoplasmic cells filled by & homogenous matrix. Hitherto this "mesochyma" has been considered a purely anatomical, structural tissue.

Results of Treatment

What are we to make of all this? In a report to the Empire Rheumatism Council, Dr. Francis Bach says that ACS was tried on forty-eight patients who suffered from various types of "rheumatism" and that the results were none too good. Definite subjective improvement was noted in seven of the forty-eight and only slight improvement in another seven. There were three cases of relapse after two months among the "definite improvement" cases. Professor Storzhesko, in whose clinic in Russia the action of the serum on "rheumatism” was also studied, reached the conclusion that the best results are obtained in cases of infection. The answer to the many questions raised will come from Dr. Harry Goldblatt (Western Reserve University); who, with the aid of a grant of $15,000, is now making ACS, testing it and distributing it to experimenters in this country. Goldblatt will issue no statement about the serum. Just now he is compiling answers to questionaires which indicate how the first 3,000 patients fared. In New York and Philadelphia groups are treating rheumatoid arthritis with the serum. At the Western Reserve University the serum has been given in cases of cancer, with relief of pain. The serum has also been administered to schizophrenics with unknown result. Even if we look at the reports from Russia skeptically it is plain that Bogomolets has at least performed the useful task of putting connective tissue in a new light. It is not the neutral, inert “cement" once supposed, but just as important as the liver and the heart. And probably ACS has its uses in medicine.

Unexplained Energy

Animals Manage to Travel Long Distances Without Food or Rest

Macey F. Deming of Tappan, N. Y., tells this department that insects in vast swarms fly from the mainland to Bermuda, more than 500 miles; from the coast of California to the Hawaiian Islands, more than 2,000 miles, and from the United States to Europe, more than 3,000 miles. Swarms of butterflies have been met in the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans 1,000 miles from land. The ocean water is salt and there is no place for the insects to obtain food, water or rest. They have to be in constant motion all of the time. The golden plover, a land bird, is known to fly 1,700 miles over the ocean without stopping, and other small land birds fly 1,000 miles over the sea regularly without food or rest. Here is a field for young biologists to explore. How do animals manage to live and travel under the conditions described?
