The **_Up_ series** of [documentary films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film "Documentary film") follows the lives of ten boys and four girls in the United Kingdom, beginning in 1964, when they were seven years old. The documentary series has had ten episodes—one every seven years—spanning 63 years to the final instalment in 2026. The first film was titled _Seven Up!_, the second _7 Plus Seven_; the third _21 Up_; and the rest similarly titled through to _70 Up_, matching the subjects' age at the time of filming. The director of the first film was [Paul Almond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Almond "Paul Almond"), with the next eight directed by [Michael Apted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Apted "Michael Apted") and the last (after Apted's death) by [Asif Kapadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asif_Kapadia "Asif Kapadia"). The series was produced by [Granada Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada_Television "Granada Television") for [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_(TV_network) "ITV (TV network)"), which has broadcast all of them except _42 Up_ (1998), which was broadcast on [BBC One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_One "BBC One"). Individual films and the series as a whole have received numerous accolades.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

The first film in the series, _Seven Up!_ (1964), was directed by Canadian [Paul Almond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Almond "Paul Almond"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioTimes-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-RadioTimes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) and was commissioned by [Granada Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada_Television "Granada Television") as a programme in the _[World in Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_in_Action "World in Action")_ series.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioTimes-1)[\[2\]](#refnote-RadioTimes) _7 Plus Seven_ until _63 Up_ were directed by [Michael Apted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Apted "Michael Apted"), who had been a researcher on _Seven Up!_ and was involved in finding the original children,[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioTimes-2)[\[2\]](#refnote-RadioTimes) with [Gordon McDougall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_McDougall_(Theatre_Director_&_Academic) "Gordon McDougall (Theatre Director & Academic)").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The film's premise is from the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit "Jesuit") motto "Give me a child until he is seven and I will give you the man".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Slant-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-Slant)[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) The 1998 edition, _42 Up_, was broadcast on [BBC One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_One "BBC One") but still produced by Granada Television.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

The children were selected for the original programme to represent the range of socioeconomic backgrounds in Britain at that time, on the assumption that the children's [social class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class "Social class") would determine their futures.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Obs2019-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-Obs2019) The first instalment was made as a one-off edition of [Granada Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada_Television "Granada Television")'s series _[World in Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_in_Action "World in Action")_, directed by Almond, with involvement by "a fresh-faced young researcher, a middle-class [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") graduate", Apted, whose role in the initial programme included "trawling the nation's schools for 14 suitable subjects".[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioTimes-3)[\[2\]](#refnote-RadioTimes) Of the first programme, Apted said:

> It was Paul's film... but he was more interested in making a beautiful film about being seven, whereas I wanted to make a nasty piece of work about these kids who have it all, and these other kids who have nothing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioTimes-4)[\[2\]](#refnote-RadioTimes)

After Almond directed the original programme, Apted continued the series with new instalments every seven years, filming material from those of the 14 who chose to participate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RadioTimes-5)[\[2\]](#refnote-RadioTimes) The aim of the continuing series is stated at the beginning of _Seven Up_: "We brought these children together because we wanted a glimpse of England in the year 2000. The [shop steward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shop_steward "Shop steward") and the executive of the year 2000 are now seven years old". The ninth instalment, _63 Up_, premiered in the UK on [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_(TV_network) "ITV (TV network)") in 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

A special episode featuring celebrity fans of the series, _7 Up & Me_, also aired on ITV in 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) Apted is reported to have said, "I hope to do _84 Up_ when I'll be 99";[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) however, he died in 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-bakare2026-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-bakare2026)

The series concluded with _70 Up_, a film in two parts, broadcast on 15 and 22 September 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-tvzone-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-tvzone)[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) It is directed by [Asif Kapadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asif_Kapadia "Asif Kapadia"), with all surviving participants contributing to the final film, as well as a recorded interview with participant [Nick Hitchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Hitchon "Nick Hitchon"), who died in 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bakare2026-1)[\[13\]](#refnote-bakare2026)[&#8202;](#ref-back-70_televisual-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-70_televisual)

Two companion documentary specials were also released in 2026, with _70 Up: The Victorians_ using rediscovered and remastered footage of interviews of older people around the time _7 Up_ was first broadcast, and _7 Up: The Story_ exploring the making of the series. These specials aired on 22 and 23 September 2026 respectively.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)

## Participants

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Participants")\]

The subjects are first seen on a group visit to [London Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Zoo "London Zoo") in 1964, where the narrator announces "We brought these 20 children together for the very first time." The series then follows 14 of them: Bruce Balden, Jackie Bassett, Symon Basterfield, Andrew Brackfield, [John Brisby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brisby "John Brisby"), Peter Davies, Susan Davis, [Charles Furneaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Furneaux_(TV_producer) "Charles Furneaux (TV producer)"), [Nicholas Hitchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Nicholas_Hitchon "William Nicholas Hitchon"), [Neil Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Hughes "Neil Hughes"), Lynn Johnson, Paul Kligerman, Suzanne Lusk and Tony Walker.

The participants were chosen in an attempt to represent different social classes in Britain in the 1960s. Apted says in the _42_ commentary track that he was asked to find children at the extremes. Because the show was not originally intended to become a repeating series, no long-term contract was signed with the participants.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] According to Apted, participants in the subsequent programmes since _Seven Up!_ have been paid for their appearance in each instalment, and equal parts of any prize the film may win. Each subject is filmed in about two days. The interview itself takes more than six hours.

Apted later said that it was a poor decision to include only four girls.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)

### Andrew

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Andrew")\]

Andrew Brackfield was one of three boys chosen from the same [pre-preparatory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatory_school_(United_Kingdom)#Pre-prep "Preparatory school (United Kingdom)") in the wealthy London district of [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington "Kensington"), the other two being Charles and John. The three are introduced in _Seven Up!_ singing " [Waltzing Matilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltzing_Matilda "Waltzing Matilda")" in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"). At the age of seven, when asked which newspaper he reads, if any, Andrew said he read _[The Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Financial_Times "The Financial Times")_, though he later revealed he was just repeating what his father had told him when asked the same question. All three could say which [prep schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatory_school_(UK) "Preparatory school (UK)"), [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_(UK)#United_Kingdom "Public school (UK)") and universities they planned to attend, Oxford or Cambridge in all cases. Two named the specific [Oxbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbridge "Oxbridge") [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleges_within_UK_Universities "Colleges within UK Universities") they intended to join.

Andrew's academic career culminated in his studying at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"). Andrew became a [solicitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor "Solicitor"), married and raised a family. He is the only one of the three Kensington boys to have appeared in all the _Up_ films. Both Andrew and his wife, Jane, are most satisfied with how their children have turned out, followed by their relationship. By _70 Up_, Andrew had retired from his legal career. Looking back, he said he wished he had spent more time with his family while his children were growing up.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

### Charles

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Charles")\]

Main article: [Charles Furneaux (TV producer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Furneaux_(TV_producer) "Charles Furneaux (TV producer)")

[Charles Furneaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Furneaux_(TV_producer) "Charles Furneaux (TV producer)") was one of the three boys from the same Kensington pre-preparatory school. He did not get into Oxford, saying in _21_ he was glad to have avoided the "prep school– [Marlborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlborough_College "Marlborough College")– [Oxbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbridge "Oxbridge") conveyor belt" by going to Durham University instead. He later attended Oxford as a postgraduate student. Charles has worked in journalism in varying capacities over the years, including as a producer for the BBC, and in the making of documentary films, including _[Touching the Void](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touching_the_Void_(film) "Touching the Void (film)")_. When contacted to appear in _28_, Charles declined; a subsequent phone conversation during which Apted, by his own admission, "went berserk", destroyed the relationship to the degree that Charles had refused to participate in all of Apted's subsequent films, and even attempted to force Granada to remove archive images of him from the films in which he did not appear.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Pedersen-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-Pedersen)

During an onstage interview at London's [National Film Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI_Southbank "BFI Southbank") in 2005, Apted alleged that Charles had attempted to sue him when he refused to remove Charles from the archive sequences in _49_. Apted also commented on the irony that as a documentary maker himself, Charles was the only one who refused to continue.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

By the time of _63_, all references to Charles had been removed, except for fleeting glimpses of joint shots with Andrew and John. While he did not appear on camera for _70_ and provided photographs of himself in which his face was "deliberately obscured", he provided extensive voiceover comments about the series and his life. He said that while he liked the other two Kensington Kids from _7_, they weren't "mates" in real life and he didn't appreciate being closely linked to them. He added that his main objection to the series and a huge point of conflict between him and Apted was Charles' view of the negative effect of television on perception in modern life.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

### John

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: John")\]

Main article: [John Brisby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brisby "John Brisby")

[John Brisby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brisby "John Brisby") was one of the three boys from the same Kensington pre-preparatory school. He was vocal about politics by the age of 14, attended [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University") and became a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister "Barrister").[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27) He married Claire, the daughter of [Donald Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Logan "Donald Logan"), a former ambassador to [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria "Bulgaria").[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) Brisby devotes himself to charities related to Bulgaria, and hopes to reclaim family land there that had been nationalised. He is a great-great-grandson of the first [Prime Minister of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Bulgaria "Prime Minister of Bulgaria"), [Todor Burmov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todor_Burmov "Todor Burmov").[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)

Brisby said in _35_ that he only does the films to give more publicity to his chosen charities. In _56_, he criticised Apted's decision to originally portray him as part of the "privileged [upper class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_class "Upper class")". He disclosed that his father died when he was 9 and his mother worked to put him through elite private schools. He attended Oxford on a scholarship. In _56_, he remains a litigator who feels very blessed in almost all aspects of his life. In _63_, he calls the series a "poison pill" but says he sees that it has value. In _70_, he reveals that his interviews from _35_ onwards had not been conducted by Apted, which had been a condition for his return to the series.

### Suzy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Suzy")\]

Suzanne (Suzy) Lusk comes from a wealthy background and was first filmed at an independent London day school. Her parents divorced around the time of _7 Plus Seven_. She dropped out of school at 16, deciding to travel to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"). By 21, she had a low opinion of marriage and parenthood, but this soon changed dramatically. By _28_, she was married with two sons, and credited her marriage with bringing her the optimism and happiness that was not evident in the earlier films.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wigley2026-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-Wigley2026)

Her husband, [Rupert Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewey_baronets "Dewey baronets"), is a solicitor in [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_England "Bath, England") and they have three children. She became a [bereavement counsellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grief_counseling "Grief counseling"). In _7 Plus Seven_, she said she thought Apted's project was pointless and silly, a point she restated in _21_. At _49_, she believed she would not participate again, but in _56_, she admitted she felt an obligation to the project regardless of how she felt about it. Suzy did not appear in _63_ aside from footage from previous films.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)

She returned for _70 Up_. Suzy said she had felt "picked on" by the series and that it had misrepresented her life. Reflecting on reaching 70, she said that the deaths of younger friends had made her feel fortunate to be in reasonably good health.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wigley2026-1)[\[30\]](#refnote-Wigley2026)

### Jackie

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Jackie")\]

Jackie Bassett was one of three girls, the others being Lynn and Sue, from the same primary school in a working-class neighbourhood of east London. She eventually went to a [comprehensive school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_school "Comprehensive school") and married at 19. Jackie went through several different jobs, divorced, remarried and moved to Scotland, divorced again and raised her three sons as a single parent. As of _56_, she had been receiving disability benefit for 14 years, due to [rheumatoid arthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheumatoid_arthritis "Rheumatoid arthritis").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2012-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-Grdn2012) Her family remains close and lives near each other in Scotland. In _70 Up_, Jackie is retired and living on the Norfolk coast. She also reflects on her objections to Apted's questions about marriage and sex, which she felt he directed at the women rather than the men.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wigley2026-2)[\[30\]](#refnote-Wigley2026)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-1)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

### Lynn

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Lynn")\]

Lynn Johnson, after attending the same primary school as Jackie and Sue, attended a grammar school. She married at 19, had two daughters, and became a children's librarian at 21. She later became a school librarian and remained in that position until being made redundant due to budget cuts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33) At _56_, she continued to believe her career as a librarian was of great value and it helped define her life. She was a doting grandmother with three grandchildren, and still married to her husband, Russ, whom she considered her soulmate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Obs2019-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-Obs2019)

In 2013, after a short illness, Lynn became the first participant to die. She had served as chair of governors of St Saviour's primary school in [Poplar, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poplar,_London "Poplar, London"), for more than 25 years. A section of its library was renamed in her memory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Lynn-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-Lynn) In _63 Up_, Russ and their daughters discuss her death and its effect on them.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Obs2019-2)[\[7\]](#refnote-Obs2019) _70 Up_ also remembers Lynn; Jackie Bassett speaks about the loss of her childhood friend and fellow participant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wigley2026-3)[\[30\]](#refnote-Wigley2026)

### Sue

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Sue")\]

Susan (Sue) Davis attended the same primary school as Jackie and Lynn, and later a comprehensive school. She married at 24 and had two children before getting divorced. She had been engaged to her current boyfriend, Glenn, for 21 years as of _63_. She worked as a university administrator for [Queen Mary, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary,_University_of_London "Queen Mary, University of London"), despite not having gone to university herself, and is fond of amateur dramatics. By _63_, she was looking forward to retiring in the near future. By _70 Up_, Sue had retired from her university job and moved to Essex. She and Glenn had married.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wigley2026-4)[\[30\]](#refnote-Wigley2026)

### Tony

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Tony")\]

Tony Walker was chosen from a primary school in the [East End of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_of_London "East End of London") and was introduced along with his classmate Michelle, who Douglas Keay, the narrator, described as Tony's "girlfriend". At 7, Michelle described Tony as a "monkey". He wanted to be a jockey at 7 and was at a stable training as one by 14. By 21, his chance had come and gone after riding in three races before giving it up. He was proud to have competed against [Lester Piggott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Piggott "Lester Piggott").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-2)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

He then gained " [the Knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_of_London#Knowledge_of_London "Taxis of London")" and made a comfortable life for himself and his family as a London taxi driver. His later dream of becoming an actor met with modest success; he had small parts as an [extra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_(acting) "Extra (acting)"), almost always playing a cabbie, in several TV programmes since 1986, including _[The Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bill "The Bill")_ and twice in _[EastEnders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EastEnders "EastEnders")_, most recently in 2003.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-3)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026) His wife, Debbie, was carrying their third child in _28_, and she reveals in _35_ that she lost that baby but has since had another. She said that losing their third child placed tremendous stress on their relationship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-4)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

Tony admitted in _35_ that being in a monogamous relationship was becoming a strain, and by _42_, he had committed adultery, though he and his wife had got past it and were still together. By _42_, he had moved to [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex "Essex"), and by _49_, owned two homes, including a holiday home in Spain. In _63_, he and his wife had settled in the English countryside. In _70 Up_, he said that giving up riding was his greatest regret: a mentor had warned him that he would struggle to keep his weight low enough to be a jockey.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-5)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

### Paul

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Paul")\]

Paul Kligerman was at a Shaftesbury Homes [children's home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_home "Children's home") in [Twickenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham "Twickenham") at 7, his parents having divorced and he having been left with his father. Soon after _Seven Up!_, his father and stepmother moved the family to Australia, where he has remained in the [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne") area ever since. By 21, he had long hair and a girlfriend to whom he is still married.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Obs2019-3)[\[7\]](#refnote-Obs2019) After leaving school he was employed as a bricklayer and later set up his own business.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-6)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

In _49_, he was working for a sign-making company. In _21_, _49_ and _63_, Paul was reunited with Symon, who had attended the same children's home. Portions of their time together are included in all three films. By _56_, Paul had started work at a local retirement village with his wife Susan. He does odd jobs and maintenance of the small units and gardens. By _70 Up_, Paul was a grandfather and continued to live in Australia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-7)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

### Symon

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Symon")\]

Symon Basterfield, given name also spelt Simon in previous films, chosen from the same Shaftesbury Homes [children's home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_home "Children's home") in Twickenham as Paul, is the only mixed-race participant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2012-1)[\[32\]](#refnote-Grdn2012) He never got to know his [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people "Black people") father, and had left the orphanage in Twickenham to live with his [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people "White people") mother by the time of the _7 Plus Seven_ filming. Her [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)") is alluded to as the cause for his being in the home. As filming for _35_ took place, he was going through a divorce from his first wife and mother of his five children, and he elected not to participate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-8)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

Symon returned for _42_ and _49_, remarried with one son and one stepdaughter. In _49_, he and his wife had become foster parents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2012-2)[\[32\]](#refnote-Grdn2012) By _56_, he regretted his lack of formal education, which he felt limited his income over the years. He remained happily married and looked forward to the next chapters of his life. In _63_, his relationship with his children from his first marriage is mending and he has 10 grandchildren. In _70 Up_, Symon says that he and his second wife have fostered more than 100 children over approximately 20 years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-9)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

### Nick

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Nick")\]

Main article: [William Nicholas Hitchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Nicholas_Hitchon "William Nicholas Hitchon")

[William Nicholas "Nick" Hitchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Nicholas_Hitchon "William Nicholas Hitchon") was born on 22 October 1957 and raised on a small farm in [Arncliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arncliffe,_North_Yorkshire "Arncliffe, North Yorkshire"), a tiny village in the [Yorkshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales "Yorkshire Dales"). He was only 6 when _Seven Up!_ was first broadcast, and always remained a year younger than the episodes' nominal age.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rfhobit-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-rfhobit) He was educated in a [one-room school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-room_school "One-room school") four miles' walk from his home, and later at a boarding school. He went to [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University"), where, he mentions in _63 Up_, [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May "Theresa May") was a contemporary, and then moved to the United States to work as a nuclear physicist.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rfhobit-1)[\[35\]](#refnote-rfhobit)

He married Jackie, another British immigrant, who participated in _28_ but was displeased with how her comments were received by viewers, many of whom apparently concluded that the marriage was doomed. She declined to appear in _35_ and _42_. By _49_, the couple had divorced and Nick had remarried, this time to Cryss Brunner, ten years his senior, and at that time taught in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"). Nick was a professor at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison") in the Electrical and Computer Engineering Department from 1982 to 2022, serving as department chair from 1999 to 2002.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)[&#8202;](#ref-back-rfhobit-2)[\[35\]](#refnote-rfhobit)

He appeared as a guest on [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Radio "National Public Radio")'s quiz show _[Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wait_Wait..._Don't_Tell_Me "Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me")_ aired 21 June 2014, and spoke briefly about his participation in the _Up_ series.[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38) By _63_, he had developed a cancerous mass in his throat and had lost his father, leading him to contemplate mortality.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Obs2019-4)[\[7\]](#refnote-Obs2019) Nick retired from the university in spring 2022[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39) and died on 23 July 2023 at the age of 65, before which he filmed a final interview for _70_. It was described by ITV as "poignant and moving".[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40)

### Peter

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Peter")\]

Peter Davies went to the same middle-class [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool "Liverpool") suburban school as Neil, who, like Peter, wanted to be an astronaut. Peter drifted through university, and by 28 was an underpaid and seemingly uninspired school teacher. Peter dropped out of the series after _28_, following a [tabloid press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_press "Tabloid press") campaign against him after he criticised the government of [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher "Margaret Thatcher") in his interview. The director's commentary for _42_ revealed that he later divorced, took up study of the law, became a lawyer, remarried, had children and moved back to Liverpool. He returned in _56_ to promote his band, the Liverpool-based country-influenced The Good Intentions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41) The group was still together, although one member had died, in _63_.

By _70 Up_, Peter was working full-time as a writer and musician. He said that appearing in the series had helped promote both the band's records and his writing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Wigley2026-5)[\[30\]](#refnote-Wigley2026)

### Neil

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Neil")\]

Main article: [Neil Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Hughes "Neil Hughes")

[Neil Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Hughes "Neil Hughes"), from a [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool "Liverpool") suburb, turned out to be perhaps the most unpredictable of the group.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2019-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-Grdn2019) At seven he was a happy child, funny and full of life and hope, but by _7 Plus Seven_, he was nervous and stressed. By _21_, he was living in a [squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting "Squatting") in London, having dropped out of [Aberdeen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_University "Aberdeen University") after one term, and was finding work as he could on building sites. During the interview, he was in an agitated state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2019-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-Grdn2019)

At 28, he was still homeless, although now in Scotland. By 35, he was living in a [council house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_house "Council house") in the [Shetland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shetland_Islands "Shetland Islands"), writing and appearing in the local [pantomime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantomime "Pantomime"). By _42_, he was living in Bruce's apartment in London, and Bruce had become a source of emotional support.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2019-2)[\[42\]](#refnote-Grdn2019) Neil was involved in local council politics, as a [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_(UK) "Liberal Democrats (UK)") in the [London Borough of Hackney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hackney "London Borough of Hackney"), and had completed a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree from the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University "Open University").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2019-3)[\[42\]](#refnote-Grdn2019) In 1996, he was elected to Wick ward on [Hackney London Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackney_London_Borough_Council "Hackney London Borough Council"), and resigned his seat in 2000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43)[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)

By _49_, he was a district councillor in the [Eden district of Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden,_Cumbria "Eden, Cumbria").[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45) He was elected for [Shap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shap "Shap") on [Eden District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_District "Eden District") in 2003.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46) He was a candidate for Eden Lakes on [Cumbria County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbria_County_Council "Cumbria County Council") in 2005 and 2009, coming second to the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") candidate on both occasions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47) In 2013, following new division boundaries, Neil was elected to Eden Lakes, and did not stand again for Shap. He was re-elected to Eden Lakes in 2017.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49)

He stood as the Liberal Democrat candidate for [Stockton North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_North_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Stockton North (UK Parliament constituency)") in the [2005 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_United_Kingdom_general_election "2005 United Kingdom general election") and for [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Carlisle (UK Parliament constituency)") in the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election "2010 United Kingdom general election"), finishing third on both occasions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50)[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51) Neil stood for [Penrith and the Border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrith_and_The_Border_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Penrith and The Border (UK Parliament constituency)") – which covers the same area he represents as a councillor – at the [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_United_Kingdom_general_election "2015 United Kingdom general election") and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_United_Kingdom_general_election "2017 United Kingdom general election") general elections. In 2015, he came fourth, whilst in 2017, he came third. At the [2019 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_United_Kingdom_general_election "2019 United Kingdom general election"), Hughes contested the [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)")–Conservative [marginal seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_seat "Marginal seat") of [Workington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workington_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Workington (UK Parliament constituency)") in Cumbria. Finishing fourth, he increased the party's vote share, but lost his deposit.

By _63 Up_, Neil had married, but he and his wife had separated. He served as a lay reader in the Anglican Church and maintained a home in France.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2019-4)[\[42\]](#refnote-Grdn2019) By _70 Up_, he was living alone in France. He said that he sometimes felt lonely but was as happy as he could be given his circumstances. Neil welcomed the change of director for the final film, saying that it gave him a chance to present aspects of himself he felt had been missing from earlier instalments.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Conlan-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-Conlan)

### Bruce

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Bruce")\]

Bruce Balden, as a child, was concerned with poverty and racial discrimination and wanted to become a missionary. He was attending a private boarding school. At 7, he said his greatest desire was to see his father, a soldier in [Southern Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Rhodesia "Southern Rhodesia"), and he seemed brave though a little abandoned. In _21_, Bruce was studying mathematics at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University"). After graduation, he worked in an insurance company for a short term.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2012-3)[\[32\]](#refnote-Grdn2012)

He then began to teach maths at a school in the [East End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_of_London "East End of London") of London. He said that he enjoyed being a part of people's advancement. In _35_, he was taking a sabbatical leave and teaching maths and English in [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet "Sylhet"), [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), as well as helping teachers design courses. He returned to London and continued teaching in the East End.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2012-4)[\[32\]](#refnote-Grdn2012)

Just before _42_, he married a fellow teacher: Apted filmed the wedding, which was also attended by Neil. Eventually becoming worn down by teaching in the East End, Bruce got a teaching position at [St Albans School, Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Albans_School,_Hertfordshire "St Albans School, Hertfordshire"), a prestigious [public school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_(United_Kingdom) "Public school (United Kingdom)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2012-5)[\[32\]](#refnote-Grdn2012)

Between _42_ and _49_, he had two sons. In _56_, he admitted he still had a hard time expressing his innermost feelings, in particular to his wife, but was a happily devoted father and husband. Still teaching at St Albans School with his wife, he had no regrets about the development of his career.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grdn2012-6)[\[32\]](#refnote-Grdn2012) By _70 Up_, Bruce had retired from teaching. He sang in a local choir and volunteered with the [Royal National Lifeboat Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Lifeboat_Institution "Royal National Lifeboat Institution").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Mangan2026-10)[\[21\]](#refnote-Mangan2026)

## Future

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Future")\]

The producers have called _70 Up_ the final instalment of the series. When Apted died in 2021, the series was thought to have ended. Producer Claire Lewis was contacted by participant Nick Hitchon, who was terminally ill and asked to record a final interview. After that interview, the team decided to make a final film. Lewis has expressed interest in making separate films about individual participants using the series' archive, for which she seeks funding.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Conlan-1)[\[52\]](#refnote-Conlan)

## Motifs

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Motifs")\]

Several themes have appeared repeatedly over the course of the series. Questions about religion, family, class, happiness and psychological state dominate many of the interviews, and inquiries about the worries and concerns subjects have for their future.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ebert-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-Ebert) Questions often take a personal tone, with Apted noting that viewers often respond to his questioning of Neil's sanity or his perception of Tony's success in life as too personal, but that he has been able to do this because of the friendship he has developed with the subjects.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ebert2-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-ebert2)\[ _[full citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources#What_information_to_include "Wikipedia:Citing sources")_\]

## Critical response and accolades

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Critical response and accolades")\]

The series has received high praise over the years. [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") called it "an inspired, even noble, use of the film medium", saying the films "penetrate to the central mystery of life" and that the series is among his top ten films of all time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ebert-1)[\[53\]](#refnote-Ebert)

The _Up_ series has been criticised by both [ethnographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography "Ethnography") and the subjects themselves for its editing style. [Mitchell Duneier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Duneier "Mitchell Duneier") pointed out that Apted asserted causal relationships between a character's past and present that might not exist.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-59) Apted acknowledged this, pointing out that in _21 Up_ he believed Tony would soon be in prison, so he filmed him around dangerous areas for use in later films.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-60) Apted also portrayed the troubled marriage of Nick earlier in the film, although his time frame for anticipating their divorce was premature. Apted said in interviews that his "tendency to play God" with the interviews was "foolishness and wrong".[&#8202;](#ref-back-ebert2-1)[\[54\]](#refnote-ebert2) In _21 Up_, the female participants were offended that all the questions concerned domestic affairs, marriage and children, rather than politics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-61)

In _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_, [Rebecca Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Mead "Rebecca Mead") wrote, "\[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[Apted\] can be unbearably patronizing toward his subjects, particularly the working-class women, while he sets his more affluent participants up to look ludicrous". But, she added: "To his credit, Apted has shown participants arguing back against the show's premise and against his own prejudices. One of the most exhilarating moments in the series occurs in _49 Up_, when Jackie \[...\] rounds on Apted, castigating him for his decades of underestimating her. Apted's implied humility is ultimately, if belatedly, Jackie's vindication."[\[58\]](#refnote-62)

In 1984, _28 Up_ (the then-latest instalment) was included on [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert")'s list of the ten greatest films of all time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-63)

Apted won an Institutional [Peabody Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Award "Peabody Award") in 2012 for his work on the _Up_ series.[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-64) In a list of the [100 Greatest British Television Programmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Greatest_British_Television_Programmes "100 Greatest British Television Programmes") drawn up by the [British Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Film_Institute "British Film Institute") in 2000, voted for by industry professionals, _28 Up_ placed 26th.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-65) In 2024, _Up_ was voted number 1 in a list of the most influential shows from the previous 50 years compiled by the [Broadcasting Press Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting_Press_Guild "Broadcasting Press Guild").[&#8202;](#ref-back-bakare2026-2)[\[13\]](#refnote-bakare2026)[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-66)

## Influence

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Influence")\]

### On participants

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: On participants")\]

Over the course of the project the programme has in varying degrees had a direct effect on the lives of its participants. The series participants often speak of the series having become sufficiently popular that they were recognised in public. For instance, in _56 Up_, Tony related an anecdote about giving a ride to [Buzz Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Aldrin "Buzz Aldrin"), and being surprised when a passerby asks him, not Aldrin, for an autograph.[&#8202;](#ref-back-pedersen-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-pedersen)

The participants' opinions regarding being involved in the series are often mentioned, and have varied greatly among the participants. John refers to the programme as a poison pill that he is subjected to every seven years, while Paul's wife credits the series for keeping their marriage together. Apted commented that one of the big surprises between filming _42 Up_ and _49 Up_ was the impact of [reality television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_television "Reality television")—meaning that the subjects wanted to talk about their contribution to the series in the light of this genre.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

There have also been instances of the interactions of participants being engineered by the programme's producers. Paul and Nick were flown back to England at Granada's expense for the filming of _35 Up_ and _42 Up_ respectively. Paul was flown back again for _49 Up_ and visited Symon. Symon and his wife were flown to Australia to visit Paul in _63 Up_.

Bruce was affected by Neil's plight and offered him temporary shelter in his home shortly before _42 Up_, allowing Neil time to get settled in London. Despite Neil's eccentricities during his two-month stay, they clearly remained friends, with Neil later giving a reading at Bruce's wedding. In _56 Up_, Suzy and Nick are interviewed together, having become friends owing to their shared rural upbringing.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Cultural and broader influences

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Up_(film_series)&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Cultural and broader influences")\]

The original hypothesis of _Seven Up!_ was that [class structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_structure "Class structure") is so strong in the UK that a person's life path would be set at birth. The producer of the original programme had at one point thought to line the children up on the street, have three of them step forward and narrate "of these twenty children, only three will be successful", an idea which was not used. The idea of class immobility held up in most, but not all, cases as the series has progressed. The children from the working classes have by and large remained in those circles, though Tony seems to have become more middle class. Apted said that one of his regrets was that they did not take feminism into account, and consequently had fewer girls in their study and did not select them on the basis of any possible careers they might choose.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Although it began as a political documentary, the series has become a film of human nature and [existentialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existentialism "Existentialism"). In the director's commentary for _42 Up_, Apted comments that he did not realise the series had changed tone from political to personal until _21 Up_, when he showed the film to American friends who encouraged him to submit it successfully to American film festivals. Apted said that this realisation was a relief to him and allowed the films to breathe a little more.[&#8202;](#ref-back-pedersen-1)[\[63\]](#refnote-pedersen)

The series has also been satirised. A 2007 episode of _[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")_, " [Springfield Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield_Up "Springfield Up")", is narrated by an [Apted-like filmmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declan_Desmond "Declan Desmond") who depicts the past and current lives of a group of [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield_(The_Simpsons) "Springfield (The Simpsons)") residents he has revisited every eight years. The "37 Up" segment of _[Tracey Ullman: A Class Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracey_Ullman:_A_Class_Act "Tracey Ullman: A Class Act")_, first aired in 1992, parodies the series. [Harry Enfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Enfield "Harry Enfield") parodied the series in a spoof titled "2 Up" with his characters [Tim Nice-but-Dim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Enfield_&_Chums#Tim_Nice-but-Dim_Esq. "Harry Enfield & Chums") and [Wayne Slob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Enfield_&_Chums#The_Slobs_(Wayne_and_Waynetta) "Harry Enfield & Chums"). The Australian comedy TV series _[The Late Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_(1992_TV_series) "The Late Show (1992 TV series)")_ satirised the series with a version in which participants were interviewed every seven minutes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-68)
