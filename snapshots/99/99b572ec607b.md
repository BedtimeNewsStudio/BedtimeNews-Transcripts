XIX Olympic Winter Games

The **2002 Winter Olympics**, officially the **XIX Olympic Winter Games** and commonly known as **Salt Lake 2002** ( [Arapaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arapaho_language "Arapaho language"): _Niico'ooowu' 2002_; [Gosiute Shoshoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosiute_dialect "Gosiute dialect"): _Tit'-so-pi 2002_; [Navajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_language "Navajo language"): _Sooléí 2002_; [Shoshoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshoni_language "Shoshoni language"): _Soónkahni 2002_), were an international winter [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event "Multi-sport event") that was held from February 8 to 24, 2002, in and around [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City "Salt Lake City"), Utah, United States.

Salt Lake City was selected as the host city in June 1995 at the 104th [IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOC_Session "IOC Session"). They were the eighth Olympics to be hosted by the United States, and the most recent to be held in the country until 2028, when Los Angeles hosts the [Games of the XXXIV Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Summer_Olympics "2028 Summer Olympics"). The 2002 Winter Olympics and [Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Paralympic_Winter_Games "2002 Paralympic Winter Games") were both organized by the [Salt Lake Organizing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_Organizing_Committee_for_the_Olympic_and_Paralympic_Winter_Games_of_2002 "Salt Lake Organizing Committee for the Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games of 2002") (SLOC), the first time that both events were organized by a single committee, and inspiring other Olympic and Paralympic Games to be organized by such since then.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLOC_Official_Report_p35-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-SLOC_Official_Report_p35) These were the first Olympic Games under the [International Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympic_Committee "International Olympic Committee") (IOC) [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee "President of the International Olympic Committee") of [Jacques Rogge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Rogge "Jacques Rogge").

The Games featured 2,399 athletes from 78 nations, participating in 78 events in 15 disciplines. Norway topped the medal table, with 13 gold and 25 medals overall, while Germany finished with the most total medals, winning 36 (with 12 of them gold). The hosting United States was third by gold medals and second by overall medals, with 10 and 34 respectively. Australia notably became the first [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere") country to ever win gold medals at the Winter Olympics.

The Games finished with a budgetary surplus of US$40 million; the surplus was used to fund the formation of the Utah Athletic Foundation—which has continued to maintain the facilities built for these Olympics. The venues have continued to be used for national and international winter sports events after the Olympics, leading to the Winter Olympics return to Salt Lake City for the [2034 games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_Winter_Olympics "2034 Winter Olympics"), 32 years after the city played host in 2002.

## Host city selection

Main article: [Bids for the 2002 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2002_Winter_Olympics "Bids for the 2002 Winter Olympics")

Salt Lake City was chosen over Québec City, Canada; Sion, Switzerland; and Östersund, Sweden, on June 16, 1995, at the 104th [IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOC_Session "IOC Session") in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest "Budapest"), Hungary.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-3) Salt Lake City had previously come in second during the bids for the [1998 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Winter_Olympics "1998 Winter Olympics"), awarded to [Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano,_Nagano "Nagano, Nagano"), Japan, and had offered to be the provisional host of the [1976 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Olympics "1976 Winter Olympics") when the original host, [Denver, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver,_Colorado "Denver, Colorado"), withdrew. The 1976 Winter Olympics were ultimately awarded to [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck "Innsbruck"), Austria.

There was a [scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympic_bid_scandal "2002 Winter Olympic bid scandal") involving allegations of bribery used to win the rights to the Games. Prior to its successful bid, Salt Lake City had attempted four times to secure the games, failing each time. In 1998, members of the [International Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympic_Committee "International Olympic Committee") (IOC) were accused of taking gifts from the [Salt Lake Organizing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_Organizing_Committee_for_the_Olympic_and_Paralympic_Winter_Games_of_2002 "Salt Lake Organizing Committee for the Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games of 2002") (SLOC) during the bidding process. The allegations resulted in the expulsion of several IOC members and the adoption of new IOC rules. Although nothing strictly illegal had been done, it was felt that the acceptance of the gifts was morally dubious. In addition, legal charges were brought against the leaders of Salt Lake's bid committee by the [United States Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice "United States Department of Justice").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NY_Times_dec2003-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-NY_Times_dec2003) Investigations were also launched into prior bidding process by other cities, finding that members of the IOC received bribes during the bidding process for both the [1998 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Winter_Olympics "1998 Winter Olympics") and [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics "2000 Summer Olympics").[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-5) In response to the scandal, [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney") was hired as the new president and CEO of the Salt Lake Organizing Committee in February 1999.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-7)

## Development and preparation

### Budget

In February 1999, in response to the [bid scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympic_bid_scandal "2002 Winter Olympic bid scandal") and a financial shortfall for the Games, [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney"), then CEO of the private equity firm [Bain Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bain_Capital "Bain Capital") (and future [U.S. presidential candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election "2012 United States presidential election"), [U.S. Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senator "U.S. Senator"), and [Governor of Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Massachusetts "Governor of Massachusetts")), was [hired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney#2002_Winter_Olympics "Mitt Romney") as the new president and CEO of the [Salt Lake Organizing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_Organizing_Committee_for_the_Olympic_and_Paralympic_Winter_Games_of_2002 "Salt Lake Organizing Committee for the Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games of 2002").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-:0) Romney, Kem C. Gardner, a Utah commercial real estate developer, and Don Stirling, the Olympics' local marketing chief, raised "millions of dollars from Mormon families with pioneer roots: the Eccles family, whose forebears were important industrialists and bankers" to help rescue the Games, according to a later report.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-9) An additional [US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") 410million was received from the federal government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-10) U.S. federal subsidies amounted to $1.3billion (for infrastructure improvements only), compared to $45billion of federal funding received by the organizing committee of the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics "2014 Winter Olympics") from the Russian government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-The_Guardian-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-The_Guardian)[&#8202;](#ref-back-GAO-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-GAO) The Games were financially successful, raising more money with fewer sponsors than any prior Olympic Games, which left SLOC with a surplus of [$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD "USD") 40million. The surplus was used to create the Utah Athletic Foundation, which maintains and operates many of the remaining Olympic venues.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Marketing_Matters_6_2002-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-Marketing_Matters_6_2002)

_The Oxford Olympics Study_ established the outturn cost of the Salt Lake City 2002 Winter Olympics at US$2.5billion in 2015-dollars and cost overrun at 24% in real terms.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-14) This includes sports-related costs only, that is, (i) _operational costs_ incurred by the organizing committee to stage the Games, e.g., expenditures for technology, transportation, workforce, administration, security, catering, ceremonies, and medical services, and (ii) _direct capital costs_ incurred by the host city and country or private investors to build, e.g., the competition venues, the Olympic village, international broadcast center, and media and press center, which are required to host the Games. Indirect capital costs are _not_ included, such as for road, rail, or airport infrastructure, or for hotel upgrades or other business investment incurred in preparation for the Games but not directly related to staging the Games. The cost and cost overrun for Salt Lake City 2002 compares with costs of US$2.5billion and a cost overrun of 13% for Vancouver 2010, and costs of US$51billion[&#8202;](#ref-back-The_Guardian-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-The_Guardian) and a cost overrun of 289% for Sochi 2014, the latter being the most costly Olympics to date. The average cost for Winter Games since 1960 is US$3.1billion, average cost overrun is 142%.

### Security

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/WinterOlympicsMP2002.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:WinterOlympicsMP2002.png) Spc. Patrick Jean-Mary, of Warwick, R.I., inspects two forms of identification during the 2002 Winter Olympic Games in Salt Lake City

The 2002 Winter Games were the first Olympic Games to take place since the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"), which meant a higher level of security than ever before provided for the games. As a result, the [Office of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Homeland_Security "Office of Homeland Security") (OHS) designated the Olympics a [National Special Security Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Special_Security_Event "National Special Security Event") (NSSE). Aerial surveillance and radar control was provided by the [U.S. Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marine_Corps "U.S. Marine Corps") of Marine Air Control Squadron 2, Detachment C, from [Cherry Point, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Point,_North_Carolina "Cherry Point, North Carolina"). The [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") and NSA arranged with [Qwest Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qwest "Qwest") to use intercept equipment for a period of less than six months around the time of the 2002 Winter Olympics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-15)

### Transportation

The largest [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport "Public transport") project completed for the Games was the [TRAX light rail system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRAX_(light_rail) "TRAX (light rail)"), which first began operations ahead of the Games in 1999.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-16)[&#8202;](#ref-back-2linesopen-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-2linesopen) The system had two operational lines during the Games to provide service to Downtown Salt Lake City and [Rice–Eccles Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice%E2%80%93Eccles_Stadium "Rice–Eccles Stadium") on the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah") campus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-18) The local organizing committee also organized 1,000 buses to transport athletes, workers, and spectators between venues; 3,800 transit bus drivers and mechanics were recruited from across the United States to run the system.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-19)

To help reduce vehicle traffic to Soldier Hollow, and to provide a tourist attraction for attendees, [Heber Valley Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heber_Valley_Railroad "Heber Valley Railroad") offered service to [Wasatch Mountain State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasatch_Mountain_State_Park "Wasatch Mountain State Park") on [steam locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotives "Steam locomotives") during the Games. After arriving, passengers then embarked to Soldier Hollow on horse-drawn [sleighs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sled "Sled").[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-auto)[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-21)

### Venues

Main article: [Venues of the 2002 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venues_of_the_2002_Winter_Olympics "Venues of the 2002 Winter Olympics")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Slcolympics.jpg/500px-Slcolympics.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Slcolympics.jpg) Salt Lake City during the 2002 Winter Olympics

Work on venues for the 2002 Winter Olympics began as early as 1989, following the passing of a state referendum that authorized the use of taxpayer money to publicly fund the construction of new facilities for a Winter Olympics bid in 1998 or 2002. Their construction was overseen by the Salt Lake Olympic Bid Committee and the Utah Sports Authority—a body created under the referendum.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-23)

New facilities built for the Games included the [Utah Olympic Oval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Olympic_Oval "Utah Olympic Oval") in [Kearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kearns,_Utah "Kearns, Utah"), [Utah Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Olympic_Park "Utah Olympic Park") in [Summit County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_County,_Utah "Summit County, Utah"), [The Ice Sheet at Ogden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ice_Sheet_at_Ogden "The Ice Sheet at Ogden"), and [Soldier Hollow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier_Hollow "Soldier Hollow") at the [Wasatch Mountain State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasatch_Mountain_State_Park "Wasatch Mountain State Park")—the furthest competition venue from Salt Lake City.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-auto) The [E Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_Center "E Center") in [West Valley City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Valley_City,_Utah "West Valley City, Utah")[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-24) and the [Peaks Ice Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaks_Ice_Arena "Peaks Ice Arena") in [Provo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provo,_Utah "Provo, Utah") were also built with support from the SLOC, and co-hosted hockey.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-26)

[Delta Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Center "Delta Center") hosted figure skating and short track speed skating; it was renamed _Salt Lake Ice Center_ for the duration of the Games due to IOC sponsorship rules.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:02-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-:02) [Rice-Eccles Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice-Eccles_Stadium "Rice-Eccles Stadium") at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah") hosted the opening and closing ceremonies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-:1) The [Olympic Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Village "Olympic Village") was built at historic [Fort Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Douglas "Fort Douglas"), whose land had been acquired by the University of Utah to construct new residences. The SLOC provided funding to the project in exchange for its use during the Olympics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-29)

Main Street in [Park City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_City,_Utah "Park City, Utah") was converted into a pedestrian plaza during the Games, with festivities such as concerts, firework shows, and sponsor presences.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-31) Medal presentations took place in downtown Salt Lake City; the stage for the ceremony featured the [Hoberman Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoberman_Arch "Hoberman Arch"), an arch-shaped metal "curtain" designed by [Chuck Hoberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Hoberman "Chuck Hoberman").[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLT_unveiling-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-SLT_unveiling)

### 2002 Cultural Olympiad

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Gnome-mime-sound-openclipart.svg/120px-Gnome-mime-sound-openclipart.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

["Call of the Champions"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Call_of_the_champions_sample.ogg "File:Call of the champions sample.ogg")

composed by [John Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Williams "John Williams") for the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City. Performed by the [Mormon Tabernacle Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_Tabernacle_Choir "Mormon Tabernacle Choir") and the [Utah Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Symphony_Orchestra "Utah Symphony Orchestra"). The choir sings the Olympic motto _"Citius, Altius, Fortius"_ which is Latin for "Faster, Higher, Stronger."[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-42)

The 2002 Cultural Olympiad, which ran from January to March 2002,[&#8202;](#ref-back-prog-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-prog) was an arts festival that accompanied the 2002 Winter Olympics. It included specially commissioned works, such as [Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Ailey_American_Dance_Theater "Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater")'s work _Here...Now_ inspired by the life of Olympian [Florence Griffith Joyner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Griffith_Joyner "Florence Griffith Joyner"), accompanied by music by [Wynton Marsalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wynton_Marsalis "Wynton Marsalis").[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-44) An art exhibition was held at [Springville Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springville_Museum_of_Art "Springville Museum of Art") that celebrated 150 years of Utah's art history.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-45) The programme also featured many other performances, including dance, theatre, singing, and literature, as well as the glass art of [Dale Chihuly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Chihuly "Dale Chihuly").[&#8202;](#ref-back-prog-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-prog)

### Olympic Music

Call of the Champions is a cantata-fanfare for orchestra and choir composed by [John Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Williams "John Williams") for the 2002 Winter Olympics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Filmtracks-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-Filmtracks) Premiering at the Opening Ceremony on February 8, 2002, it began with the call by the [Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabernacle_Choir_at_Temple_Square "Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square") of "Citius! Altius! Fortius!" (Faster, Higher, Stronger), which is the Olympic Motto chosen by the founder of the modern Games, Baron [Pierre de Coubertin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_de_Coubertin "Pierre de Coubertin"). Williams added another Latin word as well: "Clarius," meaning "clearer."

### Torch relay

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Salt_Lake_2002_torch_cu.jpg/330px-Salt_Lake_2002_torch_cu.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Salt_Lake_2002_torch_cu.jpg)

Detail of the 2002 Winter Olympic Torch

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/57/Olympic_torch.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Olympic_torch.jpg)

Torchbearer in Olympic [livery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livery "Livery")

Main article: [2002 Winter Olympics torch relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics_torch_relay "2002 Winter Olympics torch relay")

The torch relay ceremonially began on November 19, 2001, with the traditional kindling of an Olympic flame in Olympia, Greece.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-47) On December 3, Greek skier Thanassis Tsailas lit the first torch from the cauldron, and transferred its flame to a ceremonial lantern for transport to Atlanta, where it arrived on December 4 to officially launch the U.S. leg of the relay.[&#8202;](#ref-back-KSL_torch_relay-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-KSL_torch_relay)[&#8202;](#ref-back-KSL_arrival-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-KSL_arrival) The route of the relay covered 13,500 miles (21,700km), passed through 300 communities and 46 U.S. states, and was carried by 12,012 torchbearers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLOC_Official_Report_route_plan-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-SLOC_Official_Report_route_plan)

The torch was modeled after an icicle, with a slight curve to represent speed and fluidity. The torch measures 33 inches (84cm) long, 3 inches (7.6cm) wide at the top, 0.5 inches (1.3cm) at the bottom, and was designed by Axiom Design of Salt Lake City.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLOC_torch_info-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-SLOC_torch_info)[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-52) It was created with three sections, each with its own meaning and representation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLOC_torch_info-1)[\[50\]](#refnote-SLOC_torch_info)

## Marketing

### Branding & Emblem

The overall branding of the 2002 Winter Olympics, the "Look of the Games" design was named "Land of Contrast — Fire and Ice", which featured a palette of warm and cool colors to contrast the warmer, rugged, [red-rock areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Plateau "Colorado Plateau") of Southern Utah from the colder, mountainous regions of Northern Utah.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLOC_Official_Report_designs-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-SLOC_Official_Report_designs)
The three main color palettes were: _Amber Gold_, _Sunset Orange_ and _Mountain Shadow Blue_: With accented hue variations of the palette.[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-54)

The emblem was named "Crystal Rhythm". It featured a six pointed [snow crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake "Snowflake"), again with the three main colors representing Utahs southern deserts and its high snowy mountain ranges. Designed to resemble an Olympic cauldron, a flame, as well as a sun rising from behind mountains. The orange center section of the flame was intended to reflect traditional [Navajo weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_weaving "Navajo weaving").[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLOC_Official_Report_designs-1)[\[52\]](#refnote-SLOC_Official_Report_designs) The emblem logo was designed by [Landor Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landor_Associates "Landor Associates") and unveiled in August 1997.

The official event pictograms were inspired by [branding irons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branding_irons "Branding irons"), and the line thickness and 30-degree angles mirrored those of the emblem.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SLOC_Official_Report_designs-2)[\[52\]](#refnote-SLOC_Official_Report_designs)

### Mascots

Main article: [Powder, Copper and Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powder,_Copper_and_Coal "Powder, Copper and Coal")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/3/39/Salt_Lake_2002_Olympic_Mascots.svg/500px-Salt_Lake_2002_Olympic_Mascots.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Salt_Lake_2002_Olympic_Mascots.svg) [Powder, Copper and Coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powder,_Copper,_Coal_and_Otto "Powder, Copper, Coal and Otto"), the 2002 Olympic mascots

The designs of the mascots of the 2002 Winter Olympics were unveiled on May 19, 1999, during an event marking 1,000 days until the opening ceremony.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-55) The mascots represent three animals native to the [western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States "Western United States") — a [snowshoe hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowshoe_hare "Snowshoe hare"), [coyote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyote "Coyote"), and [American black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_black_bear "American black bear") respectively, with each mascot symbolizing a character from the legends of local Native Americans, and wearing a charm around their neck with an original [Anasazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_Puebloans "Ancestral Puebloans") or [Fremont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_culture "Fremont culture")-style [petroglyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph "Petroglyph"). For the first time in Olympic history, the names of the mascots were determined by a public vote, using name suggestions submitted by local students; on September 25, the names of the mascots were officially announced as Powder, Copper, and Coal respectively.[&#8202;](#ref-back-DN_names_announced-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-DN_names_announced)

## The Games

### Opening ceremony

Main article: [2002 Winter Olympics opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics_opening_ceremony "2002 Winter Olympics opening ceremony")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/2002_Winter_Olympics_flame.jpg/500px-2002_Winter_Olympics_flame.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2002_Winter_Olympics_flame.jpg) The Olympic cauldron being lit by the 1980 U.S. men's hockey team.[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/US_Navy_020208-N-3995K-002_2002_Olympics_-_WTC_Flag.jpg/500px-US_Navy_020208-N-3995K-002_2002_Olympics_-_WTC_Flag.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:US_Navy_020208-N-3995K-002_2002_Olympics_-_WTC_Flag.jpg) Members of the US Olympic team at the [Rice–Eccles Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice%E2%80%93Eccles_Stadium "Rice–Eccles Stadium") holding the American flag that flew over the World Trade Center on September 11, 2001

The opening ceremony of the 2002 Winter Olympics was held at Rice–Eccles Stadium at the University of Utah on February 8, 2002. The facility was renovated and expanded for the Games.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[27\]](#refnote-:1) The Games were officially opened by President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush"), who was standing among the US athletes (previous heads of state opened the Games from an official box), while the Olympic cauldron was lit by members of the gold medal-winning [U.S. men's ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_ice_hockey_team "United States men's national ice hockey team") from the [1980 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1980_Winter_Olympics "Ice hockey at the 1980 Winter Olympics") in [Lake Placid, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Placid,_New_York "Lake Placid, New York") (as made famous by the " [Miracle on Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_on_Ice "Miracle on Ice")").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-:2)

In an acknowledgment of the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"), the ceremony opened with the entrance of a damaged [American flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_United_States "Flag of the United States") recovered from the wreckage of the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_(1973%E2%80%932001) "World Trade Center (1973–2001)"), carried by an [honor guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_of_honour "Guard of honour") of police officers from the [Port Authority of New York and New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey "Port Authority of New York and New Jersey"), the [New York City Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Police_Department "New York City Police Department"), and firefighters from the [New York City Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Fire_Department "New York City Fire Department"), joined by athletes nominated by fellow members of the U.S. team. The flag was presented during the playing of the U.S. national anthem " [The Star-Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner "The Star-Spangled Banner")", as performed by the [Tabernacle Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabernacle_Choir "Tabernacle Choir").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-1)[\[58\]](#refnote-:2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-60)[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-61)[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-62)

[Jacques Rogge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Rogge "Jacques Rogge"), presiding over his first Olympics as the IOC president, told the athletes of the United States:[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-63)

> Your nation is overcoming a horrific tragedy, a tragedy that has affected the whole world. We stand united with you in the promotion of our common ideals and hope for world peace.

The Olympic cauldron was designed to look like an [icicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icicle "Icicle") and was made of glass, allowing the fire to be seen burning within, reflecting the Games' slogan "Light the Fire Within" and an overarching "fire and ice" theme. The actual glass cauldron stands atop a twisting glass and steel support, is 12 feet (3.7m) high, and the flame within burns at 900°F (482°C).[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-64) Together with its support, the cauldron stands 117 feet (36m) tall and was made of 738 individual pieces of glass. Small jets send water down the glass sides of the cauldron to keep the glass and metal cooled (so they would not crack or melt) and give the effect of melting ice.[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-65) The cauldron was designed by [WET Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WET_Design "WET Design") of Los Angeles, its frame built by roller coaster manufacturer [Arrow Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Dynamics "Arrow Dynamics") of [Clearfield, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearfield,_Utah "Clearfield, Utah"), and its glass pieces created by Western Glass of [Ogden, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogden,_Utah "Ogden, Utah"). The cauldron's cost was $2million, and it was unveiled to the public when originally installed at Rice–Eccles Stadium on January 8, 2002.[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-66)

Production for the opening and closing ceremonies was designed by Seven Nielsen, and music for both ceremonies was directed by [Mark Watters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Watters "Mark Watters").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Air-Edel_1-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-Air-Edel_1)

### Closing ceremonies

Main article: [2002 Winter Olympics closing ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics_closing_ceremony "2002 Winter Olympics closing ceremony")

The closing ceremony of the 2002 Winter Olympics was held on February 24, 2002, at Rice–Eccles Stadium. It was narrated by Utah natives [Donny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donny_Osmond "Donny Osmond") and [Marie Osmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Osmond "Marie Osmond") (who voiced [animatronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animatronics "Animatronics") [dinosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur "Dinosaur") skeletons designed by [Michael Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Curry_(puppet_designer) "Michael Curry (puppet designer)")),[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-68)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-:3) and featured performances by a number of musicians and bands, including [Bon Jovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Jovi "Bon Jovi"), [Christina Aguilera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Aguilera "Christina Aguilera"), [Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_(band) "Creed (band)"), [Dianne Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Reeves "Dianne Reeves"), Donny and Marie Osmond, [Earth, Wind & Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth,_Wind_&_Fire "Earth, Wind & Fire"), [Gloria Estefan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Estefan "Gloria Estefan"), [Harry Connick Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Connick_Jr. "Harry Connick Jr."), [Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_(band) "Kiss (band)"), [Moby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moby "Moby") and [Angie Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angie_Stone "Angie Stone"), [NSYNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSYNC "NSYNC"), [R. Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Kelly "R. Kelly"), [Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_(singer) "Sting (singer)"), [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-70) and [Yo Yo Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo_Yo_Ma "Yo Yo Ma"). It also featured appearances by figure skaters such as [Kurt Browning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Browning "Kurt Browning"), [Dorothy Hamill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hamill "Dorothy Hamill"), and [Ilia Kulick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilia_Kulik "Ilia Kulik"), as well as dancer [Savion Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savion_Glover "Savion Glover").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[68\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-71)[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-72)

Departing from [Juan Antonio Samaranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Antonio_Samaranch "Juan Antonio Samaranch")'s tradition of declaring each Olympics the "best ever", IOC president Jacques Rogge began a tradition of assigning each Games their own identity in his comments, describing the 2002 Winter Olympics as having been "flawless".[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-73)

Italian singers [Irene Grandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Grandi "Irene Grandi") and [Elisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisa_(Italian_singer) "Elisa (Italian singer)") performed during the cultural presentation by [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin "Turin"), host city of the [2006 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics "2006 Winter Olympics"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-2)[\[68\]](#refnote-:3) while [Josh Groban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Groban "Josh Groban") and [Charlotte Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Church "Charlotte Church") performed a duet of " [The Prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prayer_(Celine_Dion_and_Andrea_Bocelli_song) "The Prayer (Celine Dion and Andrea Bocelli song)")" as the Olympic cauldron was extinguished.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-3)[\[68\]](#refnote-:3)

### Sports

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/2002_Olympic_curling.jpg/500px-2002_Olympic_curling.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2002_Olympic_curling.jpg) Curling at [The Ice Sheet at Ogden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ice_Sheet_at_Ogden "The Ice Sheet at Ogden") on February 22, 2002[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Andrea_Nahrgang_2002_Olympics.jpg/500px-Andrea_Nahrgang_2002_Olympics.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Andrea_Nahrgang_2002_Olympics.jpg) Andrea Nahrgang competing at [Soldier Hollow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier_Hollow "Soldier Hollow") on February 18, 2002

Confirmed in 1997, this edition's sports program featured seven sports divided into 15 disciplines, totaling 78 events, an increase of 10 events over the 1998 Winter Olympics. [Skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_at_the_Winter_Olympics "Skeleton at the Winter Olympics") made its return to the Winter Olympic program for the first time since [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Winter_Olympics "1948 Winter Olympics"), while a women's doubles event was contested for the first time in bobsleigh. A fourth distance was introduced in short track speed skating for men and women, and the pursuit events were added to biathlon and cross-country skiing. The sprint event was also added to the Nordic combined program.

### Athletic Achievements

Several medal records were set or tied, including:

- Norway tied the [Soviet Union at the 1976 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_at_the_1976_Winter_Olympics "Soviet Union at the 1976 Winter Olympics") for most gold medals at a Winter Olympics, with 13.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RecordGolds-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-RecordGolds)
- Germany set a record for most total medals at a Winter Olympics, with 36.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RecordMedals-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-RecordMedals)
- The United States set a record for most gold medals at a home Winter Olympics, with 10, tying [Norway at the 1994 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_at_the_1994_Winter_Olympics "Norway at the 1994 Winter Olympics").[&#8202;](#ref-back-RecordGolds-1)[\[73\]](#refnote-RecordGolds)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Lawton_Redman_2002_Winter_Olympics_b.jpg/500px-Lawton_Redman_2002_Winter_Olympics_b.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lawton_Redman_2002_Winter_Olympics_b.jpg) The men's 10km sprint biathlon race at [Soldier Hollow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier_Hollow "Soldier Hollow") during the Games on February 13, 2002[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fa/E_center_interior_2002_olympic_venue.jpg/500px-E_center_interior_2002_olympic_venue.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:E_center_interior_2002_olympic_venue.jpg) The [E Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_Center "E Center") during a hockey match on February 11, 2002

- [Vonetta Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vonetta_Flowers "Vonetta Flowers") became the first Black athlete of any nationality to win a gold medal in a [Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Olympics "Winter Olympics") when she as brakewoman and [Jill Bakken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Bakken "Jill Bakken") as driver won the 2-person bobsled event.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-76)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Olympics-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-Olympics)
- Competition highlights included biathlete [Ole Einar Bjørndalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Einar_Bj%C3%B8rndalen "Ole Einar Bjørndalen") of Norway, winning gold in all four men's events (10km, 12.5km, 20km, 4 x 7.5km relay), [Nordic combined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_combined "Nordic combined") athlete [Samppa Lajunen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samppa_Lajunen "Samppa Lajunen") of Finland winning three gold medals, [Simon Ammann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Ammann "Simon Ammann") of Switzerland taking the double in ski jumping. In alpine skiing, [Janica Kostelić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janica_Kosteli%C4%87 "Janica Kostelić") won three golds and a silver (the first Winter Olympic medals ever for an athlete from Croatia), while [Kjetil André Aamodt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kjetil_Andr%C3%A9_Aamodt "Kjetil André Aamodt") of Norway earned his second and third career golds, setting up both athletes to beat the sport's record with their fourth golds earned at the next [Winter Olympics near Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_2006_Winter_Olympics "Alpine skiing at the 2006 Winter Olympics") (Aamodt also set the overall medal record in the sport with eight).
- [Team GB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_GB "Team GB")'s victory in [Women's Curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics "Curling at the 2002 Winter Olympics") saw them win their first gold medal in any Winter Olympic sport since [Torvill and Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torvill_and_Dean "Torvill and Dean") in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Winter_Olympics "1984 Winter Olympics").
- Ireland reached its best-ever position and came close to winning its first winter medal when [Clifton Wrottesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton_Wrottesley "Clifton Wrottesley") (Clifton Hugh Lancelot de Verdon Wrottesley, 6th Baron Wrottesley) finished fourth in the men's skeleton event.
- A feature of these Games was the emergence of extreme sports, such as snowboarding, moguls, and aerials, some of which appeared in previous Olympic Winter Games but subsequently captured greater public attention.
- The United States completed a sweep of the podium in halfpipe [snowboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowboarding "Snowboarding"), with Americans [Ross Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Powers "Ross Powers"), [Danny Kass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Kass "Danny Kass"), and [Jarret Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarret_Thomas "Jarret Thomas") all winning medals.
- American [Sarah Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Hughes "Sarah Hughes") won the gold medal in ladies' singles figure skating. Her team-mate [Michelle Kwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Kwan "Michelle Kwan") fell during her long program and received the bronze medal.
- China won its first and second Winter Olympic gold medals, both by women's short-track speed skater [Yang Yang (A)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Yang_(A) "Yang Yang (A)").
- In the [men's 1000m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1000_metres "Short track speed skating at the 2002 Winter Olympics – Men's 1000 metres") competition in short-track speed skating, Australian [Steven Bradbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Bradbury_(speed_skater) "Steven Bradbury (speed skater)") (who had won a bronze in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Winter_Olympics "1994 Winter Olympics") as part of a relay team) became both the first-ever Australian, and the first-ever athlete from a country in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere"), to win a gold medal at the Winter Olympics. Despite being off their pace, Bradbury benefitted from crashes involving his opponents in both the semi-finals and finals, with the latter occurring coming out of the final turn.[&#8202;](#ref-back-lastman-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-lastman)[&#8202;](#ref-back-fall-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-fall) A few days later in [women's aerials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_skiing_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_aerials "Freestyle skiing at the 2002 Winter Olympics – Women's aerials"), Australian skier [Alisa Camplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alisa_Camplin "Alisa Camplin") won Australia's second gold medal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-80) After the Games, the phrase "doing a Bradbury" would become a local [idiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiom "Idiom") for an unexpected victory in a sporting event at the expense of one's opponents, and was added to the second edition of [_The Australian National Dictionary_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Australian_National_Dictionary "The Australian National Dictionary") in 2016.[&#8202;](#ref-back-81-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-81)
- Belarus's [Vladimir Kopat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Kopat "Vladimir Kopat") scored a [game winning goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_winning_goal "Game winning goal") from center ice against Sweden in the men's ice hockey quarterfinals, getting Belarus to their best place in international hockey so far.
- The [Canadian men's ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_men's_national_ice_hockey_team "Canada men's national ice hockey team") defeated the United States team 5–2 to claim the gold medal, ending a 50-year drought without hockey gold. The Canadian women's team also defeated the American team 3–2 after losing to them in [Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1998_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament "Ice hockey at the 1998 Winter Olympics – Men's tournament"). In a post-game press conference after the men's gold medal game, Team Canada's executive director [Wayne Gretzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky "Wayne Gretzky") revealed that a [Canadian $1 coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loonie "Loonie") (colloquially known as a "Loonie") had been secretly placed at center ice by one of the ice technicians. The "lucky Loonie" subsequently became a notable symbol of Canada's victory in the tournament.[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-82)[&#8202;](#ref-back-OlympicHistory-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-OlympicHistory)

## Broadcasting rights

International Sports Broadcasting (ISB) served as the host broadcaster for the 2002 Winter Olympics; the [Salt Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Palace "Salt Palace") convention center served as the [International Broadcast Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Broadcast_Centre "International Broadcast Centre") and press center for the Games.[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-85) The IOC estimated that the 2002 Winter Olympics were viewed by over twobillion people worldwide, with 13billion viewer-hours watched.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Marketing_Matters_6_2002-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-Marketing_Matters_6_2002)

In the United States, the 2002 Winter Olympics were broadcast by [NBC Universal networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Olympic_broadcasts "NBC Olympic broadcasts"). They were the first Winter Olympics under a multi-year rights agreement between NBC and the IOC, under which it would hold exclusive rights to all Olympic Games from [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics "1996 Summer Olympics") through [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics "2008 Summer Olympics"). The contract had excluded the 1998 Winter Olympics, as [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Olympic_broadcasts "CBS Olympic broadcasts") had an existing deal to exclusively televise the Winter Olympics from [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Winter_Olympics "1992 Winter Olympics") through 1998.[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-86)

NBC partnered with [HDNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDNet "HDNet") to produce an eight-hour block of daily coverage in [high definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television "High-definition television"), which was carried by HDNet and on the [digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_television_in_the_United_States "Digital television in the United States") signals of participating NBC [affiliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_affiliate "Network affiliate").[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-87)[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-88) Despite being held in a [time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Time_Zone "Mountain Time Zone") only one hour ahead of [Pacific Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone "Pacific Time Zone"), NBC still [tape delayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_delay "Broadcast delay") much of its coverage for the west coast, although Salt Lake City's local NBC affiliate [KSL-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSL-TV "KSL-TV") was given permission to air the live, east coast broadcasts to ensure their availability in the Games' host city.[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-89)[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-90)

Coverage of the Games by the [Seven Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Network_Olympic_broadcasts "Seven Network Olympic broadcasts") in Australia featured _[The Ice Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ice_Dream_with_Roy_and_HG "The Ice Dream with Roy and HG")_, a miniseries presented by comedy duo [Roy and HG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_and_HG "Roy and HG") as a follow-up to _[The Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dream_with_Roy_and_HG "The Dream with Roy and HG")_—their series for the [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics "2000 Summer Olympics"). The series featured a running gag of the duo proposing an Australian bid to hold the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics "2010 Winter Olympics") in the resort town of [Smiggin Holes, New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiggin_Holes,_New_South_Wales "Smiggin Holes, New South Wales").[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-91)

## Legacy

### Ski industry and winter sport

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Utah_Olympic_Oval.jpg/500px-Utah_Olympic_Oval.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Utah_Olympic_Oval.jpg) Utah Olympic Oval[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/2002SLC_proof_gold.JPG/500px-2002SLC_proof_gold.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2002SLC_proof_gold.JPG) 2002 Olympic Winter Games $5 coin created by the [U.S. Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Mint "United States Mint")

The 2002 Winter Olympics brought a huge amount of success to the Utah skiing industry. Since hosting the Winter Games, Utah has seen a 42% increase in skier and snowboarder visits as of 2010[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2002_Winter_Olympics&action=edit)–11\. This increase resulted in direct expenditures from skiers and snowboarders growing 67% from $704million in 2002–2003 to $1.2billion in 2010–2011.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-92)

Fourteen venues were constructed or expanded in preparation for the Winter Games. One of the venues constructed for the Games was the [Utah Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Olympic_Park "Utah Olympic Park"), which has proven to be one of the most successful venues to date because it has been maintained in top competition form. Owing to the routine maintenance of the park, Utah has been able to host a large number of winter competitions since 2002, including more than 60 World Cup events (e.g. the [FIS Freestyle Skiing World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Freestyle_Skiing_World_Cup "FIS Freestyle Skiing World Cup")), as well as seven world championships, and various other sporting events. Hosting these high-profile competitions has resulted in approximately $1billion being injected into the local economy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-93) During 2013–2014, Utah held 16 various winter sport events, bringing $27.3million to the economy of Utah.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-94) After holding the Olympics, Utah became home to two National Governing Bodies of Sport.[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-95) The [U.S. Ski and Snowboard Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Ski_and_Snowboard_Association "U.S. Ski and Snowboard Association") is headquartered in [Park City, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_City,_Utah "Park City, Utah") and the U.S. Olympic speed skating team is based out of the [Utah Olympic Oval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Olympic_Oval "Utah Olympic Oval").

### University of Utah expansion

The [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah") was one of the hosts of the 2002 Winter Olympics; the planning committee approached the University of Utah and asked them to build several student dormitories which would serve as athletes' accommodation during the Games. It was agreed that the university would pay approximately $98million out of the total required amount of $110million to complete the construction. As a result, students of the university have benefited as almost 3,500 of them would be housed here after the Games. This was a great economic benefit to the university since the amount of money used to complete such dormitories could take long to be afforded. The university was also asked to expand [Rice Eccles Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_Eccles_Stadium "Rice Eccles Stadium") to accommodate 50,000 people up from 32,000. The university would then be refunded almost $59million and be given an extra $40million for its maintenance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-96)

The 2002 Olympic Games also benefited the university economically since the [Salt Lake 2002 Olympic Cauldron Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_2002_Olympic_Cauldron_Park "Salt Lake 2002 Olympic Cauldron Park") was elevated by the renovations that took place. Ice rinks were very scarce in Utah, but they became plentiful and offered several entertainment and training opportunities for hockey players and figure skaters due to the Olympic Games. The Cauldron Park located at the University of Utah which was built with $6.5million in profits and had the following features: a visitors' center which had a theater that showed a thrilling movie about the Olympic Games of 2002 and a "park" which had a dazzling pool and a V-shaped stone wall with the names of all the medalists of the 2002 Olympic Games. Besides, the park had 17 plates that hung on the stadium's fence celebrating the highlights of each day of the Olympics. All these features acted as tourist attraction that boosted the economic development of the university. In addition, it is indicated that the approximate value of media exposure through print during the Games was equated to $22.9million. Mainly, this was a huge economic benefit to the university as more and more people got to know about the educational establishment, and this also boosted enrollment and future development.

### Immigration

Holger Preuss in his book _The Economics of Staging the Olympics: A Comparison of the Games 1972–2008_ argues that "The export of the 'Olympic Games' service results in an inflow of funds to the host city, causing additional production which, in its turn, leads to employment and income effects."[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-97) According to the study "2002 Olympic Winter Games, Economic, Demographic and Fiscal Impacts", the estimated creation of new job years of employment was 35,424, and additional earnings of $1,544,203,000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-98) It was noted that the increase of Olympic related job started in 1996 and continued until 2003. These effects can be estimated on the ground of historical relationship between job and corresponding population growth. A lot of people migrated into the future place of the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games") for expanding and favorable employment opportunities that the Olympics ensured. Although residents occupied many of the higher paying jobs created by the Games, many of the vacated jobs were filled by immigrants who relocated for the better employment opportunities.

Basically, the immigration rate was even larger because the employees immigrated with their families. The additional people paid diverse taxes and fees from their income, creating additional revenue on the state and local levels.

### Employment

Olympic related jobs in Utah started in 1996 with slight job opportunities of less than 100. However, from the job measurement conducted from 1996 to 2002, steady attainment of job opportunities established and a maximum level was noted in 2001 where there were 12,500 job opportunities attained yearly, and approximately 25,070 jobs created in 2002.[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-99) Therefore, from 1996 to 2002 the sum of employment equated to 35,000 jobs which lasted a year. February 2002 is when the highest employment opportunities were created compared to other years. There were around 25,070 job opportunities created compared to 35,000 created from 1996 to 2001.

It is difficult to quantify the impact of the 2002 Olympics on the [unemployment rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rates "Unemployment rates") in Utah, due mostly to the effect of the [early 2000s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_2000s_recession "Early 2000s recession"). In 1996, the unemployment rate in Utah was approximately 3.4%, while the U.S. national average was 5.4% and by the end of 2001, the unemployment rate in Utah was around 4.8%, while the national average had risen to 5.7%.[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-100) There was a high percentage of visitors to the Games, which raised the number of tourists whose consumption and demand prompted the establishment of job opportunities to meet the demands.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\][&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-101)

### 2034 Winter Olympics

Further information: [Bids for the 2034 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2034_Winter_Olympics "Bids for the 2034 Winter Olympics")

In 2017, an exploratory committee was formed to consider a Salt Lake City bid for a future Winter Olympics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-102) In December 2018, the United States Olympic Committee (USOC, now the [United States Olympic & Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_&_Paralympic_Committee "United States Olympic & Paralympic Committee") \[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[USOPC\]) named Salt Lake City as its preferred candidate to bid for a future Winter Olympics, citing that its experience and existing venues could be leveraged.[\[102\]](#refnote-103) In February 2022, amid the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics "2022 Winter Olympics") and the 20th anniversary of the Games in Salt Lake City, the USOPC stated that it was "already in dialogue with the IOC, not yet for a specific year but as part of their evolving process", and acknowledged that there was "very high excitement and support from the local population."[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-104)

In November 2023, the IOC's Future Host Commission recommended that Salt Lake City–Utah be invited to engage in "targeted dialogue" with the IOC Executive Board as preferred host for the [2034 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_Winter_Olympics "2034 Winter Olympics"); the Future Host Commission cited the bid's reliance on private funding, as well as plans to reuse much of the infrastructure that was originally built for the 2002 Games.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-105)

On July 24, 2024, during the [142nd IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/142nd_IOC_Session "142nd IOC Session") in Paris just before the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics "2024 Summer Olympics") in that city, Salt Lake City–Utah was selected to host the 2034 Winter Olympics and [Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_Winter_Paralympics "2034 Winter Paralympics"). IOC President [Thomas Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bach "Thomas Bach") said: "Salt Lake City and Utah are long-time friends of the Olympic Movement, and we are confident that they will organize exceptional Olympic and Paralympic Games, just as they did years ago. The legacy of 2002 is tangibly and passionately alive in Utah. The legacy of 2034 starts today."[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-106) With the 2034 Winter Olympics, Salt Lake City will become the fourth city to host multiple Winter Games after Innsbruck ( [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Winter_Olympics "1964 Winter Olympics") and [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Olympics "1976 Winter Olympics")), Lake Placid ( [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Winter_Olympics "1932 Winter Olympics") and [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Winter_Olympics "1980 Winter Olympics")), and Cortina d'Ampezzo ( [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Winter_Olympics "1956 Winter Olympics") and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Winter_Olympics "2026 Winter Olympics")).

## Concerns and controversies

### Disqualifications for doping

Further information: [Doping at the Olympic Games §2002 Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping_at_the_Olympic_Games#2002_Salt_Lake_City "Doping at the Olympic Games")

The 2002 Winter Olympics were the first Winter Olympics held after the formation of the [World Anti-Doping Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Anti-Doping_Agency "World Anti-Doping Agency"), resulting in the first instances of athletes being disqualified for failing drug testing.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-107) Athletes in [cross-country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing_(sport) "Cross-country skiing (sport)") were disqualified for various reasons, including [doping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping_(sport) "Doping (sport)") by two Russians and one Spaniard, leading Russia to file protests and threaten to withdraw from the competition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-108)

### Pairs figure skating judging controversy

Main article: [2002 Winter Olympics figure skating scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics_figure_skating_scandal "2002 Winter Olympics figure skating scandal")

A major scandal emerged during the [pairs figure skating competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics "Figure skating at the 2002 Winter Olympics"); the Canadian pair of [Jamie Salé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Sal%C3%A9 "Jamie Salé") and [David Pelletier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pelletier "David Pelletier") narrowly lost to the Russian pair of [Elena Berezhnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Berezhnaya "Elena Berezhnaya") and [Anton Sikharulidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Sikharulidze "Anton Sikharulidze"), despite the Canadians being deemed the favorites to win after their free skate program. The French judge [Marie-Reine Le Gougne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Reine_Le_Gougne "Marie-Reine Le Gougne") alleged that the head of the [French Federation of Ice Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Federation_of_Ice_Sports "French Federation of Ice Sports"), [Didier Gailhaguet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Gailhaguet "Didier Gailhaguet"), had pressured her to judge the competition in favor of Russia regardless of performance. Amid criticisms of the incident by both Canadian and American media outlets, and suspicions that this was part of a [vote swapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_swapping "Vote swapping") scheme with Russia to benefit the French ice dance team, the [International Skating Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Skating_Union "International Skating Union") (ISU) voted to suspend Le Gougne for failing to immediately inform officials of Gailhaguet's actions. They also recommended to the IOC that the gold medal be jointly awarded to both pairs. An IOC panel voted in favor of the motion, resulting in both Salé and Pelletier, as well as Berezhnaya and Sikharulidze, being jointly awarded gold medals.[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-109)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Reaction2-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-Reaction2)

### Disqualification of Kim Dong-Sung

Main article: [Short track speed skating at the 2002 Winter Olympics – Men's 1500 metres §Controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres#Controversy "Short track speed skating at the 2002 Winter Olympics – Men's 1500 metres")

In the final race A, with one lap remaining and currently in second place, [Apolo Ohno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apolo_Ohno "Apolo Ohno") of the United States attempted to make a pass on the leader [Kim Dong-Sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Dong-Sung "Kim Dong-Sung") of South Korea, who then drifted to the inside and as a result, Ohno raised his arms to imply he was blocked. Kim finished first ahead of Ohno, but the Australian referee [James Hewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hewish "James Hewish") disqualified Kim for what appeared to be impeding, awarding the gold medal to Ohno.[&#8202;](#ref-back-stptimes-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-stptimes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-protestDQ-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-protestDQ)

The South Korean team immediately protested the decision of the chief official of the race, but their protests were denied by the [International Skating Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Skating_Union "International Skating Union") (ISU).[&#8202;](#ref-back-disqualified-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-disqualified)[&#8202;](#ref-back-protestDQ-1)[\[111\]](#refnote-protestDQ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sportslaw2002-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-sportslaw2002) The South Korean team then appealed to the [International Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympic_Committee "International Olympic Committee") (IOC) and the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport "Court of Arbitration for Sport") (CAS).[&#8202;](#ref-back-disqualified-1)[\[112\]](#refnote-disqualified)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sportslaw2002-1)[\[113\]](#refnote-sportslaw2002) The IOC refused to see the case, stating, "This is a matter for the ISU to decide on. At this time, the IOC has received no proposal and taken no action".[&#8202;](#ref-back-disqualified-2)[\[112\]](#refnote-disqualified)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sportslaw2002-2)[\[113\]](#refnote-sportslaw2002)

The disqualification of Kim upset South Korean supporters, many of whom directed their anger at Ohno and the IOC. A large number of e-mails protesting the race results crashed the IOC's email server, and thousands of accusatory letters, many of which contained death threats, were sent to Ohno and the IOC.[&#8202;](#ref-back-disqualified-3)[\[112\]](#refnote-disqualified)[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-115)[&#8202;](#ref-back-YNA1-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-YNA1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-YNA2-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-YNA2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-YNA3-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-YNA3) South Korean media accused Ohno of simulating foul, using the [Konglish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konglish "Konglish") word "Hollywood action".[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-119) The controversy continued at the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup "2002 FIFA World Cup"), held jointly in South Korea and Japan several months after the Olympics.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sports_Illustrated-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-Sports_Illustrated) When the [South Korean soccer team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_football_team "South Korea national football team") scored a goal during the [group stage match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_D#South_Korea_vs_United_States "2002 FIFA World Cup Group D") against the [U.S. team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_soccer_team "United States men's national soccer team"), South Korean players [Ahn Jung-Hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahn_Jung-Hwan "Ahn Jung-Hwan") and [Lee Chun-Soo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Chun-Soo "Lee Chun-Soo") made an [exaggerated move](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_celebration "Goal celebration") imitating the move Ohno had made during the speed skating event to imply the other athlete had drifted into his lane.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sports_Illustrated-1)[\[119\]](#refnote-Sports_Illustrated)
