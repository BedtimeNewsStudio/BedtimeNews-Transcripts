## TB SITUATION AND RESPONSE

Tuberculosis (TB) is the world’s leading cause of death from a single infectious
agent, ranking 13th among all causes of death in 2023. It is a leading killer of people
with HIV and a major cause of deaths related to antimicrobial resistance. TB is
airborne, preventable and usually curable.

## TB BURDEN

In 2025, an estimated 10.6 million people fell ill with TB worldwide: 5.7 million
men, 3.7 million women and 1.2 million children and young adolescents. People
living with HIV accounted for 5.6% of the total.

The TB incidence rate fell by 1.6% between 2024 and 2025, to below the level of
2020. The net reduction from 2015 to 2025 was 14%, far from the End TB
Strategy milestone of a 50% reduction by 2025.

TB caused an estimated 1.13 million deaths in 2025, including 137 000 among
people with HIV, down from 1.18 million in 2024. The 35% reduction since 2015
means the first End TB Strategy milestone has been reached, 5 years behind
schedule.

Eight countries accounted for about two thirds of people who fell ill with TB in
2025: Bangladesh, China, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, India, Indonesia,
Nigeria, Pakistan and the Philippines.

## TB CARE AND TREATMENT

Treatment of people with TB is estimated to have averted 86 million deaths
between 2000 and 2025.

In 2025, 8.4 million people were reported as newly diagnosed with TB, up slightly
from 8.3 million in 2024 and equivalent to 79% of the estimated number of
incident cases. Of these, 59% were initially tested with a rapid test, up from 54%
in 2024.

About 2.2 million people who fell ill with TB in 2025 were not diagnosed, or not
officially reported to national authorities, down from 2.3 million in 2024.

The treatment success rate for drug-susceptible TB rose to 90% (people started
on treatment in 2024).

## DRUG-RESISTANT TB

An estimated 370 000 people developed rifampicin-resistant TB (RR-TB) in 2025,
and about 140 000 died from it.

A total of 155 333 people were started on treatment for RR-TB in 2025: 42% of
those who developed it, compared with 43% in 2024.

The treatment success rate for RR-TB was 70% (people started on treatment in
2023), a substantial improvement compared with 50% in 2012.

## ADDRESSING THE CO-EPIDEMICS OF TB AND HIV

An estimated 592 000 people living with HIV developed TB in 2025, with the
highest burden in the WHO African Region.

HIV testing coverage among people diagnosed with TB was 83% in 2025, up from
82% in 2024.

Coverage of antiretroviral therapy among people with TB known to be living with
HIV fell to 87%, from 91% in 2024, at a time when international donor funding for
the HIV response fell by 18%.

## TB PREVENTIVE TREATMENT

WHO recommends TB preventive treatment (TPT) for people
living with HIV, household contacts of people with
bacteriologically confirmed pulmonary TB, and clinical risk
groups (e.g. people receiving dialysis).

In 2025, 5.2 million people at high risk of developing TB disease
were provided with TPT: 3.7 million household contacts and 1.5
million people living with HIV.

TPT coverage was 60% among people living with HIV (up from
58% in 2024) and 26% among household contacts (up from
25%), both far from the target of 90% by 2027.

## UPTAKE OF DIAGNOSTICS, NEW DRUGS AND REGIMENS

A WHO-recommended rapid test was used as
the initial diagnostic test for 59% of the 8.4
million people newly diagnosed with TB in
2025, up from 54% in 2024 and 48% in 2023.
In 73 countries, coverage was at least 80% in
2025.

Use of 6-month regimens for RR-TB expanded rapidly: they
were used for 42% of people treated in 2025, up from 18% in
2024 and 3% in 2023. By the end of 2025, they were in use in
106 countries.

Shorter (1–3 month) rifamycin-based regimens for TPT reached
3.1 million people in 2025, up from 2.1 million in 2024; 122
countries reported using them.

## TB FINANCING

At the 2023 UN high-level meeting on TB, Member States
committed to mobilizing US$ 22 billion per year by 2027 for TB
prevention, diagnosis, treatment and care.

In 2025, US$ 6.6 billion was available in low- and middleincome countries, up 9% from US$ 6.0 billion in 2024 but only
30% of the target. Funding has been stuck at US$ 6–7 billion
since 2015.

Domestic sources provided 86% (US$ 5.6 billion, up from US$
4.9 billion in 2024). Brazil, the Russian Federation, India, China
and South Africa accounted for 65% of domestic funding.

International donor funding fell by 14%, from US$ 1.1 billion to
US$ 0.94 billion. Among 23 countries particularly exposed to
these changes, total funding increased in 13; there was no
clear-cut impact on TB diagnosis and treatment in 2025, but
impacts may be delayed.

Funding for TB research was US$ 1.2 billion in 2023, only 24%
of the US$ 5 billion annual target.

## RESEARCH AND INNOVATION

In August 2026, 65 tests for diagnosis of TB disease and drug
resistance were in development, including new biomarkerbased point-of-care and near point-of-care tests.

There were 31 drugs for treatment of TB disease in clinical
trials, including 20 new chemical entities, up from 29 in 2025
and 8 in 2015.

Of 17 vaccine candidates in clinical development, 12 were
active: three in Phase 1, six in Phase 2 and three in Phase 3.

At least 42 trials and implementation studies were evaluating
TPT regimens and delivery models, including at least 17 studies
of novel delivery models.

## UHC, SOCIAL PROTECTION, DETERMINANTS AND MULTISECTORAL ACTION

47% of people treated for TB and their households face total
costs that are catastrophic (>20% of annual household income),
far from the End TB Strategy target of zero.

In most high TB burden countries, the UHC service coverage
index is 40–70 (out of 100). About 52% of the world’s population
has at least one social protection benefit (44% when weighted
for TB burden), ranging from 9.7% in low-income to 86% in
high-income countries.

In 2025, an estimated 0.96 million new TB cases were
attributable to undernutrition, 0.92 million to diabetes, 0.73
million to alcohol use disorders, 0.69 million to smoking and
0.55 million to HIV infection.

In 2026, 21 countries, including 10 high TB burden countries,
had all five core elements of WHO’s multisectoral accountability
framework for TB in place.
