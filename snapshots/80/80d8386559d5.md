The **Non-Aligned Movement** (**NAM**) is a forum of 120 countries that [are not formally aligned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-belligerent "Non-belligerent") with or against any major [power bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_bloc "Power bloc"). It was founded with the view to advancing interests of developing countries in the context of [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") confrontation.[[5]](#cite_note-5) After the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), it is the largest grouping of states worldwide.[[2]](#cite_note-NAM_members-2)[[6]](#cite_note-NAM_about-6)

The movement originated in the aftermath of the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War"), as an effort by some countries to counterbalance the rapid bi-[polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarity_(international_relations) "Polarity (international relations)") of the world during the Cold War, whereby two major powers formed blocs and embarked on a policy to pull the rest of the world into their orbits. One of these was the pro-Soviet [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist "Socialist") bloc whose best known alliance was the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact"), and the other the pro-American [capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalist "Capitalist") group of countries, many of which belonged to [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO"). In 1961, drawing on the principles agreed at the [Bandung Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_Conference "Bandung Conference") of 1955, the Non-Aligned Movement was formally established in [Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade "Belgrade"), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia"), through an initiative led by Yugoslav president [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito "Josip Broz Tito"), Egyptian president [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser "Gamal Abdel Nasser"), Indian prime minister [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru"), Ghanaian president [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah "Kwame Nkrumah"), and Indonesian president [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno "Sukarno").[[7]](#cite_note-7)[[8]](#cite_note-8)[[9]](#cite_note-9)

This led to the [first Conference of Heads of State or Governments of Non-Aligned Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "1st Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement").[[10]](#cite_note-10) The purpose of the organization was summarized by [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro "Fidel Castro") in his Havana Declaration of 1979 as to ensure "the national independence, sovereignty, territorial integrity and security of non-aligned countries" in their "struggle against [imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism "Imperialism"), [colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism"), [neo-colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-colonialism "Neo-colonialism"), [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism "Racism"), and all forms of foreign aggression, [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_occupation "Military occupation"), domination, interference or [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony "Hegemony") as well as against [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power") and bloc politics."[[11]](#cite_note-11)[[12]](#cite_note-12)

The countries of the Non-Aligned Movement represent nearly two-thirds of the United Nations' members and contain 55% of the world population. Membership is particularly concentrated in countries considered to be [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries "Developing countries"), although the Non-Aligned Movement also has a number of [developed nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country "Developed country").[[13]](#cite_note-Menon_2022-13)

The Non-Aligned Movement gained the most traction in the 1950s and early 1960s, when the international policy of non-alignment achieved major successes in [decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization "Decolonization"), [disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disarmament "Disarmament"), [opposition to racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-racism "Anti-racism") and opposition to [apartheid in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Apartheid_Movement "Anti-Apartheid Movement"), and persisted throughout the entire Cold War, despite several conflicts between members, and despite some members developing closer ties with either the Soviet Union, China, or the United States.[[13]](#cite_note-Menon_2022-13) In the years since the [Cold War's end in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union "Dissolution of the Soviet Union"), the movement has focused on developing multilateral ties and connections as well as unity among the developing nations of the world, especially those in the [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South "Global South").[[13]](#cite_note-Menon_2022-13)

## History

See also: [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War")

### Origins and the Cold War

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/NATO_vs._Warsaw_Pact_%281949-1990%29.svg/250px-NATO_vs._Warsaw_Pact_%281949-1990%29.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NATO_vs._Warsaw_Pact_(1949-1990).svg)

The *aligned countries* on the northern hemisphere: [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") in blue and the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact "Warsaw Pact") in red.

 [![refer caption](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Stevan_Kragujevic%2C_Tito%2C_Naser%2C_Nehru%2C_Dolazak_na_Brione.jpg/250px-Stevan_Kragujevic%2C_Tito%2C_Naser%2C_Nehru%2C_Dolazak_na_Brione.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stevan_Kragujevic,_Tito,_Naser,_Nehru,_Dolazak_na_Brione.jpg)

[Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito "Josip Broz Tito"), [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru"), and [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser "Gamal Abdel Nasser"), pioneers of the Non-Aligned Movement during the [Brioni Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brioni_Meeting "Brioni Meeting")

The term 'Non-Alignment' was used for the first time in 1950 at the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") by [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia"), both of which refused to align themselves with any side in the multi-alliances involving [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War").[[14]](#cite_note-Goldstein-14) Drawing on the principles agreed at the [Bandung Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_Conference "Bandung Conference") in 1955, the Non-Aligned Movement as an organization was founded on the [Brijuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brijuni "Brijuni") islands in Yugoslavia in 1956 and was formalized by signing the Declaration of Brijuni on 19 July 1956. The Declaration was signed by Yugoslavia's president, Josip Broz Tito, India's prime minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Egypt's president, Gamal Abdel Nasser. One of the quotations within the Declaration is "Peace can not be achieved with separation, but with the aspiration towards collective security in global terms and expansion of freedom, as well as terminating the domination of one country over another". According to [Rejaul Karim Laskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejaul_Karim_Laskar "Rejaul Karim Laskar"), an ideologue of the [Congress party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress") which ruled India for most part of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") years, the Non-Aligned Movement arose from the desire of Jawaharlal Nehru and other leaders of the newly independent countries of the third world to guard their independence and sovereignty "in face of complex international situation demanding allegiance to either two warring superpowers".[[15]](#cite_note-15)

The movement advocates a middle course for states in the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country") between the [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc "Western Bloc") and [Eastern Blocs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc") during the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"). The phrase itself was first used to represent the doctrine by Indian diplomat [V. K. Krishna Menon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._K._Krishna_Menon "V. K. Krishna Menon") in 1953, at the United Nations.[[16]](#cite_note-16)[*[unreliable source?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources "Wikipedia:Reliable sources")*]

But it soon after became the name to refer to the participants of the *Conference of Heads of State or Government of Non-Aligned Countries* first held in 1961. The term "non-alignment" was established in 1953 at the United Nations. Nehru used the phrase in a 1954 speech in Colombo, Sri Lanka. In this speech, Zhou Enlai and Nehru described the [Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Principles_of_Peaceful_Coexistence "Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence") to be used as a guide for [Sino-Indian relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_relations "Sino-Indian relations") called *Panchsheel* (five restraints); these principles would later serve as the basis of the Non-Aligned Movement. The five principles were:

* Mutual respect for each other's [territorial integrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_integrity "Territorial integrity") and [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty "Sovereignty").
* Mutual non-aggression.
* Mutual non-interference in domestic affairs.
* Equality and mutual benefit.
* Peaceful co-existence.

A significant milestone in the development of the Non-Aligned Movement was the 1955 [Bandung Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_Conference "Bandung Conference"), a conference of Asian and African states hosted by Indonesian president [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno "Sukarno"), who gave a significant boost to promote this movement. Bringing together Sukarno, [U Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Nu "U Nu"), Nasser, Nehru, Tito, Nkrumah and Menon with the likes of [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh "Ho Chi Minh"), [Zhou Enlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Enlai "Zhou Enlai"), and [Norodom Sihanouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norodom_Sihanouk "Norodom Sihanouk"), as well as [U Thant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Thant "U Thant") and a young [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi "Indira Gandhi"), the conference adopted a "declaration on promotion of world peace and cooperation", which included Zhou Enlai and Nehru's five principles, and a collective pledge to remain neutral in the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"). Six years after Bandung, an initiative of Yugoslav president [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito "Josip Broz Tito") led to the first *Conference of Heads of State or Government of Non-Aligned Countries*, which was held in September 1961 in Belgrade.[[17]](#cite_note-17) The term *non-aligned movement* appears first in the fifth conference in 1976, where participating countries are denoted as *members of the movement*.[[18]](#cite_note-18)

At the [Lusaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusaka "Lusaka") Conference in September 1970, the member nations added as aims of the movement the peaceful resolution of disputes and the abstention from the big power military alliances and pacts. Another added aim was opposition to stationing of [military bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_base "Military base") in foreign countries.[[19]](#cite_note-suvedi-19)

In 1975, the member nations which also were part of the [United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly "United Nations General Assembly") pushed for the [Resolution 3379](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_3379 "United Nations General Assembly Resolution 3379") along with [Arab countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world") and the support of the [Soviet bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_bloc "Soviet bloc"). It was a declarative non-binding measure that equated [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism "Zionism") with South Africa's [Apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid") and as a form of racial discrimination. The bloc voting produced a majority in the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") that systematically condemned Israel in the following resolutions: 3089, 3210, 3236, 32/40, etc.

Some Non-Aligned member nations were involved in serious conflicts with other members, notably [India and Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India–Pakistan_relations "India–Pakistan relations") as well as [Iran and Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran–Iraq_relations "Iran–Iraq relations").

#### Cuba's role

In the 1970s, Cuba made a major effort to assume a leadership role in the world's non-alignment movement. The country established military advisory missions and economic and social reform programs. The [1976 world conference of the Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "5th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement") applauded Cuban internationalism, "which assisted the people of Angola in frustrating the expansionist and colonialist strategy of South Africa's racist regime and its allies." The [next Non-Aligned conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "6th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement") was scheduled for Havana in 1979, to be chaired by [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro "Fidel Castro"), with his becoming the de facto spokesman for the movement. The conference in September 1979 marked the zenith of Cuban prestige. Most, but not all, attendees believed that Cuba was not aligned with the Soviet camp in the Cold War.[[20]](#cite_note-20)

However, in December 1979, the Soviet Union [intervened in Afghanistan's civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet–Afghan_War "Soviet–Afghan War"). Up until that time, Afghanistan was also an active member of the Non-Aligned Movement. At the United Nations, nonaligned members voted 56 to 9, with 26 abstaining, to condemn the Soviet Union. Cuba voted against the resolution, in support of the USSR. It lost its nonaligned leadership and reputation after Castro, instead of becoming a high-profile spokesman for the movement, remained quiet and inactive. More broadly the movement was deeply split over the Soviet–Afghan War in 1979, as many members of the Non-Aligned Movement, particularly the [predominantly Muslim states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world "Muslim world"), condemned it.[[21]](#cite_note-21)

### Post-Cold War

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/18th_Summit_of_Non-Aligned_Movement_gets_underway_in_Baku_026.jpg/250px-18th_Summit_of_Non-Aligned_Movement_gets_underway_in_Baku_026.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:18th_Summit_of_Non-Aligned_Movement_gets_underway_in_Baku_026.jpg)

Azerbaijan's President [Ilham Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilham_Aliyev "Ilham Aliyev") and Venezuela's President [Nicolas Maduro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Maduro "Nicolas Maduro") at the [18th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "18th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement") in Baku on 25 October 2019

With the end of the Cold War, the Non-Aligned Movement transformed. The [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia "Breakup of Yugoslavia") (a prominent founding member) in 1991–1992 also affected the movement; the regular Ministerial Meeting of the movement, held in [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") during the regular yearly session of the [General Assembly of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly "United Nations General Assembly") in 1992 suspended Yugoslavia's membership.[[22]](#cite_note-suspended-22)[[23]](#cite_note-23)[[24]](#cite_note-Najam-24) The various successor states of Yugoslavia have expressed little interest in membership, though [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina "Bosnia and Herzegovina"), [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia "Croatia"), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro "Montenegro") and [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia "Serbia") retain observer status. India, another founding member, appears to have [downgraded its emphasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_and_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "India and the Non-Aligned Movement") on the movement.[[25]](#cite_note-25)

Membership applications from [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina "Bosnia and Herzegovina") and from [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica "Costa Rica") were rejected in 1995 and 1998 respectively.[[24]](#cite_note-Najam-24) In 2004 [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta "Malta") and [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus") ceased to be members when they joined the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"), as required. [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan "Azerbaijan") and [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji "Fiji") are the most recent entrants, both having joined the movement in 2011. Azerbaijan and [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus "Belarus"), which joined in 1998, remain the only members on the continent of Europe.

Since the end of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War"), the Non-Aligned Movement has felt forced to redefine itself and to reinvent its purpose in the new [world-system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World-system "World-system"). A major question has been whether any of its foundational ideologies, principally national independence, territorial integrity, and the struggle against colonialism and imperialism, apply to contemporary issues. The movement has emphasized its principles of multilateralism, equality, and mutual non-aggression in attempting to become a stronger voice for the Global South, and an instrument that can promote the needs of member-nations at the international level and strengthen their political leverage when negotiating with developed nations. In its efforts to advance Southern interests, the movement has stressed the importance of cooperation and unity amongst member states.[[26]](#cite_note-autogenerated2-26) However, as in the past, cohesion remains a problem, since the size of the organization and the divergence of agendas and allegiances present the ongoing potential for fragmentation. While agreement on basic principles has been smooth, taking definitive action *vis-à-vis* particular international issues has been rare, with the movement preferring to assert its criticism or support rather than to pass hard-line resolutions.[[27]](#cite_note-27)

The movement continues to see a role for itself: in its view, the world's poorest nations remain exploited and marginalized, no longer by opposing superpowers, but rather in a uni-polar world,[[28]](#cite_note-xii-summit-28) and it is Western hegemony and neo-colonialism that the movement has really re-aligned itself against. It opposes foreign occupation, interference in internal affairs and aggressive unilateral measures, but it has also shifted to focus on the socio-economic challenges facing member states, especially the inequalities manifested by [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization") and the implications of [neo-liberal policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism "Neoliberalism"). The Non-Aligned Movement has identified economic [underdevelopment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underdevelopment "Underdevelopment"), [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty "Poverty"), and social injustices as growing threats to peace and security.[[28]](#cite_note-xii-summit-28)

The [16th NAM summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "16th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement") took place in [Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran "Tehran"), Iran, from 26 to 31 August 2012. According to the Teheran-based [Mehr News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehr_News_Agency "Mehr News Agency"), representatives from over 150 countries were scheduled to attend.[[29]](#cite_note-MehrNews-29) Attendance at the highest level included 27 presidents, two kings and emirs, seven prime ministers, nine vice-presidents, two parliamentary spokesmen and five special envoys.[[30]](#cite_note-tehrantimes1-30) At the summit, Iran took over from Egypt as Chair of the Non-Aligned Movement for the period 2012 to 2015.[[31]](#cite_note-allafrica-31)

In 2016 Venezuela hosted the 17th NAM Summit.[[32]](#cite_note-32)[[33]](#cite_note-33)

Azerbaijan, host of the [18th NAM summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "18th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement") in 2019, holds the Non-Aligned Movement presidency pending the [19th NAM summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "19th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement"), which took place in Kampala, Uganda in January 2024.[[34]](#cite_note-34)

The [20th NAM summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement?action=edit&redlink=1 "20th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement (page does not exist)") is expected to be hosted by [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan").[[35]](#cite_note-:1-35)[[36]](#cite_note-:2-36)

## Organizational structure and membership

The movement stems from a desire not to be aligned within a geopolitical/military structure and therefore itself does not have a very strict organizational structure.[[3]](#cite_note-organizational-3) Some organizational basics were defined at the 1996 *Cartagena Document on Methodology*[[37]](#cite_note-Methodology-37) The Summit Conference of Heads of State or Government of Non-Aligned States is "the highest decision making authority". The chairmanship rotates between countries and changes at every summit of heads of state or government to the country organizing the summit.[[37]](#cite_note-Methodology-37)

Requirements for membership of the Non-Aligned Movement coincide with the key beliefs of the United Nations. The current requirements are that the candidate country has displayed practices in accordance with the ten "Bandung principles" of 1955:[[37]](#cite_note-Methodology-37)

* Respect for fundamental human rights and for the purposes and principles of the [Charter of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_the_United_Nations "Charter of the United Nations").
* Respect for the sovereignty and territorial integrity of all nations.
* Recognition of the movements for national independence.
* Recognition of the equality of all races and of the equality of all nations, large and small.
* Abstention from intervention or interference in the internal affairs of another country.
* Respect for the right of each nation to defend itself singly or collectively, in conformity with the Charter of the United Nations.
* Refraining from acts or threats of aggression or the use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of any country.
* Settlement of all international disputes by peaceful means, in conformity with the Charter of the United Nations.
* Promotion of mutual interests and co-operation.
* Respect for justice and international obligations.

## Policies and ideology

Chairpersons[[38]](#cite_note-auto-38) of the NAM have included such diverse figures as [Suharto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suharto "Suharto"),[[39]](#cite_note-39) a militaristic[[40]](#cite_note-40) [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_mass_killings_of_1965–66 "Indonesian mass killings of 1965–66"); [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela "Nelson Mandela"), a [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist "Democratic socialist") and famous [anti-apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_resistance_to_South_African_apartheid "Internal resistance to South African apartheid") activist; [Mohamed Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Morsi "Mohamed Morsi"), a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_conservatism "Religious conservatism") [Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamism "Islamism"); [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito "Josip Broz Tito"), a [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism–Leninism "Marxism–Leninism"), [Yugoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia "Yugoslavia") leader; and [Ernesto Samper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Samper "Ernesto Samper"), a [Colombian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_in_Colombia "Liberalism in Colombia") [social liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_liberalism "Social liberalism"). Consisting of many governments with vastly different ideologies, the Non-Aligned Movement is unified by its declared commitment to [world peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace "World peace") and security. At the seventh summit held in New Delhi in March 1983, the movement described itself as "history's biggest peace movement".[[41]](#cite_note-thomas-41) The movement places equal emphasis on [disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disarmament "Disarmament"). NAM's commitment to peace pre-dates its formal [institutionalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutionalisation "Institutionalisation") in 1961. The [Brioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brijuni "Brijuni") meeting between heads of governments of India, Egypt and Yugoslavia in 1956 recognized that there exists a vital link between struggle for peace and endeavours for disarmament.[[41]](#cite_note-thomas-41)

During the 1970s and early 1980s, the NAM also sponsored campaigns for restructuring commercial relations between developed and developing nations, namely the [New International Economic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_International_Economic_Order "New International Economic Order") (NIEO), and its cultural offspring, the [New World Information and Communication Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_Information_and_Communication_Order "New World Information and Communication Order") (NWICO). The latter, on its own, sparked a Non-Aligned initiative on cooperation for communications, the [Non-Aligned News Agencies Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_News_Agencies_Pool "Non-Aligned News Agencies Pool"), created in 1975 and later converted into the [NAM News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAM_News_Network "NAM News Network") in 2005.

The Non-Aligned Movement espouses policies and practices of cooperation, especially those that are multilateral and provide mutual benefit to all those involved. Almost all of the members of the Non-Aligned Movement are also members of the United Nations. Both organizations have a stated policy of peaceful cooperation, yet the successes the NAM has had with multilateral agreements tend to be ignored by the larger, western- and developed- nation-dominated UN.[[42]](#cite_note-autogenerated3-42) African concerns about apartheid were linked with Arab-Asian [concerns about Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_nationalism "Palestinian nationalism")[[42]](#cite_note-autogenerated3-42) and multilateral cooperation in these areas has enjoyed moderate success. The Non-Aligned Movement has played a major role in various [ideological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideological "Ideological") conflicts throughout its existence, including extreme opposition to apartheid governments and support of guerrilla movements in various locations, including [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia "Rhodesia") and South Africa.[[43]](#cite_note-autogenerated1-43)

## Current activities and positions

### Reform of the UN Security Council

The movement has been outspoken in its criticism of current UN structures and power dynamics, and advocating for the [reforming of the United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council "Reform of the United Nations Security Council"), stating that the organization has been used by powerful states in ways that violate the movement's principles. It has made a number of recommendations that it says would strengthen the representation and power of "non-aligned" states. The proposed [UN reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_of_the_United_Nations "Reform of the United Nations") are also aimed at improving the transparency and democracy of UN decision-making. The [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council") is the element it considers the most distorted, undemocratic, and in need of reshaping.[[44]](#cite_note-44)

### Self-determination of Puerto Rico

Since 1961, the organization has supported the discussion of the case of [Puerto Rico's self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Puerto_Rico "Political status of Puerto Rico") before the United Nations.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] A resolution on the matter was to be proposed on the XV Summit by the [Hostosian National Independence Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostosian_National_Independence_Movement "Hostosian National Independence Movement") but did not progress.

### Self-determination of Western Sahara

Since 1973, the group has supported the discussion of the case of [Western Sahara's self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Sahara_conflict "Western Sahara conflict") before the United Nations.[[45]](#cite_note-45) The movement reaffirmed in its meeting (Sharm El Sheikh 2009) the support to the Self-determination of the [Sahrawi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_people "Sahrawi people") by choosing between any valid option, welcomed the direct conversations between the parties, and remembered the responsibility of the United Nations on the Sahrawi issue.[[46]](#cite_note-46)

### Sustainable developments

The movement is publicly committed to the tenets of [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development "Sustainable development") and the attainment of the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals "Millennium Development Goals"), but it believes that the international community has not created conditions conducive to development and has infringed upon the right to sovereign development by each member state. Issues such as globalization, the [debt burden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries'_debt "Developing countries' debt"), [unfair trade practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_justice "Trade justice"), the decline in foreign aid, [donor conditionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditionality "Conditionality"), and the lack of democracy in international financial decision-making are cited as factors inhibiting development.[[47]](#cite_note-47)

### Criticism of US foreign policy

In recent years the organization has criticized certain aspects of US foreign policy. The [2003 invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq "2003 invasion of Iraq") and the [war on terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_terrorism "War on terrorism"), its attempts to stifle [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea")'s nuclear plans, and its other actions have been denounced by some members of the Non-Aligned Movement as attempts to run roughshod over the sovereignty of smaller nations; at the most recent[*[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*] summit, [Kim Yong-nam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yong-nam "Kim Yong-nam"), chairman of North Korea's parliamentary standing committee, stated, "The United States is attempting to deprive other countries of even their legitimate right to peaceful nuclear activities."[[48]](#cite_note-48)

### NAM Centres

#### Centre for South-South Technical Cooperation

"Non-Aligned Movement Centre for South-South Technical Cooperation" redirects here. For South-South Cooperation, see [South–South cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South–South_cooperation "South–South cooperation").

The Non-Aligned Movement Centre for South-South Technical Cooperation (NAM CSSTC) as an [intergovernmental institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_organization "Intergovernmental organization"), which enables developing countries to increase national capacity and their collective [self-reliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-sustainability "Self-sustainability"),[[49]](#cite_note-49) forms part of the efforts of NAM.[[50]](#cite_note-50) The NAM CSSTC is located in [Jakarta, Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta") with a South-South Technical Cooperation focus.[[51]](#cite_note-51) The NAM CSSTC was set up a few years after the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") to promote development in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries "Developing countries") and to accelerate growth. From 18 to 20 October 1995, in [Cartagena de Indias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartagena,_Colombia "Cartagena, Colombia"), 140 nations gathered and accepted a final document stating in paragraph 313 of the Final Document the establishment of the Centre for South-South Technical Cooperation in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia").

The organization aims to achieve the development goal of developing countries to achieve [sustainable human development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals "Sustainable Development Goals") and enable developing countries to be equal partners in [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations "International relations"), in accordance with the Final Document.

The NAM CSSTC's main body is the board of directors.[[52]](#cite_note-52)[[53]](#cite_note-53) In addition, the Board of Directors has a consultative arrangement with a Governing Council under the leadership of the [Vice Minister for Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendra_Siregar "Mahendra Siregar") and its members include Ambassador of Brunei, Ambassador of Cuba and Ambassador of South Africa. The head of the administrative officer of NAM CSSTC is accredited by Ronny Prasetyo Yuliantoro, Director, current Indonesian diplomat and Ambassador of the Republic of Indonesia to the [Islamic Republic of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), who began his term of office on 1 July 2018.[[54]](#cite_note-54) The organization is financed by Indonesia's volunteer contributions.[[55]](#cite_note-55)[[56]](#cite_note-56) The NAM CSSTC, its officers, consists of a full-time staff who are not affiliated with any other governmental institution except their head of the administrative officer, who is typically nominated from Echelon-I or Echelon-II staff from the Indonesian ministries. Some say the organization is a major endeavour to [build NAM member countries' capacities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacity_building "Capacity building").[[57]](#cite_note-57)

##### History

A few years before the NAM CSSTC was set up, the NAM summit in 1992 in Jakarta to discuss efforts to strengthen collective autonomy and to review of the international economic environment in order to step up [South-South cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-South_cooperation "South-South cooperation").[[58]](#cite_note-58)

After the admission of Brunei Darussalam to the NAM during the summit, the Government of the Republic of Indonesia called for a South-South Technical Cooperation Centre (now known as the NAM CSSTC) to be established by the Government of the Republic of Indonesia and the Government of [Brunei Darussalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei_Darussalam "Brunei Darussalam") with the aim of organizing different training, research and seminar programmes and activities. The programme activities, aimed at [eradicating poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty "Poverty"), encouraging [SMEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium-sized_enterprises "Small and medium-sized enterprises") and the application of [information communication technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology "Information and communications technology").

##### Programmes

The NAM CSSTC carries out its activities through cooperation with NAM member countries' training centres and specialists and other multilateral organizations. Examples include Workshop on [IUU fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUU_fishing "IUU fishing") eradication,[[59]](#cite_note-59) dispatch of agricultural experts to [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar")[[60]](#cite_note-60) and international tissue culture training.[[61]](#cite_note-61)

##### Evaluations

NAM CSSTC reports quarterly to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Indonesia and the NAM Coordinating Bureau in New York. Annually, the Ministry and the Bureau will be given additional details on programmes and events, including their assessments.[[62]](#cite_note-62)

Other NAM Centres focus on the health, human rights (Center for Human Rights and Cultural Diversity) and technology (Centre for Science and Technology of the Non-aligned and Other Developing Countries) sectors are each located in [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba"), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India").

### Youth Organization

"Non-Aligned Movement Youth Organization" redirects here. For Youth Organizations, see [Youth organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_organizations "Youth organizations").

The Non-Alligned Movement Youth Organization, abrreviated to NAMYO, is the Non-Alligned Movement's youth branch, which collaberates with the youth of NAM member states to "exchange ideas, visions, and perspectives on current challenges."[[63]](#cite_note-63)Its first summit, at that time as the Non-Alligned Movement Youth Network, took place during the [18th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "18th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement") in [Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku "Baku"), Azerbaijan. It was officially inaugurated in a virtual meeting held on October 4-5 of 2021. In 2022, the second NAM Youth Summit resulted in the formal adoption of the Shusha Accords which transformed the NAM Youth Network into the NAM Youth Organization.[[64]](#cite_note-64) It currently holds over 60 national chapters around the world.[[65]](#cite_note-65)

### Cultural diversity and human rights

The movement accepts the universality of human rights and social justice, but fiercely resists cultural homogenization.[[66]](#cite_note-66)[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] In line with its views on sovereignty, the organization appeals for the protection of cultural diversity, and the tolerance of the religious, socio-cultural, and historical particularities that define human rights in a specific region.[[67]](#cite_note-67) [*[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

A variety of ministerial meetings are held between the summit meetings. Some are specialists, such as the meeting on "Inter-Faith Dialogue and Co-operation for Peace", held in Manila, the Philippines, 16–18 March 2010. There is a general Conference of Foreign Ministers every three years. The most recent were in Bali, Indonesia, 23–27 May 2011 and Algiers, Algeria, 26–29 May 2014.

The [7th Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement "7th Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement") was originally planned for September 1982 in [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad "Baghdad"), the Iraqi capital, during the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran–Iraq_War "Iran–Iraq War").[[75]](#cite_note-75) On 21 July of that year, the [Iran Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Air_Force "Islamic Republic of Iran Air Force") executed the "Baghdad Operation", an effort to disrupt that proposal by showing Baghdad's airspace was unsafe.[[76]](#cite_note-76) Two [McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_F-4_Phantom_II "McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II") planes bombarded Al-Dura refinery with [Mark 82 bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_82_bomb "Mark 82 bomb"). One plane returned damaged and the other (along with its pilot) was lost to Iraqi defensive fire.[[77]](#cite_note-77) Combined with threats by an Iranian-backed terror group to kill the visiting heads of state, the effort was successful. On 11 August, Iraqi President [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein "Saddam Hussein") announced that he would support Cuba's suggestion of a summit in New Delhi to be held in 1983. "Iraq will take part in the conference even if held in Tehran... We propose that the seventh conference be held in India."[[78]](#cite_note-78)

The Non-Aligned Movement [celebrated its 50th anniversary in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50th_Anniversary_Additional_Commemorative_Non-Aligned_Meeting "50th Anniversary Additional Commemorative Non-Aligned Meeting") on 5–6 September 2011.[[79]](#cite_note-79)[[80]](#cite_note-80)

An online summit titled "United Against Covid-19" conducted on 4 May 2020, on the initiative of the chairman of the NAM for the 2019–2022 period, addressed mainly the global struggle to fight the [COVID-19 pandemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019 "Coronavirus disease 2019") and supporting NAM to increase its role in dealing with and mitigating the outcomes caused by this disease in NAM, as well as other countries.[[81]](#cite_note-81)[[82]](#cite_note-82)

The Non-Aligned Movement [celebrated its 60th anniversary in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_Anniversary_Additional_Commemorative_Non-Aligned_Meeting "60th Anniversary Additional Commemorative Non-Aligned Meeting"), on 11–12 October 2021.[[83]](#cite_note-83)
