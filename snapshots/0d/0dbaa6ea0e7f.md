# French far-right leader Marine Le Pen to stand trial for embezzling EU funds

France

French prosecutors on Friday ordered far-right figurehead Marine Le Pen to stand trial over claims she used EU funds to finance party activities in France.

Issued on: Modified:

1 min Reading time

Share

By: 

[NEWS WIRES](/en/author/news-wires/ "NEWS WIRES")

The former presidential candidate will in March be joined by 26 other members of the [National Rally (RN)](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/national-rally-national-front/) party in the dock, all accused of setting up a system for embezzling [EU](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/european-union/) money to hire staff in [France](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/france/).

The fake jobs inquiry began in 2015, with prosecutors alleging that starting in 2004, National Front eurodeputies including [Le Pen](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/marine-le-pen/) took part in the fake jobs scheme.

The accused include Le Pen’s father, [Jean-Marie Le Pen](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/jean-marie-le-pen/), the co-founder of what became France’s most successful far-right party.

The party itself, as a legal entity, is suspected of receiving illicit funds, and of complicity in fraud.

Marine Le Pen was runner-up to [Emmanuel Macron](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/emmanuel-macron/) in the 2017 and 2022 presidential elections and could have another go in 2027. She was president of her party until 2021, and now leads its parliamentary group.

The charges against her are embezzlement and collusion in fraud.

Read more[French Interior Minister Darmanin warns of risk of Le Pen victory in next election](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20230827-french-interior-minister-darmanin-warns-of-risk-of-le-pen-victory-in-next-election)

The decision to go to trial was taken by two investigating magistrates from France’s financial crimes prosecuting unit.

The group is accused of using EU parliamentary funds to pay for assistants who in fact worked for the National Rally party, formerly called the National Front.

Le Pen, who stepped down as an MEP in 2017 after her election to the French parliament, has denied the claims.

The charges carry sentences of up to 10 years in prison and a fine of up to double the alleged funds embezzled.

If convicted, the court could also declare Le Pen ineligible for office for up to 10 years – threatening her plan to make a fourth run for the French presidency.

The EU Parliament estimated in 2018 that 6.8 million euros ($7.3 million at today’s rates) had been embezzled from 2009 to 2017.
