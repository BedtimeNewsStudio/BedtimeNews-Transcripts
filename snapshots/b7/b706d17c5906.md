Mega Projects in Egypt constitute one of the cornerstones of the country’s transition to the New Republic and the modernization of its economic and social infrastructure. These large-scale projects aim to reshape Egypt’s development landscape, achieve comprehensive and sustainable development, and address the demographic and economic challenges that have accumulated over past decades.

**Objectives of Mega Projects**

- **Stimulating Economic Growth:** Expanding productive and commercial activities while enhancing Egypt’s export capacity.
- **Creating Employment Opportunities:** Generating millions of direct and indirect jobs for young people and contributing to lower unemployment.
- **Attracting Investment:** Establishing modern infrastructure and integrated economic zones that create new opportunities for both domestic and foreign investors.
- **Expanding Beyond the Nile Valley:** Redistributing population growth through the development of new cities and communities, helping accommodate population growth and reduce congestion in existing urban centers.

#### The New Capital

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/788764/1.jpg?width=285&height=171&mode=max)

The New Capital is currently Egypt’s largest national and investment project. It represents a transformative step in urban planning and has been designed as a smart, sustainable city that supports Egypt’s transition to the New Republic.

**Project Overview**

- **Announcement Date:** March 2015, during the Egypt Economic Development Conference.
- **Location:** East of Cairo, approximately 45 km from downtown Cairo and 80 km from Suez, with direct access via major regional highways.
- **Total Area:** Approximately 170,000 feddans (around 688 km²), comparable in size to Singapore.
- **Developer:** Administrative Capital for Urban Development (ACUD), with a paid-in capital of EGP 204 billion.

**Strategic Objectives**

The New Capital has been developed as a world-class smart and sustainable city that serves as Egypt’s new administrative, economic, and cultural hub.

Its key objectives include:

- **Reducing Population Density:** Relieving pressure on Greater Cairo, one of the most densely populated metropolitan areas in the world.
- **Relocating Government Institutions:** Providing a modern headquarters for ministries, Parliament, the Presidency, and government agencies while facilitating faster, fully digital public services.
- **Creating a Financial and Business Hub:** Establishing an attractive environment for domestic and international investment and serving as a major economic center for Cairo, the Suez Canal region, and the Middle East.
- **Supporting Smart and Sustainable Development:** Integrating technology into all city services, including waste management, security systems, and digital government services.
- **Expanding Green Spaces:** Allocating extensive areas for parks and landscaping, including the Green River, to enhance environmental sustainability and quality of life.
- **Promoting Renewable Energy:** Increasing reliance on renewable energy sources, particularly solar power.
- **Encouraging Healthy Living:** Designing a pedestrian-friendly city, with approximately 40% of its transportation network dedicated to walking and cycling.
- **Creating Employment Opportunities:** Generating millions of jobs in construction, technology, and services while contributing to comprehensive economic development.

**Development Phases**

The city is being developed in three main phases:

- **Phase One:** Covers approximately 40,000 feddans and includes most of the city's major operational districts and landmarks.
- **Phase Two:** Covers approximately 47,000 feddans, with infrastructure works commencing during the second half of 2024.
- **Phase Three:** The largest phase, covering approximately 97,000 feddans and representing the city's long-term expansion vision. It will include major investment zones, green areas, and supporting service districts connected to the Suez Canal Corridor and Canal Cities.

**Population Capacity:** Upon full completion, the city is expected to accommodate approximately 6.5 million residents and create around 2 million job opportunities.

#### El Dabaa Nuclear Power Plant

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/788791/1.jpg?width=287&height=215&mode=max)

The El Dabaa Nuclear Power Plant is Egypt’s first nuclear power project for the peaceful generation of nuclear electricity and one of the country’s largest strategic energy projects. Located in Matrouh Governorate, the plant is expected to play a significant role in diversifying Egypt’s energy sources and advancing long-term sustainable development.

**Project Objectives**

The project aims to:

- Diversify Egypt’s energy resources.
- Provide a stable source of clean electricity.
- Support economic development and industrial growth.

**Location and Key Facts**

- **Location:** El Dabaa City, Matrouh Governorate, on Egypt’s northwestern Mediterranean coast, approximately 289 km from Cairo.
- **Technology:** Four Generation III+ VVER-1200 nuclear reactors.
- **Total Installed Capacity:** 4,800 MW.
- **Project Cost:** Approximately USD 28.75 billion.
- **Financing:** Russia is financing 85% of the project cost through a state loan, while Egypt is covering the remaining 15%.

**Construction and Implementation**

The plant consists of four reactor units being constructed in successive phases.

- **Unit 1:** Scheduled to complete construction and enter the commissioning phase before being connected to the national electricity grid by the end of 2028.
- **Full Project Completion:** Targeted for 2030.

**Recent Developments**

**Major Equipment Delivery:** In May 2026, the steam turbine condenser for Unit 1 arrived at El Dabaa Specialized Port from South Korea. Weighing approximately 1,500 tonnes, it is one of the largest components of the plant’s conventional island.

**Small Modular Reactor Studies:** Egypt and Russia’s Rosatom are studying the potential deployment of Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) as part of future cooperation in the nuclear energy sector.

**Workforce Development:** The project includes extensive training and qualification programs for Egyptian engineers and technicians to prepare them for the future operation, maintenance, and management of the facility.

**Safety and Technology:** The plant uses Generation III+ VVER-1200 reactor technology, which incorporates multiple active and passive safety systems in accordance with modern international nuclear safety standards.

Key safety features include:

- **Passive Safety Systems:** Capable of operating without external power or operator intervention during emergency situations.
- **Core Catcher:** A dedicated safety system designed to contain molten core material in the event of a severe accident.
- **Enhanced Structural Protection:** Reactor buildings are designed to withstand extreme external events, including earthquakes and aircraft impact.

**Economic and Development Impact**

**Supporting Energy Security:** The plant will contribute 4,800 MW of electricity to Egypt’s national grid, helping to meet the country’s growing energy demand.

**Supporting Natural Gas Exports:** By generating electricity from nuclear energy, the project is expected to reduce the consumption of natural gas used for power generation, making additional volumes available for industrial use and export.

**Local Content and Technology Transfer:** The project includes a progressive increase in local participation, with Egypt targeting a local content share of up to 35% in the fourth reactor unit. This will support the development of domestic industrial capabilities and expertise in nuclear technology.

**Residential and Service Facilities:** A dedicated residential community has been established near the project site to accommodate employees and support the plant’s long-term operation.

**Project Area**

The New Delta Project encompasses the reclamation and cultivation of approximately 2.2 million feddans, making it one of the largest agricultural expansion projects in the region.

#### Toshka El Kheir Project

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/788886/2.jpg?width=285&height=122&mode=max)

The Toshka El Kheir Project is the largest land reclamation project in the Middle East and one of the main pillars of Egypt’s national program to expand the country’s cultivated area by 4 million new feddans.

The project aims to reclaim more than 1 million feddans and transform them into productive agricultural land to strengthen food security and reduce the import bill.

**Project Overview**

- **Launch:** The project was launched in 1997 during the presidency of former President Hosni Mubarak with the objective of establishing a new agricultural delta parallel to the Nile Valley through the Sheikh Zayed Canal.
- **Setbacks:** By 2010, only 6.6% of the targeted area had been cultivated due to funding shortages and engineering challenges.
- **Relaunch:** In 2014, President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi relaunched the project under the name Toshka El Kheir as part of the national plan to cultivate 4 million feddans.

**Project Objectives**

- **Expand the Cultivated Area:** Reclaim approximately 500,000 feddans in the first phase, increasing to 1 million feddans.
- **Enhance Food Security:** Reduce dependence on imported wheat so that Egypt will need to import less than one-quarter of its requirements.
- **Promote Economic Development:** Increase agricultural exports, such as dates and potatoes, while reducing the trade deficit.
- **Create Employment Opportunities:** Generate hundreds of thousands of direct and indirect jobs, particularly for young people in Upper Egypt.
- **Establish New Communities:** Develop new agricultural and industrial communities while reducing population pressure on the Nile Valley.

**Location and Area**

- **Location:** Toshka Depression, south of Aswan Governorate, in the Western Desert.
- **Target Area:** Approximately 1.1 million feddans, hundreds of thousands of which have already been cultivated and developed.

**Engineering Components**

- **Main Toshka Canal:** Extends approximately 50 kilometers, conveying water from Lake Nasser with a pumping capacity exceeding 7 million cubic meters per day.
- **Main Pumping Station:** One of the largest pumping stations in the world, equipped with seven high-capacity pumps.
- **Irrigation Networks:** Utilizes modern irrigation technologies, including center-pivot and drip irrigation systems.
- **Infrastructure:** Includes the construction of new roads, regulating barrages, and the lining of secondary canals.

**Main Crops and Production**

The project focuses primarily on reducing the gap in strategic crops.

- **Wheat:** The project's principal crop, with production contributing to reducing Egypt’s grain import bill.
- **Date Palms:** The project includes the world's largest date farm, covering 38,000 feddans, with a target of planting 2.5 million premium date palms, including Medjool and Barhi varieties, for export.
- **Other Crops:** Maize, grapes, lemons, and successful cultivation trials of long-staple cotton.

**Engineering Challenges and Achievements**

- **Sheikh Zayed Canal:** Transports Nile water from Lake Nasser into the heart of the desert.
- **Overcoming Natural Obstacles:** Thousands of metric tons of explosives were used to remove a 9-kilometer rock barrier that had prevented water from reaching thousands of feddans, representing a major engineering achievement carried out by Egyptian personnel.
- **Toshka Pumping Station:** One of the world's largest water pumping stations, utilizing advanced technology to ensure a sustainable supply of irrigation water.

#### Solar Power Projects

Solar power plays a central role in Egypt’s strategy to diversify its energy sources and expand the use of renewable energy, particularly in Upper Egypt. The Obelisk and Abydos 1 projects represent the latest milestones in Egypt’s renewable energy strategy, marking a shift toward hybrid systems that combine solar power with battery energy storage to enhance the stability of the national electricity grid.

**Egypt's Renewable Energy Plan (2030–2040)**

Egypt aims to increase the share of renewable energy in its national energy mix according to the following targets:

- **By 2030:** Renewable energy to account for 42% of the total energy mix, including 22% from solar power.
- **By 2040:** Increase the share of renewable energy to between 60% and 65%.

**Future Projects**

- **Abydos 2:** Planned capacity of 1,000 MW.
- **Energy Valley Project (Minya):** Planned capacity of 1.7 GW.

**Why Battery Energy Storage?** Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) help reduce fluctuations in solar power generation, which ceases after sunset, ensuring a continuous electricity supply during periods of peak demand without increasing natural gas consumption.

**Economic Impact:** These projects reduce the use of natural gas for conventional power generation, enabling Egypt to export additional volumes and generate foreign currency revenues.

**Employment:** Solar power projects in Aswan have created thousands of direct and indirect jobs during both the construction and operational phases.

**Grid Integration:** Solar power plants, including Benban, Abydos, and Obelisk, are integrated into Egypt’s unified national electricity grid through an advanced engineering system designed to maintain grid stability and transmit electricity from Upper Egypt to major consumption centers in the north.

**Gas-Insulated Substations (GIS):** Advanced gas-insulated substations (GIS) have been constructed within the solar power complexes to serve as collection points for generated electricity. These substations receive low-voltage electricity generated by the solar panels and step it up to 220 kV or 500 kV, reducing transmission losses over long distances. The Benban Solar Park includes four main GIS substations that ensure reliable and uninterrupted operation.

**Transmission Lines:** Electricity is transmitted through high-voltage overhead transmission lines extending hundreds of kilometers. The Benban complex is connected to the national grid through the Benban–Nag Hammadi and Benban–Toshka transmission lines, allowing electricity to be distributed throughout Upper Egypt or transmitted north to Cairo and the Nile Delta.

**Smart Grid Control:** Because solar power generation varies with solar irradiation and ceases after sunset, smart control centers continuously monitor electricity generation. When solar output declines, the control centers automatically dispatch electricity from other generation sources, such as gas-fired power plants or the High Dam, to maintain grid frequency and ensure uninterrupted electricity supply.

**Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS):** Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) represent the latest advancement in Egypt’s solar power projects. In projects such as Abydos, battery systems store surplus electricity generated during periods of peak daytime production and discharge it into the national grid during periods of peak evening demand.

**Inverters:** Each solar power plant uses inverters to convert the direct current (DC) generated by photovoltaic panels into alternating current (AC) compatible with the national electricity grid.

**Importance of Grid Integration:** The integrated transmission network positions Egypt to export surplus renewable electricity through future regional electricity interconnection projects, including those with Jordan and Saudi Arabia.

**Latest Developments (2026):** In early 2026, new agreements were signed to integrate solar power plants with green hydrogen projects in the Suez Canal region. Electricity generated by the Aswan facilities will be used in electrolysis processes for green hydrogen production.

**Investment and International Recognition**

**Awards**

**Environmental and Social Impact**

**Future Expansion**

#### Ports and Logistics Corridors

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/791636/20251014105930180.jpg?width=389&height=259&mode=max)![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/791636/20251014105930180.jpg?width=0&height=0)

Since 2014, Egypt has pursued a comprehensive strategy to transform the country into a global trade and logistics hub by developing its ports and integrating them with modern logistics corridors comprising railways, highways, and dry ports.

**First: Logistics Corridors**

Egypt's logistics strategy is based on developing integrated logistics corridors that connect industrial, agricultural, and mining production areas with ports through efficient multimodal transport networks.

- **Sokhna–Dekheila Corridor:** A major logistics corridor linking Ain Sokhna Port on the Red Sea with Dekheila and Alexandria Ports on the Mediterranean. Designed to strengthen Egypt's position as a global trade and logistics hub, it is widely known as the "Land Suez Canal."
- **Arish–Taba Corridor:** A 500-km multimodal logistics corridor extending from the Mediterranean coast to the Gulf of Aqaba. It is designed to support the development of the Sinai Peninsula and enhance transit trade.
- **Cairo–Alexandria Corridor:** Connects Greater Cairo, Alexandria, major industrial zones, dry ports, and ports.
- **Tanta–Mansoura–Damietta Corridor:** Connects the agricultural and industrial production areas of the Nile Delta with Damietta Port.

**Second: Ports**

Egypt is implementing a nationwide plan to develop 18 ports, expanding their total quay length to approximately 100 km.

**Sokhna Port**

**Development:** Sokhna Port is being transformed into a major hub port on the Red Sea through the construction of 18 km of new quays and additional port basins, significantly increasing its container-handling capacity.

**Strategic Importance:** The port serves as the principal gateway for the High-Speed Electric Rail network and Egypt's green fuel projects.

**East Port Said Port**

- Ranked among the world's top ten most efficient ports, according to recent World Bank reports.
- Features a major container terminal and an integrated logistics zone serving international transit trade.

**Greater Alexandria Port (Alexandria – Dekheila – El Max)**

**Tahya Misr Multipurpose Terminal:** A fully automated multipurpose terminal designed to accommodate the world's largest vessels.

**El Max Port**

Currently under development, El Max Port will integrate with Alexandria and Dekheila Ports to form the Greater Alexandria Port, one of the largest port complexes in the Mediterranean.

**Damietta Port**

- **Tahya Misr 1 Terminal:** A container terminal with an annual handling capacity of 3.5 million TEUs, supported by river transport links connecting the port with Cairo via the Nile.

**Gargoub Port**

Located in Matrouh Governorate, Gargoub is a new commercial, industrial, and tourism port designed to strengthen trade with Libya and Europe while facilitating exports from the New Delta Project.

**Third: Dry Ports and Logistics Zones**

For the first time, Egypt has established a network of dry ports to reduce congestion at ports and accelerate cargo clearance.

**6th of October Dry Port:** Egypt's first fully operational dry port, connected to Alexandria Port by rail.

**10th of Ramadan Dry Port:** Designed to serve the major industrial zones east of Cairo.

**Economic Impact**

- **Expanding Transit Trade:** Increasing Egypt's share of global transit trade across the Mediterranean and Red Sea.
- **Reducing Transport Time and Costs:** Integrated logistics corridors reduce transport costs and transit times, enhancing the competitiveness of Egyptian exports.
- **Digital Transformation:** All Egyptian ports operate through the Single Window system, streamlining customs procedures and accelerating cargo clearance.

#### High-Speed Electric Rail Network

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/791646/5.jpg?width=363&height=191&mode=max)

The High-Speed Electric Rail (HSR) network is a cornerstone of Egypt's modern transport system, combining operating speeds of up to 250 km/h with high standards of safety, comfort, and efficiency.

Serving as a strategic logistics corridor between the Red Sea and the Mediterranean, the network connects major industrial centers—including Sokhna, 6th of October City, Borg El Arab, and Aswan—with the country's principal export ports at Sokhna and Alexandria, strengthening national supply chains and facilitating the movement of goods.

The HSR network also supports Egypt's food security by linking agricultural production areas in the New Delta, Toshka, and West Minya with major consumption centers and export gateways, reducing transport time and post-harvest losses.

It promotes integrated tourism by connecting Red Sea resorts with Egypt's archaeological and heritage destinations, while integrating airports, ports, highways, and railways into a unified passenger and freight transport network.

The project further enhances national connectivity by linking the ports of Sokhna and Alexandria with the dry ports of Sohag and Beni Suef, the border logistics hubs at Ashkeet and Salloum, and mineral production areas in Abu Tartour, Qena, and Aswan.

Supporting Egypt's urban expansion, the network connects fourth-generation cities—including the New Capital, New Alamein, New Qena, and New Aswan—while extending rail services to new development corridors such as Qena–Hurghada and Aswan–Abu Simbel.

As a key pillar of Egypt's green transport strategy, the High-Speed Electric Rail network provides high-capacity, low-emission transport and is expected to connect 60 cities through an advanced smart transport network.

**First Line**

The first line extends 660 km from Sokhna Port on the Red Sea to Marsa Matrouh on the Mediterranean via 21 stations, including 13 high-speed rail stations and 8 regional stations. Often described as a "Suez Canal on rails," it creates a direct land corridor between the Red Sea and the Mediterranean for both passengers and freight.

A proposed 59-km extension is under study to connect the line with Suez and New Suez.

**Operating Speeds**

- High-Speed Passenger Trains: 250 km/h
- Regional Trains: 160 km/h
- Freight Trains: 120 km/h

**Interchange Stations**

The first line will connect with:

- Light Rail Transit (LRT) at Capital Central Station
- The second HSR line at Hadayek October Station
- The West Nile Monorail at 6th of October Station
- The Cairo–Aswan conventional railway at Giza Station
- The planned Cairo Metro Line 6 at Ahmed Omar Hashem Station

**Capacity**

- Passengers: 1 million per day
- Freight: 4 million tonnes annually

**Project Implementation**

- Civil Works: Leading Egyptian construction companies
- Rail Systems and Rolling Stock: Siemens (Germany)
- Station Electromechanical Works: Consortium of Orascom Construction, The Arab Contractors, and Elsewedy Electric

#### Cairo Monorail

The Cairo Monorail is a two-line monorail rapid transit system, which will be the longest driverless monorail system in the world. The two lines provide the first public transit links from the New Capital and 6th of October City to the Cairo metropolitan area, facilitating the movement of residents and visitors of Greater Cairo, reducing congestion and reliance on automobiles, and improving air quality.

**East Nile Monorail**

**Overview**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/791647/2026-639096041841879312-187.jpeg?width=425&height=239&mode=max)**

The East Nile Monorail (Nasr City–New Capital Line) is a key component of Egypt's green and smart transportation system. It provides a rapid mass transit link between Greater Cairo and the New Capital while helping to reduce traffic congestion in East Cairo by serving densely populated and rapidly growing urban areas.

**Route**

The line extends 56.5 km from Cairo Stadium Station in Nasr City to Justice City Station in the New Administrative Capital and includes 22 stations.

**Technology and Rolling Stock**

The line operates Innovia 300 monorail trains, featuring lightweight, corrosion-resistant aluminum car bodies that improve energy efficiency.

- Signaling and Control: Alstom Cityflo 650 communications-based train control (CBTC) system, enabling fully automated, driverless operation.
- Maximum Operating Speed: 80–90 km/h
- End-to-End Journey Time: Approximately 60 minutes
- Capacity: Up to 45,000 passengers per hour per direction (PPHPD)
- Headway: Approximately 3 minutes, with the capability to decrease to 90 seconds as demand increases.

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/791648/5.jpg?width=352&height=185&mode=max)

**Interchange Stations**

The line connects with:

- Cairo Metro Line 3 at Cairo Stadium Station.

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/791649/6.jpg?width=352&height=185&mode=max)

The elevated monorail operates on a single-beam guideway and includes 22 stations, all equipped with Platform Screen Doors (PSDs) to enhance passenger safety.

**Power System**

The system is fully electric and consumes approximately 30% less energy than conventional electric rail systems.

**Ticketing (2026)**

The fare system is based on four travel zones, allowing fares to reflect the distance travelled.

**Official Inauguration**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi inaugurated the East Nile Monorail on the first day of Eid al-Fitr in March 2026.

The National Roads and Bridges Project is one of Egypt's largest integrated infrastructure initiatives.

- Develop east–west transport corridors linking industrial, agricultural, and mining areas with seaports and river ports to improve logistics and supply chains.
- Reduce transport costs by shortening travel times while lowering fuel consumption and carbon emissions.

**Major Road Projects**

- **Shobra-Benha Highway:** A 40-km access-controlled agricultural highway built at a cost of EGP 3.5 billion, making it Egypt's first agricultural highway without at-grade intersections.
- **Upper Egypt Western Desert Highway:** Upgraded into a dual carriageway with six lanes in each direction, including dedicated concrete lanes for heavy trucks. The first phase (Cairo–Minya, 230 km) has been completed.
- **Galala Road:** An 82 km mountain highway linking Ain Sokhna and Zaafarana via the Galala Plateau.
- **Regional Ring Road:** Completion of the 90 km northern sector, providing direct links between Delta governorates without passing through Greater Cairo.
- **Greater Cairo Ring Road:** Widening and upgrading the first phase (76 km) to seven or eight lanes in each direction.

**Nile Axes and bridges**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792476/54806-1-12.jpg?width=0&height=0)![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792476/54806-1-12.jpg?width=380&height=200&mode=max)**

The strategy marked a fundamental shift by replacing standalone Nile bridges with integrated east–west transport axes linking the road networks on both sides of the Nile at approximately 25-km intervals, serving industrial and agricultural development areas.

- **Target:** Increase the number of Nile development axes from 38 before 2014 to 73 through the construction of 35 new axes.
- **Completed:** 19 major axes, including Tahya Misr (Rod El Farag), Helwan, Banha, Girga, Tama, Samalut, Kalabsha, and the Aswan Dam Alternative Axis.

**International Recognition**

The expansion and modernization of Egypt's road network have contributed to significant improvements in international transport indicators:

- **Global Road Quality:** Egypt advanced 100 places in the global road quality rankings, rising from 118th in 2014 to 18th worldwide.
- **Port Infrastructure:** Improved road connectivity has enhanced the competitiveness of Egyptian ports, with East Port Said Port ranking third globally in the Container Port Performance Index (CPPI) and Alexandria Port ranking among the world's top 100 ports.

#### Robiki Leather City Project

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/791655/7.jpg?width=386&height=152&mode=max)

Al-Robbiki is one of the most important specialized industrial cities designed according to the latest international standards, and it is the first integrated industrial complex specializing in leather industries with local and foreign investments in the Middle East.

The city is built on a total area of 1,629 feddans at a cost of EGP 2.7 billion, and it specializes in the leather tanning industry. The zone of tanneries located on Magra El-Oyoun Wall in Old Cairo was moved to it. “Al-Robbiki” project is one of the most prominent major projects that took place during the past eight years. It is considered an integrated industrial city for the leather and textile industry, and it is the largest industrial city in the Middle East.

It was inaugurated by President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi in July 2020, and its main objective is to make Egypt a strategic center for the leather industry, not only at the local or regional level, but also at the international level.

The establishment of Al-Robbiki City aims to create an integrated system with the latest international technology in the field of leather tanning, with a commitment to maintaining the general shape of the city, avoiding randomness in construction and reducing the negative environmental effects resulting from the nature of production in the field of leather tanning.

The area of Al-Robbiki city is about 490 feddans, and the new industrial complex in Al-Robbiki includes 6 factories: a fine spinning factory, a thick spinning factory, 3 factories for preparing weaving and for circular and rectangular weaving, and a factory for dyeing and printing. The factories are equipped with the latest machines that operate according to the latest technological systems and programs.

**Project Description:**

- The city is divided into a "productive units and auxiliary services" zone on an area of 511 feddans. During the last period, Ministry of Commerce and Industry completed the first phase of the project with a total of 213 production units for 195 investors on an area of 176 feddans. Utilities have been introduced to 69 feddans in the second phase of the project.
- The industrial wastewater treatment plants zone includes the sanitary landfill zone on an area of 282 feddans, and EGP 200 million has been allocated to rehabilitate it and accommodate future expansions, in addition to the tree forest zone on an area of 280 feddans, which will be irrigated with wastewater after treatment.
- The city includes Leather Tanning Technology Centre (LTTC) on an area of 6000 square meters in cooperation with the Italian side, as well as integrated industries (tanneries, gelatin and glue factories, warehouses, chemicals, and stores). Establishment of a heritage museum dedicated to showing the stages of development of the leather industry in Egypt.
- The city contains public service centers such as banks, conference halls, exhibitions, maintenance centers, shopping outlets, licensing offices, a hospital, a fire station, a police station, and a communications center.

**Project Key Indicators:**

- The annual industrial growth rate in the leather sector is about 7%. The volume of industrial investments in the city is about EGP 2.5 billion. The city's annual production rate is about 150 million square feet.
- The daily production volume for each worker in the city is about 50 square feet. The volume of industrial exports annually ranges from 100 to $150 million. The production of Al-Robbiki Leather City increases by 40% over the production of Magra El- Oyoun so far.
- The cost of machinery and equipment specialized in the tanning industry in the city is about EGP 1 billion. The third phase of the project accommodates from 100 to 150 factories of leather products.
- The volume of direct job opportunities in the city is about 10,000 job opportunities and 25,000 indirect job opportunities by completing the project phases.
- A contract was signed with the largest international leather exhibition “APLF”, which has been held for 36 years in Hong Kong, and is visited by about 60,000 visitors annually to organize the exhibition in the Middle East and Africa in Cairo under the name of Robbiki Leather City, which will be held annually.

**Project Objectives:**

- Egypt regained its pioneering role in the industry for which Cairo has been famous for more than 1,000 years. The city has a modern infrastructure that overcomes all the environmental problems that were facing the tanneries of the ancient Magra El-Oyoun zone.
- The project aims to increase leather tanning production from 125 million square feet semi-finished to 350 million square feet fully finished annually, and increase the added value by completing the finishing stages, especially since 85% of the current exports of leather are unfinished.
- A study is currently being conducted to allocate 800 feddans next to Al-Robbiki Leather City to transfer approximately 17,600 workshops producing leather goods established in slums all over the state, including 13,400 workshops in Al-Muski and Bab Al-Sharia, with the aim of gathering them in one place in implementation of the directives of the political leadership in eliminating slums in residential areas.

#### Urban Regeneration for Historic Cairo (URHC)

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792481/1.jpeg?width=396&height=294&mode=max)

The Urban Regeneration for Historic Cairo (URHC) is one of Egypt's largest national heritage initiatives. It aims to restore the historic and cultural character of the capital, preserve its unique urban fabric—inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1979—and transform Historic Cairo into an open-air museum and a sustainable cultural, tourism, and economic destination.

**Strategic Vision**

The project is based on a comprehensive revitalization approach focused on:

- Restoring the urban fabric through the rehabilitation of historic buildings and the revitalization of public spaces and squares.
- Improving residents' quality of life by preserving traditional crafts and historic markets while upgrading infrastructure and public services.
- Redeveloping unsafe informal areas surrounding Historic Cairo and relocating residents to fully serviced housing communities, including Al Asmarat and Rawdat Al Sayeda.

**Key Development Areas**

**Fustat Hills Park and Ain El Hayah**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792482/urhc2.jpg?width=371&height=195&mode=max)**

- A 500-feddan urban park, one of the largest urban parks in the Middle East.
- Features three hills overlooking the Citadel and the Magra El Oyoun Aqueduct, Ain El Hayah Lake, a Roman theatre, and a range of recreational facilities.
- The adjacent Fustat View development includes 1,412 residential units and 240 hotel units.

**Magra El Oyoun Aqueduct and the Citadel Area**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792484/urhc4.jpg?width=399&height=299&mode=max)**

- Redevelopment of the area following the relocation of the historic tanneries to Robbiki Leather City.
- Includes residential, hospitality, and commercial developments inspired by Islamic architecture, traditional craft markets, open-air theatres, restaurants, and public spaces.

**Al-Hussein, Al-Azhar, and Al-Muizz Area**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792485/urhc5.jpeg?width=275&height=183)**

- Restoration of historic building façades.
- Upgrading of water, sewerage, and underground utility networks.
- Enhancement of pedestrian areas, traffic management, and traditional commercial districts.

**Darb El Labana, Bab Zuweila, and Gamaliya**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792487/urhc7.jpg?width=395&height=406&mode=max)**

- Restoration of historic houses and caravanserais.
- Adaptive reuse of heritage buildings as boutique hotels, cultural centres, and visitor facilities.

**Khedivial Cairo**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/792488/urhc8.jpg?width=355&height=200&mode=max)**

- Encompasses Downtown Cairo, including Tahrir Square, Talaat Harb Square, Ataba, Qasr El Nil Street, and Emad El Din Street.
- Restoration of the façades of more than 500 heritage buildings.
- Redevelopment of Azbakeya Garden, restoration of the historic Azbakeya Book Market, and upgrading of the Stock Exchange District and Sherifein Triangle.

**Key Figures**

- Estimated investment: EGP 100 billion.
- More than 500 heritage buildings targeted for restoration and rehabilitation.
- Redevelopment of unsafe informal areas, including Batn El Baqara, with residents relocated to more than 2,000 fully serviced housing units in Al Asmarat.
- Completion of Rawdat Al Sayeda 1 and 2 (formerly Tal Al Aqarib) and delivery of new housing units to residents.

**Sustainable Development**

In cooperation with Cairo Governorate, the Urban Development Fund is implementing initiatives to:

- Document and survey heritage assets to identify tourism and investment opportunities while preserving Historic Cairo's UNESCO World Heritage status.
- Promote sustainable mobility through electric shuttle vehicles operating in areas such as Al-Muizz Street and Al-Hussein, reducing emissions while protecting heritage sites.

#### State Strategic Command Headquarters

Egypt officially inaugurated its new State Strategic Command Headquarters in the New Capital on Saturday, July 4, 2026, marking a major milestone in the modernization of the country's military and administrative infrastructure and state crisis management capabilities.

President Abdel Fattah El Sisi, Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces, who has personally overseen the project over the past several years, will officially inaugurate the new headquarters at a memorable ceremony

The inauguration reflects the Egyptian state's vision for the future, including developing its strategic capabilities in line with the latest global systems, and ensuring integrated management and oversight of all vital state sectors and institutions.

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/793126/14.png?width=382&height=184&mode=max)

The facility is designed to serve as a unified, control, communications, and highly technologically and security-equipped center, in addition to being the headquarters of the Egyptian Armed Forces, rivaling the strongest military headquarters in the world.

Beyond its military role, the headquarters is intended to facilitate coordination between the Armed Forces and various government ministries, agencies, and state institutions through integrated communications and information systems.

**Location and iconic eightfold design**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/793124/12.jpg?width=354&height=199&mode=max)**

The headquarters occupies a strategically selected site at the heart of the New Capital. The complex spans approximately 22,000 feddans (around 92 square kilometres) and is divided into 13 integrated strategic and logistical zones.

Described as a "strategic brain" for the Egyptian state, the complex consists of eight interconnected main buildings, representing the various branches and strategic departments of the Egyptian Armed Forces, with two central structures dedicated to political leadership.

Its main components include:

\- **The Octagon:** The headquarters' central complex consists of eight octagonal buildings representing the various branches of the Armed Forces and sovereign departments.

At its centre sits the main command building, designed to facilitate the rapid flow of information and operational directives.

\- **Cloud data centres:** Large-scale digital infrastructure dedicated to storing, processing and protecting national data using advanced cybersecurity measures.

\- **Service and logistics facilities:** Including independent power systems, modern water and cooling networks, and residential facilities designed to ensure operational continuity for extended periods under exceptional circumstances.

**Advanced command-and-control system**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/793122/13.jpg?width=352&height=198&mode=max)**

The headquarters relies on an advanced command-and-control network incorporating fifth- and sixth-generation technologies.

Its capabilities include:

\- **Secure real-time connectivity:** High-speed fibre-optic networks and encrypted wireless communications linking the headquarters with state institutions, military formations and governorates.

\- **Artificial intelligence:** AI-powered systems capable of analysing vast amounts of data and displaying the results on large command screens to support real-time decision-making.

\- **Cybersecurity:** Multi-layered cyber defence systems designed to protect classified information and critical infrastructure from digital threats.

**Key objectives**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/793130/22.jpg?width=383&height=177&mode=max)**

The headquarters was built to address several strategic and security priorities.

These priorities include ensuring an administrative and strategic centralization, through consolidating sovereign headquarters and operations rooms into a single secure complex to facilitate rapid information sharing and decision-making.

In addition, it aims to respond to modern threats through enhancing preparedness against fourth- and fifth-generation warfare, and cyber threats that require advanced digital defence systems.

Furthermore, the facility provides a centralized crisis management through establishing a unified command centre linking Egypt's governorates, ministries, and state institutions to coordinate responses to emergencies and disasters with maximum speed and efficiency.

**President El-Sisi reviews construction progress of the Strategic Command Headquarters**

**![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/793123/15.jpg?width=371&height=296&mode=max)**

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi, President of the Republic and Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces, closely followed the construction and development of the Strategic Command Headquarters through a series of inspection visits to review progress and advanced technical preparations ahead of its inauguration.

The most notable visits included:

**November 2020:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi visited the State’s Strategic Leadership Center (now known as the Strategic Command Headquarters) in the New Capital. During the early construction phase, he reviewed the master plan for the massive "Octagon" complex, which comprises eight interconnected octagonal buildings and advanced command-and-control systems.

**May 2023:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi conducted an extensive inspection of the Strategic Command Headquarters in the New Capital. This visit allowed him to review construction progress, operational readiness, and the academic programs for new cadets, while commending the efforts to keep the project on schedule.

**July 2024:** President El-Sisi toured the headquarters' key facilities and the Egyptian Military Academy, emphasizing that the complex would play a vital role in strengthening Egypt's national security and safeguarding the country's capabilities across all strategic directions.

**June 2026:** President El-Sisi inspected the Strategic Command Headquarters in the New Capital. The President’s surprise inspection visit came as part of his close monitoring of the progress of work, as well as to ensure readiness across various specializations, which represents a significant qualitative and quantitative advancement in the development of the Armed Forces’ military capabilities.

#### Silicon Production Complex in New Alamein

![](https://sis.gov.eg/media/799307/8943f203-cbef-465e-ad90-3d3d3dccce7d_whatsapp_image_2026-06-28_at_35555_pm.jpeg?width=392&height=206&mode=max)

The Silicon Production Complex in New Alamein is one of Egypt's most significant strategic projects in the petroleum and mineral resources sector. It aims to maximize the value of Egypt's mineral wealth by developing downstream industries and reducing reliance on imports.

**Key Facts**

01. The project is a public-private partnership designed to process Egypt's mineral resources domestically rather than exporting them as raw materials, increasing value added and supporting the national economy.

02. Shareholders include the Egyptian Petrochemical Holding Company (ECHEM), the Egyptian General Authority for Mineral Resources, the Egyptian Company for Mineral Resources, the Holding Company for Metallurgical Industries, the Egyptian Ferroalloys Company, Ibda Development Company, Libra Capital, and Central Desert Mining Company.

03. The complex will be built on a 200-acre site owned by ECHEM in New Alamein.

04. The project aims to localize the production of silicon and silicon derivatives, reducing imports and supporting strategic industries.

05. Phase One will produce 45,000 tonnes of metallic silicon annually, using high-purity Egyptian quartz. The phase has an estimated investment of US$172 million.

06. The project will supply the domestic market with metallic silicon used in aluminum production and as a raw material for silicon derivatives and polysilicon.

07. Phase Two will establish facilities to produce intermediate silicon products used in insulation materials, construction, rubber, medical products, and paper manufacturing.

08. Phase Three will establish a polysilicon plant with an initial annual capacity of 10,000 tonnes to serve the electronics, semiconductor, and solar cell industries. Phase Four will develop a cluster of small and medium-sized industries producing finished products such as insulation materials, adhesives, sealants, and rubber products.

09. The complex is planned to be powered by renewable energy, including solar power, in line with Egypt's sustainable development strategy and the global transition toward clean energy.

10. The project will be implemented under the EPC+Finance model, enabling financing through the project's general contractor without imposing financial obligations on the shareholders.
