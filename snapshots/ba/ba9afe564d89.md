**Keith Allen Raniere** ([/rænˈjɛəri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*ran-YAIR-ee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); born August 26, 1960)[[2]](#cite_note-roll_NXIV-2) is an American [cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult "Cult") leader who was convicted of a pattern of [racketeering activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racketeer_Influenced_and_Corrupt_Organizations_Act "Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act"), including [human trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_trafficking "Human trafficking"), [sex offenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_offender "Sex offender"), and [fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraud "Fraud"). Raniere co-founded [NXIVM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NXIVM "NXIVM"), a purported self-help [multi-level marketing company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-level_marketing_company "Multi-level marketing company") offering [personal development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_development "Personal development") seminars and headquartered in [Albany, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_New_York "Albany, New York"). Operating from 1998 to 2018, NXIVM had 700 members at its height, including celebrities and the wealthy.[[3]](#cite_note-3)[[4]](#cite_note-4)[[5]](#cite_note-5) Within NXIVM, Raniere was referred to as "Vanguard".[[6]](#cite_note-:1-6)

Scholars in the fields of religious studies, law, and sociology describe NXIVM as a cult.[[7]](#cite_note-7)[[8]](#cite_note-8)[[9]](#cite_note-9)[[10]](#cite_note-:7-10) Mental health professionals and cult experts such as [Rick Alan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Alan_Ross "Rick Alan Ross"), Diane Benscoter, and [Steve Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Hassan "Steve Hassan") have called Raniere a cult leader who [manipulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipulation_(psychology) "Manipulation (psychology)") and exerts [coercive control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abusive_power_and_control "Abusive power and control") over his followers.[[11]](#cite_note-11)[[12]](#cite_note-12)[[13]](#cite_note-13) Multiple women have said they were [sexually abused](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_abuse "Sexual abuse") by Raniere, including three who have reported being underage at the time of the abuse.[[14]](#cite_note-Odato_&_Gish-14)

In 2018, reports of abuse related to a secret society within NXIVM, known as "DOS" or "the Vow", led to the arrests of Raniere and five other NXIVM associates.[[6]](#cite_note-:1-6)[[15]](#cite_note-15)[[16]](#cite_note-NBCNY20180326-16) On June 19, 2019, a jury in the [Eastern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_District_of_New_York "Eastern District of New York") convicted Raniere of racketeering for a pattern of crimes, including the sexual exploitation of a child, sex trafficking of women, and conspiracy to commit [forced labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labor "Forced labor").[[1]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice_1-1)[[17]](#cite_note-NYT_Convicted-17) The court received more than 100 [victim impact statements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victim_impact_statement "Victim impact statement") detailing the harm Raniere caused.[[18]](#cite_note-18)[[19]](#cite_note-vice_1-19) On October 27, 2020, Judge [Nicholas Garaufis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Garaufis "Nicholas Garaufis") sentenced Raniere to 120 years incarceration and a $1.75 million fine.[[20]](#cite_note-:0-20)[[21]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice-21)

## Early life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Raniere was born on August 26, 1960, in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York City, the only child of James Raniere (1932–2020), an advertising executive, and Vera Oschypko (1931–1978), a ballroom-dancing instructor.[[22]](#cite_note-defsent-22)[[23]](#cite_note-CBC180912-23)[[24]](#cite_note-24) When Raniere was five years old, his family relocated to [Suffern, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffern,_New_York "Suffern, New York"). His parents [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marital_separation "Marital separation") when he was eight.[[25]](#cite_note-FR180713-25)[[26]](#cite_note-26)

Raniere attended [Suffern High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffern_High_School "Suffern High School") for ninth grade before transferring to [Rockland Country Day School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockland_Country_Day_School "Rockland Country Day School"). He graduated in June 1978, two months prior to his eighteenth birthday.[[14]](#cite_note-Odato_&_Gish-14)[[22]](#cite_note-defsent-22)[[27]](#cite_note-27) As an adult, Raniere reported that he read [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov "Isaac Asimov")'s [mind control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_control_in_popular_culture "Mind control in popular culture")-themed work *[Second Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Foundation "Second Foundation")* at age 12 and credited the novel with inspiring his work in NXIVM.[[28]](#cite_note-28)[[29]](#cite_note-forbes_yakowicz_may152019-29) In 1982, Raniere graduated from [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute "Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute") with a 2.26 [GPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPA "GPA"), barely passing and failing many classes.[[30]](#cite_note-30) He was employed as a computer programmer for the [New York State Division of Parole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Division_of_Parole "New York State Division of Parole").[[31]](#cite_note-TU120218-31)

Describing a conversation with Raniere's father, his former partner, Barbara Bouchey, recalled, "[James] said ... [they] told Keith about how gifted and how intelligent he was. And he said it was almost like a switch went off. And suddenly, overnight, [Raniere] turned into ... [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ "Jesus Christ") ... [thinking] he was superior and better than everybody, like he was a deity. [James] said it was that [snaps] dramatic and that profound; he said it went right to [Raniere's] head."[[32]](#cite_note-cbc.ca-32) According to Bouchey, Raniere's mother also reported hearing "dozens of young girls ... calling the house, and [that Raniere] ... was telling every single girl the same thing: 'I love you. You're the special one. You're important. You are the only one in my life, and I love you.' And Vera says, 'He's saying this to all these girls. He's clearly lying 'cause all of them are not special!'"[[32]](#cite_note-cbc.ca-32)

In June 1988, the [Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_New_York "Albany, New York") *[Times Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Union_(Albany) "Times Union (Albany)")* profiled Raniere, reporting on his membership in the [Mega Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_Society "Mega Society") after he achieved a high score on the MEGA test, an unsupervised, 48-question test published in the April 1985 issue of [*Omni*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omni_(magazine) "Omni (magazine)") magazine.[[33]](#cite_note-33)[[34]](#cite_note-34) Although the MEGA test has been widely criticized as not having been properly validated, the 1989 edition of the *[Guinness Book of World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_Book_of_World_Records "Guinness Book of World Records")* (the last edition to include a category for highest [IQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_quotient "Intelligence quotient"))[[35]](#cite_note-35) described the Mega Society as "the most exclusive ultrahigh IQ society", and the 1989 Australian edition identified Raniere, [Marilyn vos Savant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_vos_Savant "Marilyn vos Savant"), and [Eric Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Langan "Christopher Langan") as the highest-scoring members of the group.[[25]](#cite_note-FR180713-25)[[36]](#cite_note-36)[[37]](#cite_note-37)[[38]](#cite_note-38)

## Career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]

Throughout the 1980s, Raniere was involved with the [multi-level marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-level_marketing "Multi-level marketing") company [Amway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amway "Amway").[[23]](#cite_note-CBC180912-23)[[25]](#cite_note-FR180713-25) In 1990, he founded a multi-level marketing company of his own, Consumers' Buyline Inc. (CBI),[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39) a buying club that offered discounts in exchange for recruitment.[[40]](#cite_note-TU171012-40) At a CBI pitch meeting, Raniere met Toni Natalie,[[41]](#cite_note-democratandchronicle.com-41) who subsequently became a top seller for the organization, along with her then-husband.[[41]](#cite_note-democratandchronicle.com-41) Natalie and her son later moved to [Clifton Park, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton_Park,_New_York "Clifton Park, New York"), to be near Raniere; her marriage ended shortly thereafter. Natalie and Raniere dated for the next eight years.[[41]](#cite_note-democratandchronicle.com-41)

In 1993, CBI began to decline, and regulators in twenty states launched investigations into the entity. New York State filed a lawsuit alleging that CBI was a [pyramid scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_scheme "Pyramid scheme").[[42]](#cite_note-Forbes2003-42) In 1994, Raniere created National Health Network, a multi-level seller of vitamins;[[29]](#cite_note-forbes_yakowicz_may152019-29)[[43]](#cite_note-43) that business failed three years later.[[44]](#cite_note-44) In 1996, Raniere signed a [consent order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consent_order "Consent order") with New York State resolving the case against CBI;[[40]](#cite_note-TU171012-40) without admitting wrongdoing, he agreed to pay a $40,000 [fine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_(penalty) "Fine (penalty)").[[42]](#cite_note-Forbes2003-42) He ultimately only paid $9,000 to the state; despite having claimed a multimillion-dollar net worth, he said he was unable to pay the remainder.[[42]](#cite_note-Forbes2003-42) The consent order also permanently barred Raniere from "promoting, offering or granting participation in a chain distribution scheme".[[40]](#cite_note-TU171012-40)

## NXIVM: Executive Success Programs

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: NXIVM: Executive Success Programs")]

See also: [NXIVM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NXIVM "NXIVM")

### Foundation

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Foundation")]

In 1998, Natalie met [Nancy Salzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Salzman "Nancy Salzman"), a nurse and a practitioner of [hypnotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnosis "Hypnosis") and [neuro-linguistic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-linguistic_programming "Neuro-linguistic programming"). Natalie recalled:

> Nancy said, "You're so wonderful; how can I help you?" So I said, "Well, you can help me with my boyfriend." He had grandiose ideas and his hours were becoming erratic again ... She listened and she said, "Oh that's easy, I can help you. He's a [sociopath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisocial_personality_disorder "Antisocial personality disorder") ..." They met, and four days later she came out with the glazed eyes and gave me the, "You don't know who he is", and I was like, "Wow, there goes another one."[[45]](#cite_note-45)

Raniere and Salzman founded Executive Success Programs (ESP), a personal-development company offering a range of techniques aimed at [self-improvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-improvement "Self-improvement").[[23]](#cite_note-CBC180912-23)[[46]](#cite_note-NYT20180530-46) A few years later, the program was rebranded under the name NXIVM.[[32]](#cite_note-cbc.ca-32) Raniere "adopted the title 'Vanguard' from a [favorite arcade game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard_(video_game) "Vanguard (video game)") in which the destruction of one's enemies increased one's own power."[[47]](#cite_note-THR201805-47) Much of NXIVM was influenced by the teachings of [Ayn Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayn_Rand "Ayn Rand"), one of Raniere's favorite authors.[[23]](#cite_note-CBC180912-23)[[46]](#cite_note-NYT20180530-46)[[48]](#cite_note-48) In 2002, Raniere and Salzman succeeded in recruiting members of the influential [Bronfman family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronfman_family "Bronfman family"), heirs to the multibillion-dollar [Seagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagram "Seagram") fortune.[[49]](#cite_note-49) [Sara Bronfman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Bronfman "Sara Bronfman") initially became involved, followed by sister [Clare Bronfman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Bronfman "Clare Bronfman"). Their father, [Edgar Bronfman Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Bronfman_Sr. "Edgar Bronfman Sr."), took a NXIVM course the following year.[[50]](#cite_note-50)

NXIVM teachings drew upon diverse influences, including Ayn Rand ("parasites"), [L. Ron Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Ron_Hubbard "L. Ron Hubbard") ("suppressives"), [Milton Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Erickson "Milton Erickson")'s hypnosis, Isaac Asimov's science fiction, [Rudolf Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Steiner "Rudolf Steiner"), [Tony Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Robbins "Tony Robbins") and [neuro-linguistic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-linguistic_programming "Neuro-linguistic programming"). NXIVM incorporated elements of multi-level marketing and practices from [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo "Judo"), with colored cloth for rank and bowing.[[51]](#cite_note-51)[[52]](#cite_note-52)

### *Forbes* exposé

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Forbes exposé")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Edgar_Bronfman_Headshot.JPG/250px-Edgar_Bronfman_Headshot.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Edgar_Bronfman_Headshot.JPG)

In 2003, billionaire [Edgar Bronfman Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Bronfman_Sr. "Edgar Bronfman Sr.") took a NXIVM course at the encouragement of his daughters. Later that year, he denounced the group as a "cult" in a quote he gave to *Forbes* magazine.

In October 2003, Raniere was featured, cloaked in shadows, on the cover of [*Forbes* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes"), accompanied by the appellation "The World's Strangest Executive Coach".[[53]](#cite_note-53) The "devastating" cover story, penned by Michael Freedman and entitled "Cult of Personality", was later described as "a gold mine of previously unpublished information".[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39) It discussed Raniere's title of "Vanguard"; detailed the failure of CBI; and included a quote from Edgar Bronfman accusing the organization of being a cult.[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39)

[*Vanity Fair*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)") subsequently reported on the *Forbes* article's impact within the group: "People at NXIVM were stunned. Expecting a positive story, the top ranks had spoken to *Forbes*, including Raniere, Salzman, and Sara Bronfman. What upset them above all were Edgar Bronfman's remarks."[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39) According to *Vanity Fair*, the article was a turning point in Raniere's relationship with Edgar: one anonymous source said that the article's publication "was when Edgar Bronfman became NXIVM's enemy".[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39) A witness at Raniere's trial later testified that Bronfman's computer was compromised and his emails monitored by group members for a period of years.[[54]](#cite_note-NYT190528-54)

### Commodities trades

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Commodities trades")]

From January 2005 until late 2007, Raniere lost nearly $70 million in [commodities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodities "Commodities") trading.[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39) Bouchey spent $1.6 million covering losses of commodities trades which Raniere had made in her name.[[32]](#cite_note-cbc.ca-32) Raniere suggested to Clare Bronfman that the losses were due to [market manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_manipulation "Market manipulation") by her father.[[55]](#cite_note-TU151018-55) Beginning in August 2005, the Bronfman sisters covered the losses, ultimately using $150 million of their own funds in support of Raniere and his organization.[[25]](#cite_note-FR180713-25)[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39)

### Collaborations with the Dalai Lama

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Collaborations with the Dalai Lama")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/The_Dalai_Lama_in_2012.jpg/250px-The_Dalai_Lama_in_2012.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Dalai_Lama_in_2012.jpg)

In 2009, the [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama "14th Dalai Lama") appeared at a NXIVM event.

### Departure of Kristin Keeffe

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Departure of Kristin Keeffe")]

Kristin Keeffe was a longtime partner of Raniere and the mother of his first child.[[66]](#cite_note-TU150920-66) In 2010, it was reported that Raniere had ordered that the child be kept away from peers and that he was being cared for by nannies speaking five different languages.[[39]](#cite_note-VF101013-39) In February 2014, Keeffe broke up with Raniere and left NXIVM. After she fled the [Albany area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_District_(New_York) "Capital District (New York)") with her son, an email bearing her name explained: "I have full sole legal custody of Gaelyn. Keith was experimenting on him. I had to get Gaelyn away."[[67]](#cite_note-TU140511-67) Keeffe publicly described Raniere as "dangerous".[[67]](#cite_note-TU140511-67)

In 2015, Keeffe publicly alleged that Raniere directed the Canadian investigative firm Canaprobe to obtain financial information on six federal judges and a [United States senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_senator "United States senator") as well as a reporter, an editor, and the publisher of the *Times Union*.[[66]](#cite_note-TU150920-66) That same year, Keeffe further alleged that Raniere had planned to lure his critics to Mexico with an invitation to an anti-cult conference; once there, the critics were to be arrested on false charges by order of a local judge who had been [bribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bribery "Bribery").[[32]](#cite_note-cbc.ca-32)[[55]](#cite_note-TU151018-55)[[68]](#cite_note-68) Keeffe stated at Raniere's sentencing that he had never paid [child support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_support "Child support") for their son.[[20]](#cite_note-:0-20)

### Patent-infringement litigation

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Patent-infringement litigation")]

In 2015, Raniere sued [AT&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T "AT&T") and [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft"), alleging they had infringed on his [patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent "Patent"). The following year, the case was dismissed with prejudice. The trial court ruled that Raniere's "conduct throughout this litigation, culminating in his untruthful testimony at the hearing on the motion to dismiss, demonstrates a pattern of obfuscation and bad faith".[[69]](#cite_note-69) Raniere was sanctioned and ordered to pay $450,000 in attorneys' fees.[[70]](#cite_note-70)

### *Times Union* exposé

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Times Union exposé")]

In 2012, the *Times Union* reported that Raniere had three sexual relationships with girls under the age of consent during the 1980s and 1990s; *Times Union* reporters spoke to the girls and their families.[[14]](#cite_note-Odato_&_Gish-14) One of the victims, who spoke out on condition of anonymity, said that she met Raniere in 1990, when she was aged 12 and he was aged 30.[[31]](#cite_note-TU120218-31) Raniere offered to tutor the girl, whose mother worked for CBI, and one of his live-in partners, Pamela Cafritz, hired her to walk their dog and encouraged her to visit their townhouse.[[31]](#cite_note-TU120218-31) The woman said that the tutoring sessions were a pretext for sexual encounters and that she and Raniere had over sixty such encounters over a course of months, ending around 1991. In 1993 she received counseling for victims of sex abuse and filed a report with the [New York State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Police "New York State Police") against Raniere.[[31]](#cite_note-TU120218-31) The complaint was not forwarded to prosecutors because she had declined to wear [a wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert_listening_device "Covert listening device") to obtain a confession from Raniere.[[31]](#cite_note-TU120218-31)[[78]](#cite_note-78)[[79]](#cite_note-79)

### Abuse of sisters from Monterrey

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Abuse of sisters from Monterrey")]

In the 2019 criminal case against Raniere, prosecutors alleged that, starting in 2002, Raniere gained a [Svengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svengali "Svengali")-like control over a Mexican family who all moved from [Monterrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey "Monterrey") to the NXIVM community in Albany.[[80]](#cite_note-VICE-80)[[81]](#cite_note-81)[[82]](#cite_note-:3-82) The three sisters in this family eventually became sexual partners of Raniere.[[80]](#cite_note-VICE-80) The eldest, Marianna, is the mother of Raniere's second biological child.[[83]](#cite_note-83) The middle child, Daniela, testified that she was groomed as a minor and began a sexual relationship with Raniere days after her [18th birthday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ages_of_consent_in_the_United_States "Ages of consent in the United States").[[84]](#cite_note-CNN_Daniela-84) The youngest, Camila, stated that Raniere began sexually abusing her in 2005, when she was aged 15.[[20]](#cite_note-:0-20) The prosecution established that Raniere impregnated each sister at various times and compelled them to obtain [abortions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion "Abortion"). In addition, Daniela testified that Cafritz coached each sister to refuse to answer questions about paternity to prevent his abuse from becoming known to any [nurse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing "Nursing") who might be a [mandated reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandated_reporter "Mandated reporter").[[84]](#cite_note-CNN_Daniela-84)[[85]](#cite_note-85)

#### Daniela

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Daniela")]

According to Daniela, who testified for the prosecution at Raniere's trial, Raniere claimed that he had "mystical" powers and could help people grow spiritually and "fix disintegrations" through sex.[[80]](#cite_note-VICE-80)[[86]](#cite_note-86) The sisters were strongly discouraged from discussing their sexual relationships with Raniere, even between each other.[[80]](#cite_note-VICE-80)

Beginning in 2010, Daniela was confined to a bedroom for two years in her family home on the pretext that she had stolen money.[[87]](#cite_note-87) Daniela maintained her captivity began when she told Raniere she had romantic feelings for another man.[[84]](#cite_note-CNN_Daniela-84) Raniere accused her of an "ethical breach" and assigned Lauren Salzman (Nancy Salzman’s daughter) to help her "learn from her mistakes".[[84]](#cite_note-CNN_Daniela-84)[[88]](#cite_note-Daily_Beast_Salzman-88) Daniela described the spartan conditions of the room, consisting of blacked-out windows with only a mattress on the floor, a pen, and paper. Daniela was only allowed to speak to Salzman; other family members were instructed to [shun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunning "Shunning") her. This alleged confinement ended in 2012, after she almost attempted [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide"), but instead decided to leave her family home.[[84]](#cite_note-CNN_Daniela-84) After escaping her captivity, Daniela went to a [volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball "Volleyball") game to confront Raniere, who attempted to hide.[[89]](#cite_note-89) She was later escorted out of the NXIVM community and was left at the U.S.–Mexico border with less than $80 and no [personal documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_certificate "Birth certificate").[[84]](#cite_note-CNN_Daniela-84)[[90]](#cite_note-Courthouse_News-90)

Salzman corroborated Daniela's account at the trial, testifying that her efforts to leave the room (including cleaning her room, exercising, and writing daily letters to Raniere) were all rejected, and that Raniere had told Salzman to ignore a letter sent by Daniela pleading to be let out, brushing it off as "a tantrum". Salzman admitted that she and others were "incredibly abusive" to Daniela and that "[n]othing [Daniela] could do was the right thing".[[88]](#cite_note-Daily_Beast_Salzman-88)

## DOS: secret "slaves" and branding

### Creation, membership, and collateral

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Creation, membership, and collateral")]

In 2015, Raniere created a [secret subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_society "Secret society") within NXIVM called "DOS", an [acronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym "Acronym") for "Dominus Obsequious Sororium".[[115]](#cite_note-115)[[116]](#cite_note-Daily_Beast_2-116) DOS operated with levels of women "slaves" headed by "masters". Slaves were expected to recruit slaves of their own, becoming masters themselves. Slaves owed service not only to their own masters but also to masters above them in the DOS pyramid.[[117]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice_2-117) An estimated 150 women joined DOS.[[19]](#cite_note-vice_1-19) Raniere was the only male in DOS and sat at the top of the pyramid as the "grandmaster".[[118]](#cite_note-118)[[119]](#cite_note-119)

Prosecution exhibit depicting Raniere's first-line masters

Raniere initially recruited eight women to join DOS, including [Allison Mack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Mack "Allison Mack"), [Nicki Clyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicki_Clyne "Nicki Clyne") and Lauren Salzman, who became his inner-circle members and DOS "first-line masters".[[17]](#cite_note-NYT_Convicted-17)[[120]](#cite_note-120) DOS masters recruited new members by telling them they were joining a [women-only](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women-only_space "Women-only space") organization that would empower them, with Raniere's status as the leader of DOS concealed.[[121]](#cite_note-121)[[122]](#cite_note-122)[[123]](#cite_note-123)

Raniere maintained command and control over DOS members by collecting "collateral"—which included highly damaging personal information, sexually explicit photos or videos, and rights to personal assets—and relied on his inner-circle members to carry out his orders and build the DOS pyramid group.[[1]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice_1-1)[[124]](#cite_note-CNN_Salzman-124)[[125]](#cite_note-125) Women were required to provide collateral before joining DOS and continue to give new collateral every month after joining it.[[126]](#cite_note-Forbes-126)[[127]](#cite_note-decider-127) Slaves were told that their [collateral could be released](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackmail "Blackmail") for any number of reasons, including if they left DOS, spoke publicly about DOS, or repeatedly failed DOS obligations or assignments.[[128]](#cite_note-BBC_1-128)

Prosecution exhibit: a photo of the DOS [brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_branding "Human branding"), which consisted of Raniere's initials K.A.R.

DOS members were [branded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_branding "Human branding") in their [pelvic regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvis "Pelvis") with a symbol that, unbeknownst to them, [incorporated Raniere's initials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogram "Monogram").[[142]](#cite_note-142)[[143]](#cite_note-143) Some slaves were told the brand stood for the [elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element "Classical element").[[130]](#cite_note-Hollywood_Reporter-130)[[144]](#cite_note-courthousenews_1-144) Only Raniere's inner-circle members knew the brand was his initials.[[124]](#cite_note-CNN_Salzman-124)[[145]](#cite_note-145) At Raniere's trial, prosecutors introduced a recording of a private meeting in 2016 with inner-circle members where Raniere stated, "The monogram as it is right now is very directly related to my initials." The group discussed how to obscure that fact.[[146]](#cite_note-Sacrifice-146)[[147]](#cite_note-courthousenews-147)

The DOS branding ritual followed a script created by Raniere: slaves were required to be fully naked, with the ceremonies filmed and used as more collateral on the DOS slaves.[[144]](#cite_note-courthousenews_1-144)[[146]](#cite_note-Sacrifice-146) In a recorded conversation between Raniere and Mack discussing the branding, Raniere said, "The person should probably ask to be branded. They should probably say that before they're held down, so it doesn't seem like they're being coerced."[[132]](#cite_note-Law_&_Crime-132) NXIVM member Danielle Roberts performed the branding of DOS members using a [cauterizing pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauterization#Electrocautery "Cauterization"). The [New York State Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Department_of_Health "New York State Department of Health") later revoked Roberts' [medical license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_license "Medical license") following an investigation into the branding.[[148]](#cite_note-148)[[149]](#cite_note-149)

### The purpose and aim of DOS

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: The purpose and aim of DOS")]

At Raniere's trial, Lauren Salzman stated that the DOS pyramid had four levels of slaves, each reporting to their master on the level above them, and all of them ultimately accountable to Raniere, the grandmaster.[[136]](#cite_note-NYT_Salzman-136) Salzman testified that the overriding emphasis of DOS was to venerate Raniere and foster an atmosphere of total obedience and secrecy. DOS members communicated using encrypted phone applications like [Telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegram_(software) "Telegram (software)") and [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_(messaging_app) "Signal (messaging app)"), and members' identities were not always known to one another.[[136]](#cite_note-NYT_Salzman-136) DOS members were encouraged to recruit people of power and influence with DOS sororities across the country.[[136]](#cite_note-NYT_Salzman-136)[[150]](#cite_note-150) Salzman also testified that she was helping Raniere edit his DOS [manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto "Manifesto") that enforced the master-slave relationships.[[136]](#cite_note-NYT_Salzman-136)[[151]](#cite_note-VICE_2-151) Excerpts from the book included: "The best slave derives the highest pleasure from being her Master's ultimate tool" and, "Your sole highest desire must be to further your Master from whom all good things come and are related."[[151]](#cite_note-VICE_2-151)

### Exposure

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Exposure")]

On June 5, 2017, Frank Parlato reported the existence of DOS and the practice of human branding.[[152]](#cite_note-152) On October 17, 2017, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* published a story detailing DOS abuses, including the requirement of slaves to provide collateral.[[153]](#cite_note-NYT18Oct-153)[[154]](#cite_note-ABC171216-154)

Following the *NYT* piece, Raniere sent a draft position statement to DOS first-line masters, writing that women who had been branded were being shamed by a "misogynistic strike" and that DOS was an "autonomous" organization, "separate and distinct from NXIVM" and that he had "little knowledge or authority in the issue".[[146]](#cite_note-Sacrifice-146) In a separate letter to NXIVM members he wrote, "I feel it is important to clarify the sorority is not part of NXIVM and that I am not associated with the group."[[155]](#cite_note-155)

## Arrest, trial, and conviction

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Arrest, trial, and conviction")]

[![Wikisource logo](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Wikisource-logo.svg/40px-Wikisource-logo.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wikisource-logo.svg)

English [Wikisource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikisource "Wikisource") has original text related to this article:

**[Superseding Indictment, United States of America v. Keith Raniere, et al.](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/en:Superseding%20Indictment,%20United%20States%20of%20America%20v.%20Keith%20Raniere,%20et%20al. "s:en:Superseding Indictment, United States of America v. Keith Raniere, et al.")**

In the wake of the *Times* piece, Raniere and members of his inner circle fled to Mexico.[[156]](#cite_note-156) A [search warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_warrant "Search warrant") was issued for his email account on January 18, 2018. The FBI filed a sealed criminal complaint and obtained a warrant for Raniere's arrest from the [United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_New_York "United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York") on February 14.[[157]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice_Complaint-157) On March 26, [Mexican Federal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Police_(Mexico) "Federal Police (Mexico)") located Raniere in a luxury villa outside [Puerto Vallarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Vallarta "Puerto Vallarta"), arresting and deporting him.[[158]](#cite_note-158)[[159]](#cite_note-159) Salzman later testified that when the police arrived, she and Raniere barricaded themselves in the master suite, with Raniere attempting to hide in a [walk-in closet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk-in_closet "Walk-in closet").[[160]](#cite_note-160)[[161]](#cite_note-BuzzFeed-161)

### Pre-trial proceedings

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Pre-trial proceedings")]

American authorities took custody of Raniere and presented him in federal court in [Fort Worth, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas "Fort Worth, Texas"), and later transferred him to custody at the [Metropolitan Detention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Detention_Center,_Brooklyn "Metropolitan Detention Center, Brooklyn") (MDC) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City").[[162]](#cite_note-162)[[163]](#cite_note-163) The unsealed complaint accused Raniere of a variety of crimes related to DOS, including sex trafficking, [conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_(crime) "Conspiracy (crime)") for sex trafficking, and conspiracy to commit forced labor. The complaint alleged that at least one woman was coerced into sex with Raniere, who forced DOS members to undergo the branding ritual alleged by former DOS members.[[164]](#cite_note-NBCNews0516-164)[[165]](#cite_note-CBS20180327-165) [United States Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney "United States Attorney") Richard Donoghue stated that Raniere "created a secret society of women with whom he had sex and had branded with his initials, coercing them with the threat of releasing their highly personal information and taking their assets."[[166]](#cite_note-166)

From his 2018 arraignment in the United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York through his sentencing in 2021, prosecutors sought to keep Raniere [remanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remand_(detention) "Remand (detention)") to the MDC. Raniere's attorneys petitioned for his release on bail,[[167]](#cite_note-167)[[168]](#cite_note-168) while prosecutors opposed the petition, citing Raniere's inconsistent answers about his income and his [previous flight to Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bail_in_the_United_States "Bail in the United States").[[169]](#cite_note-169)[[170]](#cite_note-170)

### *United States v. Keith Raniere* trial

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: United States v. Keith Raniere trial")]

Video of U.S. Attorney Richard Donoghue announcing the conviction of Keith Raniere outside the [Theodore Roosevelt United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt_United_States_Courthouse "Theodore Roosevelt United States Courthouse") on June 19, 2019

Raniere's federal racketeering trial began on May 7, 2019.[[171]](#cite_note-news10.com-171) The charged acts included:

* Sexual exploitation of Camila as a minor and possession of child sexual abuse material depicting her;[[1]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice_1-1)[[172]](#cite_note-RaniereIndictment-172)
* Sex trafficking and forced labor of "Nicole";[[172]](#cite_note-RaniereIndictment-172)[[173]](#cite_note-173)
* Attempted sex trafficking of "Jay";[[141]](#cite_note-New_York_Daily_News-141)[[172]](#cite_note-RaniereIndictment-172)
* [Identity theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_theft "Identity theft") against Edgar Bronfman, James Loperfido, Ashana Chenoa, "Marianna", and Pam Cafritz;[[174]](#cite_note-GuiltyAllCounts-174)
* Subjecting Daniela to "document servitude" for labor and services;[[172]](#cite_note-RaniereIndictment-172)[[174]](#cite_note-GuiltyAllCounts-174)
* Conspiracy to alter records for use in an official proceeding;
* Sex trafficking conspiracy;
* Forced labor conspiracy;
* Racketeering conspiracy, and;
* [Wire fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_fraud "Wire fraud") conspiracy.[[172]](#cite_note-RaniereIndictment-172)[[174]](#cite_note-GuiltyAllCounts-174)

In opening statements, prosecutors contended that Raniere was not a mentor but a "predator" who targeted people looking to improve their lives and took advantage of them once he gained their trust.[[175]](#cite_note-175) The defense argued that Raniere was akin to a strict sports coach, emphasizing that "[t]his [was] something these people signed up for."[[176]](#cite_note-176) Prosecution witnesses included NXIVM member Lauren Salzman; filmmaker [Mark Vicente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Vicente "Mark Vicente"); victims "Sylvie", "Daniela", "Jay", and "Nicole"; and cult educator [Rick Alan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Alan_Ross "Rick Alan Ross"). The defense rested without calling any witnesses.

At closing arguments, prosecutor Moira Penza argued that the organization Raniere founded was secretly a sex cult set up like a [pyramid scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_scheme "Pyramid scheme") and that Raniere was "a con man" and "a crime boss with no limits and no checks on his power" who "tapped into a never-ending flow of women and money".[[177]](#cite_note-Dickson-177)[[178]](#cite_note-closing_arguments-178) Defense attorney Marc Agnifilo, conversely, said that Raniere's interactions were all consensual, saying that those who left the group were not subject to punishment.[[177]](#cite_note-Dickson-177) Agnifilo concluded Raniere was an eccentric who lived an alternative polyamorous lifestyle with consenting adults, noting that their "lifestyles aren't criminal".[[177]](#cite_note-Dickson-177)[[179]](#cite_note-179) On June 19, 2019, the jury found Raniere guilty on all charges after five hours of deliberation.[[1]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice_1-1)[[180]](#cite_note-180)

## Post-conviction

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Post-conviction")]

While awaiting sentencing, Raniere filed two motions for a new trial: a March 2020 motion claimed witnesses [perjured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perjury "Perjury") themselves,[[181]](#cite_note-181) then an October 2020 motion claimed that prosecutors had [intimidated witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witness_intimidation "Witness intimidation").[[182]](#cite_note-182) Judge Garaufis denied both motions.[[183]](#cite_note-183)[[184]](#cite_note-184)

### Loyalists' activities at Metropolitan Detention Complex

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Loyalists' activities at Metropolitan Detention Complex")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Brooklyn_MDC_jeh.JPG/250px-Brooklyn_MDC_jeh.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Brooklyn_MDC_jeh.JPG)

The [Metropolitan Detention Center, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Detention_Center,_Brooklyn "Metropolitan Detention Center, Brooklyn"), where Keith Raniere was jailed during his trial. According to [Federal Bureau of Prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Prisons "Federal Bureau of Prisons"), Raniere demanded "a group of women to show up regularly and dance provocatively for inmates to view through their cell windows".

Convicted in June 2019, the 2020 outbreak of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") and its [impact on prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_prisons#Federal_correctional_system "Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on prisons") delayed Raniere's sentencing until over a year later. He remained in the MDC awaiting sentencing. While there, he began communicating with a supporter, Suneel Chakravorty, who recorded some of their conversations. These recordings were released through a website as a [podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast "Podcast") called *Raniere Speaks*, with copyright held by Dialogue Productions, LLC.[[185]](#cite_note-185) Raniere was subsequently forbidden by MDC officials from contacting Chakravorty.[[186]](#cite_note-:2-186)

In the summer of 2020, with the pandemic preventing in-person visitation to the MDC, Raniere's remaining followers, including Clyne, began assembling to dance near the jail. Though they initially claimed to be entertaining all of the detainees, they were seen with a sign addressed to "Kay Rose", a name sharing Raniere's initials.[[187]](#cite_note-187) The group began calling itself "The Forgotten Ones" and "We Are As You". Former NXIVM member turned prosecution witness [Mark Vicente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Vicente "Mark Vicente") dismissed the group as a "cover movement" for support of Raniere.[[188]](#cite_note-188)

A July 16, 2020, intelligence analysis memorandum from the Bureau of Prisons Counter Terrorism Unit stated that Raniere instructed Chakravorty to get more women to dance "erotically" outside the MDC. In response, authorities moved Raniere to another unit to keep the dancers out of his line of sight. A frustrated Raniere instructed his followers to help get him moved back by ingratiating themselves to prison staff, including offering coffee and donuts as they left their shifts.[[186]](#cite_note-:2-186)[[189]](#cite_note-189)[[190]](#cite_note-190)

### Sentencing, restitution, and appeal

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Sentencing, restitution, and appeal")]

Ahead of his sentencing, prosecutors submitted a number of Raniere's communications and disciplinary issues in prison as evidence of remorselessness and of his continued control over his followers. The communications included Raniere instructing his followers to have [Alan Dershowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Dershowitz "Alan Dershowitz"), the attorney who successfully negotiated a non-prosecution agreement for the late [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein "Jeffrey Epstein"), speak on his behalf; Dershowitz did not comment on the matter.[[191]](#cite_note-191) Prosecutors also submitted documentation that Raniere and Chakravorty used a false name and "[burner phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prepaid_mobile_phone "Prepaid mobile phone")" to evade detection, with Raniere instructing Chakravorty to "get scrutiny" on Judge Nicholas Garaufis, explaining that "the judge needs to know he's being watched".[[192]](#cite_note-auto4-192)[[193]](#cite_note-193)[[194]](#cite_note-194)

As sentencing proceeded, federal prosecutors asked for [life imprisonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_imprisonment "Life imprisonment") for the severity of Raniere's crimes and his lack of remorse—they said he showed "a complete lack of acceptance of responsibility for his crimes" and argued that he would continue to commit crimes if released. Prosecutors said Raniere has stayed in touch with members of NXIVM since his conviction, casting himself in emails as a victim and encouraging them to keep the group alive.[[195]](#cite_note-Yahoo_2-195)[[196]](#cite_note-Associated_Press-196) Prosecutors argued in their sentencing memorandum that Raniere "concealed his abuse behind the smoke screen of his supposed 'personal growth' programs—a charade he continues to this day".[[197]](#cite_note-New_York_Times-197)

On October 27, 2020, Judge Nicholas Garaufis sentenced Raniere to 120 years in prison and fined him $1.75 million.[[20]](#cite_note-:0-20)[[21]](#cite_note-Department_of_Justice-21) To begin serving his 120-year sentence, Raniere was first transferred to [United States Penitentiary, Lewisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Penitentiary,_Lewisburg "United States Penitentiary, Lewisburg"), a medium-security penitentiary in [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania"), and then to the [United States Penitentiary, Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Penitentiary,_Tucson "United States Penitentiary, Tucson") (USP Tucson) in [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona "Arizona"). The Arizona facility is the sole facility in the [federal prison system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_prison "Federal prison") that is both specially designated for [sex offenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_offender "Sex offender") and also at the [maximum-security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarceration_in_the_United_States#Security_levels "Incarceration in the United States") level.[[198]](#cite_note-198)[[199]](#cite_note-199)[[200]](#cite_note-200) As of 2026, he is incarcerated at [FCI Butner I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Correctional_Complex,_Butner "Federal Correctional Complex, Butner").

On July 20, 2021, Raniere made a virtual appearance from his USP Tucson prison cell for a hearing on [restitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restitution "Restitution"), the last remaining material from the trial. Imprisoned co-defendant Clare Bronfman paid attorneys Marc Fernich and [Jeffrey Lichtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Lichtman "Jeffrey Lichtman") to represent him.[[201]](#cite_note-:4-201) Judge Garaufis ruled that twenty-one victims of Raniere should receive a total of $3.46 million in restitution. This included payments to cover the cost removing the DOS-related scarification, ongoing mental health care, and making labor trafficking victims whole.[[202]](#cite_note-202) Garaufis's order also stated that "all lower-ranking DOS members are statutorily entitled to the return of their collateral" and ordered Raniere "to effectuate that return to the fullest extent practicable". Due to [Fifth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution") concerns, this order was stayed until sixty days after a ruling on Raniere's appeal to the [United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Second_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit").[[203]](#cite_note-203)

Raniere gave notice of [appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal "Appeal") of both his conviction and sentence to the Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit in November 2020.[[204]](#cite_note-204) Oral arguments were heard on May 3, 2022.[[205]](#cite_note-205) The Circuit upheld the decision and rejected the appeal on December 9, 2022. Judge [José A. Cabranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_A._Cabranes "José A. Cabranes") wrote, "Raniere has failed to persuade us that there is insufficient evidence to sustain his convictions."[[206]](#cite_note-206)[[207]](#cite_note-207)

### *Edmondson v. Raniere* lawsuit

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=30 "Edit section: Edmondson v. Raniere lawsuit")]

In January 2020, Raniere and several other NXIVM leaders were named as defendants in a civil lawsuit filed in federal court by eighty former NXIVM members. The lawsuit details allegations of fraud and abuse and charges NXIVM with being a pyramid scheme, exploitation of its recruits, conducting illegal [human experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_experimentation "Human experimentation"), and making it "physically and psychologically difficult, and in some cases impossible, to leave the coercive community."[[208]](#cite_note-208)[[209]](#cite_note-209) This case is ongoing as of March 2024[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit).[[210]](#cite_note-210)

### Prison lawsuits

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Keith_Raniere&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: Prison lawsuits")]

In May 2022, Raniere filed suit against the [U.S. Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Justice "U.S. Department of Justice") and [Federal Bureau of Prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Prisons "Federal Bureau of Prisons"), alleging that his civil rights are violated as a prisoner in USP Tucson. Raniere sought an [injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injunction "Injunction") allowing visitation and phone calls from Chakravorty, who he claims is a [paralegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralegal "Paralegal") working on his appeals.[[211]](#cite_note-211) The Department of Justice, Bureau of Prisons, and authorities at USP Tucson moved to deny the injunction, on grounds that Chakravorty is not a paralegal but merely "an ardent former ESP and NXIVM coach with whom [Raniere] is banned from associating."[[212]](#cite_note-212) Judge [Raner Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raner_Collins "Raner Collins") granted the Department of Justice's motion to dismiss the suit on grounds that Raniere failed to exhaust administrative remedies in line with the [Prison Litigation Reform Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison_Litigation_Reform_Act "Prison Litigation Reform Act"), and for insufficient service of process.[[213]](#cite_note-213)
