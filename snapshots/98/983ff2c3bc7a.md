**Skellefteå** (Swedish:[\[ɧɛˈlɛ̌ftɛɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish"), locally[\[ʂɛˈlɛ̌ftɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/df/Sv-skelleftea.ogg/Sv-skelleftea.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sv-skelleftea.ogg "File:Sv-skelleftea.ogg"); [Meänkieli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me%C3%A4nkieli_language "Meänkieli language"): _Heletti_) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_in_Sweden "Cities in Sweden") in [Västerbotten County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4sterbotten_County "Västerbotten County"), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden"), with a population of 36,388. It is the seat of [Skellefteå Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5_Municipality "Skellefteå Municipality"), which had 77,322 inhabitants in 2024.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-3)

The city is historically industrial, with mining being a large part of that industry, especially for gold, leading to the city being nicknamed _Guldstaden_ ("gold town"). Politically, Skellefteå is a [Social Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrats_(Sweden) "Social Democrats (Sweden)") stronghold. The city is a well-known [ice hockey town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockeytown "Hockeytown"), with its main team [Skellefteå AIK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5_AIK "Skellefteå AIK") playing in the Swedish top division: the [SHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Hockey_League "Swedish Hockey League"), which they have won on several occasions; most recently in [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_SHL_season "2025–26 SHL season").

The city was incorporated in 1845 and grew to its current population size in the 1950s and 1960s, growing only slowly since. It is the second largest city in Västerbotten after [Umeå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ume%C3%A5 "Umeå") and is located roughly halfway between it and [Luleå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lule%C3%A5 "Luleå"). The [Skellefte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte_River "Skellefte River") passes through the city and it is located around 15 kilometres (9.3mi) from the [Bothnian Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bothnian_Bay "Bothnian Bay") open sea. Skellefteå is served by [Skellefteå Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5_Airport "Skellefteå Airport"), IATA airport code SFT but locally known as _Falmark_ because of the village nearby, also around 15 kilometres (9.3mi) from the city centre to the south.

The name Skellefteå is recorded to having been spelled as _Skelepht_ in 1327. \[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] On [Carta marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carta_marina "Carta marina") the name is spelt _Skellitta_. The origin of the name remains unknown, but is assumed to be of Sami origin.

From the 14th century on, attempts were made to Christianize Skellefteå. A parish was formed and a church built. However, for the most part, the entire large Northern Swedish territory of [Norrland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrland "Norrland") was not Christianized until several hundred years after the rest of Sweden, and many northern areas such as Skellefteå remained unexplored well past the Middle Ages.

Not before the very end of the 17th century did the indigenous [Sami people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_people "Sami people") of Northern Sweden begin turning to Christianity, much due to the efforts by the Northern Swedish superintendent [Mathias Steuchius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Steuchius "Mathias Steuchius"), who worked hard to accomplish this.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-4) Several [Sami shamans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noaidi "Noaidi") were killed for this reason.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Eventually, the reason for the sudden awakened interest towards Skellefteälven and the surrounding areas was the great northern fishing grounds of [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon "Salmon").

The actual city of Skellefteå is one of the youngest cities of Norrland. It was founded in 1845 by the vicar Nils Nordlander.

## Economy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Skellefte%C3%A5&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Economy")\]

Major industries include:

- [Boliden AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boliden_AB "Boliden AB"), a big mining and smelting company
- [Skellefteå Kraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5_Kraft "Skellefteå Kraft"), largest power company in Skellefteå
- [Northvolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northvolt "Northvolt"), lithium-ion battery cell manufacturing company[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-5)

The city became home of a massive battery plant made by [Northvolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northvolt "Northvolt") in 2021.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-6) Northvolt announced layoffs in September 2024, and filed for bankruptcy in March 2025.

The largest private employer in Skellefteå is the mining company [Boliden AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boliden_AB "Boliden AB"), with about 1,200 employees.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The mine's copper ore contains particles of gold, silver and platinum. Skellefteå is still referred to as the _"Goldtown"_.\[ _[by whom?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch")_\]

During the 1990s, the computer industry flourished, subsidiaries of [Ericsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ericsson "Ericsson") and [Tieto Enator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tieto_Enator "Tieto Enator") becoming important employers.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Climate

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Skellefte%C3%A5&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Climate")\]

Skellefteå has a [subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate "Subarctic climate") ( [Dfc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification "Köppen climate classification")) with mild summers and cold, long and snowy winters. The climate is somewhat moderated by the [Bothnia Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bothnia_Bay "Bothnia Bay"), although maritime effects are limited, ensuring warm summer temperatures for a coastal area so far north. In the last century, as part of global climate warming, an increase in summer temperatures as well as an earlier arrival of spring and a later onset of winter have been observed. Precipitation is moderate, the maximum precipitation is in the second half of summer. The difference in average hours of sunshine between months is large: December has very little due to both very short days and high cloud cover, while June has a lot due to long days and relatively clear weather.

## Transportation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Skellefte%C3%A5&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Transportation")\]

The [European route E4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E4 "European route E4") highway provides the main road connection to the city, providing direct connections to cities like [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") and [Sundsvall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundsvall "Sundsvall") going south and [Luleå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lule%C3%A5 "Luleå") going north. [Riksväg 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riksv%C3%A4g_95?action=edit&redlink=1 "Riksväg 95 (page does not exist)") also serves the city.

There is a railway [branch line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_line "Branch line") running through Skellefteå, although with no passenger traffic, making it the largest city in Sweden without it. The line is still served by [Freight traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_freight_transport "Rail freight transport").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-10) There were plans to start night trains going from [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") to Skellefteå, with service being expected to commence in April 2022. Due to a decision from [Trafikverket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafikverket "Trafikverket"), the planned night trains were cancelled before they started running.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-12) The closest [train station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_station "Train station") in use is located in [Bastuträsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastutr%C3%A4sk "Bastuträsk"), from which trains heading to several cities including [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm"), [Umeå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ume%C3%A5 "Umeå") and [Luleå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lule%C3%A5 "Luleå") depart, operated by [Norrtåg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrt%C3%A5g "Norrtåg") and [Vy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vy_T%C3%A5g "Vy Tåg"). There goes a bus to Skellefteå from Bastuträsk, intended for transfers to and from the trains.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-13)

The [High-speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail "High-speed rail") [Norrbotniabanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bothnia_Line "North Bothnia Line") is planned to go through the city, providing it with rail service for the first time since 1990.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-14) As a part of this, a new [station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_station "Train station") will be constructed close to the city centre.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-15) Service is expected to begin in 2030.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skellefte%C3%A5#cite_note-16)
