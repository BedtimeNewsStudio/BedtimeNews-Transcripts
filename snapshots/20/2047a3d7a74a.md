A Message to Garcia

_**A Message to Garcia**_ is a widely distributed essay written by [Elbert Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbert_Hubbard "Elbert Hubbard") in 1899, expressing the value of individual initiative and conscientiousness in work. The essay's primary example is a dramatized version of a daring escapade performed by an American soldier, [First Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_lieutenant "First lieutenant") [Andrew S. Rowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Summers_Rowan "Andrew Summers Rowan"), just before the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War "Spanish–American War"). The essay describes Rowan carrying a message from President [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McKinley "William McKinley") to "Gen. [Calixto García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calixto_Garc%C3%ADa "Calixto García"), a leader of the Cuban insurgents somewhere in the mountain [fastnesses](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fastness "wikt:fastness") of [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba")—no one knew where". The essay contrasts Rowan's self-driven effort against "the imbecility of the average man—the inability or unwillingness to concentrate on a thing and do it".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hubbard-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-Hubbard):17–18

> The point I wish to make is this: McKinley gave Rowan a letter to be delivered to Garcia; Rowan took the letter and did not ask, "Where is he at?" By the Eternal! there is a man whose form should be cast in deathless bronze and the statue placed in every college of the land. It is not book-learning young men need, nor instruction about this and that, but a stiffening of the vertebrae which will cause them to be loyal to a trust, to act promptly, concentrate their energies: do the thing—"Carry a message to Garcia!"

## Publication history

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/New_York_Central_edition_of_%22A_Message_to_Garcia%22.jpg/500px-New_York_Central_edition_of_%22A_Message_to_Garcia%22.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:New_York_Central_edition_of_%22A_Message_to_Garcia%22.jpg "File:New York Central edition of \"A Message to Garcia\".jpg") _A Message to Garcia_, New York central edition

_A Message to Garcia_ was originally published as filler without a title in the March 1899 issue of _[The Philistine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbert_Hubbard "Elbert Hubbard")_, a periodical which, at that time, was written entirely by Hubbard.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) His complaints about lazy and incompetent workers struck a chord with many corporate executives. One of these was [George H. Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._Daniels?action=edit&redlink=1 "George H. Daniels"), a promotion-minded executive with the [New York Central Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Central_Railroad "New York Central Railroad"). Daniels reprinted the essay hundreds of thousands of times as part of the railroad's Four-Track Series of pamphlets. Hubbard's Roycroft Press, the publishing arm of an [arts and crafts community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roycroft "Roycroft") he founded in [East Aurora, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Aurora,_New_York "East Aurora, New York"), reprinted and sold the essay in a variety of bindings—suede, embossed, paperback, and so on—and as paid promotional literature for organizations as disparate as [Wanamaker's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanamaker's "Wanamaker's") department store, the [Boy Scouts of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scouts_of_America "Boy Scouts of America"), and the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hubbard-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-Hubbard):9–11[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) It was also reprinted in many anthologies of inspirational literature. Modern editions are readily available today on the Internet.

## Historical accuracy

In Hubbard's version of Rowan's journey, President McKinley needed to communicate with Gen. Calixto Garcia, a leader of the Cuban insurgents.

> \[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hubbard-2)[S\]omeone said to the president there was 'a fellow by the name of Rowan will find Garcia for you if anybody can.' Rowan was sent for and given a letter to be delivered to Garcia. How the 'fellow by the name of Rowan' took the letter, sealed it up in an oilskin pouch, strapped it over his heart, in four days landed by night off the coast of Cuba from an open boat, disappeared into the jungle, and in three weeks came out on the other side of the Island, having traversed a hostile country on foot, and delivered his letter to Garcia – are things I have no special desire now to tell in detail.[\[1\]](#refnote-Hubbard):18–19

In fact, the only true statement Hubbard wrote was that Rowan "landed...off the coast of Cuba from an open boat". All the rest, including McKinley's need to communicate with Garcia and Rowan's delivery of a letter to the general, was false.

It was Maj. [Arthur L. Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_L._Wagner "Arthur L. Wagner"), head of the [Military Information Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Information_Division_(United_States) "Military Information Division (United States)"), who successfully petitioned Adj. Gen. [Henry Clark Corbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Clark_Corbin "Henry Clark Corbin") for permission to send spies to Cuba and Puerto Rico to gather military information. Wagner selected 40-year-old 1st Lt. Andrew S. Rowan to join García, who led the rebel forces in eastern Cuba.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) On April 9, Rowan, posing as a civilian, boarded a steamer in New York bound for [Kingston, Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Jamaica "Kingston, Jamaica").[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5) With the help of the U.S. consul in Kingston, he connected with the [Cuban Revolutionary Junta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Revolutionary_Junta "Cuban Revolutionary Junta"), some of whose members transported him by open boat during one of their trips to the southeastern coast of Cuba.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kirchman-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-Kirchman):46–52 They went ashore the morning of April 25.

Following an eight-day horseback journey with rebels through the [Sierra Maestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Maestra "Sierra Maestra") Mountains, Rowan met with García in the city of [Bayamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayamo "Bayamo") on May 1. Rowan's assignment was to keep the [War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War "United States Department of War") informed as to "the strength, efficiency, movements and general military situation". His orders were to stay in Cuba, to "accompany the Insurgent Forces, and to send back dispatches".[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7) Disregarding his orders, Rowan said he was there to learn what García needed to cooperate with the U.S. armed forces during a possible invasion. He added that he was eager to return to the U.S.[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) García, seeing an opportunity, sent him back to the U.S. within hours of his arrival. Traveling with him were members of García's staff to confer with U.S. officials. After a five-day horseback journey to [Manatí Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manati "Manati") on Cuba's north coast, they "drew a little cockle-shell of a boat from under a mangrove bush" and set sail for Florida. A passing sponging steamer carried them to Nassau, and from there they eventually sailed to Tampa, arriving on May 13.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

Rowan had no sooner landed in Cuba on April 25 than details of his secret mission were splashed across the pages of America's newspapers. It was learned that, while in Jamaica, Rowan had revealed this information to an [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") correspondent named [Elmer Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Roberts?action=edit&redlink=1 "Elmer Roberts").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kirchman-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-Kirchman):50 This was not what Adj. Gen. Corbin anticipated. Had the news reports not made Rowan a popular hero, however falsely, Corbin might have had him [court-martialed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court-martial "Court-martial").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kirchman-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-Kirchman):11 Instead he was deemed as popular as [Buffalo Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bill "Buffalo Bill"), lauded by Maj. Gen [Nelson A. Miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_A._Miles "Nelson A. Miles"), commanding general of the army, and temporarily promoted to [lieutenant-colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-colonel "Lieutenant-colonel") in the [6th Regiment Volunteer Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Regiment_Volunteer_Infantry?action=edit&redlink=1 "6th Regiment Volunteer Infantry").[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

## Exaggerated circulation claims

In 1914, Hubbard claimed the essay had been reprinted over 40 million times.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hubbard-3)[\[1\]](#refnote-Hubbard):13–14 His son Bert later reported 80 million times,[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) and in a 1926 bill to promote Andrew Summers Rowan to major general, retired, it was stated there were 225 million copies printed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) In their 1977 survey of best sellers, Alice Hackett and James H. Burke estimated a circulation of four million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13) Since then there has been an uncounted—and uncountable—number of copies, particularly if every digital copy is included, but no one has yet been able to prove a circulation of 40 million. In an oft-repeated story, Hubbard wrote that when "Mr. Daniels \[of the NY Central\] was distributing the _Message to Garcia_, Prince Hilakoff ( [Mikhail Khilkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Khilkov "Mikhail Khilkov")), Director of Russian Railways, was in this country...and made a tour of the country under the personal direction of Mr. Daniels....When he got home he had the _Message to Garcia_ translated into Russian, and a copy of the booklet given to every railroad employee in Russia," apparently unaware that at that time a large percentage of Russian railroad workers were illiterate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) Hubbard further claimed that "\[D\]uring the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War "Russo-Japanese War"), every soldier who went to the front was given a copy." The Japanese found the pamphlet on Russian prisoners of war, had it translated, and the " [Mikado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Japan "Emperor of Japan")" ordered a copy given "to every man in the employ of the Japanese government, soldier or civilian".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hubbard-4)[\[1\]](#refnote-Hubbard):11–13 The narrative is false: Khilkov was in the U.S. in October 1896, long before the essay was written, and only occasionally was he in the company of Daniels.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) Not a single copy of the purported Russian or Japanese government-issued pamphlets has been found. When Hubbard was writing about the Russian and Japanese translations, he also declared that the pamphlet traveled from Russia to "Germany, France, Spain, Turkey, Hindustan, and China", and claimed that the essay had been translated into "all written languages".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hubbard-5)[\[1\]](#refnote-Hubbard):11 That has yet to be demonstrated; however, it can today be found in many recent translations: Czech, German, Romanian, Chinese, Korean, and other languages.

## In popular culture

The phrase "to carry a message to Garcia" was in common use for years to indicate taking initiative when carrying out a difficult assignment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") can be heard using it on the [Watergate tapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_White_House_tapes "Nixon White House tapes") during conversations with [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger "Henry Kissinger") and [John Ehrlichman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ehrlichman "John Ehrlichman").[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18) It has also been used as the title of children's games and dramatized on radio shows, and it was tailor-made for the Boy Scouts of America. A passage in the 1917 _Boy Scouts Yearbook_ emphasizes the connection: "If you give \[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[a Boy Scout\] a 'Message to Garcia' you know that message will be delivered, although the mountains, the wilderness, the desert, the torrents, the broad lagoons or the sea itself, separate him from 'Garcia'."[\[19\]](#refnote-19)

There have been two movies based on the essay, both titled _A Message to Garcia_. The first, a silent movie made in 1916 by [Thomas A. Edison, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Edison,_Inc. "Thomas A. Edison, Inc."), was directed by [Richard Ridgely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Ridgely "Richard Ridgely") and featured [Robert Conness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Conness "Robert Conness"), [Mabel Trunnelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabel_Trunnelle "Mabel Trunnelle"), and [Charles Sutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Sutton_(actor) "Charles Sutton (actor)"). As noted on the [Turner Classic Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Classic_Movie "Turner Classic Movie") site, "accuracy was not really a concern".[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) The [second film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Message_to_Garcia_(film) "A Message to Garcia (film)") was produced in 1936 by [Twentieth Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twentieth_Century_Fox "Twentieth Century Fox") and directed by [George Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marshall_(director) "George Marshall (director)"). It featured [John Boles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boles_(actor) "John Boles (actor)") as Andrew Rowan, [Wallace Beery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Beery "Wallace Beery"), [Barbara Stanwyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Stanwyck "Barbara Stanwyck"), [Alan Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hale_Sr. "Alan Hale Sr."), [Herbert Mundin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Mundin "Herbert Mundin"), [Mona Barrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mona_Barrie "Mona Barrie"), and [Enrique Acosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Acosta "Enrique Acosta") as Garcia—an "agreeable embroidery" according to [Leslie Halliwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Halliwell "Leslie Halliwell").[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21)
