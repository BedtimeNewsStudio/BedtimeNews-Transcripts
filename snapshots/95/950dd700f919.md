General elections were held in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Singapore_(Malaysia) "State of Singapore (Malaysia)") on 21 September 1963 to elect all 51 members of the [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Singapore "Legislative Assembly of Singapore"). This was the first and only general election held when Singapore was [part of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Agreement "Malaysia Agreement") as an [autonomous state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division "Autonomous administrative division"), and just days after it became fully independent from the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") on [16 September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Day "Malaysia Day") following [full internal self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance_of_Singapore#Full_internal_self-government_(1959%E2%80%931963) "Self-governance of Singapore") in 1959. It was also the only election to date without any [boundary changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistricting "Redistricting") to constituencies. The [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party "People's Action Party") (PAP) under [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") won 37 of the 51 seats, while 13 went to the [Barisan Sosialis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Sosialis "Barisan Sosialis") (BS) led by [Lee Siew Choh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Siew_Choh "Lee Siew Choh"), its [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing "Left-wing") splinter party. The [United People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_People's_Party_(Singapore) "United People's Party (Singapore)") (UPP) secured one seat through its leader [Ong Eng Guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Eng_Guan "Ong Eng Guan"), a former PAP member.

The ruling [Alliance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Party_(Malaysia) "Alliance Party (Malaysia)") of Malaysia led by the [United Malays National Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Malays_National_Organisation "United Malays National Organisation") (UMNO) contested the election through its [Singapore branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Alliance_Party "Singapore Alliance Party") (SAP) in an attempt to unseat the PAP, straining [PAP–UMNO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAP%E2%80%93UMNO_relations "PAP–UMNO relations"). However, the Alliance lost all seven of its seats which they held in Singapore, including those in [Malay dominated areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Singaporeans "Malay Singaporeans"). In response, the PAP contested seats in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia") during the [1964 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Malaysian_general_election "1964 Malaysian general election") the following year, further deepening tensions and mistrust between the Alliance federal government and the PAP state government.

A total of 210 candidates contested the elections, marking the largest slate in Singapore for over six decades until it was surpassed in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Singaporean_general_election "2025 Singaporean general election"). The ruling PAP fielded candidates in all 51 seats, while its breakaway parties, BS and the UPP, each put forward 46 and the SAP fielded 42. This was the last election in which any party other than the PAP contested more than half the parliamentary seats. With the two PAP splinter groups and the Alliance fielding nearly full slates, it became one of the most fiercely contested elections and posed a serious challenge to the ruling PAP. Voter turnout reached 95.11%, the highest at the time in Singapore's history.

This was the most recent general election in which the PAP received less than half of the popular vote, at only 47%, and the last parliamentary election to date where MPs (29 out of 51) were elected by a [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_(voting) "Plurality (voting)") due to the [first-past-the-post voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-past-the-post_voting "First-past-the-post voting") system. It was also the last general election in which an incumbent minister was defeated until [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Singaporean_general_election "2011 Singaporean general election") and the last general election in which all seats were contested until [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Singaporean_general_election "2015 Singaporean general election"). With the [Independence of Singapore Agreement 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Singapore_Agreement_1965 "Independence of Singapore Agreement 1965"), this election was the only one that was held when Singapore was a state of Malaysia. After independence, the elected members of the Legislative Assembly subsequently become members of the [inaugural Parliament of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Parliament_of_Singapore "1st Parliament of Singapore").

## Background

Although the [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party "People's Action Party") (PAP) had won 43 seats in the [1959 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Singaporean_general_election "1959 Singaporean general election"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-stresults-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-stresults) they lost four seats in 1961 (two were from the [by-election defeats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Singaporean_by-elections "1961 Singaporean by-elections"), and two defected to the new [United People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_People's_Party_(Singapore) "United People's Party (Singapore)") (UPP)). A further 13 legislators were expelled from PAP for voting against the government in a no-confidence motion on 20 July 1961; the dissidents subsequently formed a new party, the [Barisan Sosialis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Sosialis "Barisan Sosialis") (BS), alleging PAP as a communist front. The combination of by-election defeats, defections and expulsions reduced the PAP by 17 seats down to 26, leaving PAP with a one-seat majority.

On 3 July 1962, while the [integration referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Singaporean_integration_referendum "1962 Singaporean integration referendum") debate was in procession, the PAP lost its majority following the resignation of legislator Ho Puay Choo, who later joined BS on 11 August. Five days later, UPP legislator S. V. Lingam rejoined PAP, giving it back its one-seat majority. However, the majority was lost again on 21 August following the death of health minister [Ahmad Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Ibrahim_(Singaporean_politician) "Ahmad Ibrahim (Singaporean politician)"), who was the MP for [Sembawang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sembawang_Constituency "Sembawang Constituency").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-:6) BS planned to field [Lim Chin Siong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Chin_Siong "Lim Chin Siong") to contest in a by-election, but it was not held.

On 31 August 1963, Singapore was declared fully independent from the United Kingdom (full internal self-government was granted in 1959) with PAP declared as trustees until the merger with Malaysia could be complete. On 3 September, Lee Kuan Yew dissolved the Legislative Assembly in accordance with procedure, the [Proclamation of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Malaysia "Proclamation of Malaysia") was ratified on 16 September, and elections are to be held on 21 September. As part of the [Malaysia Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Agreement "Malaysia Agreement"), the [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heads_of_state_governments_of_Malaysia "Heads of state governments of Malaysia") in Singapore were permitted to retain the title of "Prime Minister" despite there already being a [prime minister of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Malaysia "Prime minister of Malaysia"), as Singapore were given more autonomy in some areas than the other states of Malaysia.

## Campaign

The elections, held in the midst of Singapore's merger with Malaysia, are remembered as the PAP's hardest-fought as the party faced intensive challenges from three other parties that fielded nearly full slates. BS collated with [Parti Rakyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti_Rakyat_Malaysia "Parti Rakyat Malaysia"), and fielded candidates in all but two seats, while UPP had an unusually large number of candidates. Earlier that year on 2 February, as part of an [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communist "Anti-communist") security operation, the PAP government with support of the Malaysian federal government and the British government had launched [Operation Coldstore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Coldstore "Operation Coldstore") to detain several BS leaders, including [Lim Chin Siong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Chin_Siong "Lim Chin Siong"), which severely hampered BS.

On the final night of campaigning, PAP officials issued a sudden warning: if BS were to win the election and defeat the PAP, the federal government in Kuala Lumpur might deploy troops to Singapore and invoke [emergency powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_powers "Emergency powers"), bypassing the incoming BS-led government, allegedly due to its perceived pro- [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist "Communist") stance. This last-minute tactic, seen by many as [fearmongering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearmongering "Fearmongering"), left BS with no opportunity to respond. Nevertheless, it appeared to sway public sentiment and may have contributed significantly to the PAP's victory the following day.

The sole [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_of_Singapore "Workers' Party of Singapore") (WP) legislator [David Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Marshall_(Singaporean_politician) "David Marshall (Singaporean politician)") resigned from the party he founded and became the only independent. Another participant was [Singapore Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Alliance "Singapore Alliance"), an extension of the ruling federal [Alliance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Party_(Malaya) "Alliance Party (Malaya)") in Malaysia, which was a coalition consisting of the [Singapore People's Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_People's_Alliance "Singapore People's Alliance") (SPA) along with the local branches of UMNO, the [Malayan Chinese Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese_Association "Malaysian Chinese Association") and [Malayan Indian Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian_Congress "Malaysian Indian Congress"). However, former Chief Minister and leader [Lim Yew Hock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Yew_Hock "Lim Yew Hock") opted not to run in the elections, citing a defamation campaign by the PAP.[&#8202;](#ref-back-A_Moment_of_Anguish-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-A_Moment_of_Anguish)

## Aftermath and analysis

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/The_Straits_Times%2C_22_September_1963.jpg/500px-The_Straits_Times%2C_22_September_1963.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Straits_Times,_22_September_1963.jpg) _[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Straits_Times "The Straits Times")_ the day after the 1963 election, reporting on the results.

The PAP won a landslide victory, securing 37 seats and retaining its [two-thirds majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-thirds_majority "Two-thirds majority") in the Legislative Assembly. This result had been uncertain in the lead-up to the vote, and while the PAP emerged as the clear winner, its overall vote share was only 46.93%, the lowest in its history. Several key factors contributed to this outcome despite strong competition from BS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-meninwhite-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-meninwhite) One major reason was the PAP's last-minute warning that if BS formed the government, the federal government in Kuala Lumpur could respond by sending troops into Singapore and enforcing [emergency powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_powers "Emergency powers"). Many voters were also concerned that BS' opposition to merger with Malaya would jeopardise such negotiations. Support for merger was strong among the public, and BS' alignment with [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") and the [Communist Party of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Indonesia "Communist Party of Indonesia") (PKI) raised further alarm, especially as Indonesia had declared a _[Konfrontasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konfrontasi "Konfrontasi")_ and begun provocative military activity in [Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo").[&#8202;](#ref-back-meninwhite-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-meninwhite)

The English-educated middle class, worried about the rise of communism, voted tactically for the PAP after the left-wing split that created BS. The PAP also gained credit for its achievements in government since 1959. These included the construction of 26,000 flats by the [Housing and Development Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_and_Development_Board "Housing and Development Board") (HDB), a reduction in unemployment and expanded investment in public services. Many voters saw continued PAP rule as essential for stability and progress.[&#8202;](#ref-back-meninwhite-2)[\[5\]](#refnote-meninwhite) Although the opposition secured a total of 14 seats, both BS and UPP failed to win most of the constituencies they contested because of the [split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_splitting "Vote splitting") among anti-PAP voters. Additionally, 16 incumbent candidates were defeated, and the PAP experienced some notable losses, including cabinet ministers [K. M. Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._M._Byrne "K. M. Byrne") and [Tan Kia Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Kia_Gan "Tan Kia Gan") in [Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawford_Single_Member_Constituency "Crawford Single Member Constituency") and [Paya Lebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paya_Lebar_Single_Member_Constituency "Paya Lebar Single Member Constituency") respectively, marking the first time a cabinet minister had lost in their own constituency. This would not happen again until [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Singaporean_general_election "2011 Singaporean general election"), 48 years and 11 elections later, when cabinet ministers [Lim Hwee Hua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Hwee_Hua "Lim Hwee Hua") and [George Yeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Yeo "George Yeo") were defeated in their constituency of [Aljunied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljunied_Group_Representation_Constituency "Aljunied Group Representation Constituency").

In the aftermath of the election, the PAP government passed a constitutional amendment stipulating that legislators who resign or are expelled from the parties they were elected under would lose their seats, in order to discourage future defections. As a result, by-elections were subsequently held in [Hong Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Hong_Lim_by-election "1965 Hong Lim by-election") in 1965, [seven constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Singaporean_by-elections "1966 Singaporean by-elections") in 1966 and [five constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Singaporean_by-elections "1967 Singaporean by-elections") in 1967. Those victories resulted PAP in achieving a parliament [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly "Monopoly") that would last for the next 15 years until the first elected opposition MP in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Anson_by-election "1981 Anson by-election"). The changes during the 1963–1968 parliamentary term remains the biggest turnover of MPs in post-independence Singapore.

### Federal government response

The [Singapore Alliance Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Alliance_Party "Singapore Alliance Party") also lost all seven seats it had held before dissolution. The federal Malaysian prime minister [Tunku Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunku_Abdul_Rahman "Tunku Abdul Rahman") and the rest of UMNO were shocked by the Alliance's wipeout in the election. The [Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Party_(Malaysia) "Alliance Party (Malaysia)") which ruled the federal government had expected strong support from the [Malay Singaporean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Singaporean "Malay Singaporean") electorate, as was the case in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia"), but the PAP swept all of UMNO's Malay dominated constituencies, including [Kampong Kembangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampong_Kembangan_Single_Member_Constituency "Kampong Kembangan Single Member Constituency"), [Geylang Serai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geylang_Serai_Single_Member_Constituency "Geylang Serai Single Member Constituency") and the [Southern Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Islands_Single_Member_Constituency "Southern Islands Single Member Constituency"). Tunku attributed the setback to "traitors" within Singapore UMNO and among sections of the Singaporean Malay community. On 27 September, Tunku declared that he would "personally direct" UMNO's affairs in Singapore and play "an important part" in its future election campaigns. This deepened mistrust between the PAP state government and the Malaysian federal government, and became one of the factors leading to Singapore's expulsion two years later.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nlb2-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-nlb2)

The Secretary General of UMNO, [Syed Jaafar Albar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Jaafar_Albar "Syed Jaafar Albar"), reacted even more strongly to the defeat, publicly vowing in the Malaysian federal parliament to "fix" [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew") using "both words and fists". Other Malay extremists delivered equally heated speeches at various meetings and alleged that the PAP had "intimidated" Malays into voting against UMNO, with some even burning an [effigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effigy "Effigy") of Lee at a meeting in Singapore to display their anger.[&#8202;](#ref-back-nlb2-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-nlb2) The Singapore Alliance became dormant in 1965,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Singapore_Alliance_Background-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-Singapore_Alliance_Background) and the [Singapore People's Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_People's_Alliance "Singapore People's Alliance"), one of its membership parties, was dissolved on 16 May 1965, shortly before Singapore's independence from Malaysia on 9 August 1965.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-9)[&#8202;](#ref-back-nlb-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-nlb)

### Distribution of Singapore's seats in the Dewan Rakyat

The distribution of 15 seats from Singapore in Malaysia's lower house of Parliament, the [Dewan Rakyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewan_Rakyat "Dewan Rakyat"), was based on the outcome of the election. PAP was allocated 12, which were given to Prime Minister Lee, Deputy Prime Minister [Toh Chin Chye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toh_Chin_Chye "Toh Chin Chye"), ministers [Goh Keng Swee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Keng_Swee "Goh Keng Swee"), [Ong Pang Boon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ong_Pang_Boon "Ong Pang Boon"), [S. Rajaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Rajaratnam "S. Rajaratnam"), [Yong Nyuk Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yong_Nyuk_Lin "Yong Nyuk Lin"), [Jek Yeun Thong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jek_Yeun_Thong "Jek Yeun Thong"), [Lim Kim San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Kim_San "Lim Kim San"), [Othman Wok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Othman_Wok "Othman Wok") and assembly members [Abdul Rahim Ishak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rahim_Ishak "Abdul Rahim Ishak"), Wee Toon Boon and Ho See Beng. BS was allocated 3, which were given to [Chia Thye Poh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chia_Thye_Poh "Chia Thye Poh"), Lim Huan Boon and Kow Kee Seng.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-12)
