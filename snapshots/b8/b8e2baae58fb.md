# Free Economic Zone of Manaus - Wikipedia

[Jump to content](#bodyContent)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Brazilian industrial zone

The **Free Economic Zone of Manaus** ( [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language "Portuguese language"): _Zona Franca de Manaus_, Portuguese pronunciation:[\[ˈzõnɐˈfɾɐ̃kɐdʒimɐˈnaws\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese "Help:IPA/Portuguese")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/51/Pt-br-Zona_Franca_de_Manaus.ogg/Pt-br-Zona_Franca_de_Manaus.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pt-br-Zona_Franca_de_Manaus.ogg "File:Pt-br-Zona Franca de Manaus.ogg") \- **ZFM**) is a [free economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_economic_zone "Free economic zone") in the city of [Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaus "Manaus"), the capital of the [State of Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_(Brazilian_state) "Amazonas (Brazilian state)"), [Northern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Brazil "Northern Brazil"). The initial idea, a [free trade port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_port "Free port") in Manaus, came from Deputy Francisco Pereira da Silva and was subsequently formalized by Law No. 3.173 on June 6, 1957. The project was approved by the National Congress on October 23, 1951 under No. 1.310 and regulated by Decree No. 47.757 on February 2, 1960. It was then amended by rapporteur Maurício Jopper, engineer, who by agreement with the original author, justified the creation of a free economic zone instead of a free trade port.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)

For the first ten years the ZFM (Manaus Free Trade Zone) was located in a warehouse rented from Manaus [Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour "Harbour"), in the [Port of Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Manaus "Port of Manaus"), and relied on federal funds. It was perhaps due to this lack of its own resources that there was little credibility in the project.

On February 28, 1967, President Castello Branco signed Decree-Law No. 288,[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) whose draft accompanied the Exposition of Motives. Decree-Law No. 288 amended the provisions of Law No. 3.173/57 and redefined the Manaus Free Trade Zone in more concrete terms. The new Decree-Law stipulated that the Manaus Free Trade Zone would have an area of 10,000km2 (3,900sqmi) with an industrial centre as well as an agricultural center and that these would be given the economic means to allow for regional development in order to lift the Amazon out of the economic isolation that it had fallen into at that time. Decree No. 61.244 of August 28, 1967 created the Manaus Free Trade Zone Superintendence, SUFRAMA, an autarchy with its own legal status and assets and having financial and administrative autonomy.

[Tax incentives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_incentive "Tax incentive") and the subsequent complementary legislation created [comparative advantages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_advantage "Comparative advantage") in the region with respect to other parts of the country and as a result the Manaus Free Trade Zone attracted new investment to the area.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) These incentives constituted tax exemptions administered federally by SUFRAMA and SUDAM.
