The **BMW 501** was a [luxury car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_car "Luxury car") manufactured by [BMW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW "BMW") from 1952 to 1958. Introduced at the first [Frankfurt Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Motor_Show "Frankfurt Motor Show") in 1951, the 501 was the first BMW model to be manufactured and sold after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), and the first BMW automobile actually built in [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria "Bavaria"). The 501 and its derivatives, including the [V8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_engine "V8 engine") powered **BMW 502**, were nicknamed “Baroque Angels” for their graceful if dated styling by the German public. The BMW 502 was the first postwar German car to be manufactured with a V8 engine.

While the 501 and 502 model numbers were discontinued in 1958, variations of the model, with the same platform and body, were continued until 1963.

## Background

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")]

### Awtowelo restarts "BMW" production

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Awtowelo restarts ")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Emw340-2-bild2.jpg/250px-Emw340-2-bild2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Emw340-2-bild2.jpg)

[EMW 340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_340 "BMW 340")

BMW's World War II-era satellite motor car factory in [Eisenach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenach "Eisenach") fell in the post-war [Soviet occupation zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_zone "Soviet occupation zone") in the newly created Communist [GDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDR "GDR"). Manufacturing of pre-war BMW models was restarted postwar in late 1945 by the Soviet created entity  [Sowjetische AG Maschinenbau Awtowelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobilwerk_Eisenach "Automobilwerk Eisenach"). Despite not being made by BMW AG, these cars bore the BMW logo and were being sold as BMWs.[[2]](#cite_note-Norbye75-2)

Meanwhile, BMW AG in Bavaria restarted manufacture on a much smaller scale, beginning with pots and pans and eventually moving up to household hardware and bicycles. Eventually, with permission from the U.S. authorities and funding from the banks under which BMW had been put into receivership, it began manufacturing motorcycles in 1948.[[3]](#cite_note-Norbye76-3)

To end Awtowelo's continued [trademark infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trademark_infringement "Trademark infringement"), the Eisenach branch of BMW was legally dissolved effective 28 September 1949 and severed as of 11 October. To avoid illegally using BMW's name and logo, Awtowelo simply renamed the concern EMW ([Eisenacher Motorenwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenacher_Motorenwerk "Eisenacher Motorenwerk"), Eisenach Motors' Work) in 1952, retained all the tooling and equipment, and replaced the blue in the BMW logo to red.[[3]](#cite_note-Norbye76-3)

### Three approaches to car manufacture

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Three approaches to car manufacture")]

#### Donath

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Donath")]

Kurt Donath, technical director of BMW and general manager of the Milbertshofen factory,[[3]](#cite_note-Norbye76-3) was soliciting manufacturers, including [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company") and [Simca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simca "Simca"), to produce their vehicles under licence. In particular, Donath was looking to produce old products under licence, so that he could buy tooling along with the licence.[[4]](#cite_note-Norbye86-4)

#### Böning

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Böning")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/BMW_531_1951_01.jpg/250px-BMW_531_1951_01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BMW_531_1951_01.jpg)

1951 BMW 331 prototype

While Donath was trying to find a car to build under licence, chief engineer [Alfred Böning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Böning "Alfred Böning") developed a prototype for a small economy car powered by a motorcycle engine. Called the BMW 331, the prototype used a 600 cc motorcycle engine, a four-speed gearbox, and a live rear axle. The body was designed by Peter Szymanowski and resembled a [BMW 327](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_327 "BMW 327") in miniature.[[4]](#cite_note-Norbye86-4)

#### Grewenig

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Grewenig")]

The BMW 331 was proposed for production to the management, where it was vetoed by sales director Hanns Grewenig. Grewenig, a banker and former Opel plant manager, believed that BMW's small production capacity was best suited to luxury cars with high profit margins, similar to the cars BMW made just before the war. To this end, he had Böning and his team create the car that would become the 501.[[5]](#cite_note-Norbye87-5)

## Design and engineering

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Design and engineering")]

The 501 was an all-new [platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_platform "Automobile platform"), with a perimeter [frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_(vehicle) "Frame (vehicle)"), double A-arm front suspension with [torsion bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_bar "Torsion bar") springs,[[5]](#cite_note-Norbye87-5) and a [live axle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_axle "Live axle") with torsion bar springs at the rear.[[6]](#cite_note-Norbye88-6) The steering mechanism was similar to a rack and pinion system except that the rack was semi-circular instead of straight.[[6]](#cite_note-Norbye88-6)[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7)

The 501 was powered by the 2 L [straight-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight-six_engine "Straight-six engine") [OHV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_valve_engine "Overhead valve engine") [M337](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_M337 "BMW M337") engine, a development of the [BMW M78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_M78 "BMW M78") used in the pre-war [BMW 326](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_326 "BMW 326").

The four-speed gearbox was not bolted to the engine, but was a separate shaft-driven unit mounted between the second and third crossmembers.[[6]](#cite_note-Norbye88-6)[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7) While the remote gearbox placement led to a complicated linkage to the column-mounted shifter, resulting in vague shifter action, it also improved legroom for the front passengers.[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7)

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/SuicideDoorsonBMW.jpg/250px-SuicideDoorsonBMW.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SuicideDoorsonBMW.jpg)

Coach doors led to a luxurious interior

The body was designed in house by Peter Szymanowski. After seeing the prototype, BMW's management commissioned Pininfarina to build an alternative. The Pininfarina prototype was thought to be too similar to his design for the [Alfa Romeo 1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo_1900 "Alfa Romeo 1900") saloon, so BMW stayed with Szymanowski's design.[[5]](#cite_note-Norbye87-5) The steel body was far heavier than Szymanowski had calculated it to be, resulting in the completed car having a dry weight of 1,430 kilograms (3,150 lb).[[6]](#cite_note-Norbye88-6) Performance suffered as a result, with a top speed of 135 km/h (84 mph) and acceleration to 100 km/h (62 mph) taking 27 seconds, both of which compared unfavourably with the six-cylinder [Mercedes-Benz 220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_W187 "Mercedes-Benz W187").[[8]](#cite_note-Norbye89-8)

One innovation was the attention paid to passive safety. The car featured a robust chassis providing above average side impact protection, an unusually short steering column with the steering gear set well back from the front of the car, and a fuel tank placed in a carefully protected location above the rear axle in order to minimize fire risk in the event of an accident.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## Reception and production

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Reception and production")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F014934-0025%2C_M%C3%BCnchen%2C_Staatsbesuch_Vizepr%C3%A4sident_von_Zypern.jpg/330px-Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F014934-0025%2C_M%C3%BCnchen%2C_Staatsbesuch_Vizepr%C3%A4sident_von_Zypern.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F014934-0025,_München,_Staatsbesuch_Vizepräsident_von_Zypern.jpg)

A line of 501s in 1963

The 501 was introduced to the public in April 1951 at the [Frankfurt Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Motor_Show "Frankfurt Motor Show"),[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7)[[8]](#cite_note-Norbye89-8) as was its less expensive, production-ready rival, the [Mercedes-Benz 220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_W187 "Mercedes-Benz W187"). The 501 made an impression on the public with its solid engineering and its extravagance.[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7) Its list price of more than fifteen thousand [Deutsche Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mark "Deutsche Mark")[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7)[[9]](#cite_note-Norbye90-9) was about four times the average yearly salary in Germany at the time.[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7) The public nicknamed the 501 "*Barockengel*" (Baroque Angel) in reference to the curved, flowing style of the body, which was reminiscent of the [Baroque era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_era "Baroque era").[[5]](#cite_note-Norbye87-5)[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7)

Development issues delayed the start of production until late 1952,[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7) and even then BMW still did not have equipment for pressing body panels in operation.[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7)[[8]](#cite_note-Norbye89-8) The first 2,045 four-door saloon bodies were built by [Karosserie Baur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karosserie_Baur "Karosserie Baur")[[8]](#cite_note-Norbye89-8) and were shipped from Baur in Stuttgart to BMW's factory in Munich for assembly.[[7]](#cite_note-Noakes46-7) The thousandth 501 was completed on 1 September 1953.[[9]](#cite_note-Norbye90-9)

The 501 and derivatives built at BMW were four-door saloons. Coupe and convertible versions were available as custom orders from Baur or Autenrieth.[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)

A road test of the 501 in March 1953 by *Auto- und Motorrad-Welt* reported better than average [wind resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_drag_coefficients "Automobile drag coefficients"), as well as good ride quality and an effective heating system. The [fuel consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_consumption "Fuel consumption") was reported to be 10.3 L/100 km (27 mpg‑imp; 23 mpg‑US).[[11]](#cite_note-DeutscherSportverlag-11)

## Development of the BMW 501

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Development of the BMW 501")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/BMW-501-Achtzylinder.jpg/250px-BMW-501-Achtzylinder.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BMW-501-Achtzylinder.jpg)

BMW 501 V8

The 501A was released in 1954 as a replacement for the original 501 with similar trim and equipment, but which sold for DM14,180, a price reduction of more than eight hundred Deutsche Mark from the original 501.[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)[[12]](#cite_note-Noakes48-12) The 501B was a decontented version that sold for DM500 less than the 501A.[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10) Both the 501A and 501B used a revised [M337](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_M337 "BMW M337") engine.

The engine and the model designation were altered again in the spring of 1955. The 501A and 501 B were replaced by the 501/3, with an updated [M337](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_M337 "BMW M337") engine. The 501/3 was introduced alongside the 501 V8, which featured a detuned version of the 2.6-litre V8 introduced in the 502 the previous year. The 501/3 and 501 V8 were continued until 1958, when the six-cylinder engine and the 501 model designation were discontinued.[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)

Before the construction of the first prototype of the 501, Böning had calculated the mass of the car as designed, and realized that the six-cylinder engine would be barely adequate to power the car. He proposed the development of a larger engine to power future versions to the management, who accepted his proposal. Böning began the design and development of a [V8 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_engine "V8 engine") similar in general design to the then-new 303-cubic-inch (5.0 L) [overhead valve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_valve "Overhead valve") [Oldsmobile Rocket V8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldsmobile_V8_engine "Oldsmobile V8 engine"), with a single in-block camshaft operating overhead valves in wedge-shaped [combustion chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion_chamber "Combustion chamber") through pushrods.[[9]](#cite_note-Norbye90-9) The [BMW OHV V8 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_OHV_V8_engine "BMW OHV V8 engine") differed from its Detroit contemporaries in the use of an [aluminium alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_alloy "Aluminium alloy") block[[12]](#cite_note-Noakes48-12) with cast-iron cylinder liners,[[13]](#cite_note-Norbye91-13) and in being half the size or smaller, initially with a 74 mm (2.9 in) bore and a 75 mm (3.0 in) stroke, giving a displacement of 2,580 cc (157 cu in).[[13]](#cite_note-Norbye91-13)[[14]](#cite_note-Noakes49-14) The development of the V8 was completed by [Fritz Fiedler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Fiedler "Fritz Fiedler"), who replaced Böning as BMW's chief engineer in 1952.[[9]](#cite_note-Norbye90-9)

The V8 engine was introduced at the 1954 [Geneva Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Motor_Show "Geneva Motor Show") as the engine of the new BMW 502 saloon car.[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)[[12]](#cite_note-Noakes48-12) Using the same chassis and basically the same body as the 501,[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10) the 502 was more luxuriously appointed[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)[[12]](#cite_note-Noakes48-12) and, with its light V8 engine producing 100 horsepower (75 kW) with a single two-barrel Solex carburettor,[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)[[14]](#cite_note-Noakes49-14) was much faster.[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)[[12]](#cite_note-Noakes48-12) The published top speed of 160 km/h (99 mph) was far higher than that of the first six-cylinder version of the [Ponton Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_W180 "Mercedes-Benz W180") launched the same year. At the time of its introduction the 502 was reportedly Germany's fastest production passenger sedan.[[15]](#cite_note-AutoMotoruSport1974-15)

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/2007-07-22_BMW_502_-_Baur-Cabriolet_in_Montabaur_%2801a_klein_r%29.jpg/250px-2007-07-22_BMW_502_-_Baur-Cabriolet_in_Montabaur_%2801a_klein_r%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2007-07-22_BMW_502_-_Baur-Cabriolet_in_Montabaur_(01a_klein_r).jpg)

BMW 502 cabriolet by Baur

 [![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/2007-07-22_BMW_502_-_Baur-Cabriolet_Cockpit.jpg/250px-2007-07-22_BMW_502_-_Baur-Cabriolet_Cockpit.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2007-07-22_BMW_502_-_Baur-Cabriolet_Cockpit.jpg)

Steering wheel of BMW 502

The 502 was acclaimed as Germany's first post-[war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") V8 powered car,[[15]](#cite_note-AutoMotoruSport1974-15) but its high price of DM17,800 led to low sales; only 190 were sold in its first year of production.[[10]](#cite_note-Norbye92-10)

The 502 was distinguished from the 501 by additional chrome trim[*[where?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Naming_conventions_(geographic_names) "Wikipedia:Naming conventions (geographic names)")*] and more lavish interior fittings. Fog lights and individual front seats were also now included as standard features.[[15]](#cite_note-AutoMotoruSport1974-15) The 502 was mildly restyled in 1955 with a wraparound rear window.[[12]](#cite_note-Noakes48-12)

As well as the saloon version, BMW offered [Baur built](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karosserie_Baur "Karosserie Baur") two-door cabriolet and coupé versions of the 502 in 1954 and 1955.[[16]](#cite_note-AlleAutosder50er-16) 501s and 502s were also converted into ambulances and hearses.[[14]](#cite_note-Noakes49-14)

### BMW 2.6 and 2.6 Luxus

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: BMW 2.6 and 2.6 Luxus")]

The 501 and 502 model designations were discontinued in 1958, when the 501 V8 was renamed the BMW 2.6 and the 502 was renamed the 2.6 Luxus.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)[[18]](#cite_note-Norbye252-18)[[19]](#cite_note-Noakes57-19) The cars were continued under these model designations until 1961 with only two notable changes: [power steering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_steering "Power steering") became an option in 1959, while front disc brakes were added in 1960.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)

### BMW 2600 and 2600L

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: BMW 2600 and 2600L")]

In 1961, the model designations were changed again, to 2600 and 2600L. The engine in the 2600L was tuned to give 110 horsepower (82 kW). Production ended in 1963.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)

## BMW 3.2 and 3.2 Super

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: BMW 3.2 and 3.2 Super")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/BMW_3200_L%2C_Bj._1962.jpg/250px-BMW_3200_L%2C_Bj._1962.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BMW_3200_L,_Bj._1962.jpg)

1962 BMW 3200L

A further development of the V8 engine was introduced at the 1955 [Frankfurt Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Motor_Show "Frankfurt Motor Show"). The engine had a 82 mm (3.2 in) bore, giving a capacity of 3,168 cc (193.3 cu in).[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17) The engine made its debut in four new cars at the show, the [507](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_507 "BMW 507") two-seat gran turismo, the [503](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_503 "BMW 503") 2+2 gran turismo,[[20]](#cite_note-Norbye113-20)[[21]](#cite_note-Norbye117-21) the 505 limousine prototype,[[21]](#cite_note-Norbye117-21) and the BMW 3.2, a development of the 502 that did not have a model number and was identified simply by its displacement in litres.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)[[21]](#cite_note-Norbye117-21) As used in the 3.2 and the 505, the engine had a compression ratio of 7.2:1, up from the 7.0:1 of the original 2.6 L V8 engine. In this tune, the engine yielded 120 horsepower (89 kW)[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)[[21]](#cite_note-Norbye117-21) The final drive ratio was raised from 4.225:1 on the smaller-engined sedans[[6]](#cite_note-Norbye88-6) to 3.89:1 on the 3.2 to reduce the fuel consumption of the larger engine.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)

In 1957, the 3.2 Super with a 140 horsepower (100 kW) engine, was released. The 3.2 and 3.2 Super were continued under these model designations until 1961 with only two notable changes: [power steering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_steering "Power steering") became an option in 1959, while front disc brakes were added to the 3.2 Super in October 1959 and to the 3.2 in 1960.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)

### BMW 3200L and 3200S

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: BMW 3200L and 3200S")]

In 1961, the 3.2 and 3.2 Super were replaced by the 3200L and 3200S respectively. The 3200L had a single carburettor engine that produced 140 horsepower (100 kW), while the 3200S had a twin carburettor engine that produced 160 horsepower (120 kW) at 5600 rpm.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)

A report on a 3.2-litre BMW saloon estimated the 502's consumption at 15 L/100 km (19 mpg‑imp; 16 mpg‑US).[[22]](#cite_note-AutoModelle63_64-22)

Production ended in 1963.[[17]](#cite_note-Norbye93-17)

### BMW 503 and 507

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: BMW 503 and 507")]

Main articles: [BMW 503](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_503 "BMW 503") and [BMW 507](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_507 "BMW 507")

The BMW 503 and 507 were [grand touring cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_tourer "Grand tourer"); the 503 was a four-seat coupé or convertible while the 507 was a two-seat convertible. Conceived by US importer [Max Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Hoffman "Max Hoffman"),[[23]](#cite_note-Norbye96-23) designed by German-American designer [Albrecht von Goertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_von_Goertz "Albrecht von Goertz"),[[20]](#cite_note-Norbye113-20) and engineered by [Fritz Fiedler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Fiedler "Fritz Fiedler"), the 503 and 507 used variations of a chassis specially designed for them,[[23]](#cite_note-Norbye96-23) along with reworked twin carburettor versions of the 3.2 engines.[[20]](#cite_note-Norbye113-20) They were supposed to be priced at about five thousand dollars in the United States and be sold in the thousands by Hoffman. Instead, the 503 and 507 ended up being priced at about ten thousand dollars, and only 412 and 252, respectively, were sold.[[24]](#cite_note-Norbye115-24)

### BMW 505 prototype

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: BMW 505 prototype")]

The [Frankfurt Motor Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Motor_Show "Frankfurt Motor Show") in 1955 saw the presentation of the BMW 505, a [limousine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limousine "Limousine") based on the new 3.2 saloon.[[21]](#cite_note-Norbye117-21) Styled by [Giovanni Michelotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Michelotti "Giovanni Michelotti"), the 505 was 5.1 metres long, with a wheelbase of about 3.1 metres.[[25]](#cite_note-Norbye118-25) [Konrad Adenauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer "Konrad Adenauer"), the [Chancellor of West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Germany_(Federal_Republic) "Chancellor of Germany (Federal Republic)"), tried out the 505.[[26]](#cite_note-Noakes52-26) It is reported that, on entering the car, Adenauer knocked his hat off.[[25]](#cite_note-Norbye118-25) Regardless of the reason, Adenauer continued to use the [Mercedes-Benz 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_W186 "Mercedes-Benz W186") as his official car to the extent that the type acquired the nickname “Adenauer Mercedes”.[[26]](#cite_note-Noakes52-26) The BMW 505 never went into series production,[[26]](#cite_note-Noakes52-26) and only two prototypes were built.[[25]](#cite_note-Norbye118-25)

The 505 was briefly used on May 20, 2023, during the wedding of Prince Ludwig Heinrich of Bavaria and Sophia-Alexandra Evekink.[[27]](#cite_note-27)

### BMW 3200 CS

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: BMW 3200 CS")]

Main article: [BMW 3200 CS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_3200_CS "BMW 3200 CS")

The BMW 3200 CS was a [coupé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupé "Coupé") built from 1961 to 1965.[[28]](#cite_note-Norbye132-28) The body was designed and built by [Bertone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppo_Bertone "Gruppo Bertone") and shipped to BMW's factory in Munich, where it was fitted to a 3200S chassis.[[29]](#cite_note-Norbye131-29) This was the last variant of the 501 to be manufactured by BMW.[[29]](#cite_note-Norbye131-29)[[30]](#cite_note-Noakes63-30)

## Police vehicles

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=BMW_501&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Police vehicles")]

During the 1950s and 1960s, the 501 radio patrol cars were widely used as police vehicles by many police departments in Bavaria, including [Bavarian State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Police "Bavarian State Police") and Munich City Police (*Stadtpolizei München*). The car with its relatively fast 145 km/h (90 mph) top speed by 1950s standards, was a blessing for police, as most criminals did not have easy access to faster cars like a [Mercedes-Benz 300 SL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_300_SL "Mercedes-Benz 300 SL") or a [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche "Porsche"). The traffic police units for country roads and Autobahns were even issued V8-powered cars, which were even faster.[[33]](#cite_note-33) The police cars also featured a Lorenz radio telephone, allowing police officers to easily communicate with the dispatch. Munich police force used seventeen 501 patrol cars, which were used for about 10 years and about 130,000 service kilometers before being retired.[[34]](#cite_note-34)[[35]](#cite_note-35) The last two police cars were delivered to [Amberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amberg "Amberg") in 1965, after 501's production had ended. They were retired by 1971 due to rust issues.

A 501 patrol car was the centerpiece of early 1960s police drama [Funkstreife Isar 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkstreife_Isar_12 "Funkstreife Isar 12").
