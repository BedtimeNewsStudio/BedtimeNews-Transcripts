Overview of the education system in Prussia

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Johann_Julius_Hecker_%28bust%29.JPG/250px-Johann_Julius_Hecker_%28bust%29.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Johann_Julius_Hecker_(bust).JPG)

[Johann Julius Hecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Julius_Hecker "Johann Julius Hecker") memorial in Berlin honors him founding the first Prussian teachers' seminary in 1748. Hecker's bust thrones over a future teacher in classical regalia and posture.

 [![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Schulmuseum_Reckahn_085.jpg/250px-Schulmuseum_Reckahn_085.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Schulmuseum_Reckahn_085.jpg)

School Museum in Reckahn, [Brandenburg an der Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_an_der_Havel "Brandenburg an der Havel") quoting Mark 10:14 at the entrance. Founded by [Friedrich Eberhard von Rochow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Eberhard_von_Rochow "Friedrich Eberhard von Rochow") in 1773, Reckahn was the first [one-room school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-room_school "One-room school") with two age-related [classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_(education) "Form (education)") in Prussia.

The **Prussian education system** was established in [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia") as a result of educational reforms in the late 18th and early 19th century, and has had widespread influence since. The Prussian education system was introduced as a basic concept in the late 18th century and was significantly enhanced after Prussia's defeat in the early stages of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars"). The Prussian educational reforms inspired similar changes in other countries, and remain an important consideration in accounting for modern [nation-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation-building "Nation-building") projects and their consequences.[[1]](#cite_note-1)

The term itself is not used in German literature, which refers to the primary aspects of the [Humboldtian education ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldtian_education_ideal "Humboldtian education ideal") respectively as the [Prussian reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_reforms "Prussian reforms"); however, the basic concept has led to various debates and controversies. Twenty-first century primary and secondary [education in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Germany "Education in Germany") still embodies the legacy of the Prussian education system. The [United States public schooling system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_United_States "Education in the United States") and other countries also adopted certain aspects of the Prussian education model. Critics of the Prussian education model argue it was designed to foster [obedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obedience "Obedience") and [social control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_control "Social control"), aiming to "destroy [free will"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will "Free will") rather than encourage true education.[[2]](#cite_note-:0-2)[[3]](#cite_note-3) It has been described as a "factory-model education," emphasizing discipline, conformity, and loyalty to the state over individual thought.[[4]](#cite_note-4)[[2]](#cite_note-:0-2)[[5]](#cite_note-5)

## Origin

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Origin")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Reckahn_Bruns_memorial.jpg/250px-Reckahn_Bruns_memorial.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Reckahn_Bruns_memorial.jpg)

Bruns-Memorial in Reckahn, "He was a teacher"

The basic foundations of a generic Prussian primary education system were laid out by [Frederick the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_the_Great "Frederick the Great") with his *Generallandschulreglement*, a decree of 1763 which was written by [Johann Julius Hecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Julius_Hecker "Johann Julius Hecker"). Hecker had already before (in 1748) founded the first teacher's seminary in Prussia. His concept of providing teachers with the means to cultivate mulberries for homespun silk, which was one of Frederick's favorite projects, found the King's favour.[[6]](#cite_note-VM-6) It expanded the existing schooling system significantly and required that all young citizens, both girls and boys, be educated by mainly municipality-funded schools from the age of 5 to 13 or 14. Prussia was among the first countries in the world to introduce tax-funded and generally compulsory primary education.[[7]](#cite_note-7) In comparison, in France and Great Britain, compulsory schooling was not successfully enacted until the 1880s.[[8]](#cite_note-says-8)

The Prussian system consisted of an eight-year course of primary education, called *[Volksschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksschule "Volksschule")*. It provided not only basic technical skills needed in a modernizing world (such as reading and writing), but also music (singing) and religious (Christian) education in close cooperation with the churches and tried to impose a strict ethos of duty, sobriety and discipline. Mathematics and [calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus "Calculus") were not compulsory at the start, and taking such courses required additional payment by parents. Frederick the Great also formalized further educational stages, the [Realschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realschule "Realschule") and as the highest stage the [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") (state-funded secondary school), which served as a [university-preparatory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University-preparatory_school "University-preparatory school").[[9]](#cite_note-9)

Construction of schools received some state support, but they were often built on private initiative. [Friedrich Eberhard von Rochow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Eberhard_von_Rochow "Friedrich Eberhard von Rochow"), a member of the local gentry and former cavalry officer in Reckahn, Brandenburg, installed such a school. Von Rochow cooperated with [Heinrich Julius Bruns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Julius_Bruns?action=edit&redlink=1 "Heinrich Julius Bruns (page does not exist)") (1746–1794), a talented teacher of modest background. The two installed a model school for rural education that attracted more than 1,200 notable visitors between 1777 and 1794.[[10]](#cite_note-10)

The Prussian system, after its modest beginnings, succeeded in reaching compulsory attendance, specific training for teachers, national testing for all students (both female and male students), a prescribed national [curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum#Curriculum_in_formal_schooling "Curriculum") for each [grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_stages "Educational stages") and mandatory [kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten "Kindergarten").[[11]](#cite_note-11) Training of teachers was increasingly organized via private [seminaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary "Seminary"). Hecker had already in 1748 founded the first "Lehrerseminar", but the density and impact of the seminary system improving significantly until the end of the 18th century.[[12]](#cite_note-12) In 1810, Prussia introduced state certification requirements for teachers, which significantly raised the standard of teaching.[[13]](#cite_note-13) The final examination, *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur "Abitur")*, was introduced in 1788, implemented in all Prussian secondary schools by 1812 and extended to all of Germany in 1871. Passing the Abitur was a prerequisite to entering the learned professions and higher echelons of the civil service. The state-controlled Abitur remains in place in modern Germany.

The Prussian system had by the 1830s attained the following characteristics:[[14]](#cite_note-14)

* Free primary schooling, at least for poor citizens
* Professional teachers trained in specialized colleges
* A basic salary for teachers and recognition of teaching as a profession
* An extended school year to better involve children of farmers
* Funding to build schools
* Supervision at national and classroom level to ensure quality instruction
* Curriculum inculcating a strong national identity, involvement of science and technology
* Secular instruction (but with religion as a topic included in the curriculum)

The German states in the 19th century were world leaders in prestigious education and Prussia set the pace.[[15]](#cite_note-15)[[16]](#cite_note-16) For boys free public education was widely available, and the gymnasium system for elite students was highly professionalized. The modern university system emerged from the 19th century German universities, especially Friedrich Wilhelm University (now named [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin")). It pioneered the model of the research university with well-defined career tracks for professors.[[17]](#cite_note-17) The United States, for example, paid close attention to German models. Families focused on educating their sons. The traditional schooling for girls was generally provided by mothers and governesses. Elite families increasingly favoured Catholic convent boarding schools for their daughters. Prussia's [Kulturkampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulturkampf "Kulturkampf") laws in the 1870s limited Catholic schools thus providing an opening for a large number of new private schools for girls.[[18]](#cite_note-18)

## Outreach

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Outreach")]

The overall system was soon widely admired for its efficiency and reduction of illiteracy, and inspired education leaders in other German states and a number of other countries, including [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Japan "Education in Japan") and [the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_United_States "Education in the United States").[[19]](#cite_note-Jeismann,_Karl-Ernst_2006_pp._21-19)

The underlying [Humboldtian educational ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldtian_science "Humboldtian science") of brothers [Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt "Alexander von Humboldt") and [Wilhelm von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Humboldt "Wilhelm von Humboldt") was about much more than primary education; it strived for academic freedom and the education of both cosmopolitan-minded and loyal citizens from the earliest levels. The Prussian system had strong backing in the traditional German admiration and respect for *Bildung* as an individual's drive to cultivate oneself from within.[[20]](#cite_note-20)

### Drivers and hindrances

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Drivers and hindrances")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/ID003743_B175_FriedrichsCollegium.jpg/250px-ID003743_B175_FriedrichsCollegium.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ID003743_B175_FriedrichsCollegium.jpg)

[Collegium Fridericianum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Fridericianum "Collegium Fridericianum") in Königsberg

Major drivers for improved education in Prussia since the 18th century had a background in the middle and upper middle strata of society and were pioneered by the *[Bildungsbürgertum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsbürgertum "Bildungsbürgertum")*. The concept as such faced strong resistance both from the top, as major players in the ruling nobility feared increasing literacy among peasants and workers would raise unrest, and from the very poor, who preferred to use their children as early as possible for rural or industrial labor.[[6]](#cite_note-VM-6)

The system's proponents overcame such resistance with the help of foreign pressure and internal failures, after the defeat of Prussia in the early stages of the Napoleonic Wars. After the military blunder of Prussian drill and [line formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_formation "Line formation") against the [levée en masse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levée_en_masse "Levée en masse") of the French revolutionary army in the [Battle of Jena–Auerstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jena–Auerstedt "Battle of Jena–Auerstedt") in 1806, reformers and German nationalists urged for major improvements in education. In 1809 [Wilhelm von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Humboldt "Wilhelm von Humboldt"), having been appointed minister of education, promoted his idea of a generic education based on a neohumanist ideal of broad [general knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_knowledge "General knowledge"), in full [academic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_freedom "Academic freedom") without any determination or restriction by status, profession or wealth. Humboldt's [Königsberger Schulplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberger_Schulplan?action=edit&redlink=1 "Königsberger Schulplan (page does not exist)")[[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberger%20Schulplan "de:Königsberger Schulplan")] was one of the earliest [white papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_papers "White papers") to lay out a reform of a country's educational system as a whole.[[21]](#cite_note-21) Humboldt's concept still forms the foundation of the contemporary German education system.[[22]](#cite_note-22) The Prussian system provided compulsory and basic schooling for everyone, but the significantly higher fees for attending *gymnasium* or a university imposed a high barrier between upper social strata and middle and lower social strata.[[23]](#cite_note-23)

### Interaction with the German national movement

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Interaction with the German national movement")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Grimm.jpg/250px-Grimm.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Grimm.jpg)

[Wilhelm Grimm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm "Wilhelm Grimm") (left) and [Jacob Grimm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm "Jacob Grimm") (right) in an 1855 painting

In 1807 [Johann Gottlieb Fichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Gottlieb_Fichte "Johann Gottlieb Fichte") had urged a new form of education in his *[Addresses to the German Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addresses_to_the_German_Nation "Addresses to the German Nation")*. While Prussian (military) drill in the times before had been about obedience to orders without any leeway, Fichte asked for shaping of the personality of students: "The citizens should be made able and willing to use their own minds to achieve higher goals in the framework of a future unified German nation state."[[24]](#cite_note-24) Fichte and other philosophers, such as the [Brothers Grimm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_Grimm "Brothers Grimm"), tried to circumvent the nobility's resistance to a common German nation state via proposing the concept of a *[Kulturnation](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulturnation "de:Kulturnation")*, nationhood without needing a state but based on a common language, musical compositions and songs, shared fairy tales and legends and a common ethos and [educational canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_canon?action=edit&redlink=1 "Educational canon (page does not exist)").[[25]](#cite_note-25)

Various German national movement leaders engaged themselves in educational reform. For example, [Friedrich Ludwig Jahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Ludwig_Jahn "Friedrich Ludwig Jahn") (1778–1852), dubbed the *Turnvater*, was the father of German [gymnastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnastics "Gymnastics") and a student fraternity leader and nationalist but failed in his nationalist efforts; between 1820 and 1842 Jahn's gymnastics movement was forbidden because of his proto-Nazi politics.[[26]](#cite_note-26) Later on, Jahn and others were successful in integrating physical education and sports into Prussian and overall German curricula and popular culture.[[27]](#cite_note-Goodbody-27)

By 1870, the Prussian system began to privilege [High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_German "High German") as an official language against various ethnic groups (such as Poles, [Sorbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbs "Sorbs") and Danes) living in Prussia and other German states. Previous attempts to establish "Utraquism" schools ([bilingual education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilingual_education "Bilingual education")) in the east of Prussia had been identified with high illiteracy rates there.[[28]](#cite_note-28)

### Interaction with religion

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Interaction with religion")]

[Pietism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietism "Pietism"), a reformist group within [Lutheranism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheranism "Lutheranism"), forged a political alliance with the King of Prussia based on a mutual interest in breaking the dominance of the Lutheran state church. The Prussian Kings, [Calvinists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinist "Calvinist") among Lutherans, feared the influence of the Lutheran state church and its close connections with the provincial nobility, while Pietists suffered from persecution by the Lutheran orthodoxy.

Bolstered by royal patronage, Pietism replaced the Lutheran church as the effective state religion by the 1760s. Pietist theology stressed the need for "inner spirituality" ([Innerlichkeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innerlichkeit?action=edit&redlink=1 "Innerlichkeit (page does not exist)")[[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innerlichkeit "de:Innerlichkeit")]), to be found through the reading of Scripture. Consequently, Pietists helped form the principles of the modern public school system, including the stress on literacy, while more Calvinism-based educational reformers (English and Swiss) asked for externally oriented, utilitarian approaches and were critical of internally soul searching idealism.[[29]](#cite_note-the-29)

Prussia was able to leverage the Protestant Church as a partner and ally in the setup of its educational system. Prussian ministers, particularly [Karl Abraham Freiherr von Zedlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Abraham_Freiherr_von_Zedlitz?action=edit&redlink=1 "Karl Abraham Freiherr von Zedlitz (page does not exist)"), sought to introduce a more centralized, uniform system administered by the state during the 18th century. The implementation of the Prussian General Land Law of 1794 was a major step toward this goal. However, there remains in Germany to the present a complicated system of burden sharing between municipalities and [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany "States of Germany") administration for primary and secondary education. The various [confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination "Christian denomination") still have a strong say, contribute religious instruction as a regular topic in schools and receive state funding to allow them to provide preschool education and [kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten "Kindergarten").

In comparison, the French and Austrian education systems faced major setbacks due to ongoing conflicts with the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") and its educational role.[[8]](#cite_note-says-8) The introduction of compulsory schooling in France was delayed till the 1880s.

### Political and cultural role of teachers

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Political and cultural role of teachers")]

Generations of Prussian and also German teachers, who in the 18th century often had no formal education and in the very beginning often were untrained former petty officers, tried to gain more academic recognition, training and better pay and played an important role in various protest and reform movements throughout the 19th and into the 20th century. Namely, the [Revolutions of 1848 in the German states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_German_states "Revolutions of 1848 in the German states") and the [protests of 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_of_1968 "Protests of 1968") saw a strong involvement of (future) teachers.[[6]](#cite_note-VM-6) There is a long tradition of parody and ridicule, where teachers were being depicted in a [janus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janus "Janus")-faced manner as either authoritarian drill masters or, on the other hand, poor wretches which were suffering the constant spite of pranking pupils, negligent parents and spiteful local authorities.[[30]](#cite_note-fut-30)

A 2010 book title like "Germany, your teachers; why the future of our children is being decided in the classroom" shows the 18th and 19th century [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment") ideals of teachers[[30]](#cite_note-fut-30) educating the nation about its most sacred and important issues.[[31]](#cite_note-lohr-31) The notion of [Biedermeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biedermeier "Biedermeier"), a petty bourgeois image of the age between 1830 and 1848, was coined on [Samuel Friedrich Sauter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Friedrich_Sauter?action=edit&redlink=1 "Samuel Friedrich Sauter (page does not exist)"), a school master and poet which had written the famous German song "Das arme Dorfschulmeisterlein" (The poor little schoolmaster).[[30]](#cite_note-fut-30) Actually the 18th primary teachers income was a third of a parish priest[[30]](#cite_note-fut-30) and teachers were being described as being as uppity as proverbially poor. However German notion of homeschooling was less than favorable, Germans deemed the school system as being necessary. E.g. [Heinrich Spoerls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Spoerl "Heinrich Spoerl") 1933 "escapist masterpiece"[[32]](#cite_note-Seesslen-32) novel (and movie) [Die Feuerzangenbowle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Feuerzangenbowle "Die Feuerzangenbowle") tells the (till the present) popular story of a writer going undercover as a student at a small town school after his friends in Berlin tell him that he missed out on the best part of growing up by being homeschooled.

## Spread to other countries

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Spread to other countries")]

State-oriented mass educational systems were instituted in the 19th century in the rest of Europe. They have become an indispensable component of modern nation-states.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Public education was widely institutionalized throughout the world and its development has a close link with nation-building, which often occurred in parallel. Such systems were put in place when the idea of mass education was not yet taken for granted.[[33]](#cite_note-ced-33)

### Examples

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Examples")]

In Austria, Empress [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa_of_Austria "Maria Theresa of Austria") had already made use of Prussian pedagogical methods in 1774 as a means to strengthen her hold over Austria.[[34]](#cite_note-k&G-34) The introduction of compulsory primary schooling in Austria based on the Prussian model had a powerful role, in establishing this and others modern nation states shape and formation.[[34]](#cite_note-k&G-34)

The Prussian reforms in education spread quickly through Europe, particularly after the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution"). The Napoleonic Wars first allowed the system to be enhanced after the 1806 crushing defeat of Prussia itself and then to spread in parallel with the rise and territorial gains of Prussia after the Vienna Congress. [Heinrich Spoerl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Spoerl "Heinrich Spoerl")'s son [Alexander Spoerl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Spoerl?action=edit&redlink=1 "Alexander Spoerl (page does not exist)")'s *[Memoiren eines mittelmäßigen Schülers](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memoiren%20eines%20mittelmäßigen%20Schülers "de:Memoiren eines mittelmäßigen Schülers")* (*Memories of a Mediocre Student*) describes and satirises the role of the formational systems in the Prussian [Rhine Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_Province "Rhine Province") during the early 20th century, in a famous novel of 1950, dedicated to [Libertas Schulze-Boysen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertas_Schulze-Boysen "Libertas Schulze-Boysen").

While the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") was among the most reactionary regimes with regard to common education, the [German ruling class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_German "Baltic German") in [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia "Estonia") and [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia "Latvia") managed to introduce the system there under Russian rule.[[35]](#cite_note-35)[[36]](#cite_note-36) The Prussian principles were adopted by the governments in [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden") to create the basis of the primary (*grundskola*) and secondary (*gymnasium*) schools across Scandinavia. Unlike in Prussia, the Swedish system aimed to expand even secondary schooling to the peasants and workers. As well in Finland, then a Russian grand duchy with a strong Swedish elite, the system was adopted. Education and the propagation of the [national epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_epic "National epic"), the *[Kalevala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevala "Kalevala")*, was crucial for the Finnish [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_nationalism "Ethnic nationalism") [Fennoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennoman "Fennoman") movement. The Finnish language achieved equal legal status with Swedish in 1892.

France and the UK failed until the 1880s to introduce compulsory education, France due to conflicts between a radical secular state and the Catholic Church. In Scotland, local church-controlled schools were replaced by a state system in 1872. In England and Wales, the government started to subsidise schooling in 1833, various measures followed until local school boards were set up under the [Elementary Education Act 1870](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_Education_Act_1870 "Elementary Education Act 1870"), local school boards providing free (taxpayer financed) and compulsory schooling were made universal in England and Wales by the [Elementary Education Act 1891](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_Education_Act_1891?action=edit&redlink=1 "Elementary Education Act 1891 (page does not exist)"), schooling having been made compulsory by the [Elementary Education Act 1880](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_Education_Act_1880 "Elementary Education Act 1880"). However, both private schools and education by means other than schooling remained legal in the United Kingdom.

### United States

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: United States")]

Early 19th-century American educators were also fascinated by German educational trends. In 1818, [John Griscom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Griscom "John Griscom") gave a favorable report of Prussian education. English translations were made of French philosopher [Victor Cousin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Cousin "Victor Cousin")'s work, *Report on the State of Public Education in Prussia*. [Calvin E. Stowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_E._Stowe "Calvin E. Stowe"), [Henry Barnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Barnard "Henry Barnard"), [Horace Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Mann "Horace Mann"), [George Bancroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bancroft "George Bancroft") and [Joseph Cogswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cogswell "Joseph Cogswell") all had a vigorous interest in German education.

In 1843, Mann traveled to Germany to observer firsthand how Prussia delivered education to its children. Upon his return to the United States, he incorporated his experiences in his advocacy for the [common school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_school "Common school") movement in [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts"). Mann persuaded his fellow modernizers, especially those in his [Whig Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_Party_(United_States) "Whig Party (United States)"), to legislate tax-supported elementary public education in their states as well as fund teacher preparation.[[37]](#cite_note-37) At the same time, educators from other states, especially New York, saw limited application of the Prussian system to their state.[[38]](#cite_note-38) Combined with the work of advocates of the teaching profession and "[normal schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_schools_in_the_United_States "Normal schools in the United States")" such as [Catharine Beecher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catharine_Beecher "Catharine Beecher"), advocates for wide-spread tax-payer funded systems in Northern states such as Michigan were able to leverage Prussia's reputation to advocate for expanding public education in their states.[[39]](#cite_note-39)

## Policy borrowing and exchange

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Policy borrowing and exchange")]

The basic concept of a state-oriented and administered mass educational system is still not granted in the English-speaking world, where either the role of the state as such or the role of state control specifically in education faces still (respectively again) considerable skepticism.[[33]](#cite_note-ced-33) The actual process of "policy borrowing" between different educational systems has been rather complex and differentiated.[[40]](#cite_note-40) Mann himself had stressed in 1844 that the US should copy the positive aspects of the Prussian system but not adopt Prussia's obedience to the authorities.[[41]](#cite_note-ReferenceA-41) One of the important differences is that in the German tradition, there is stronger reference to the state as an important principle, as introduced for example by [Hegel's philosophy of the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegelianism "Hegelianism"), which is in opposition to the Anglo-American contract-based idea of the state.[[42]](#cite_note-42)

### Drill and serfdom

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Drill and serfdom")]

Early Prussian reformers took major steps to abandon both serfdom and the [line formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_formation "Line formation") as early as 1807 and introduced [mission-type tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission-type_tactics "Mission-type tactics") in the Prussian military in the same year. The latter enlarged freedom in execution of overall military strategies and had a major influence in the German and Prussian industrial culture, which profited from the Prussian reformers' introduction of greater [economic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_freedom "Economic freedom"). The mission-type concept, which was kept by later German armed forces, required a high level of understanding, literacy (and intense training and education) at all levels and actively invited involvement and independent decision making by the lower ranks. Its intense interaction with the Prussian education system has led to the proverbial statement, "The battles of [Königgrätz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Königgrätz "Battle of Königgrätz") (1866) and [Sedan (1870)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sedan_(1870) "Battle of Sedan (1870)") have been decided by the Prussian primary teacher".[[43]](#cite_note-nipp-43)

### Legacy of the Prussian system after the end of the monarchy

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Legacy of the Prussian system after the end of the monarchy")]

In 1918, the Kingdom of Prussia became a republic. Socialist [Konrad Haenisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Haenisch "Konrad Haenisch"), the first education minister (*Kultusminister*), denounced what he called the "demons of morbid subservience, mistrust, and lies" in secondary schools.[[44]](#cite_note-44) However, Haenisch's and other radical left approaches were rather short-lived. They failed to introduce an *Einheitsschule*, a one-size-fits-all unified secular [comprehensive school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_school "Comprehensive school"), throughout Germany.[[45]](#cite_note-pb-45)

The [Weimarer Schulkompromiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimarer_Schulkompromiss?action=edit&redlink=1 "Weimarer Schulkompromiss (page does not exist)")[[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimarer%20Schulkompromiss "de:Weimarer Schulkompromiss")] ([Weimar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic") educational compromise) of 1919 confirmed the tripartite Prussian system, ongoing church influence on education, and religion as a regular topic, and it allowed for peculiarities and individual influence of the German states, widely frustrating the ambitions of radical leftist educational reformers.[[45]](#cite_note-pb-45) Still, Prussian educational expert [Erich Hylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Hylla?action=edit&redlink=1 "Erich Hylla (page does not exist)")[[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich%20Hylla "de:Erich Hylla")] (1887–1976) provided various studies (with titles such as "School of Democracy") of the US education system for the Prussian government in the 1920s.[[41]](#cite_note-ReferenceA-41)

The [Nazi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_government "Nazi government")'s 1933 [Gleichschaltung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleichschaltung "Gleichschaltung") did away with state's rights, church influence and democracy and tried to impose a unified [totalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian "Totalitarian") education system and a Nazi version of the *Einheitsschule*, with strong premilitary and [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitic "Antisemitic") aspects.

### Legacy of the Prussian System after 1945

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Legacy of the Prussian System after 1945")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F014934-0005%2C_Alois_Hundhammer.jpg/250px-Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F014934-0005%2C_Alois_Hundhammer.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_B_145_Bild-F014934-0005,_Alois_Hundhammer.jpg)

[Alois Hundhammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Hundhammer "Alois Hundhammer"), a Bavarian defender of the educational legacy of Prussia, photographed in 1963

After 1945, the Weimar educational compromise again set the tone for the reconstruction of the state-specific educational system as laid out in the Prussian model. In 1946 the US occupation forces failed completely in their attempt to install comprehensive and secular schooling in the US Occupation Zone. This approach had been endorsed by High Commissioner [John J. McCloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._McCloy "John J. McCloy") and was led by the high-ranking [progressive education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_education "Progressive education") reformer [Richard Thomas Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Thomas_Alexander "Richard Thomas Alexander"),[[46]](#cite_note-tent-46) but it faced determined German resistance.[[46]](#cite_note-tent-46)

The fiercest defender of the originally Prussian tripartite concept and humanist educational tradition was archconservative [Alois Hundhammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Hundhammer "Alois Hundhammer"), a former Bavarian monarchist, devout Catholic enemy of the Nazis and (with regard to the individual statehood of Bavaria) firebrand anti-Prussian coauthor of the 1946 [Constitution of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bavaria "Constitution of Bavaria"). Hundhammer, as soon as he was appointed Bavarian minister of Culture and Education, was quick to use the newly granted freedoms, attacking Alexander in radio speeches and raising rumors about Alexander's secularism, which led to parents' and teachers' associations expressing fears about a reduction in the quality of education.[[47]](#cite_note-HUnd-47) Hundhammer involved [Michael von Faulhaber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_von_Faulhaber "Michael von Faulhaber"), [Archbishop of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Munich "Archbishop of Munich"), to contact New York Cardinal [Francis J. Spellman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_J._Spellman "Francis J. Spellman"), who intervened with the US forces; the reform attempts were abolished as soon as 1948.[[47]](#cite_note-HUnd-47)

### Current debates referring to the Prussian legacy

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Prussian_education_system&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Current debates referring to the Prussian legacy")]

The Prussian legacy of a mainly tripartite system of education with less comprehensive schooling and selection of children as early as the fourth grade has led to controversies that persist to the present.[[48]](#cite_note-48) It has been deemed to reflect 19th-century thinking along class lines.[[49]](#cite_note-49) One of the basic tenets of the specific Prussian system is expressed in the fact that education in Germany is, against the aim of the 19th-century national movement, not directed by the federal government. The individual states maintain *Kulturhoheit* (cultural predominance) on educational matters.

The Humboldt approach, a central pillar of the Prussian system and of German education to the present day, is still influential and being used in various discussions. The present German universities charge no or moderate [tuition fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuition_fee "Tuition fee"). The perceived lack of universities on the cutting edge in both research and education has been recently countered via the [German Universities Excellence Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Universities_Excellence_Initiative "German Universities Excellence Initiative"), which is mainly driven and funded at the federal level.

Germany still focuses on a broad *Allgemeinbildung* (both 'generic knowledge' and 'knowledge for the common people') and provides an internationally recognized in-depth [dual-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_education_system "Dual education system") [vocational education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_education "Vocational education") system, but leaves educational responsibility to individual states. The country faces ongoing controversies about the Prussian legacy of a stratified tripartite educational system versus [Comprehensive schooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_school#Germany "Comprehensive school") and with regard to the interpretation of the [PISA studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment "Programme for International Student Assessment").[[50]](#cite_note-50) Some German PISA critics opposed its utilitarian "value-for-money" competence approach, as being in conflict with teaching freedom, while German proponents of the PISA assessment referred to the practical usability of Humboldt's approach and the Prussian educational system derived from it.[[29]](#cite_note-the-29)
