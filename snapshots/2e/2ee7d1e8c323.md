## The OpenAI Funding Round Cut: $30B Target to $20B, $11B Committed

The newest development is a step back. OpenAI went into the fall seeking $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation. On October 30, 2026, the Financial Times reported that the target had been cut by $10 billion: "The ChatGPT maker is now seeking about $20 billion and its desired valuation has dropped to $1.25 trillion from $1.4 billion, the report said," per [the Reuters account of the FT story](https://ktwb.com/2026/10/30/openai-cuts-funding-target-by-10-billion-ft-reports/) (the $1.4 billion in that sentence is the wire's typo for $1.4 trillion, which TechCrunch's summary confirms). The same report said OpenAI set a Friday deadline for commitments, but the first close "is expected to raise less than $20 billion after some investors reduced their allocations."

A day later, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/31/openai-reportedly-lowers-funding-target-for-new-round/) summarized a Wall Street Journal follow-up: the company "has secured $11 billion so far, and that its fundraising efforts had met with a 'tepid' response from some large investors." TechCrunch also reported that the round will be led by a group of investors from the United Arab Emirates and that "A subsequent retail offering for wealthy individuals could bring the total funding amount to the $20 billion target." The Reuters account names the expected participants: a consortium of UAE investors that includes state-backed technology fund MGX, expected to be the largest participant, plus Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz. No individual allocations have been reported, and OpenAI had not commented when TechCrunch and Reuters published.

Three numbers in this story get mixed up, so keep them apart. $30 billion was the original target. About $20 billion is the revised target, and it is the total for the round, not an extra amount on top of what is already committed. $11 billion is what has been secured in commitments so far, a little over half of the revised target. None of these is a closed raise. Our own [Pulse story on the cut](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/openai-11b-raised-30b-target-cut-2026) described the family-office tranche as up to $20 billion more; under the FT and TechCrunch framing, the wealthy-individual offering helps reach the $20 billion total rather than adding $20 billion to the $11 billion.

The valuation math is still large. $1.25 trillion would be $150 billion below the $1.4 trillion OpenAI wanted in September, but about 47% above the $852 billion mark from the closed March round. It would also put OpenAI back above [Anthropic's $965 billion Series H](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/anthropic-valuation-2026-965b-series-h-and-the-ipo-filing-explained) on private marks. One open question: reports have not agreed on whether $1.25 trillion is a pre-money or post-money figure, so we report it as a valuation without picking one. Why the cut happened is also only reported, not stated by OpenAI: the Journal's "tepid" investor response, the FT's reduced allocations, a US tech selloff driven by worries over AI spending, and competition for the same investors from Anthropic's imminent IPO, which Reuters has reported OpenAI wanted to get ahead of.

Rechecked November 1, 2026: no OpenAI announcement of a closed round; $852 billion (March 2026) remains the last closed valuation. Figures above are reported by the FT and WSJ via the opened Reuters (KTWB) and TechCrunch accounts, not confirmed by OpenAI.

## What Is OpenAI's Valuation in 2026?

OpenAI is valued at $852 billion following a [$122 billion funding round](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-31/openai-valued-at-852-billion-after-completing-122-billion-round) that closed in March 2026 at an $852 billion post-money mark (the round itself launched at a $730 billion pre-money valuation). That figure held flat as of [a $7 billion employee tender offer](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/openai-wraps-7-billion-share-sale-ahead-of-potential-ipo-.html) OpenAI completed on August 10, 2026 — notable because OpenAI funded the buyback itself rather than bringing in new outside capital at a step-up. It is up from $500 billion in October 2025 and roughly 4x the $230 billion figure OpenAI was reportedly seeking in early-2025 fundraising talks. In mid-September, reports put talks for a new private round at $1.2 trillion ( [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/16/openai-ipo-sam-altman-vc-funding-valuation-1-2-trillion/)) to as much as $1.5 trillion ( [multiple reports](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-reportedly-weighing-funding-round-063904793.html)). On September 29, Bloomberg reported a narrower target: OpenAI aims to raise at least $30 billion at a valuation of about $1.4 trillion, excluding the new capital, in a bridge round that would provide capital in place of an IPO, per [Investing.com's summary of the report](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-seeks-30-billion-funding-182211197.html). The talks are at an early stage and terms could change. On October 5, Bloomberg added that UAE funds including Abu Dhabi's MGX had discussed investing as much as $10 billion collectively to anchor the round and that BlackRock was also in talks, per [Investing.com](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/openai-talks-uae-funds-anchor-005609702.html). On October 19, Reuters reported that OpenAI is speeding up the raise ahead of Anthropic's expected mid-November IPO, seeking about $30 billion from investors including SoftBank and MGX at a valuation that could exceed $1.4 trillion, per [Investing.com's summary of the Reuters report](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-races-to-raise-30-bln-ahead-of-anthropic-ipo-reuters-reports-4958611). On October 30-31, the FT and WSJ reported the target cut to about $20 billion at $1.25 trillion, with $11 billion secured so far (see the section above). As of November 1, 2026, no closed round at a new valuation has been announced, so $852 billion remains the last confirmed mark.

## Does the OpenAI Valuation Make Sense on Revenue?

The multiple has actually improved. At $852 billion against an annualized revenue run rate that [topped $40 billion in August 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/openai-s-revenue-run-rate-tops-40-billion-ahead-of-ipo), per Bloomberg, OpenAI was trading at roughly 21x revenue — and against the run rate a source told [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/openais-annual-recurring-revenue-nears-70-billion-axios-reports-2026-09-29/) was approaching $70 billion by September 29, the implied multiple looked like roughly 12x. That figure was later corrected: the Financial Times and CNBC reported OpenAI's late-September run rate was about $50 billion ( [Quartz via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/openai-corrects-annualized-revenue-figure-183756345.html), Oct 8), since the higher number counted gross partner revenue, which puts the multiple at roughly 17x, down from ~34x when this post first tracked the $852 billion mark in March. Revenue itself has kept accelerating: OpenAI passed $1 billion ARR in mid-2023, hit $3.7 billion for full-year 2024, closed 2025 at $21.4 billion, reached ~$25 billion by mid-2026 (per The Information, with about $17 billion from ChatGPT subscriptions, $6.5 billion from API consumption, and $1.5 billion from Sora video and licensing), then roughly doubled again to $40 billion-plus by August — co-founder Greg Brockman told staff monthly run rate grew more than 20% in July alone — and reached about $50 billion by late September (the ~$70 billion first reported included partner revenue), up more than 70% since the start of the third quarter, with enterprise sales more than doubling since July and third-quarter consumer revenue exceeding all of 2025, according to the Reuters source (Axios first reported the figure). Annualized run rate typically multiplies a single month's revenue by 12, so it is a pace, not booked annual revenue. [CFO Sarah Friar told investors](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/openai-cfo-friar-tells-investors-that-enterprise-bigger-than-consumer.html) that same week that enterprise revenue had overtaken consumer revenue for the first time.

What doesn't scale as cleanly is spending. OpenAI's own internal projections, first reported by the Financial Times and covered by [Bloomberg on September 18, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/openai-projects-burning-through-278-billion-by-2030-ft-says), show cumulative cash burn of roughly $278 billion from 2026 through 2030, against about $856 billion in projected compute spend over the same window — even as OpenAI itself projects revenue climbing from $36 billion in 2026 to $350 billion by 2030. Those are the company's own forecasts, not third-party estimates, and forecasts that far out are exactly the kind of number that gets revised. ## OpenAI Valuation vs. Anthropic and xAI: Who's Actually Winning?

OpenAI held the "most valuable AI startup" title for most of 2025, but Anthropic took it in May 2026 with a $65 billion Series H at a $965 billion post-money valuation, and has since pulled further ahead: by [August 10, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/openai-wraps-7-billion-share-sale-ahead-of-potential-ipo-.html), Nasdaq Private Market secondary data priced Anthropic above $1.1 trillion — roughly $250 billion clear of OpenAI's $852 billion tender price, the first time that crossover has happened. Fortune reported on September 16 that Anthropic was preparing to list as early as this fall at a target valuation north of $2 trillion, and it has since filed a public S-1 (see our [Anthropic IPO tracker](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/anthropic-2-trillion-ipo-october-2026-largest-ever-spacex)). OpenAI is choosing the opposite path: staying private through a bridge round that Bloomberg first reported on September 29 at $30 billion-plus and about $1.4 trillion, and that the FT and WSJ reported in late October had been cut to about $20 billion at $1.25 trillion, which would still sit above Anthropic's $965 billion Series H. Meanwhile xAI, after merging into SpaceX at a combined $1.25 trillion in February 2026, went public as part of SpaceX on June 12, 2026 at a $1.77 trillion IPO valuation and closed its first trading day near $2.1 trillion in market capitalization.

## The $1.15 Trillion Bet Behind OpenAI's Valuation

OpenAI's valuation is a bet that the company can make good on roughly $1.15 trillion in infrastructure commitments stretching from 2025 to 2035, spread across seven vendors: Broadcom ($350B), Oracle ($300B), Microsoft Azure ($250B), Nvidia ($100B), AMD ($90B), AWS ($38B), and CoreWeave ($22B). The Oracle deal alone — more than $300 billion over five years — was reported as one of the largest single infrastructure contracts in tech history, and the Nvidia commitment includes 3 gigawatts of dedicated inference capacity plus 2 gigawatts of training capacity on next-generation Vera Rubin systems.

That's the tension at the center of the $852 billion number: even at a run rate of about $50 billion, revenue doesn't come close to covering commitments of $1.15 trillion, so the valuation is really a bet that either revenue keeps compounding at its current pace, or that OpenAI raises again — probably at an even higher number — before any of these bills come fully due. OpenAI's own multi-year burn projection, detailed above, is that bet made explicit. For broader context on how capex-heavy bets like this ripple through public markets, see our [Big Tech Earnings tracker](https://valueaddvc.com/big-tech-earnings).

**What the headline misses:** a flat tender price isn't the same thing as a market-tested valuation. OpenAI chose to fund its own August buyback rather than bring in outside investors at a new mark — a structure that let it avoid setting (and having to defend) a fresh external price. A company confident a step-up was available usually takes it; self-funding a tender is also consistent with a company that would rather not print a number below $852 billion, or isn't ready to negotiate a new one publicly while a reported $1.4 trillion round is still being worked out privately.

## Will OpenAI IPO at This Valuation?

The timeline has gotten more specific, and further out. CFO Sarah Friar told staff in mid-August 2026 that OpenAI "will be a public company in 2027," according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/open-ai-ipo-timing-2027-friar.html) — a later window than the September 2026 debut some bankers had once modeled. Sam Altman went further on September 12, telling Fortune that given the current climate around AI safety, "right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public," and that OpenAI feels no pressure to do so. The timing reports still conflict: Reuters said on October 30 that OpenAI is pursuing a listing in 2027, and no IPO date, price or public filing has been confirmed. That's the clearest signal yet that OpenAI is choosing private mega-rounds — the reported $30 billion-plus raise at about $1.4 trillion, which Bloomberg described as a bridge round in place of an IPO — over a near-term listing, even though the company confidentially filed IPO paperwork with the SEC back in June. Until a listing actually happens, accredited investors have [several ways to buy OpenAI exposure today](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/openai-stock-can-you-buy-it-today-6-ways-in-at-852b) through Forge, EquityZen, and Hiive secondaries. Much of the IPO clock also depends on how cleanly Microsoft's 27% stake and the nonprofit's roughly $130 billion equity position hold up through [the nonprofit-to-PBC restructuring](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/openais-for-profit-conversion-what-the-restructuring-means-for-investors-and-employees).

Anthropic is racing the opposite direction. It's reportedly preparing to list as early as this fall at a target valuation above $2 trillion — a bet that public-market liquidity gets employees and early backers paid faster than OpenAI's private-round treadmill does. OpenAI's capped-profit structure still needs to be reconciled with a conventional listing before bankers can realistically run a roadshow — [the for-profit conversion Sam Altman pushed through](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/sam-altmans-openai-restructuring-what-the-for-profit-conversion-means-for-investors) was largely built to clear that exact obstacle — while Anthropic faces a comparatively cleaner path, which is part of why some allocators view Anthropic as the more IPO-ready of the two labs even though OpenAI got to a trillion-dollar-plus conversation first.

## What's Changed Since March: A Tender, a Revenue Surge, and a New $1.4T Ask

Ten developments since the March round moved this story, in order: On **August 10, 2026**, [OpenAI completed a $7 billion employee tender offer](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/openai-wraps-7-billion-share-sale-ahead-of-potential-ipo-.html), self-funded rather than backed by new investors, holding the valuation flat at $852 billion rather than printing a step-up. The same week, **Anthropic's secondary price crossed $1.1 trillion** on Nasdaq Private Market, pulling roughly $250 billion ahead of OpenAI for the first time, per that same CNBC report. On **August 13-14**, Bloomberg and CNBC reported OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate topped $40 billion — roughly double its late-2025 level — and that enterprise revenue had overtaken consumer revenue for the first time. On **August 19**, CFO Sarah Friar told staff OpenAI "will be a public company in 2027," pushing the IPO window later than earlier speculation. On **September 16**, reports put talks for a new private round at $1.2–1.5 trillion — unsigned, and coming just days after Altman called an IPO "ill-advised" right now. On **September 18**, the Financial Times obtained the internal burn-rate projection covered above, adding a hard number to the capex-vs-revenue tension the valuation already implied. On **September 29**, Bloomberg reported OpenAI aims to raise at least $30 billion at about $1.4 trillion, excluding the new money, as a bridge round in place of an IPO, per [Investing.com](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-seeks-30-billion-funding-182211197.html), and the same day a source told [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/openais-annual-recurring-revenue-nears-70-billion-axios-reports-2026-09-29/) the annualized run rate was approaching $70 billion. On **October 1**, SoftBank said it had completed its $30 billion investment tied to the March round, bringing its cumulative investment to $64.6 billion for a 13% stake, per [Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026:newsml_L4N45N1DV:0-softbank-completes-final-phase-of-30-billion-investment-in-openai/). And on **October 5**, Bloomberg reported that UAE funds including MGX had discussed investing as much as $10 billion collectively to anchor the new round, with BlackRock also in talks, per [Investing.com](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/openai-talks-uae-funds-anchor-005609702.html). On **October 8**, the Financial Times and CNBC reported that the late-September run rate was about $50 billion, not ~$70 billion, because the higher number counted gross revenue from partner companies, per [Quartz via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/openai-corrects-annualized-revenue-figure-183756345.html).

Read together, these point the same direction: OpenAI's fundamentals (revenue, enterprise mix) improved faster than its headline valuation moved, which is why the implied multiple compressed from ~34x to roughly 17x even with the price technically unchanged — but the financing story got more aggressive, not less, with a reported $1.4 trillion target arriving in the same month as a $278 billion self-reported burn projection. Separately, [OpenAI](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/company/openai) added Nubank CEO David Vélez and BNY CEO Robin Vince to its board in July, standard pre-IPO governance building even with the listing itself pushed to 2027 or later.

## What OpenAI's Valuation Signals for the Rest of the Market

For every other founder raising a Series B or C right now, the OpenAI-Anthropic valuation race is both a gift and a distortion. It's a gift because it drags multiples up across the entire AI stack — infrastructure, tooling, and application-layer companies all get priced with some reference to what the frontier labs are doing. It's a distortion because most companies raising today don't have OpenAI's distribution (800M+ weekly ChatGPT users) or Anthropic's enterprise foothold, and founders anchoring their own ask to a double-digit revenue multiple because "that's what OpenAI trades at" will get corrected hard in diligence.

The more useful signal is the gap itself. Anthropic pulled ahead of OpenAI on secondary markets not by having dramatically more revenue in absolute terms (Anthropic's $47B run rate as of May vs OpenAI's $40B+ as of August, both real but not overwhelming relative to $1T+ valuations) but by trading at a lower multiple while growing just as fast — investors rewarded the more capital-efficient growth curve, not just scale. That's a pattern worth remembering the next time someone tells you a bigger valuation always means a better business.

There's also a portfolio-construction lesson buried in here for LPs. A fund that got pro-rata rights in OpenAI at the $29 billion mark in 2023 is sitting on roughly a 29x markup by book value alone — before accounting for the fact that most of that gain is unrealized and illiquid until a secondary window or IPO actually opens. That's the same dynamic we track across VC fund performance broadly: paper markups compound fast in a bull market for a sector, but [DPI (cash actually returned) lags TVPI (paper value)](https://valueaddvc.com/vc-performance) by years in exactly this kind of concentrated, mega-round-heavy portfolio. Funds that got early OpenAI or Anthropic exposure look extraordinary on paper right now; whether that translates to distributions depends entirely on how and when these companies eventually go public or get acquired.

The Bottom Line

OpenAI is still worth $852 billion — a mark that held flat through an August 2026 tender offer even as its run rate topped $40 billion in August and was about $50 billion by late September (corrected from the ~$70 billion first reported), cutting the implied multiple from ~34x to roughly 17x. That's the good news. The complication is what's stacked next to it: Anthropic pulled roughly $250 billion ahead on secondary markets, the $30 billion round at about $1.4 trillion has reportedly shrunk to about $20 billion at $1.25 trillion with $11 billion committed, and its own five-year burn forecast is now public. Watch the multiple and the burn rate together, not just the headline number — that's where the real risk in this market is sitting right now.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What is OpenAI's valuation in 2026?

OpenAI has been valued at $852 billion since its $122 billion funding round closed in March 2026, and that mark held flat through a $7 billion employee tender offer completed on August 10, 2026, per CNBC. As of September 29, 2026, OpenAI is in early talks to raise at least $30 billion at a valuation of about $1.4 trillion, not including the new capital, Bloomberg reported; the talks are at an early stage and terms could change. On October 5, Bloomberg reported that UAE funds including MGX had discussed investing as much as $10 billion collectively to anchor the round, and that BlackRock was also in talks. On October 19, Reuters reported that OpenAI is accelerating the raise to lock in commitments before Anthropic's expected mid-November stock market debut, seeking about $30 billion from investors including SoftBank and MGX at a valuation that could exceed $1.4 trillion. On October 30, the Financial Times reported that OpenAI had cut the target to about $20 billion and its desired valuation to $1.25 trillion, and on October 31 TechCrunch, summarizing the Wall Street Journal, reported that OpenAI had secured $11 billion so far after a tepid response from some large investors. As of November 1, 2026, no closed round at a new valuation has been announced, so $852 billion is still the last closed mark.

How much did OpenAI raise, $11 billion or $20 billion?

Neither is a closed total. About $11 billion is what OpenAI has secured in commitments so far, per the Wall Street Journal as summarized by TechCrunch on October 31, 2026. About $20 billion is the revised target, cut from $30 billion, per the Financial Times on October 30. The first close is expected to raise less than $20 billion after some investors reduced their allocations, and a later offering to wealthy individuals could bring the total to the $20 billion target. The $20 billion is the whole round, not an amount on top of the $11 billion, so the two should not be added together. OpenAI has not confirmed any of these figures.

Why did OpenAI cut its funding target?

OpenAI has not given a reason. As reported: the Wall Street Journal described a tepid response from some large investors, the Financial Times said some investors reduced their allocations, and the cut came as US tech stocks sold off amid worries about heavy AI spending. Reuters has also reported that OpenAI wanted to close the round partly to get ahead of Anthropic's imminent listing, since the two are tapping a similar investor base.

Who is investing in OpenAI's new funding round?

As reported by the Financial Times and carried by Reuters on October 30, 2026, a consortium of UAE investors that includes the state-backed technology fund MGX is expected to be the largest participant, and venture firms including Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz are also expected to participate. Individual allocations have not been reported in any source we could open, and none of the participants has confirmed.

Is OpenAI's valuation higher than Anthropic's?

No, not anymore. Anthropic closed a $65 billion Series H in May 2026 at a $965 billion post-money valuation, and by August-September 2026 its secondary-market price on Nasdaq Private Market had climbed above $1.1 trillion — roughly $250 billion ahead of OpenAI's $852 billion tender price, per CNBC and TechFundingNews reporting. If OpenAI's reported round closes at about $1.4 trillion, it would place OpenAI above Anthropic's most recent private-market valuation, Bloomberg reported on September 29, 2026. The valuation target has since been reported at $1.25 trillion (Financial Times, October 30), which would still sit above Anthropic's $965 billion Series H mark if the round closes there.

How much revenue does OpenAI make in 2026?

OpenAI's annualized revenue was about $50 billion as of late September 2026, per the Financial Times and CNBC (reported October 8). That corrects the ~$68–70 billion figure Axios and a Reuters source reported on September 29, which included gross revenue from partner companies; OpenAI had told investors its run rate rose more than 70% since the start of the third quarter, with enterprise sales more than doubling since July. That follows a run rate that topped $40 billion in August 2026, roughly doubling from where it stood at the end of 2025, according to Bloomberg. That's up from the ~$25 billion run rate reported for mid-2026, when about $17 billion came from ChatGPT subscriptions, $6.5 billion from API usage, and $1.5 billion from Sora and licensing, per The Information.

Is OpenAI profitable?

No. OpenAI's own internal projections, reported by the Financial Times and Bloomberg in September 2026, show it burning through roughly $278 billion in cash between 2026 and 2030 as compute spending (about $856 billion over the same period) outpaces even fast-growing revenue, which OpenAI projects will climb from $36 billion in 2026 to $350 billion by 2030.

How much has OpenAI committed to spend on AI infrastructure?

OpenAI has committed roughly $1.15 trillion in infrastructure spending from 2025 through 2035 across seven vendors: Broadcom ($350B), Oracle ($300B), Microsoft Azure ($250B), Nvidia ($100B), AMD ($90B), AWS ($38B), and CoreWeave ($22B) — separate from the ~$856 billion in cumulative compute spend OpenAI now projects through 2030 alone.
