From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Largest field of space debris in history

On 11 January 2007, [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") conducted an anti-satellite missile test. A Chinese [weather satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_satellite "Weather satellite")—the [FY-1C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FY-1C "FY-1C") (COSPAR 1999-025A) [polar orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_orbit "Polar orbit") satellite of the [Fengyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fengyun "Fengyun") series, at an altitude of 860 kilometres (530mi), with a mass of 960 kilograms (2,120lb)[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)—was destroyed by a [kinetic kill vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_kill_vehicle "Kinetic kill vehicle") traveling with a speed of 8km/s (18,000mph) in the opposite direction[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) (see _[Head-on engagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-on_engagement "Head-on engagement")_). It was launched with a multistage solid-fuel missile from [Xichang Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xichang_Satellite_Launch_Center "Xichang Satellite Launch Center") or nearby.

_[Aviation Week & Space Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Week_&_Space_Technology "Aviation Week & Space Technology")_ magazine first reported the test on 17 January 2007.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) The report was confirmed on 18 January 2007 by a United States [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Security_Council "United States National Security Council") (NSC) spokesperson.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BBC-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-BBC) The [Chinese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China "Government of the People's Republic of China") did not publicly acknowledge that the test had occurred until 23 January 2007 when the [Chinese Foreign Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China "Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the People's Republic of China") issued a statement confirming the test.[&#8202;](#ref-back-bbc2-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-bbc2) China claims it formally notified the US, Japan and other countries about the test in advance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

It was the first known successful satellite intercept test since September 1985, when the United States destroyed the Solwind [P78-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P78-1 "P78-1") satellite with [ASM-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM-135_ASAT "ASM-135 ASAT") [anti-satellite missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_missile "Anti-satellite missile") released by a [F-15 Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15_Eagle "F-15 Eagle") flying at a speed of Mach 0.934 and an altitude of 38,100ft (11.6km). The satellite was orbiting at 345miles (555km).[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

_The New York Times_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT423-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-NYT423) _The Washington Times_[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) and _[Jane's Intelligence Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Intelligence_Review "Jane's Intelligence Review")_[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12) reported that the Chinese test came after at least two [direct ascent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_ascent "Direct ascent") tests that intentionally did not result in an intercept, on 7 July 2005 and 6 February 2006.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)

A [leaked classified diplomatic cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak "United States diplomatic cables leak") indicates that the same system was tested against a ballistic target in January 2010[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) in what the Chinese government publicly described as a test of "ground-based midcourse missile interception technology".[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) That description also closely matches the Chinese government's description of another test in January 2013,[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) which has led some analysts to conclude that it was yet another test of the same ASAT system, again against a ballistic target and not a satellite.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-17)

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

During the 1999 [NATO bombing of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_bombing_of_Yugoslavia "NATO bombing of Yugoslavia"), the [United States bombed the Chinese embassy in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_bombing_of_the_Chinese_embassy_in_Belgrade "United States bombing of the Chinese embassy in Belgrade").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-:0):16–17 The US stated that the bombing was accidental.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-:0):17 Chinese leadership believed that the US had intentionally bombed the embassy and viewed China as significantly lacking in leverage against the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[18\]](#refnote-:0):17 Among other efforts to reduce its gap in leverage such as developing cyberwarfare capabilities, China began to develop its counterspace abilities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-3)[\[18\]](#refnote-:0):17

In January 2001, a US congressionally mandated space commission headed by [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld "Donald Rumsfeld") recommended that "the US government should vigorously pursue the capabilities called for in the National Space Policy to ensure that the president will have the option to deploy weapons in space to deter threats to, and, if necessary, defend against attacks on US interests."[&#8202;](#ref-back-aca-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-aca) Moreover, the subsequent US withdrawal from the [Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Ballistic_Missile_Treaty "Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty") in 2002 allowed the United States to pursue missile defenses, including those that were space-based.[&#8202;](#ref-back-aca-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-aca)

In response to the actions by the US towards potential space weaponization, the Chinese started developing their own anti-satellite missiles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-aca-2)[\[19\]](#refnote-aca) The 2007 test was part of a broader approach of strategic substitution to develop leverage from "information age weapons" instead of nuclear weapons.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-4)[\[18\]](#refnote-:0):1

## Weaponry

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Weaponry")\]

The Chinese anti-satellite system was named by Lieutenant General [Michael Maples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Maples "Michael D. Maples") (then Director of US [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency "Defense Intelligence Agency")), in a Senate Armed Services Committee Hearing, as the **SC-19**.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20) The SC-19 has been described as being based on a modified [DF-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DF-21 "DF-21") [ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile "Ballistic missile") with a [Kinetic Kill Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectile#Kinetic_projectiles "Projectile") mounted. The ASAT kill vehicle relies on an imaging infrared seeker and also has been described as a modified [HQ-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-19 "HQ-19") with a KT-1 rocket booster.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) The program is said to have been at least partially funded by China's [863 Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/863_Program "863 Program") (specifically, the 863-409 focus area).[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22)

The closing velocity of the intercept was approximately 8kilometers per second (17,900mph), comparable to the American [National Missile Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Missile_Defense "National Missile Defense") system.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

## Aftermath

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Aftermath")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Fengyun-1C_debris.jpg/250px-Fengyun-1C_debris.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Fengyun-1C_debris.jpg) Known orbit planes of Fengyun-1C debris one month after its disintegration by the Chinese ASAT (orbits exaggerated for visibility)

### Political reactions

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Political reactions")\]

Several nations responded negatively to the test and highlighted the serious consequences of engaging in the [militarisation of space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarisation_of_space "Militarisation of space"). Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman [Liu Jianchao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Jianchao "Liu Jianchao") stated, "There's no need to feel threatened about this" and argued that "China will not participate in any kind of [arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_race "Arms race") in [outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_space "Outer space")."[&#8202;](#ref-back-afp1-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-afp1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-NYT) China had publicly been advocating to ban space weapons, which had been rejected by the United States under [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") because of certain loopholes in the treaty.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT423-1)[\[10\]](#refnote-NYT423)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)

The United States had not tested an anti-satellite weapon since 1985. In February 2008, the US [launched its own strike to destroy a non-functioning US satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Burnt_Frost "Operation Burnt Frost"), which demonstrated the capability to strike in space, though at a much lower altitude than the Chinese test. The US claimed that the strike was not a military test but a necessary mission to remove the threat posed by the decaying orbit of a faulty spy satellite with a full tank of [hydrazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrazine#Rocket_fuel "Hydrazine") fuel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

In early 2013, the Russian concept satellite [BLITS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLITS "BLITS") collided with what is believed to be a piece of debris from Fengyun-1C, was knocked out of its orbit and soon afterwards data retrieval from the satellite ceased.

### Space debris tracking

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Space debris tracking")\]

Further information: [List of space debris producing events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_space_debris_producing_events "List of space debris producing events")

Anti-satellite missile tests, especially ones involving kinetic kill vehicles as in this case, contribute to the formation of orbital [space debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_debris "Space debris") which can remain in orbit for many years and could interfere with future space activity ( [Kessler syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kessler_syndrome "Kessler syndrome")).[&#8202;](#ref-back-aviationweek1-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-aviationweek1) The 2007 Chinese ASAT test created the largest field of [space debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_debris "Space debris") in history, with more than 3,000 pieces of trackable size (golf ball size and larger) officially catalogued in the immediate aftermath, and an estimated 150,000 debris particles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)As of October2016[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit), a total of 3,438 pieces of debris had been detected, with 571 decayed and 2,867 still in orbit nine years after the incident.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)

More than half of the tracked debris orbits the Earth with a mean altitude above 850 kilometres (530mi), so they would likely remain in orbit for decades or centuries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASA_2008-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-NASA_2008) Based on 2009 and 2013 calculations of solar flux, the [NASA Orbital Debris Program Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Orbital_Debris_Program_Office "NASA Orbital Debris Program Office") estimated that around 30% of the larger-than-10-centimeter (3.9in) debris would still be in orbit in 2035.[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34)

In April 2011, debris from the Chinese test passed 6 kilometres (3.7mi) away from the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station").[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)

As of April 2019, 3000 of the 10,000 pieces of space debris routinely tracked by the US military as a threat to the International Space Station were known to have originated from the 2007 satellite shoot down.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)

### Official responses

### Unofficial or indirectly related responses

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Unofficial or indirectly related responses")\]

Desmond Ball of the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University "Australian National University") while commenting on China's anti-satellite (ASAT) test of January 2007 said: “China's ASAT test of 11 January involved a fairly primitive system, limited to high-inclination [LEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit "Low Earth orbit") satellites. It is the sort of capability available to any country with a store of MRBMs/IRBMs or satellite launch vehicles, and a long-range radar system, such as Japan, India, Iran and even [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"). However, its LEO coverage does include some extremely valuable satellites, including imaging and ELINT satellites, and the test is likely to generate reactions in several countries”.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NIRMIT-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-NIRMIT)

### Related treaties

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Related treaties")\]

The [Outer Space Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Space_Treaty "Outer Space Treaty") banned [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction "Weapons of mass destruction") in [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit "Orbit") and [outer space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_space "Outer space") but does not ban [conventional weaponry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_weapons "Conventional weapons") in orbit. It is [ratified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratify "Ratify") by 98 countries, including China, and signed by 27 others.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41)
