message to his Japanese counterpart.<br>“The respected Comrade Kim Jong Un expressed his deep sympathy and condolences to the prime minister and, through him, to the bereaved families and victims, upon the sad news that big casualties and material losses were caused by earthquakes in Japan from the outset of the new year,”<br>Kim Jong Un has sent a letter to Japanese Prime Minister Fumio Kishida to express condolences over casualties from the powerful earthquake that struck the island nation on New Year’s Day, in what appears to be the first time a North Korean leader has sent such a message to his Japanese counterpart.<br>“The respected Comrade Kim Jong Un expressed his deep sympathy and condolences to the prime minister and, through him, to the bereaved families and victims, upon the sad news that big casualties and material losses were caused by earthquakes in Japan from the outset of the new year,” the official party newspaper _Rodong Sinmun_ [reported](https://kcnawatch.org/newstream/1704492200-545477449/%EA%B2%BD%EC%95%A0%ED%95%98%EB%8A%94-%EA%B9%80%EC%A0%95%EC%9D%80%EB%8F%99%EC%A7%80%EA%BB%98%EC%84%9C-%EC%9D%BC%EB%B3%B8%EA%B5%AD-%EC%B4%9D%EB%A6%AC%EB%8C%80%EC%8B%A0%EC%97%90%EA%B2%8C-%EC%9C%84/) Saturday.<br>Kim reportedly expressed hope that those affected would “eradicate the aftermath of earthquakes” and that their lives would return to normal soon.<br>Japan’s Chief Cabinet Secretary Yoshimasa Hayashi [expressed](https://newsdig.tbs.co.jp/articles/-/928118) his government’s “gratitude” for Kim Jong Un’s letter.<br>The 7.6 earthquake that struck the country on Jan. 1 has resulted in [over 100 casualties](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/japan-earthquake-death-toll-tops-100-with-hundreds-still-missing-media-2024-01-06/) so far, with many more still missing.<br>Justin Yeo, a political researcher at Kookmin University with expertise in Japan-Koreas relations, told _NK News_ that Kim’s rare letter appears to be an attempt to make the DPRK look like a “normal country” that communicates diplomatically with other countries.<br>Kishida has previously [expressed openness](https://nknews.org/2023/07/under-pressure-why-japan-is-pushing-for-a-north-korea-summit-with-new-urgency/) to the possibility of a summit with Kim to resolve historic issues such as North Korea’s [abduction of Japanese nationals](https://nknews.org/2021/11/decades-later-still-no-clear-solution-in-sight-for-japanese-abduction-issue/) in the 1970s and 1980s. But Yeo said North Korea’s outreach is unlikely to affect Tokyo’s [close relations](https://nknews.org/pro/how-camp-david-summit-deals-on-north-korea-complicate-peninsula-security/) with the current U.S. and South Korean administrations. <br>“There may be a purpose to drive a wedge in the Korea-U.S.-Japan alliance, but that will not happen because of a single letter,” he said.<br>However, he added that if Japan responds favorably to this outreach with actions such as providing subsidies to Chongryon-affiliated schools, it could open the door to a more positive relationship.<br>Fyodor Tertitskiy, a researcher at Seoul’s Kookmin University, said Kim Jong Un’s letter suggests Pyongyang could be hopeful about improving relations with Tokyo, after [unconfirmed reports](https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/15017105) that Japan initiated secret talks with North Korea last year.<br>“It is not that they have anything to lose by showing some basic diplomatic decency,” he told _NK News_.<br>Kim Jong Un often [sends condolence messages](https://nknews.org/2022/03/kim-jong-un-sends-condolences-to-chinese-president-over-tragic-airliner-crash/) after tragic events in countries with which North Korea has a close relationship, but this is the first time state media has published a leader’s letter of sympathy to the prime minister of Japan, a country Pyongyang has long viewed as an enemy.<br>North Korean Premier Kang Song San [sent a letter of condolence](https://www.mofa.go.jp/announce/press/1995/1/120.html) to Japanese Prime Minister Tomiichi Murayama after the Great Hanshin Earthquake in 1995, but in general Pyongyang addresses such messages to organizations unaffiliated with Japan’s government.<br>North Korea’s former head of state Kim Yong Nam sent similar condolence messages to [Chongryon](https://kcnawatch.xyz/newstream/1452007842-558938916/%C2%B7%EA%B9%80%EC%98%81%EB%82%A8%EC%9C%84%EC%9B%90%EC%9E%A5-%EC%B4%9D%EB%A0%A8%EC%A4%91%EC%95%99-%EC%9D%98%EC%9E%A5%EC%97%90%EA%B2%8C-%EC%9C%84%EB%AC%B8%EC%A0%84%EB%AC%B8-%EB%8C%80%EC%A7%80%EC%A7%84/), a pro-DPRK group representing ethnic Koreans in Japan, following a massive earthquake and tsunami in 2011. The DPRK branch of the Red Cross also sent a message of sympathy to its Japanese counterpart after earthquakes in [2004](https://kcnawatch.xyz/newstream/1451913545-959794238/%EC%A1%B0%EC%84%A0%EC%A0%81%EC%8B%AD%EC%9E%90%ED%9A%8C-%EC%9D%BC%EB%B3%B8%EC%A0%81%EC%8B%AD%EC%9E%90%EC%82%AC%EC%97%90-%EB%8B%88%EC%9D%B4%EA%B0%80%EB%8B%A4-%EC%A7%80%EC%A7%84-%EC%9C%84%EB%AC%B8/) and [2011](https://kcnawatch.org/newstream/1451995305-103310108/%C2%B7%EC%A1%B0%EC%84%A0%EC%A0%81%EC%8B%AD%EC%9E%90%ED%9A%8C-%EC%9D%BC%EB%B3%B8%EC%A0%81%EC%8B%AD%EC%9E%90%EC%82%AC%EC%97%90-%EC%9C%84%EB%AC%B8%EC%A0%84%EB%AC%B8-%EB%8F%99%EB%B6%81%EB%B6%80%EC%A7%80/).<br>However, state media did not report on any leader-to-leader letter of sympathy in 2004 to Junichiro Koizumi or in 2011 to Naoto Kan. In Kan’s case, in the months after the 2011 disasters, North Korean outlets instead highlighted [criticism](https://kcnawatch.xyz/newstream/1451995530-276603644/%C2%B7%EC%9D%BC%EB%B3%B8%EC%97%90%EC%84%9C-%EC%88%98%EC%83%81%EC%9D%98-%EC%9E%90%EA%B8%88%EA%B4%80%EB%A6%AC%EB%8B%A8%EC%B2%B4%EA%B0%80-%EC%9C%84%EB%B2%95%ED%97%8C%EA%B8%88%EC%9D%84-%EB%B0%9B%EC%9D%80/) of the prime minister’s handling of the situation.<br>Propagating negative narratives about former colonial ruler Japan is a common theme for North Korea, and in the past it has seldom adopted positive tones about Tokyo’s leadership.<br>An exception was a short-lived period of diplomacy around the time of a 2002 summit between North Korean leader Kim Jong Il and Koizumi, when [state media](https://kcnawatch.org/newstream/1451911216-585138734/%EC%9D%BC%EB%B3%B8%EC%B4%9D%EB%A6%AC%EC%9D%98%E3%80%80%EC%A1%B0%EC%84%A0%EB%B0%A9%EB%AC%B8%EC%9D%80%E3%80%80%EA%B4%80%EA%B3%84%EC%A0%95%EC%83%81%ED%99%94%EC%8B%A4%ED%98%84%EC%97%90%EC%84%9C%E3%80%80/) expressed hope that the occasion would help normalize relations after decades of tensions.<br>_Edited by Bryan Betts_<br>[Foreign Relations](https://www.nknews.org/tag/foreign-relations/) [Leadership](https://www.nknews.org/tag/leadership/)<br>© Korea Risk Group.
