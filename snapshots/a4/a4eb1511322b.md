Due to the ongoing conflict,the task of collecting data was difficult[118](#footnote_18_UmrkEKR51QZ5dvWwKen8jsH9Q184rxQwKceYqsVSa2E_nYxdR75z7rkM "Dahab, M. et al., War-Time Mortality in Sudan: A Capture-Recapture Analysis, 12 November 2024, url, pp. 2, 4; Guardian (The), Monday briefing: Charting the forgotten crisis in Sudan, 26 February 2024, url")and many deaths have gone unreported.[119](#footnote_19_0ylUPvsE6AWuJ8Z7jnn7uJZcNuhwauEbMkANvw9OOpE_sqYa8qq6qhKJ "Asharq Al-Awsat, Sudan Doctors’ Union: War Death Toll Tops 40,000, 26 June 2024, url") Several sources explained how the insecurity caused by the conflict generally affected the reporting across the country: journalists being intimidated, harassed, arrested,[120](#footnote_20_lfUIKosjnRoGiDt7NBpXs8a7JSMlJDtR6m4mOWHySE_b7tEOcsgE7f7 "Sudan Tribune, Journalists under attack, media devastated amidst Sudanese conflict, 4 May 2024, url") killed or driven into exile, as well as interruptions of internet and mobile services affected journalistic reporting in the conflict zones.[121](#footnote_21_2jqw4bM5NouhrHptnoU7n-zHIYrd8bfkXf5uLUaJb9A_lxj1NwNBYihp "Free Press Unlimited, Support for Sudan Media Forum's Silence Kills' campaign, 6 November 2024, url") Internet blackouts rendered the task of monitoring violations on the ground difficult.[122](#footnote_22_aa2aOpiH0o9pGpKaSmtHt9VcyWiKZ2zZBXWsk0pvDc_uEnWHyk2grqd "HRW, World Should Rally to Halt Unfolding Atrocities in Darfur, 17 May 2024, url; Sudan War Monitor and OSINT Sudan, Map of the Areas of Control in Sudan, 31 May 2024, url; Ayin Network et al., Sudan Conflict Monitor # 11, 12 March 2024, url, p. 4") The dangerous environment for journalists has led to self-censorship,[123](#footnote_23_AFrpT9x9PDNd2Ue9Hh-DtlVHnkq3E0CcF7myy0-VO8_m34i8CeNvQSf "Index on Censorship, The deadly challenges of reporting on Sudan’s “forgotten war”, 15 February 2024, url ; UN Human Rights Council, Findings of the investigations conducted by the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan into violations of international human rights law and international humanitarian law, and related crimes, committed in the Sudan in the context of the conflict that erupted in mid-April 2023, A/HRC/57/CRP.6, 23 October 2024, url, para. 271") lack of reporting on the ground[124](#footnote_24_FpCLfBtxXBoNCmYXyzVxWx2aB7coSbqkMWilPOqiFk_a5H5FA1Uuy2Q "Index on Censorship, The deadly challenges of reporting on Sudan’s “forgotten war”, 15 February 2024, url") and underreporting of events.[125](#footnote_25_zJosW0yl8tNPeFZmQ0l22x6h3yFOh0zQnz4PafcmoM4_zZX2VUcChtcA "Free Press Unlimited, Support needed for Sudanese media to keep All Eyes On Sudan, 8 July 2024, url") International media coverage of the conflict was limited.[126](#footnote_26_BMmUbLkwNuP-9LjXyG40UBd63DajlIWK6X4nXaI6jw_y3e7JNu4Shv2 "International Crisis Group, Inside Sudan’s Catastrophic Civil War, 7 November 2024, url; Middle East Council on Global Affairs, The War in Sudan: Unfolding Humanitarian Crisis and Diplomatic Impasse, 4 November 2024, url") Foreign journalists faced difficulties accessing Sudan[127](#footnote_27_FpCLfBtxXBoNCmYXyzVxWx2aB7coSbqkMWilPOqiFk_vKyX3yJsPS0i "Index on Censorship, The deadly challenges of reporting on Sudan’s “forgotten war”, 15 February 2024, url") and few managed to enter the country,[128](#footnote_28_aj1HSORnz3ddvDyQUdzmzvsxB642C-cvbWswyfCMZQ_dZSz9TZ3CWTW "World (The), Journalist says parts of Sudan’s capital are now ‘almost unrecognizable’, 7 June 2024, url") let alone spend significant amounts of time observing the conflict there.[129](#footnote_29_aj1HSORnz3ddvDyQUdzmzvsxB642C-cvbWswyfCMZQ_wAMVAggMC8H0 "World (The), Journalist says parts of Sudan’s capital are now ‘almost unrecognizable’, 7 June 2024, url") Moreover, it was noted that there was a general lack of global media and geopolitical attention to the conflict.[130](#footnote_30_QcU4t8HrIQZuEF2kZfD4SY2vnEOQXwKbmFdAHcTag_hvdjiytMVNzN "Scales, S.E. et al., Sudan’s civil war has left at least 62,000 dead by our estimate − but the true figure could be far higher, The Conversation, 31 October 2024, url")

Estimates of the death toll for the conflict vary widely.[131](#footnote_31_qXMXgH7adwUiG9dUIYsxCqaI5LvlIWE0uKxP1rgF6M_sXOYcc9fiKp1 "New York Times (The), Disaster by the Numbers: The Crisis in Sudan, 7 January 2025, url") Several sources have stated that the death counts for the conflict were significantly underestimated.[132](#footnote_32_W3t3fmAhOvQCnKvXIBCitL1iTHfilwusrqNk9QvKQE_mkIMRRVutAUM "Khair, K., [@KholoodKhair, X], posted 21 December 2024, url; LSHTM, 'Invisible and severe' death toll of Sudan conflict revealed, 13 November 2024, url; Blanchard, L., [@LaurenBinDC, X], posted 13 December 2024, url; Scales, S.E. et al., Sudan’s civil war has left at least 62,000 dead by our estimate − but the true figure could be far higher, The Conversation, 31 October 2024, url; Guardian (The), Monday briefing: Charting the forgotten crisis in Sudan, 26 February 2024, url") Besides telecommunication blackouts and insecurity reportedly hampering the recording of fatalities,[133](#footnote_33_n20QzsZusgbSDIL9jOSOJM5HpdcwNT-XPoTDtPNW9DA_dUbWaRUqSe8t "Dahab, M. et al., War-Time Mortality in Sudan: A Capture-Recapture Analysis, 12 November 2024, url, p. 4; Protection Cluster Sudan, Protection of Civilians’ Note: Conflict in Darfur: Key Protection Impacts in September-October 2024, 28 November 2024, url, p. 1") many indirect cases of death resulting from war-exacerbated factors – such as lack of emergency care, essential food, medicine and vaccination programs – were not recorded.[134](#footnote_34_QcU4t8HrIQZuEF2kZfD4SY2vnEOQXwKbmFdAHcTag_aaWmOngljXZM "Scales, S.E. et al., Sudan’s civil war has left at least 62,000 dead by our estimate − but the true figure could be far higher, The Conversation, 31 October 2024, url")

While no data apart from those collected by ACLED (detailed further below) could be found on conflict-related incidents and fatalities during the reference period, several sources provided estimates on fatality figures for the entire period since the outbreak of the conflict on 15 April 2023. The UN’s Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan reported in October 2024 that over 18 800 persons had been killed across the country since that date,[135](#footnote_35_tpZhz0cZMIsSEixZOhA34u0FbJL0rnZoWkJzAYsovaU_alYyhhVoCTlx "UN Human Rights Council, Findings of the investigations conducted by the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan into violations of international human rights law and international humanitarian law, and related crimes, committed in the Sudan in the context of the conflict that erupted in mid-April 2023, A/HRC/57/CRP.6, 23 October 2024, url, para. 124") while ACLED recorded 28 608 fatalities resulting from battles, explosions/remote violence and violence against civilians as of November 2024.[136](#footnote_36_Fq0SrM426nyfHF7VeyOFm3YwAGpKqtUpvTaAFqqSPM_dFMOMVWl6L9O "EUAA analysis based on publicly available ACLED data. ACLED, Curated Data Files, Africa, as of 6 December 2024, url")

Other sources gave significantly higher fatalities estimates, including by also taking into account deaths arising from conflict-exacerbated factors. As early as May 2024, the US government’s special envoy for Sudan, Tom Perriello, pointed to some estimates suggesting that up to 150 000 people had been killed in the conflict.[137](#footnote_37_5gCorigvJixYzbku-j0uMdoxYN0oyiy7bkMnrE01yy8_fjuxREJw8ngp "BBC News, Sudan death toll far higher than previously reported – study, 14 November 2024, url") An October 2024 report by a group of academic researchers from the USA and Belgium estimated that the conflict had caused more than 62 000 fatalities when taking into account such indirect fatalities resulting from factors exacerbated by the conflict such as lack of emergency care, vaccinations, essential food and medicine, in addition to the direct conflict-related fatalities recorded by ACLED.[138](#footnote_38_QcU4t8HrIQZuEF2kZfD4SY2vnEOQXwKbmFdAHcTag_uwBqvVoFMiNr "Scales, S.E. et al., Sudan’s civil war has left at least 62,000 dead by our estimate − but the true figure could be far higher, The Conversation, 31 October 2024, url") Meanwhile, the Sudan Research Group of the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine (LSHTM), focusing exclusively on Khartoum state, estimated that, between 15 April 2023 and 4 June 2024, there had been 61 202 all-cause wartime deaths (which also included deaths from accidents, disease and starvation) and 26 024 deaths from intentionally inflicted injury in that state. These deaths from intentionally inflicted injuries surpassed those documented by ACLED for the entire country during the same period.[139](#footnote_39_OcDNlQXRCiBgmDDai9x-GEZXJJX7wINUuJt1aXlHAE_qSMq25EoUrdS "Dahab, M. et al., War-Time Mortality in Sudan: A Capture-Recapture Analysis, 12 November 2024, url, pp. 2, 7-8") Given that more than 90 % of both all-cause and intentional-injury deaths in Khartoum state had gone unrecorded according to this estimate, the LSHTM suggested that the death toll in other parts of the country must have also been considerably higher than the respective recorded figures.[140](#footnote_40_VWO1tlssV8biBUH8xGtRHMjLFWh1kTCMCb8mYqnSyU_jXhTJmjZwGH0 "LSHTM, 'Invisible and severe' death toll of Sudan conflict revealed, 13 November 2024, url")

From 1 February 2024 to 30 November 2024, ACLED observed 4 230 security incidents (battles, explosions/remote violence, and violence against civilians) at national level for a total estimate of 12 144 fatalities, both combatants and civilians.[141](#footnote_41_Fq0SrM426nyfHF7VeyOFm3YwAGpKqtUpvTaAFqqSPM_eie56jcvUMz4 "EUAA analysis based on publicly available ACLED data. ACLED, Curated Data Files, Africa, as of 6 December 2024, url") However, such figures are likely to be underestimates, ACLED[142](#footnote_42_Bzlq9fcidaRm7JusfcUCqXxSt1jFAdm-WNrbqcVAi40_dvmv147BING3 "ACLED, Sudan: Setting the Stage for a Long War, 17 January 2024, url") and other sources explained.[143](#footnote_43_biLpF6lqf71MCg5X7-IpmkJgUvSGCaDzbZhud6hIwM_lLNJoZmAX7l7 "Asharq Al-Awsat, Sudan Doctors’ Union: War Death Toll Tops 40,000, 26 June 2024, url; Guardian (The), Monday briefing: Charting the forgotten crisis in Sudan, 26 February 2024, url") Among the 4 230 security events recorded by ACLED, 1 464 (35 %) were coded as battles, 1446 (34 %) as explosions/remote violence and 1 320 (31 %) as incidents of violence against civilians.In 1 688 instances (40 %), civilians were the primary or only target.[144](#footnote_44_Fq0SrM426nyfHF7VeyOFm3YwAGpKqtUpvTaAFqqSPM_jrVgusg3zzGl "EUAA analysis based on publicly available ACLED data. ACLED, Curated Data Files, Africa, as of 6 December 2024, url")While ACLED recorded 458 securityincidents in February 2024, the number dropped to between 316 and 418 from March to August but then increased to 502 in September and 616 in October before dropping to 398 in November.[145](#footnote_45_Fq0SrM426nyfHF7VeyOFm3YwAGpKqtUpvTaAFqqSPM_bg6sV2uSiNxm "EUAA analysis based on publicly available ACLED data. ACLED, Curated Data Files, Africa, as of 6 December 2024, url")

Security incidents were recorded in all regions, with Khartoum, Darfur, and Al Jazirah registering the highest numbers during the reference period. Khartoum reported the highest overall number of incidents (1 610, accounting for 38 % of all incidents). The Darfur region reported 1 024 (24 %) incidents, the vast majority of which occurred in North Darfur (770). This was followed by Al Jazirah (821 incidents, 19 %) and Sennar (208 incidents, 5 %).[147](#footnote_47_Fq0SrM426nyfHF7VeyOFm3YwAGpKqtUpvTaAFqqSPM_aSQ6qgSAoAnS "EUAA analysis based on publicly available ACLED data. ACLED, Curated Data Files, Africa, as of 6 December 2024, url")The Darfur region accounted for 37 % of the overall fatalities (with 89 % of the fatalities in the region registered in North Darfur), followed by Khartoum (18 %).[148](#footnote_48_Fq0SrM426nyfHF7VeyOFm3YwAGpKqtUpvTaAFqqSPM_rQ0JyvTWGNp4 "EUAA analysis based on publicly available ACLED data. ACLED, Curated Data Files, Africa, as of 6 December 2024, url")

With regards to casualties solely attributable to explosions/remote violence, Action on Armed Violence (AOAV) recorded 148 incidents and 3 078 civilian casualties (including 1 307 fatalities) in Sudan between February and October 2024. Of 3 001 civilian casualties recorded between March[151](#footnote_51_wbp9y9azNukiJPosRHbpj-PTWv9jU2mTtQ852TYdmBk_cQ8N63YLFBlE "For February 2024, figures on types of areas where civilian casualties were recorded, as well as on types of weapons causing casualties were not available.") and October 2024, 925 (31 %) were recorded in urban residential areas, 663 (22 %) across multiple urban areas, 689 (23 %) in markets and 205 (7 %) in villages. Ground-launched weapons have caused most cases of civilian harm, accounting for 1 635 civilian casualties (54 %). Meanwhile, air-launched weapons including air strikes accounted for 1 261 civilian casualties (42 %).[152](#footnote_52_aKeYQXzC8O9ktek760AOTeX4d7wdewpKECtCS7EuJ0_t6hcp1cryXey "AOAV, Explosive Violence in October 2024, 10 November 2024, url; AOAV, Explosive Violence in September 2024, 10 October 2024, url; AOAV, Explosive Violence in August 2024, 10 September 2024, url; AOAV, Explosive Violence in July 2024, 10 August 2024, url; AOAV, Explosive Violence in June 2024, 10 July 2024, url; AOAV, Explosive Violence in May 2024, 10 June 2024, url; AOAV, Explosive Violence in April 2024, 10 May 2024, url; AOAV, Explosive Violence in March 2024, 10 April 2024, url")
