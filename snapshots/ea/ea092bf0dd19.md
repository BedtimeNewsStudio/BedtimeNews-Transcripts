Investing.com -- Despite rising competition in the artificial intelligence sector, OpenAI is offering compensation packages unmatched by any tech startup in recent history, according to the Wall Street Journal.

The WSJ reported that the company's stock-based compensation reached an average of $1.5 million per employee in 2025, based on financial data shown to investors.

That figure, spread across a workforce of roughly 4,000 people, is more than seven times higher than what Google disclosed in 2003 before its IPO and about 34 times the average stock compensation of 18 large tech companies in the year prior to going public, according to the WSJ's analysis of Equilar data.

The report said OpenAI's equity awards now represent nearly half of its projected 2025 revenue, far exceeding the levels seen at peers such as Palantir, Meta and Salesforce.

According to the WSJ, the pay surge reflects an intensifying AI talent war, with frontier labs competing for a limited pool of senior researchers and engineers.

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg has been offering packages worth hundreds of millions, and in a few cases up to $1 billion, to lure top specialists, prompting a wave of high-profile departures from OpenAI.

In response, OpenAI issued one-time bonuses worth millions to certain staff in August and recently scrapped a policy requiring employees to stay six months before equity begins to vest, changes that could push compensation even higher.

The WSJ added that OpenAI expects its stock-based compensation to rise by about $3 billion annually through 2030, underscoring both the cost of retaining talent and the escalating stakes of the AI arms race.

# OpenAI reports steep losses despite quarterly revenue rise

OpenAI's quarterly revenue rose about 50% to $10 billion in the third quarter. However, the group behind ChatGPT continued to ‌post steep operating losses, according to the Wall Street Journal.

According to documents viewed by The Wall Street Journal, OpenAI told investors that its adjusted operating margin improved to -109% from -150% in the previous quarter.



The company's operating losses, however, widened to $10.9 billion (€9.72bn) from $10.1 billion (€9.01bn), the WSJ reported on Tuesday.



The disclosure comes as investors closely scrutinise the finances of AI companies, including rival Anthropic, which is preparing for an [IPO that could value it at around $1.4 trillion (€1.25trn).](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/11/anthropic-targets-valuation-of-up-to-14-trillion-for-blockbuster-ipo)



The stock market debut will be the first by one of the leading AI model developers and is being seen as a gauge of how long the AI spending boom lifting the US economy can last.



The maker of the Claude AI models said in a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission that it aims to sell 825 million shares at between $70 and $78 each (€62.44–€69.58). The shares will trade under the ticker "ANT."



Seven tech companies including Google, Nvidia, AMD and Microsoft have also agreed to buy $26 billion (€23.2bn) of non-voting shares at the IPO price in a separate private placement, the filing showed.



The companies are investors in Anthropic or major suppliers of its computing power.



Anthropic had been expected to raise up to $100 billion (€89.2bn), which would have made it the largest IPO on record.



Anthropic's listing is "just another chapter" in investors' worries over the cost of the AI buildout and how it gets financed, Art Hogan, chief market strategist at B. Riley Wealth Management, told AFP.



"But at the end of the day, I think there are three massive companies that investors have wanted to be part of for the last three years and that's SpaceX for sure, Anthropic is next, and perhaps next year OpenAI," he said.



The listing comes as Wall Street digests the results of the US midterm elections, in which Democrats, who are more wary of AI, won control of both chambers of Congress and have vowed to put the brakes on President Donald Trump's agenda.



The slide has brought volatility to shares of companies linked to the AI boom, with AI chip-maker Nvidia particularly in focus after chief executive Jensen Huang was caught up in a spy scandal.

**Investing.com --** OpenAI's artificial intelligence computing commitments could translate into $614 billion of infrastructure spending through 2030, substantially below widely cited figures of $750 billion, according to Bernstein analyst Mark Moerdler, who sees limited immediate risk for major cloud providers.

The estimate assumes every identified contract proceeds on schedule, an outcome considered unlikely as construction delays, cancellations and flexible hardware purchases could reduce or defer payments.

Annual infrastructure spending could peak at $184 billion in 2031, with costs increasing gradually as data centers become operational rather than arriving as immediate liabilities.

The distinction matters for investors concerned that AI developers have promised more computing capacity than their revenues can support. Some announced partnerships lack fixed delivery schedules or binding purchase quantities, making headline commitments an unreliable measure of near-term cash requirements.

OpenAI reportedly reached nearly $50 billion in annualized revenue in September, with revenue growing 77% year over year. Sustained growth could help fund the expanding infrastructure requirements, although projections remain uncertain.

Oracle's relationship centers on approximately $300 billion in contracted capacity, delivered in stages under agreements lasting five to six years. OpenAI accounts for less than half of Oracle's remaining performance obligations, limiting concentration relative to some investor fears.

Microsoft has a separate $250 billion Azure services commitment from OpenAI, with computing capacity supplied in six-year tranches. Its diversified cloud operations could cushion delays better than smaller infrastructure specialists.

Amazon has agreements totaling $138 billion across seven- and eight-year terms, including capacity involving its Trainium processors.

CoreWeave's OpenAI agreements total up to $22.4 billion, but those figures represent contract ceilings rather than guaranteed revenue. Renewal prospects remain less certain as competing capacity becomes available.

The bigger industry risk may emerge when computing shortages ease, exposing speculative construction projects and providers dependent on expensive financing. Existing contracts with established cloud companies appear more resilient than future demand for specialized computing rentals.

Investing.com -- OpenAI has completed the first portion of a new funding round, receiving $11 billion that values the artificial intelligence company at $1.25 trillion, Bloomberg reported Friday citing people familiar with the matter. The funds were reportedly transferred to the company on Friday.

The funding represents the initial segment of a round that may grow to $20 billion total in the coming weeks, the report said. The company originally sought to raise $30 billion or more at a $1.4 trillion valuation before reducing its targets amid a selloff in AI stocks on public markets.

# OpenAI will keep investing in data centers despite results of midterms - CFO

Investing.com -- OpenAI will continue to invest in artificial intelligence infrastructure despite voter opposition to data centers in Tuesday's midterm elections, CFO Sarah Friar said Friday.

"When I think about the midterms, I need to keep thinking about how do we keep investing to meet that customer demand," Friar told CNBC's Sara Eisen at the Citi TMT Leadership Summit in Pebble Beach, California. "It starts early with having built relationships with all of the hyperscalers, and it will continue to mean that we raise capital."

Voters rejected data center development across the country on Tuesday. Out of 41 local ballot measures across 10 states, voters backed restrictions or rejected pro-development measures in all but one, according to an analysis by Ballotpedia and additional results gathered by CNBC on Wednesday. Americans grew worried about potential environmental harm, higher utility bills and job loss.

The Nasdaq on Thursday posted its steepest drop in 19 months, led by declines in chipmaker and neocloud company shares tied to AI buildout. The stocks recovered partially on Friday.

Friar said the election results did not dramatically change anything for OpenAI. The company published a policy blueprint and commitments on Thursday that it pledged to apply to company-owned and operated data centers in the future.

"If the midterm means more focus on data centers and communities, we're going to keep doing the work to get into those communities and to build trust with them," Friar said.

OpenAI filed its prospectus confidentially with the Securities and Exchange Commission in June. Friar said Friday the company would continue to look into a 2027 debut. Anthropic, OpenAI's main rival, aims to list on the Nasdaq in mid-November.

Anthropic's revenue in the first nine months of the year rose roughly 1,200% to $33.2 billion, according to its prospectus. The company entered into purchase commitments and lease agreements with an aggregate committed spend of about $895.4 billion, substantially all related to compute and infrastructure.

OpenAI is in talks to raise funding at a $1.25 billion pre-money valuation, Friar said Friday. She said "a good CFO always raises money when she can" and that the round is ongoing.

"The demand for AI is real," Friar said. "I don't think anyone's arguing with that, or if they are, I think it's a losing argument."

# Anthropic revenue jumps 46% to $16.8bn as AI firm eyes record $2 trillion IPO valuation

AI developer Anthropic grew its sales by about 46% to $16.8 billion (€15bn) last quarter and turned an adjusted profit, it told investors on Wednesday, weeks before a November stock market debut that could value it at up to $2 trillion (€1.79bn).

15 October 20263 min read





















































Anthropic, the San Francisco-based developer of the Claude AI models, generated $16.8 billion (€15bn) in revenue between July and September, up about 46% from $11.5 billion (€10.3bn) in the previous three months, and posted an adjusted profit.



It presented the figures to investors at its headquarters on Wednesday, according to Bloomberg citing people familiar with the matter, weeks before a planned initial public offering in November that could value the company at up to [$2 trillion](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/17/anthropic-priced-above-2-trillion-as-markets-eagerly-await-ipo-filing) (€1.79bn).



The company told investors it was profitable for a second straight quarter on an adjusted basis, a measure that strips out certain costs. On the same basis, it had positive free cash flow, meaning it brought in more cash than it spent, and an operating margin of 11%, the share of revenue left after running costs.



Each dollar it spent on computing power brought in $1.70 of revenue in the quarter, but growth is slowing.



Revenue had more than doubled from the first to the second quarter, and the latest figures fell short of some investors' hopes cited by the Financial Times.

## Record IPO in sight

# OpenAI infrastructure lead Sachin Katti to depart next month, WSJ reports

Investing.com -- OpenAI's (NASDAQ:OAI) head of computing capacity, Sachin Katti, is set to step down next month after roughly a year in the role, according to a report from The Wall Street Journal. The departure marks the latest high-profile management shift within a critical unit tasked with overseeing hundreds of billions of dollars in infrastructure investments over the coming years.

Katti joined the artificial intelligence startup in late 2025 to streamline its ambitious Stargate data center initiative, which experienced initial delays following stalled negotiations with SoftBank and Oracle. To bolster capacity, Katti successfully forged separate agreements with both firms while securing supplemental computing arrangements with major providers, including Amazon Web Services.

Beyond vendor partnerships, Katti spearheaded efforts to revive OpenAI's strategy around directly leasing data center facility footprints rather than relying solely on rented third-party capacity. That initiative culminated in August with a 10-gigawatt data center lease alongside SB Energy, backed by a financial guarantee from Nvidia.

OpenAI plans to formally announce the departure internally on Wednesday, though Katti is expected to remain involved with the company as an adviser, people familiar with the matter told The Wall Street Journal. The transition comes as global technology leaders aggressively deploy capital toward vast infrastructure projects essential for supporting next-generation model training and deployment.

Uday Ruddarraju, a former xAI executive who was elevated to chief technology officer of computing capacity at OpenAI earlier this summer, will assume leadership over the group's computing strategy. Reporting directly to OpenAI President Greg Brockman, Ruddarraju takes charge of securing compute power that remains fundamental to maintaining the company's competitive trajectory.
