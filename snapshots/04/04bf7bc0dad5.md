# Quota system of Bangladesh Civil Service - Wikipedia

[Jump to content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#bodyContent)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Quota system for positions in the Bangladesh Civil Service

The **quota system of the Bangladesh Civil Service** ( **BCS**) is a [recruitment policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruitment "Recruitment"), established in 1972 following the [1971 independence of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Bangladesh "Independence of Bangladesh"), that reserves a percentage of [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Civil_Service "Bangladesh Civil Service") job positions for specific demographic groups.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-1) These include descendants of [freedom fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini "Mukti Bahini") from the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War"), [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_Bangladesh "Ethnic minorities in Bangladesh") and [religious minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Bangladesh "Religion in Bangladesh"), residents of underrepresented districts, and persons with disabilities, and initially set a quota of 20 percent of the jobs for merit-based candidates.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:1-2)

Quotas in Bangladesh Civil Service jobs (2025)

1. Merit-based (93.0%)
2. Descendants of freedom fighters (5.00%)
3. Minority ethnic groups (1.00%)
4. Disabled and third genders (1.00%)

While all applicants must pass preliminary and written examinations, quotas are applied during the final stage of recruitment, particularly in the [viva voce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_exam "Oral exam") (oral interview).[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:1-2)

Functioning as a form of [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action "Affirmative action"), the quota system aims to improve representation for historically marginalized or underprivileged communities. By 2010, as freedom fighters were aging out of the workforce, eligibility was extended to include not only children but also grandchildren of freedom fighters, while also expanding to 44 percent of the jobs for merit-based candidates.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-3)

However, from the 35th to 40th BCS examinations, many reserved posts went unfilled due to a shortage of qualified candidates, while a majority of successful applicants (around 65%) were recruited on merit, despite only 44% of the positions being allocated for merit-based selection.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:0-4)

The system has been a consistent focus of national controversy and protest, giving rise to the [quota reform movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_quota_reform_movement "Bangladesh quota reform movement"), which unfolded in three major phases.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:1-2) The [first phase occurred in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Bangladesh_quota_reform_movement "2013 Bangladesh quota reform movement"), followed by a second and more widespread phase in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Bangladesh_quota_reform_movement "2018 Bangladesh quota reform movement"), which prompted the government to announce the abolition of the quota system for first and second class public service posts.

In 2024, [a third and final phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bangladesh_quota_reform_movement "2024 Bangladesh quota reform movement") of the movement emerged after the [High Court Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "High Court Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh") ruled that the 2018 abolition lacked legal standing and ordered the reinstatement of the quota policy.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-6)

The High Court verdict was subsequently stayed by the [Appellate Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "Appellate Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh") as the [Government of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Bangladesh "Government of Bangladesh") challenged the ruling.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-8) On 21 July 2024, the [Supreme Court of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "Supreme Court of Bangladesh") issued a decision reducing the scope of quotas, thereby raising the merit-based recruitment share to 93% of available positions.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-CNN-9)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

[Bangladesh Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Civil_Service "Bangladesh Civil Service") traces its origins to the [Civil Service of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Service_of_Pakistan "Civil Service of Pakistan") which was based on the [Indian Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Civil_Service "Indian Civil Service") of the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) After the [independence of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Bangladesh "Independence of Bangladesh"), the [Awami League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awami_League "Awami League") government under president [Sheikh Mujibur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "Sheikh Mujibur Rahman") established a [quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action "Affirmative action") for the Bangladesh Civil Service through an order of the [Ministry of Cabinet Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Division "Cabinet Division").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:4-11) Professor [Muzaffar Ahmed Chowdhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffar_Ahmed_Chowdhury "Muzaffar Ahmed Chowdhury"), [vice-chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-chancellor_(education) "Vice-chancellor (education)") of the [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka "University of Dhaka"), opposed the move and called for [merit-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy "Meritocracy") recruitment.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:4-11) The government gave a 30 percent quota to veterans of the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War "Bangladesh Liberation War"), referred to as [freedom fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini "Mukti Bahini"), 10 percent to [women who were raped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birangana "Birangana") during the war, and 40 percent reserved to residents of underrepresented districts.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) This left 20 percent of the jobs for merit-based candidates.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10)

Rahman was [assassinated in 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Sheikh_Mujibur_Rahman "Assassination of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman"), and [BAKSAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAKSAL "BAKSAL") was removed from power.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-12) In 1976, the government of Bangladesh reduced the quota for people from underrepresented districts to 20 percent which increased jobs for merit-based candidates to 40 percent.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) As the jobs for women who were raped during the war were unclaimed, the quota was changed to include all women in 1985.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) The district-based quota was reduced to 10 percent.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) The government created a new five percent quota for [indigenous communities of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_Bangladesh "Ethnic minorities in Bangladesh"). This change in 1985 increased the merit-based jobs to 45 percent.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10)

By 1997, 26 years after the Bangladesh Liberation War, freedom fighter recruitment decreased due to age and in response, the government of Bangladesh extended the quota to children of freedom fighters.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10)

In 2008, [Akbar Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Ali_Khan_(economist) "Akbar Ali Khan (economist)"), former civil servant and freedom fighter, and [Kazi Rakibuddin Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Rakibuddin_Ahmad "Kazi Rakibuddin Ahmad"), civil servant, provided Dr. [Saadat Hossain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadat_Husain "Saadat Husain"), chairman of the [Bangladesh Public Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Public_Service_Commission "Bangladesh Public Service Commission"), a report on the quota system essentially describing it as a flawed system and calling for reforms.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:0-4) The report stated without reforms doubts would be raised on the qualification of recruits under the Bangladesh Civil Service Examination.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:0-4)

In 2010, the government of Bangladesh further extended the quota for freedom fighters to include their grandchildren. Bangladesh Public Service Commission added a one percent quota for disabled candidates in 2012.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) This decision decreased the merit-based jobs to 44 percent.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) Despite the 30 percent quota for children and grandchildren of freedom fighters, recruitment in that category never exceeded 10 percent.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:1-2)

On 8 March 2018, the [Bangladesh High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "High Court Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh") rejected a petition challenging the legality of the quota system.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:0-4) Prime Minister [Sheikh Hasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Hasina "Sheikh Hasina") stated on 21 March she intended to keep the quota for descendants of freedom fighters.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:0-4) [Bangladeshi students protested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Bangladesh_quota_reform_movement "2018 Bangladesh quota reform movement") against the quota. Hasina issued an executive order removing all quotas from the Bangladesh Civil Service.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) The protestors were demanding a reformation of the quota, not an abolition.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:3-13) On 1 July 2020, the decision to abolish quota became effective.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-14)

A descendant of a freedom fighter and six others appealed the verdict in 2021 to challenge the government order canceling the quota system.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:4-11) The government filed an appeal with the [Appellate Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "Appellate Division, Supreme Court of Bangladesh").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) On 5 June 2024, the Bangladesh High Court issued a verdict that canceled the government notification, declaring it illegal, thus restoring the quota in Bangladesh Civil Service recruitment.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) The students of public and private universities and colleges in Bangladesh launched the [2024 Bangladesh quota reform movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bangladesh_quota_reform_movement "2024 Bangladesh quota reform movement").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) The Appellate Division issued an order of status quo which halts the High Court verdict till the Appellate Division finishes its hearing on the government appeal.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:2-10) Pro-government [Bangladesh Chhatra League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Chhatra_League "Bangladesh Chhatra League") and [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Police "Bangladesh Police") injured four hundred protesting students.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:5-15) The protests took place in the backdrop of rising [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment "Unemployment") levels in Bangladesh.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:5-15) The protestors called themselves [Anti-discrimination Students Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-discrimination_Students_Movement "Anti-discrimination Students Movement").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-:5-15)

On 21 July 2024, [Supreme Court of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh "Supreme Court of Bangladesh") increased recruitment to government jobs based on merit to 93%, while reducing the quota for freedom fighters and their descendants to 5%, 1% for ethnic minorities and 1% for the [third gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_gender "Third gender") and [disabled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability "Disability").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_system_of_Bangladesh_Civil_Service#cite_note-CNN-9)
