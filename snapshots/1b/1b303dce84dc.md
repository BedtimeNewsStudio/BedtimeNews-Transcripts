The **Syrian Arab Kingdom** ( [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): المملكة العربية السورية, __al-Mamlakah al-ʿArabiyyah al-Sūriyyah__)[\[a\]](#ref-3) was an [unrecognized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrecognized_Countries "Unrecognized Countries") monarchy existing briefly in the territory of [historical Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilad_al-Sham "Bilad al-Sham"). It was announced on 5 October 1918 as a fully independent Arab constitutional government with the permission of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire").[\[3\]](#ref-Zeine1977) It gained independence as an [emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate "Emirate") after the withdrawal of British forces from [OETA East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Enemy_Territory_Administration "Occupied Enemy Territory Administration") on 26 November 1919,[\[4\]](#ref-Tauber2013) and was proclaimed a kingdom on 8 March 1920.

As a kingdom, the state existed for a little over four months, from 8 March to 25 July 1920.[\[5\]](#ref-Anthony_John_Kuhn)[\[6\]](#ref-Antonius) During its brief existence, the kingdom was led by [Faisal bin Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I "Faisal I"), son of [Hussein bin Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein,_King_of_Hejaz "Hussein, King of Hejaz"), [Sharif of Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_of_Mecca "Sharif of Mecca"). Despite its claims over the [region of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_(region) "Syria (region)"), Faisal's government controlled a limited area and was dependent on Britain, which, along with France, generally opposed the idea of a [Greater Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_(region) "Syria (region)") and refused to recognize the kingdom.[\[7\]](#ref-8) After a [four-month-long war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Syrian_War "Franco-Syrian War"), the kingdom surrendered to French forces on 25 July 1920.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Foundations

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Foundations")\]

See also: [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt "Arab Revolt")

The Arab Revolt and the [McMahon–Hussein Correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMahon%E2%80%93Hussein_Correspondence "McMahon–Hussein Correspondence") were crucial factors in the foundation of the Arab Kingdom of Syria. In the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence promises of an Arab kingdom were made by the British in return for an Arab uprising against the Ottomans.[\[3\]](#ref-Zeine1977):209–215 As the British were promising independence, the French made the [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement "Sykes–Picot Agreement"). Ultimately, the implementation of the Sykes–Picot Agreement would lead to the undermining and end of the Arab Kingdom of Syria. Despite the significance of the Arab Revolt to modern Arab countries formed in its wake, at the time there was significant distrust and even opposition to the idea of an Arab kingdom or series of Arab kingdoms.

This opposition was due in part to the heavy influence of the French and the British in facilitating the revolt and the establishment of what would be considered, by modern standards, puppet states.[\[8\]](#ref-Karsh1999):185–191 The involvement of foreign powers in distributing large sums of money and military support to establish an empire that would be led by imperial aspirants, rather than legitimate Arab nationalists, was instrumental in the disintegration of the majority of the early [Hashemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemite "Hashemite") kingdoms ( [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hejaz "Kingdom of Hejaz") and [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq "Kingdom of Iraq")). Additionally, at the time, many Arabs expressed grave concerns that the family of the [sharif of Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_of_Mecca "Sharif of Mecca"), the Hashemites, could wrest control from the Ottoman sultan, with whom their loyalty had rested for centuries.[\[8\]](#ref-Karsh1999):187

### Arab constitutional government

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Arab constitutional government")\]

[Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus "Damascus") was captured in the beginning of October 1918 when British troops, the [Desert Mounted Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Mounted_Corps "Desert Mounted Corps"), [Prince Feisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq "Faisal I of Iraq")'s [Sherifial Hejaz Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharifian_Army "Sharifian Army") and an Arabic army under Sharif Nasir of Medina had encircled the city.[\[9\]](#ref-10) During the pursuit to Damascus, many rearguards established by remnants of the Ottoman [Fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Army_(Ottoman_Empire) "Fourth Army (Ottoman Empire)") and [Seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Army_(Ottoman_Empire) "Seventh Army (Ottoman Empire)") Armies [captured Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Damascus_(1918) "Capture of Damascus (1918)") on 30 September 1918. Faisal's jubilation would be short-lived, as Faisal would soon be made aware of the Sykes–Picot agreement. Faisal had come to expect an independent Arab kingdom in the name of his father but was soon told of the division of territory and how Syria fell under French protective power. Faisal saw this admission as a betrayal by the British, but believed the actual settlement would be worked out at a later date when the war had ended. There was some likelihood that by then the British would have changed their support for French pretensions in Syria.

On 5 October, with the permission of General Allenby, [Faisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq "Faisal I of Iraq") announced the establishment of a fully and absolutely independent Arab constitutional government.[\[3\]](#ref-Zeine1977):34 Faisal announced it would be an Arab government based on justice and equality for all Arabs regardless of religion.[\[10\]](#ref-Allawi2014) The French Prime Minister [Georges Clemenceau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Clemenceau "Georges Clemenceau") found disconcerting the establishment of a semi-independent Arab state without international recognition and under the auspices of the British. Even reassurances by Allenby that all actions taken were provisional did not ease the looming tensions between the British, the French, and the Arabs. For Arab nationalists, and many of the Arabs who fought in the Arab Revolt, this independent state would be the realization of a long hard-fought goal.

### Determining the status

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Determining the status")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Proclamation_of_King_Faisal_I_as_King_of_Syria.jpg/250px-Proclamation_of_King_Faisal_I_as_King_of_Syria.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Proclamation_of_King_Faisal_I_as_King_of_Syria.jpg) Proclamation of Faisal I as King of Syria in 1920.

After the war, at the [Paris Peace Conference of 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919 "Paris Peace Conference, 1919"), Faisal pushed for Arab independence. At the Conference, the victorious Allies decided what was to become of the defeated nations of the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers "Central Powers"), especially who was to control their territories, such as the Ottoman Empire's Middle East possessions. The status of the Arab lands in the Middle East was the subject of intense negotiations between the French and British. In May 1919, the French and British prime ministers met in [Quai d’Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quai_d'Orsay "Quai d'Orsay") to decide between them their respective claims to territories or spheres of influence in the Middle East. The meeting determined that in return for a British guarantee of French control in Syria, the British would be given a mandate over Mosul and Palestine.

At about the same time, an American compromise resulted in an agreement to set up a commission to determine the wishes of the inhabitants. Though they initially supported the idea, Britain and France eventually backed out, leaving the [King–Crane Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%E2%80%93Crane_Commission "King–Crane Commission") of 1919 solely American.[\[8\]](#ref-Karsh1999):268 The findings of the commission, not published until 1922 after the vote on the mandates in the League of Nations, indicated strong Arab support for an independent Arab state and opposition to a French presence.[\[11\]](#ref-12)

### Creation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Creation")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Royal_Standard_of_the_King_of_Syria_%281920%29.svg/250px-Royal_Standard_of_the_King_of_Syria_%281920%29.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Royal_Standard_of_the_King_of_Syria_(1920).svg) Royal Standard of the King of Syria[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Coin_of_the_former_Syrian_kingdom.jpg/250px-Coin_of_the_former_Syrian_kingdom.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Coin_of_the_former_Syrian_kingdom.jpg) Currency of the Arab Kingdom[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/SYRIAN_25_PIASTRES_1919.jpg/250px-SYRIAN_25_PIASTRES_1919.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SYRIAN_25_PIASTRES_1919.jpg) 25 Syrian piastre banknote issued in [Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut "Beirut") by the Bank of Syria in 1919. The Bank of Syria was later renamed the Bank of Syria and Greater Lebanon and continued issuing currency for both Syria and Lebanon until the 1950s.

These events in Europe led Syrian nationalist societies like [al-Fatat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fatat "Al-Fatat") (the Young Arab Society) to make preparations for a national congress. These Syrian nationalist societies advocated complete independence for an Arab Kingdom that united Arabs under Faisal. The [King–Crane Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%E2%80%93Crane_Commission "King–Crane Commission") encouraged efforts to unify, and hasty elections were called including representatives from all over the Arab lands, including Palestine and Lebanon, although French officials prevented many of their representatives from arriving.[\[12\]](#ref-Rogan_Arabs) The first official session of the Syrian Congress was held on 3 June 1919 and al-Fatat member [Hashim al-Atassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashim_al-Atassi "Hashim al-Atassi") was elected its president.[\[13\]](#ref-Tauber1995):17

When the King–Crane Commission arrived in Damascus on 25 June 1919, it was met with a flurry of leaflets saying "Independence or Death".

On 2 July the [Syrian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Congress "Syrian National Congress") in Damascus passed the Damascus Program, a series of resolutions calling for a completely independent constitutional monarchy with Faisal as king, asking for assistance from the United States, and rejecting any rights claimed by the French.[\[13\]](#ref-Tauber1995):19 The resolutions defined the borders as

> on the north, the [Taurus Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Mountains "Taurus Mountains"); on the south, a line running from [Rafah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafah "Rafah") to [Al-Jauf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumat_al-Jandal "Dumat al-Jandal") and following the Syria-Hejaz border below ' [Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqaba "Aqaba"); on the east, the boundary formed by the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates "Euphrates") and [Khabur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabur_(Euphrates) "Khabur (Euphrates)") rivers and a line stretching from some distance east of [Abu-Kamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Kamal "Abu Kamal") to some distance east of al-Jauf; on the west, the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea "Mediterranean Sea").[\[14\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEAntonius1938439,_appendix_G)

Any hope that Faisal may have had that either the British or Americans would come to his aid and counter French moves quickly faded, especially after the Anglo-French Agreement for the withdrawal of British troops from Syria and the end of the British military government in Syria. The British withdrew from the region on 26 November 1919.[\[4\]](#ref-Tauber2013)

In January 1920, Faisal was forced into an agreement with France which stipulated that France would uphold the existence of the Syrian state and would not station troops in Syria as long as the French government remained the only government supplying advisers, counselors and technical experts.[\[15\]](#ref-16):167 News of this compromise did not bode well with Faisal's vehemently anti-French and independence-minded supporters who immediately pressured Faisal to reverse his commitment, which he did. In the aftermath of this reversal, violent attacks against French forces took place and the Syrian Congress assembled in March 1920 to declare Faisal the king of Syria as well as to officially set up the Arab Kingdom of Syria with Hashim al-Atassi as Prime Minister and [Yusuf al-'Azma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_al-'Azma "Yusuf al-'Azma") as Minister of War and Chief of Staff.

This unilateral action was immediately repudiated by the British and French and the [San Remo Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Remo_Conference "San Remo Conference") was called by the Allied Powers in April 1920 to finalise the allocation of [League of Nations mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate "League of Nations mandate") in the Middle East. This was in turn repudiated by Faisal and his supporters. After months of instability and failure to make good on the promises to the French, the commander of French forces General [Henri Gouraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Gouraud_(French_Army_officer) "Henri Gouraud (French Army officer)") gave an ultimatum to King Faisal on 14 July 1920 declaring he surrender or fight.[\[13\]](#ref-Tauber1995):215

### Dissolution

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Dissolution")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Yusuf_al-Azma.jpg/250px-Yusuf_al-Azma.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Yusuf_Al_Azma.jpg) General [Yusuf al-Azma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_al-Azma "Yusuf al-Azma")

Main article: [Franco-Syrian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Syrian_War "Franco-Syrian War")

Worried about the results of a long bloody fight with the French, King Faisal surrendered. However, Yusuf al-'Azma, the defense minister, ignored the King's order, and led a small army to confront the French advance into Syria. This army depended mainly on individual weapons and were no match to the French artillery. At the [Battle of Maysalun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maysalun "Battle of Maysalun"), the Syrian army was easily defeated by the French, with General al-'Azma being killed during the battle. The loss led to [the siege and capture of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Damascus_(1920) "Siege of Damascus (1920)") on 24 July 1920 and the [French Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon "French Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon") was put into effect thereafter.[\[16\]](#ref-Moubayed452)

## Legacy

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Legacy")\]

After surrendering to French forces, [Faisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq "Faisal I of Iraq") was expelled from Syria and went to live in the United Kingdom in August 1920. In August 1921 he was offered the crown of Iraq under the [British Mandate of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Mandate_of_Iraq "British Mandate of Iraq").

A pro-French government under the leadership of [Aladdin Al-Droubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_Al-Droubi "Aladdin Al-Droubi") was installed one day after the fall of Damascus, on 25 July 1920.[\[13\]](#ref-Tauber1995):37 On 1 September 1920, General Gouraud divided the French mandate territory of Syria into several smaller states as part of a French scheme to make Syria easier to control.

The Kingdom, through its short and tumultuous existence, would become a subject of great inspiration to later Arab liberation movements. It would be the often-repeated story of an Arab people, breaking out from their colonial bonds only to be castigated for their revolutionary fervor and for their resistance to the imperial powers. The symbolism of the fall of the Kingdom of Syria also imparted deep mistrust of European powers, who were seen as liars and oppressors.

## Politics of Syria (1918–1920)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Politics of Syria (1918–1920)")\]

The Kingdom of Syria had a structure such as a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state "Unitary state") with [government decentralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralization#In_government_policy "Decentralization")[\[17\]](#ref-Tabayyun) put in place,[\[18\]](#ref-Karim) but Adam Mestyan of the [Princeton University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Press "Princeton University Press") argued instead that the Kingdom of Syria was a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation "Federation").[\[19\]](#ref-Mestyan) It also operated under a [parliamentary system of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system").[\[17\]](#ref-Tabayyun)[\[20\]](#ref-constitutionnet.org)
