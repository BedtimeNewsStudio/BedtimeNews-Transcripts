# Brazil cuts to zero soy, corn import’s tariff

- Market: Agriculture, Biofuels, Fertilizers
- 19/10/20

Brazil's government has eliminated import tariffs for soybean and corn, seeking to maintain the supply balance amid record domestic prices for both commodities.

For soybean, tariff-free imports are valid until 15 January 2021, while for corn the license goes until 31 March 2021. The measure was announced 16 October by Brazil's international trade chamber after a request of the ministry of agriculture.

Brazil's imports of soybean and corn from countries outside the Mercosur trade bloc have an 8pc tariff.

But both commodities have been at record internal prices even after historical harvests — 125mn t in the case of soybean and nearly 103mn t regarding corn.

A depreciation of more than 30pc of the Brazilian real against the US dollar in the past year has contributed to higher soybean and corn prices. The currency exchange has also led to a record pace of soy exports, reducing the domestic inventories and supporting its values. Brazil is on track to export more than 80mn t of the oilseed this year.

For corn, local consumption remained high even amid the Covid-19-related economic slowdown, with the meat industry demanding higher volumes of the cereal for animal feed.

Brazil's government said there does not appear to be an immediate lack of supply of both commodities.

Recently, the government also cut to zero the import tariff for rice until 31 December for a 400,000t quota. Brazil has already negotiated 225,000t within this quota with the US, India and Guyana.

There is no defined quota for soybean and corn imports with no tariff.
