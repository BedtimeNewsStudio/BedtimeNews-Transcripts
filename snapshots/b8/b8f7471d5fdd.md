General elections were held in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") on 3 November 2001 to elect members of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Singapore "Parliament of Singapore"). They were the eleventh general elections since the introduction of [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance_of_Singapore "Self-governance of Singapore") in 1959 and the ninth since [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Singapore_Agreement_1965 "Independence of Singapore Agreement 1965") in 1965. The number of parliamentary seats increased from 83 to 84 following adjustments to electoral boundaries. With 55 seats won uncontested by the [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party "People's Action Party") (PAP) on nomination day, the outcome of the elections was effectively determined before polling day for the third consecutive election.

The ruling PAP secured a [landslide victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_victory "Landslide victory") and retained its [supermajority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajority "Supermajority") by winning all but two of the 84 parliamentary seats, while also increasing its popular vote share for the second consecutive election to 75.29%, its strongest showing since the [1980 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Singaporean_general_election "1980 Singaporean general election"). The only opposition candidates elected were [Chiam See Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiam_See_Tong "Chiam See Tong") of the [Singapore Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Alliance "Singapore Democratic Alliance") (SDA), who retained his seat in [Potong Pasir SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_SMC "Potong Pasir SMC"), and [Low Thia Khiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Thia_Khiang "Low Thia Khiang") of the [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "Workers' Party (Singapore)") (WP), who retained his seat in [Hougang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_SMC "Hougang SMC"). As the opposition secured two elected seats, one [Non-constituency Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-constituency_Member_of_Parliament "Non-constituency Member of Parliament") (NCMP) seat was offered to [Steve Chia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Chia "Steve Chia") of the SDA in [Chua Chu Kang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chua_Chu_Kang_SMC "Chua Chu Kang SMC"), who was the best-performing losing opposition candidate. He accepted the seat.

This was [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong")'s last election as [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Prime Minister of Singapore") before he handed over to [deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Singapore "Deputy Prime Minister of Singapore") [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong "Lee Hsien Loong"), who is the son of [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew"), in 2004. Voter turnout in contested constituencies stood at 94.61%, slightly lower from the preceding general election. This election was the first in which the total eligible voter population exceeded 2 million, although only one third of them (675,306 of the 2,036,923 eligible voters) were able to vote due to the large number of [walkovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkover "Walkover"). As of [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Singaporean_general_election "2025 Singaporean general election"), it remains the most recent general election in which the PAP returned to power on nomination day, as well as the leading opposition party represented in Parliament not being WP.

## Background

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")]

Further information: [9th Parliament of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Parliament_of_Singapore "9th Parliament of Singapore")

A seat in [Jalan Besar GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalan_Besar_GRC "Jalan Besar GRC") was vacated in 1999 following the conviction of [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party "People's Action Party") (PAP) MP [Choo Wee Khiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choo_Wee_Khiang "Choo Wee Khiang") for cheating. However, no by-election was held, as the vacancy occurred within a [group representation constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representation_constituency "Group representation constituency") (GRC). The responsibilities of Choo's division were instead redistributed among the remaining MPs in the GRC. Under Singapore law, a by-election can only be called in a GRC if all of its seats are vacated. The last instance of this occurring was the [Marine Parade GRC by-election in 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Marine_Parade_by-election "1992 Marine Parade by-election").

### Global situation

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Global situation")]

The ruling PAP government led by prime minister [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong") had initially planned to hold the election as scheduled in 2002. However, it was brought forward after Singapore experienced an economic slowdown linked to the lingering effects of the [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis "1997 Asian financial crisis"), the [dot-com bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-com_bubble "Dot-com bubble") and the [early 2000s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_2000s_recession "Early 2000s recession"), which were further worsened by the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks") in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). The 2001 election had the shortest election period in Singapore's history, lasting only 17 days from the release of the electoral boundaries report and the certification of the registers of electors towards polling day. Seeking to renew his mandate swiftly, Goh aimed to secure public confidence amid the downturn.

### Effects of the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks")

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Effects of the September 11 attacks")]

Earlier on 15 May, five months before the election, the legislature passed a bill to amend the Parliamentary Elections (Amendment) Act 2001, allowing [voting in overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_citizen_voting "Non-resident citizen voting") for the first time.[[1]](#cite_note-2) The 2001 elections could've marked the first time overseas voters are allowed to cast their ballots; however, Elections Department announced that the overseas voting and lunchtime rallies will both be cancelled per security concerns, though campaigning in the evening will not be affected. Additional security measures will also apply during campaigning and polling day.[[2]](#cite_note-3)[[3]](#cite_note-4)

As also a result of the economic shifts and inflation, the election deposit was raised sharply by [S$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_dollar "Singapore dollar")5,000 from $8,000 to $13,000, marking one of the largest increases to date.[[4]](#cite_note-NDI01-5) The maximum amount for candidates in terms of campaigning expenses per voter was also raised from $2.50 to $3.[[5]](#cite_note-6)

### Non-PAP political parties

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Non-PAP political parties")]

#### Singapore Democratic Alliance (SDA)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Singapore Democratic Alliance (SDA)")]

For the first time since the [1963 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Singaporean_general_election "1963 Singaporean general election"), a formal opposition coalition was formed. Known as the [Singapore Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Alliance "Singapore Democratic Alliance") (SDA), comprised four parties: the leading [Singapore People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_People's_Party "Singapore People's Party") (SPP), the [National Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_Party_(Singapore) "National Solidarity Party (Singapore)") (NSP), the [Pertubuhan Kebangsaan Melayu Singapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertubuhan_Kebangsaan_Melayu_Singapura "Pertubuhan Kebangsaan Melayu Singapura") (PKMS) and the [Singapore Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Justice_Party "Singapore Justice Party") (SJP). It was led by SPP secretary-general [Chiam See Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiam_See_Tong "Chiam See Tong"). The SDA fielded 13 candidates in the election, with the NSP contributing nine, the SPP four and the PKMS providing the required [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Singaporeans "Malay Singaporeans") minority candidate.

All but one of their contests were straight fights; the sole exception being [Bukit Timah SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Timah_SMC "Bukit Timah SMC"), which saw the only three-cornered contest of the election involving the SDA and an independent candidate. According to the SDA, four other opposition parties declined invitations to join the coalition, including the more established [Singapore Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Party "Singapore Democratic Party") (SDP) and the [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "Workers' Party (Singapore)") (WP). The other two were the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party_(Singapore) "Democratic Progressive Party (Singapore)") (DPP) and the [Singapore National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_National_Front "Singapore National Front") (SNF), the latter of which had not yet contested any election since its founding in 1991.

#### Singapore Democratic Party (SDP)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Singapore Democratic Party (SDP)")]

The [Singapore Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Democratic_Party "Singapore Democratic Party") (SDP) fielded 11 candidates across three constituencies. Its secretary-general [Chee Soon Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chee_Soon_Juan "Chee Soon Juan") and his sister, Chee Siok Chin, led the party's team in the newly created [Jurong GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_GRC "Jurong GRC"). Former MPs [Cheo Chai Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheo_Chai_Chen "Cheo Chai Chen") ([Nee Soon Central SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nee_Soon_Central_SMC "Nee Soon Central SMC")) and [Ling How Doong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ling_How_Doong "Ling How Doong") ([Bukit Gombak SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Gombak_SMC "Bukit Gombak SMC")), both of whom had served in the [8th Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Parliament_of_Singapore "8th Parliament of Singapore") after being elected in the [1991 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Singaporean_general_election "1991 Singaporean general election"), contested in [Hong Kah GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kah_GRC "Hong Kah GRC") and Nee Soon Central SMC respectively in an effort to return to Parliament.

#### Workers' Party (WP)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Workers' Party (WP)")]

Former [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_(Singapore) "Workers' Party (Singapore)") (WP) [Non-Constituency Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Constituency_Member_of_Parliament "Non-Constituency Member of Parliament") (NCMP) [Joshua Benjamin Jeyaretnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Jeyaretnam "J. B. Jeyaretnam"), who lost his seat in July 2001 after being declared bankrupt following defamation lawsuits filed by PAP leaders, resigned from the party citing disagreements with its current leadership. The party's only MP, [Low Thia Khiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Thia_Khiang "Low Thia Khiang"), subsequently assumed the position of secretary-general. On nomination day, the WP was successfully nominated in only two constituencies, [Hougang SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_SMC "Hougang SMC") and [Nee Soon East SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nee_Soon_East_SMC "Nee Soon East SMC").[[6]](#cite_note-7)

Its proposed team for [Aljunied GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljunied_GRC "Aljunied GRC"), comprising chairman Tan Bin Seng, second vice-chairman Huang Seow Kwang (both of whom had contested in [Cheng San GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_San_GRC "Cheng San GRC") in 1997) and new members Abdul Rahim Abdul Rahman, [James Gomez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gomez_(politician) "James Gomez (politician)") and [Yaw Shin Leong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaw_Shin_Leong "Yaw Shin Leong"), was disqualified for submitting incomplete nomination papers.[[7]](#cite_note-8)[[8]](#cite_note-9) Low described the Aljunied team's disqualification as an "oversight" and accepted responsibility for the error. Plans to contest in [Ang Mo Kio GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Mo_Kio_GRC "Ang Mo Kio GRC"), [East Coast GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_GRC "East Coast GRC") and [Pasir Ris–Punggol GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasir_Ris–Punggol_GRC "Pasir Ris–Punggol GRC") were also later withdrawn after the party's teams failed to appear at their respective nomination centres, and as a result, the election marked the fewest number of seats contested by the WP in over three decades since the [1968 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Singaporean_general_election "1968 Singaporean general election"), when the party also fielded candidates in only two constituencies during a period when most opposition parties boycotted the polls.[[9]](#cite_note-10)

## Electoral boundaries

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Electoral boundaries")]

See also: [List of Singaporean electoral divisions (2001–06)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Singaporean_electoral_divisions_(2001–06) "List of Singaporean electoral divisions (2001–06)")

The report of the [Electoral Boundaries Review Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Boundaries_Review_Committee "Electoral Boundaries Review Committee") (EBRC) for the 2001 general election was published on 17 October 2001, only a day before the dissolution of Parliament. The 10th Parliament was to return 84 elected Members of Parliament, comprising nine [single member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_member_constituencies "Single member constituencies") (SMCs), nine five-member [group representation constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representation_constituencies "Group representation constituencies") (GRCs) and five six-member GRCs, with all previous four-member GRCs eliminated.

The election saw the introduction of [Holland–Bukit Panjang GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland–Bukit_Panjang_GRC "Holland–Bukit Panjang GRC"), [Jurong GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_GRC "Jurong GRC") (restructured from most of [Bukit Timah GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Timah_GRC "Bukit Timah GRC"), with Bukit Timah itself restored as an SMC) and [Pasir Ris–Punggol GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasir_Ris–Punggol_GRC "Pasir Ris–Punggol GRC") (renamed from [Pasir Ris GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasir_Ris_GRC "Pasir Ris GRC") and expanded to include the [Punggol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punggol "Punggol") and [Sengkang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengkang "Sengkang") areas from the dissolved [Cheng San GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_San_GRC "Cheng San GRC")). [Bukit Timah GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Timah_GRC "Bukit Timah GRC"), [Cheng San GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng_San_GRC "Cheng San GRC") and [Kreta Ayer–Tanglin GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreta_Ayer–Tanglin_GRC "Kreta Ayer–Tanglin GRC") were dissolved and their divisions reorganised into new constituencies.

Two SMCs, [Bukit Timah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Timah_SMC "Bukit Timah SMC") and [Joo Chiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joo_Chiat_SMC "Joo Chiat SMC"), were reinstated, while [Boon Lay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boon_Lay_SMC "Boon Lay SMC"), [Bukit Gombak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Gombak_SMC "Bukit Gombak SMC") and [Kampong Glam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampong_Glam_SMC "Kampong Glam SMC") were absorbed into neighbouring GRCs. For the first time, certain GRCs retained their exact boundaries from the previous election; specifically [Bishan–Toa Payoh GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishan–Toa_Payoh_GRC "Bishan–Toa Payoh GRC") and [Marine Parade GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Parade_GRC "Marine Parade GRC"), a pattern that has since recurred in subsequent elections.

## New candidates

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: New candidates")]

A total of 40 candidates made their political debut in 2001, including 25 from the PAP. Notable newcomers included future [Presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Singapore "President of Singapore") [Tharman Shanmugaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharman_Shanmugaratnam "Tharman Shanmugaratnam") and [Halimah Yacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halimah_Yacob "Halimah Yacob")[[b]](#cite_note-Halimah-13) (both who also stood in [Jurong GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_GRC "Jurong GRC")), future cabinet ministers [Gan Kim Yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_Kim_Yong "Gan Kim Yong"), [Indranee Rajah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indranee_Rajah "Indranee Rajah"), [Maliki Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maliki_Osman "Maliki Osman"), as well as [Balaji Sadasivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaji_Sadasivan "Balaji Sadasivan"), [Khaw Boon Wan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaw_Boon_Wan "Khaw Boon Wan"), [Raymond Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Lim "Raymond Lim"), [Ng Eng Hen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng_Eng_Hen "Ng Eng Hen") and [Vivian Balakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Balakrishnan "Vivian Balakrishnan"), who were dubbed by the PAP as the "super seven" alongside Shanmugaratnam and backbencher [Cedric Foo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Foo "Cedric Foo").[[12]](#cite_note-14) Among the 15 new opposition candidates were Chee Siok Chin, sister of SDP leader [Chee Soon Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chee_Soon_Juan "Chee Soon Juan"); [Desmond Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Lim "Desmond Lim"), who would later head the SDA; and [Ooi Boon Ewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ooi_Boon_Ewe "Ooi Boon Ewe"), who went on to become a [perennial candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perennial_candidate "Perennial candidate") in subsequent elections.

## Results

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Results")]

The PAP won a [landslide victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_victory "Landslide victory"), marking its best performance since [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Singaporean_general_election "1980 Singaporean general election") and its third highest result since it first gained power in [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Singaporean_general_election "1959 Singaporean general election"). The PAP secured 75.29% of the vote, reflecting strong public support for its leadership during a period of global uncertainty and economic recession. However, this election also recorded the highest number of [walkovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncontested_election "Uncontested election") since [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Singaporean_general_election "1968 Singaporean general election"), reflecting the opposition's unwillingness to mount a serious challenge against the PAP that year. Chong Weng Chiew and [Penny Low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Low "Penny Low") of the PAP became the first MPs born in post-independence Singapore.

At the time, [political apathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_apathy "Political apathy") among [Singaporeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporeans "Singaporeans") towards a stronger opposition presence in Parliament appeared to be high, with only 29 out of 84 seats contested and the opposition narrowly winning just two of them. While the opposition-held constituencies of [Hougang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hougang_Single_Member_Constituency "Hougang Single Member Constituency") and [Potong Pasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_Single_Member_Constituency "Potong Pasir Single Member Constituency") from the [previous election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Singaporean_general_election "1997 Singaporean general election") were retained, both saw reduced majorities, each garnering less than 55% of the vote for the WP and the SDA respectively. The outcome raised concerns that an all-PAP elected Parliament and an "opposition wipeout" for the first time since 1980, which was the PAP's justification of introducing the [Non-Constituency Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Constituency_Member_of_Parliament "Non-Constituency Member of Parliament") (NCMP) scheme in 1984, was a distinct possibility. With two opposition seats in Parliament, a NCMP seat was offered to and accepted by [Steve Chia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Chia "Steve Chia") of the SDA, making him the first NCMP since the scheme's introduction to not be from the WP.

Two candidates, Tan Kim Chuang and [Tan Lead Shake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Lead_Shake "Tan Lead Shake"), forfeited their $13,000 election deposits. The latter was involved in a straight fight in which [Tan Cheng Bock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Cheng_Bock "Tan Cheng Bock") recorded the election's highest contested result of 87.96% in [Ayer Rajah SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayer_Rajah_Single_Member_Constituency "Ayer Rajah Single Member Constituency").

### By [Community Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Development_Council "Community Development Council")

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: By Community Development Council")]

The 2001 election was the first election with a unified format with five CDCs that have been used till this day. The [South West CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_CDC "South West CDC") have the most number of candidates fielded in the election, with [West Coast GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_GRC "West Coast GRC") being the only constituency for the CDC to be elected as a [walkover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkover "Walkover"); nonetheless, South West had the highest contested vote share by CDC. While only one constituency in the [Central Singapore CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Singapore_CDC "Central Singapore CDC") had a contest, which is [Jalan Besar GRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalan_Besar_GRC "Jalan Besar GRC"), the CDC with the fewest contested seats was the [North West CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_CDC "North West CDC"), with only two, while [South East CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_CDC "South East CDC") had the next fewest number of seats being contested, with three. The lowest-scoring CDC this election was the [North East CDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_CDC "North East CDC"), being the only CDC polling under 70% of the PAP contested vote. It was the only election where [Potong Pasir SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potong_Pasir_SMC "Potong Pasir SMC") would be redistricted in the South East CDC, before being redistricted to the Central CDC starting in the next election.

Of the opposition vote share, the other parties outside the largest opposition SDA contributed a combined 12.68% of the overall popular vote, in contrast with SDA's 12.03%.

### Chee–Goh hawker centre incident

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Chee–Goh hawker centre incident")]

During the campaigning period on 28 October, SDP leader [Chee Soon Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chee_Soon_Juan "Chee Soon Juan") drew widespread public attention after an encounter with Prime Minister [Goh Chok Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goh_Chok_Tong "Goh Chok Tong") at a [hawker centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_centre "Hawker centre") in [Hong Kah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kah "Hong Kah"). Using a [megaphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaphone "Megaphone"), Chee shouted at Goh, "Where is the $17 billion that you have lent to [Suharto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suharto "Suharto")?" Later that day at an election rally in [Nee Soon Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishun "Yishun"), Chee referenced [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew "Lee Kuan Yew"), who was serving as [Senior Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Minister_of_Singapore "Senior Minister of Singapore") at the time. He concluded his speech with: "...I want to ask Mr Lee Kuan Yew ... So Mr Lee Kuan Yew, I challenge you, tell us about the $17 billion you loaned to Suharto."

In the aftermath of the [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis "1997 Asian financial crisis"), Singapore provided substantial financial assistance to [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") through billions of dollars in loans to stabilise the struggling Indonesian economy, with the country also seeing [increasing civil unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1998_Indonesia_riots "May 1998 Indonesia riots"). The Singaporean government considered these loans necessary, as an unstable Indonesia was viewed as potentially having serious negative repercussions for Singapore.[[13]](#cite_note-16)

Goh and Lee later filed defamation suits against Chee shortly after the election. Chee lost the lawsuit in 2002 as well as his appeals in 2003 and was ordered to pay damages of [S$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_dollar "Singapore dollar")300,000 to Goh and S$200,000 to Lee. Ahead of the [2006 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Singaporean_general_election "2006 Singaporean general election"), Chee was declared bankrupt on 10 February, rendering him ineligible to contest. He eventually returned to electoral politics in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Singaporean_general_election "2015 Singaporean general election") after being discharged from bankruptcy in 2012.[[14]](#cite_note-17)[[15]](#cite_note-18)

### DPP's expulsions

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2001_Singaporean_general_election&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: DPP's expulsions")]

Following the election, the DPP announced the expulsion of Tan Soo Phuan and his son Tan Lead Shake from the party for breaching party directives. Tan Soo Phuan failed to inform the party of his decision to contest [MacPherson SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacPherson_SMC "MacPherson SMC"), while Tan Lead Shake ran in [Ayer Rajah SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayer_Rajah_SMC "Ayer Rajah SMC") instead of the designated [Joo Chiat SMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joo_Chiat_SMC "Joo Chiat SMC"). The DPP subsequently become dormant following the expulsions.[[16]](#cite_note-19)
