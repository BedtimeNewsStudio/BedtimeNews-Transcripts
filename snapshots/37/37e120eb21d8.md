Total BBC income in 2024/25 was £5.90 billion compared with £5.39 billion in the previous year. The estimated evasion rate increased in each year from 2014/15 to 2018/19. The general increase in the evasion rate has partly been explained as being due to a change in viewing habits and the closure of the iPlayer loophole in 2016. It has also been put down to changes in people’s incomes and the cost of the TV Licence, as well as Television Licensing enforcement activities.19 In 2019/20, the BBC used a second evasion rate measure that looked at the average annual evasion rate over the 12 months of that year as opposed to the evasion rate at the end of the financial year.20 This measure is believed by the BBC to be a more reliable measure of the evasion rate as it is less likely to be subject to monthly variations in the rate that can occur based on how people consume media.21 BBC analysis of licence fee evaders found that half of evaders in 2024/25 used BBC content once a week. It was also estimated that in the past year licence evasion cost the BBC £550 million in lost income.22 Additionally, as of 31st March 2025, it was reported that there were 3.6 million 'no licence needed' (NLN) declarations recorded. This amounted to an additional £617 million in lost income and £1.1 billion when summed with TV Licence evasion lost income. The 2025 figure was an increase of 0.3 million in unneeded TV licences compared with the previous year. 23 The chart below shows that the estimated licence fee evasion rate rose from 6.95% in 2019/20, to a high of 12.52% in 2024/25.24 A further new measure for estimating the proportion of households watching licensable content was introduced in 2024/25, affecting the calculation of the evasion rate. Data collection for this index was commissioned from Harris Interactive. These estimates ware produced using surveys of the general population and TV Licence evaders.28 Data suggests that TV Licensing was more positively viewed among the general population in 2015/16 compared to 2010/11. Delayers (those who pay the licence fee late) and evaders, however, had more negative attitudes in 2015/16 than six years before. Delayers and evaders’ views towards TV Licensing had been more positive than those of the general population in 2013/14. It has since become more negative than in 2010/11. This was a decrease from an index score of 104 in 2013/14 to 99 in 2015/16. The number of complaints to TV Licensing can give an indication about overall satisfaction and attitudes towards TV Licensing.
