Domestic content rules require Apple to produce part of its smartphones or components onshore, while the AirTag is only an accessory, Industry Minister Agus Gumiwang Kartasasmita said in a briefing on Wednesday.

"As of this afternoon, the government does not have a basis for issuing the local content certificates" that Apple needs to sell its flagship device in Indonesia, he said. "Apple needs to negotiate with us so that we can issue a certificate."

Indonesia blocked iPhone 16 sales in October, part of a strategy to persuade the US technology company to invest more in Southeast Asia's largest economy. Delays in the resumption of sales is depriving Apple revenue from a promising growth market of about 280 million consumers, where it's fighting for foothold with rivals such as Samsung Electronics Co.

According to Kartasasmita, Apple could be sanctioned for its continued non-compliance with local investment rules, though that would be the government's last resort. "We will look for other ways or options," he said, adding that the government has already sent a counterproposal to Apple.

An Apple representative in Indonesia declined to comment.

The decision marks an unexpected turn just hours after Investment Minister Rosan Roeslani told reporters on Tuesday evening that Indonesia had approved Apple's plan to put up an AirTag facility. The domestic content requirements are under the purview of the industry minister.

Apple had proposed to build a factory by early 2026 and commence production of AirTags, a device that allows users to track their luggage, pets, or other belongings, according to Roeslani. Company executives are in Jakarta to negotiate with the government on the investment proposal.

Rival phone makers like Samsung and Xiaomi Corp. have set up factories in Indonesia to comply with the domestic content regulations introduced in 2017. Other ways to boost local content include sourcing materials, hiring workers, developing apps and investing in developer academies in the country.

"There's no deadline for compliance," Kartasasmita said. "If Apple wants to sell the iPhone 16, and especially if they plan to launch the iPhone 17, the decision is entirely up to them."

I come to you today as a humble servant of the business and investing world with both good and bad news.

First, the sprinkles of good news, because I think we can agree we need something uplifting after watching the market get [torched, blown up, and shredded to pieces](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/micron-and-sandisk-valuation-multiples-are-unbelievable-as-chip-stocks-get-crushed-180250881.html) in the [aftermath of the blue wave](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/article/wall-street-reacts-to-the-stunning-election-day-blue-wave-winners-losers-and-those-in-between-102511437.html).

Nothing changes in D.C. overnight, if at all. Companies' fundamentals are what will drive their stock prices in the long term. I don't think the fundamentals of companies have been destroyed this week, at least not to the extent that would warrant [some of the sell-offs](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/3-reasons-why-the-tech-stock-rout-is-growing-more-alarming-134122762.html) we witnessed.

A Hut 8 ( [HUT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/HUT/)) data center build-out isn't getting a work order stoppage today that will derail the fourth quarter numbers. Dems aren't carting off AI data center C-suites to jail and sending their companies into bankruptcy.

Texas governor-elect Hinojosa isn't sending construction crews to data centers under an order to tear them all down and turn the land into parks. Elon Musk's SpaceX ( [SPCX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SPCX/)) — based in Texas — is still operational this weekend, from what I can tell. Musk is still on pace to churn out Tesla ( [TSLA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TSLA/)) robotaxis and robots in large numbers in 2027.

AMD ( [AMD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMD/)) didn't offer a huge 2027 outlook this week on its earnings call because it fears a blue wave stopping its CPU momentum in its tracks.

In fact, AMD CEO Lisa Su told me the [demand she is seeing is off the charts](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/amd-ceo-lisa-su-sees-ai-becoming-essential-to-every-aspect-of-our-lives-172825495.html)!

Hilton ( [HLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/HLT/)) CEO Chris Nassetta is so confident in his business trends that he told me this week he felt compelled to share directional guidance for 2027 with investors. Nassetta normally doesn't share these types of year-ahead specifics.

"Here's the reality. Our political system has been built pretty well," [Nassetta said on Sozzi Unleashed](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/hiltons-ceo-doesnt-see-any-dramatic-impact-from-the-blue-wave-171837839.html). "Our founders did a pretty good job of creating a resilient system. We always figure it out. We'll figure it out this time. The reality is, in the next two or three years, I don't see any real dramatic impact as a result of the last couple days."

Nassetta is going on year 20 as CEO of Hilton, an almost unheard-of run in the C-suite. I don't think you get to log 20 years as Hilton CEO if you are wrong on a lot of stuff — and I think Chris makes a valid point on the political winds that are always blowing.

Now the bad news — and it's short and to the point.

We can no longer believe earnings estimates for 2027 and 2028. While in one breath I'm telling you corporate fundamentals haven't been destroyed this week, in the other breath I acknowledge they have taken a minor hit.

Data center projects won't come to a screeching halt in the US. It will just take a higher bar to get them built. And it will be costlier to get the deals done.

Story Continues

This was a surprise factor to the earnings estimates for the AI ecosystem, and that's what the market is adjusting to.

Should the selling in markets persist, it could be a surprise factor to the industrial companies supporting the AI boom — for example, Caterpillar ( [CAT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CAT/)) dump trucks. And from there, we get into the economic damage from a more restricted AI boom.

I am making a lot of assumptions here.

But will leave you with this: The world didn't end this week.

This is not the dot-com bust.

This is an adjustment period, and soon it'll be a period where you can no longer ignore the historically cheap valuations on names like Nvidia ( [NVDA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA/)), Micron, and Sandisk.

Himax Technologies (NASDAQ:HIMX) expects fourth-quarter revenue to fall sharply from the third quarter as some automotive display customers reduce orders after building inventory earlier in the year. The forecast comes despite third-quarter profit that exceeded the company's guidance.

The display chipmaker expects fourth-quarter revenue to decline 22% to 26% sequentially, with gross margin flat to slightly higher. Profit attributable to shareholders is projected at $0.045 to $0.065 per diluted American depositary share (ADS), excluding a potential gain from a proposed investment divestiture.

President and Chief Executive Officer Jordan Wu described the automotive order correction as a short-term setback and said the company expects double-digit revenue growth in 2027, excluding its emerging co-packaged optics business.

## Automotive orders drive fourth-quarter decline

Wu said the revenue forecast primarily reflects an abrupt reduction in orders for automotive display driver ICs, including traditional display driver ICs and touch and display driver integration products. Some panel makers had accelerated purchases in the second and third quarters to meet orders tied to Chinese automakers' overseas expansion, he said.

He attributed the change in part to earlier aggressive export expectations and concerns about inventory in distribution channels. Himax still expects full-year automotive IC revenue to grow approximately 10% in 2026, even after the fourth-quarter correction. Wu said the company's 2027 outlook assumes roughly flat global vehicle shipments and is supported by new project launches, more displays per vehicle and higher chip content per display.

## Third-quarter profit tops guidance

Third-quarter revenue rose 8.5% from the prior quarter to $246.8 million, while gross margin increased to 34.2% from 33.1%. Profit was $0.107 per diluted ADS, above the company's $0.080 to $0.100 guidance range because operating expenses were lower than expected, Head of IR/PR Karen Tiao said.

Net profit was $18.6 million, compared with $19.9 million in the second quarter and $1.1 million a year earlier. Operating expenses increased 21.9% sequentially to $61.8 million, largely because of the company's annual employee bonus expense.

Small and medium-sized display driver IC revenue rose 8.9% sequentially to $176.8 million, aided by double-digit growth in automotive driver sales and higher smartphone IC shipments. Non-driver revenue increased 11.2% to $51.0 million, supported by automotive timing controller, or Tcon, shipments. Large display driver IC revenue slipped 1% to $19.0 million.

Himax ended September with $285.3 million in cash equivalents and other financial assets. Its proposed divestiture of a stake in an equity-method investee is expected to close in the fourth quarter, subject to customary conditions and regulatory approvals. The company expects an after-tax gain of approximately $19 million to $20 million upon closing.

## Growth plans and supply costs

Although automotive driver IC sales are expected to decline in the fourth quarter, automotive Tcon sales are projected to rise by a low-teens percentage sequentially. Wu said full-year automotive Tcon sales are on track to grow more than 40%, with another strong year expected in 2027.

Wu also pointed to opportunities in electronic paper displays and the company's WiseEye AI processor. Alibaba adopted WiseEye for its Qwen Glasses, he said, and numerous smart-glasses projects involving the technology are expected to enter mass production starting in 2027.

Himax expects non-driver products to approach 30% of revenue next year, up from about 20% currently, Wu said. He added that those products generally carry higher gross margins than driver ICs. Despite announced price increases by foundries and packaging and testing providers, the company expects gross margin to remain relatively stable or improve slightly in 2027, excluding co-packaged optics.

Wu said Himax has secured 2027 capacity commitments from key suppliers for key product areas and is working with customers to share rising costs.

## Optics production remains a focus

Himax is working with strategic partner FOCI on optical components for co-packaged optics applications. Wu said engineering ramps are underway and that shipments are expected to grow over coming quarters, although the timing of mass production rests with end customers.

FOCI's board approved approximately $100 million in capital expenditures for additional manufacturing space and equipment, Wu said. Himax expects the business to contribute meaningfully to revenue and profitability starting in 2027, but excluded it from its double-digit growth outlook because the pace of the initial ramp remains uncertain.

During the question-and-answer session, Wu said Himax remains exclusive with FOCI for the time being and is focused on establishing reliable mass production. He said it was too early to assess market-share changes in the developing market.

## About Himax Technologies (NASDAQ:HIMX)

Himax Technologies, Inc is a fabless semiconductor company headquartered in Tainan, Taiwan. Founded in 2001, the company designs and supplies display-related integrated circuits and other semiconductor components for manufacturers of consumer electronics, automotive systems and industrial devices. Himax has been listed on the Nasdaq since 2006 under the symbol HIMX.

The company's product portfolio includes display driver ICs, timing controllers, touch and display driver integration products, and other components used in televisions, monitors, notebook computers, smartphones, tablets and automotive displays.
