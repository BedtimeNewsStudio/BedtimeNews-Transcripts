# EUROPE’S COAL EXIT

OVERVIEW OF NATIONAL COAL PHASE OUT COMMITMENTS

Last updated: 30 March 2026

In the Europe’s Coal Exit page you can find an overview of country coal phase out announcements and plans in Europe.

Once governments announce a coal phase out they then need to set about implementing it which means confirming closure dates at plant level, and producing plans for a managed just transition away from coal. These actions need to be in line with climate, environmental and health responsibilities, and must address the needs of affected workers, communities, and regions.

[To be in line with the UN Paris Climate Agreement European and OECD countries need to be coal-power free by 2030 at the latest.](https://climateanalytics.org/briefings/coal-phase-out/)

#### EUROPE’S COAL EXIT

COUNTRY BY COUNTRY OVERVIEW

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/austria_flag_icon.png) AUSTRIA: COAL FREE SINCE 2020

Austria was Europe’s second country to exit coal, closing its last two coal plants in 2019 and 2020 respectively. The Austrian coal phase out was not government driven. In January 2018, Austrian environment minister, Elisabeth Köstinger,[announced](https://www.wienerzeitung.at/nachrichten/politik/oesterreich/941155_Anreize-statt-Verbote.html)that the country would phase out coal by 2020, but the goal was[missing](https://www.global2000.at/presse/global-2000-fordert-kohleausstieg-bis-2020-als-teil-der-klima-und-energiestrategie)from the country’s energy strategy published shortly after and no measures were taken to ensure it.

In June 2022, [the Austrian government ordered](https://www.derstandard.at/story/2000136710649/kohlekraftwerk-mellach-viele-offene-fragen-kritik-der-opposition) the operator of the country’s 246 MW Mellach coal plant to prepare it for operation as an emergency measure amidst the prospect of a total Russian fossil gas cutoff.

However, the coal plant never restarted because the government failed to obtain a two-thirds majority vote and the intolerable cost burden it would have placed on taxpayers. At the end of 2023, the country announced its commitment to decarbonise its entire energy system by 2035.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Belgium.png) BELGIUM: COAL FREE SINCE 2016

Belgium was Europe’s first country to exit coal, with its [last coal plant closing in March 2016](http://www.caneurope.org/publications/press-releases/987-belgium-says-goodbye-to-coal-power-use). It was not an announced government policy objective, but rather resulted from the progressive closure of ageing coal plants impacted by EU pollution control regulations.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Bosniajpg.jpg) BOSNIA-HERZEGOVINA: NO COAL PHASE OUT DISCUSSION

Bosnia-Herzegovina is still planning new coal power plants. It is a hotspot for China’s exportation of coal technologies, with several coal plant projects under loan consideration by China’s policy banks. The existing plants are only expected to be compliant with EU pollution laws by 2028, while the current fleet is old and [in breach of existing pollution limits](https://bankwatch.org/press_release/new-report-western-balkan-coal-plants-in-severe-breach-of-air-pollution-limits).

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Bulgaria.png) BULGARIA: COAL PHASE OUT 2038-40

In October 2021, the Bulgarian government announced that the country will phase out coal in 2038 or 2040, in its National Resilience and Recovery Plan submitted to the European Commission. Bulgaria’s coal industry is on the brink of collapse with an estimated one billion euro of upgrades required to bring it in line with EU emission standards. Bulgaria’s late phase out date leaves it falling far short of its responsibilities on climate change and will delay crucial investments in renewable energy projects and retraining schemes for workers, which are needed to transition to a future without fossils. Old and polluting coal power plants are still in operation. The government relies on derogations from EU laws to keep them operating and avoids taking responsibility for just transition actions.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/croatia.png) CROATIA: COAL PHASE OUT 2033 OR EARLIER

Croatia joined the Powering Past Coal Alliance on 30 June 2021 as a non-declaration member with a commitment to work with the Powering Past Coal Alliance to set a coal phase out date in the near future. The country has two relatively small hard coal units, one of which was scheduled for closure at the end of 2017 to comply with the conditions under which Croatia became an EU member state. Croatian Prime Minister Andrej Plenković used his opening address at the COP26 climate meeting in Glasgow to announce that his country will phase out coal by 2033 at the latest.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Czech.png) CZECHIA: COAL PHASE OUT BY 2033

In August 2019, a multi-stakeholder coal commission was established to assess the possibility of a coal phase out in Czechia. Delayed by the Covid-19 pandemic, in December 2020, the commission finally recommended a 2038 coal exit. The government adopted a 2033 coal phase out in January 2022 and included it as a strategic objective in the [NECP](https://commission.europa.eu/publications/czechia-final-updated-necp-2021-2030-submitted-2024_en) submitted in 2024. In January 2026, the new Czech government published a [policy statement](https://vlada.gov.cz/cz/vlada/programove-prohlaseni/programove-prohlaseni-vlady-224629/#hospodarstvi_prumysl_energetika), which states that some coal-fired power plants will be maintained “until sufficient other stable energy sources are available”, without providing any further information on the timeline.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Denmark.png) DENMARK: COAL PHASE OUT BY 2028

In November 2017, Denmark became one of the first signatories to the [Powering Past Coal Alliance](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/666736/Final_Declaration_PPCA_111217.pdf), declaring it would work to phase out coal by 2030. All Danish coal plants now have individual closure dates, the latest being Nordjylland, which is scheduled to close in 2028. In 2017, Ørsted (formerly DONG) became the first Danish utility to [announce it will cease coal use by 2023. CEO Henrik Poulson later used the 2019 UN Climate Action Summit to state that the company will stop using coal from 2022 onwards.](https://orsted.com/en/Media/Newsroom/News/2017/02/DONG-Energy-to-stop-all-use-of-coal-by-2023) The only other remaining operational combined heat and power (CHP) plant, Fyns, will close in 2022.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Finland.png) FINLAND: COAL PHASE OUT BY MID 2029

Following a year-long period of pressure from civil society groups to end coal, in October 2018, the Finnish Government [introduced a legislative proposal to ban the use of coal in power generation after May 1, 2029](https://tem.fi/en/article/-/asset_publisher/lakiehdotukset-kivihiilikielto-2029-lisaa-biopolttoaineita-liikenteeseen-seka-biopolttooljya-lammitykseen-ja-tyokoneisiin), which the parliament [adopted on 27 February 2019.](https://www.finlex.fi/fi/laki/alkup/2019/20190416) While the phase out falls short of the 2025 date argued for by Finnish NGOs, the law includes the establishment of a €90 million fund for energy companies that opt to end burning coal by 2025. Unfortunately, half of this money will be diverted away from supporting wind power and will be made available for biomass to substitute coal in combined heat and power plants. However, the other half will go to better renewable solutions.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/France.jpg) FRANCE: COAL PHASE OUT 2027

In November 2016, France [committed to a coal phase out by 2023](http://in%20november%202016,%20france%20committed%20to%20a%20coal%20phase%20out%20by%202023%20at%20cop%2022.xn--%20president%20macrons%20administration%20later%20reaffirmed%20frances%20commitment%20to%20exit%20coal,%20by%20bringing%20the%20phase%20out%20forward%20to%202022-e038hnb.%20in%20june%202019,%20agreement%20over%20the%20coal%20phase%20out%20legislation%20was%20reached,%20which%20was%20enacted%20through%20the%20energy%20and%20climate%20law%20in%20november%20that%20year.%20the%20law%20contains%20an%20emission%20cap%20(0,7%20kt%20eqco2/MW%20on%20an%20annual%20basis%20for%20power%20plants%20emitting%20more%20than%200,55%20t%20eqCO2/MWh),%20which%20will%20become%20effective%20on%201%20January%202022,%20and%20is%20supposed%20to%20make%20coal%20power%20plants%20unprofitable.%20Unfortunately,%20this%20leaves%20the%20door%20open%20for%20low%20running%20hours%20of%20coal%20plants%20beyond%20that%20date,%20or%20the%20unsustainable%20conversion%20to%20a%20mix%20of%20coal%20and%20biomass.%20However,%20with%20all%20but%20one%20of%20France%E2%80%99s%20coal%20plants%20announced%20to%20close%20by%202022,%20this%20loophole%20only%20concerns%20the%20Cordemais%20coal%20plant%20where%20there%20are%20plans%20to%20co-fire%20it%20with%20biomass.) at COP 22. [President Macron’s administration later reaffirmed France’s commitment](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-climatechange-france/france-to-end-sale-of-diesel-and-gasoline-vehicles-by-2040-idUSKBN19R0VZ) to exit coal, by bringing the phase out forward to 2022.

In June 2019, [agreement over the coal phase out](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-france-energy/france-sets-2050-carbon-neutral-target-with-new-law-idUSKCN1TS30B) legislation was reached, which was enacted through the [energy and climate law in November that year](https://www.vie-publique.fr/loi/23814-loi-energie-et-climat-du-8-novembre-2019). The law contains an emission cap (0,7 kt eqCO2/MW on an annual basis for power plants emitting more than 0,55 t eqCO2/MWh), which will become effective on 1 January 2022, and is supposed to make coal power plants unprofitable. Unfortunately, this leaves the door open for low running hours of coal plants beyond that date, or the unsustainable conversion to a mix of coal and biomass.

However, with all but one of France’s coal plants announced to close by 2022, this loophole only concerns the Cordemais coal plant where there are plans to co-fire it with biomass.

In June 2022, [the French government confirmed](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20220626-ukraine-war-pushes-france-to-rethink-coal-power-station-closure) it will retain the option to restart the country’s 647 MW Emile Huchet coal power plant for the 2022-23 winter amidst the prospect of a total Russian fossil gas cutoff.

In August 2023, France extended the life of its last two coal plants Emile-Huchet and Cordemais until the end of 2024, effectively delaying the country’s coal exit from 2023 to 2024.

On 24 September 2023, President Emmanuel Macron pushed back France’s coal exit date from 2024 to 2027.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Germany.png) GERMANY: COAL PHASE OUT 2038

In July 2020, the German [coal power exit law](https://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/drucksachen/2020/0301-0400/392-20.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1), as well as a law that provides support to coal regions for the transition, was adopted. The first law stipulates a coal exit by 2038 with an option to forward it to 2035 and creates the basis for the retirement of an assumed 40 coal plants with a capacity of 23 GW by 2030 in addition to retirements that were announced already. We assume that about 15 coal plants with a capacity of nearly 19 GW are meant to operate after 2030, making the law incompatible with the UN Paris climate agreement [1.5 degrees C requirements](https://climateanalytics.org/publications/2019/coal-phase-out-insights-from-the-ipcc-special-report-on-15c-and-global-trends-since-2015/). A public- private contract between the German government and the lignite industry, still to be signed, is supposed to grant 4.35 bln EUR in compensation to operators for closing down their lignite business. The compensation is still subject to approval by the state aid department of the EU Commission.

According to German civil society organisations and former coal commission members, the law is substantially weaker than what was proposed by the multi-stakeholder government coal commission, which had [presented its final report](https://www.cleanenergywire.org/news/commission-watch-managing-germanys-coal-phase-out) in February 2019. The criticism centres on the slowness of the exit, that it does not foresee the rescue of the villages threatened by lignite mining, the non-linear phase out pathway of lignite plants, allowing the new coal plant Datteln IV to come online, the massive overcompensation for the coal industry for uneconomic and late coal plant closures, and subsidies for coal-to-gas/biomass switching. Many factors are in play that will force Germany to exit coal in the power sector faster. This was evidenced in December 2020, when a set of three younger hard coal plants unexpectedly won in the first auction to determine hard coal power retirements.

In a landmark [German supreme court ruling](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2021/apr/29/historic-german-ruling-says-climate-goals-not-tough-enough), the German government was required to strengthen its 2030 climate targets in order to account for intergenerational climate equity. While the measures to be able to reach those climate targets have yet to be agreed, a coal phase out by 2030 has become inevitable because the power sector will have to contribute much of the early emission cuts. Following Germany’s September 2021 elections, the SPD, Greens and FDP entered into coalition negotiations and produced a consultation paper which establishes that a coal phase out by 2030 is an ideal scenario, but without referring to concrete steps. Two solutions to overcome dependence on coal are mentioned: a massive expansion of renewables and the construction of hydrogen-ready fossil gas-fired power plants.

After the collapse of the German government triggered early elections, the CDU and SPD formed a new ruling coalition. The new coalition have [agreed to a late coal phase-out by 2038](https://www.cleanenergywire.org/factsheets/what-germanys-aspiring-coalition-government-agreement-means-climate-and-energy) in order to accommodate their plans to more than [double fossil gas expansion](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/germany-plans-develop-20-gw-gas-power-plant-capacity-2030.html#:~:text=Germany%20plans%20to%20develop%2020%20GW%20of%20gas%20power%20plant%20capacity%20by%202030,-mail&text=The%20new%20German%20coalition%20government,of%20growing%20global%20political%20tensions.).

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Greece.png) GREECE: COAL PHASE OUT 2027

At the United Nations Climate Action Summit in New York in September 2019, Greek prime minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis announced that Greece will close all of its lignite power plants by 2028. The following December, operator PPC decided to cease operating all of its existing lignite-fired power plants by 2023. That left just its under-construction Ptolemaida V plant to operate until 2028. In April 2021, PPC announced that it would cease burning lignite at Ptolemaida V in 2025, making Greece’s coal phase out date de facto 2025.

Although prime minister Mitsotakis announced in April 2022 that the country would likely extend the operation of all its coal-fired power plants to 2028, which is reflected in the draft NECP and the Greek Climate Law, PPC pushed back and in January 2024, announced its commitment to end operations of all its coal assets by 2026. On 15 May 2026, the Agios Dimitrios power plant was [shut down](https://balkangreenenergynews.com/ag-dimitrios-shutdown-today-leaves-greece-with-last-coal-plant/), leaving only one other lignite plant to go – Ptolemaida V. However, in September 2026 it was [reported](https://energypress.eu/raaey-backs-ptolemaida-v-extension-amid-energy-security-risks/) that the remaining plant’s closure might be postponed until the end of March 2027 due to energy security concerns.

- ### ![](https://beyondfossilfuels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Hungary.png) HUNGARY: COAL PHASE OUT 2029

In September 2019, at the United Nations Climate Action Summit in New York, the president of Hungary János Áder [said that the country will stop using coal to produce electricity by 2030](https://twitter.com/martonkruppa/status/1176259333149069313?s=20). In March 2021, at the Powering Past Coal Alliance’s global summit, Hungary [joined the Powering Past Coal Alliance](https://poweringpastcoal.org/news/PPCA-news/ppca-global-summit-strengthens-international-commitment-to-accelerate-coal-power-phase-out-ahead-of-cop26), stating that the country would phase out coal at its last remaining lignite power plant (Matra, 884 MW) by 2025. Matra is the country’s number one CO2 emitter and its closure by the end of 2025, [officially confirmed](https://kormany.hu/hirek/elkezdodott-a-matrai-eromu-atalakitasa-a-munkahelyek-nincsenek-veszelyben) by the government, is reward for years of dedicated effort on the part of environmental groups in Hungary which have long advocated for its closure by 2025. Since the UK introduced a carbon tax for power plants in 2013, installed coal capacity and coal power generation has fallen sharply. In February 2020, England [decided](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2020/feb/21/house-coal-and-wet-wood-to-be-phased-out-by-2023-to-cut-pollution) to phase out house coal and wet wood for burning at home by 2023.

On 1 October 2024, the UK’s last coal power plant, Uniper’s Ratcliffe-on-Soar, closed its doors for the final time, marking a historic end to coal-generated electricity in the country that spearheaded the Industrial Revolution and making the UK the fifth European country to exit coal.
