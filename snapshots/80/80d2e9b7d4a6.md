The **People's Army of Vietnam** ( **PAVN**; _Quân đội nhân dân Việt Nam_)[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[a\]](#refnote-14) is the national [military force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military "Military") of the [Socialist Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam") and the armed wing of the ruling [Communist Party of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "Communist Party of Vietnam") (CPV). PAVN is the core component of the [Vietnam People's Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Armed_Forces "Vietnam People's Armed Forces") and includes: Ground Force, [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Navy "Vietnam People's Navy"), [Air Defence - Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Air_Defence_-_Air_Force "Vietnam Air Defence - Air Force"), [Border Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Border_Guard "Vietnam Border Guard") and [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Coast_Guard "Vietnam Coast Guard"). Vietnam has not organized a formally-separated and structured ground force or [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army "Army") service command; instead, all ground troops, army corps, military districts and [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Army_of_Vietnam_Special_Forces "People's Army of Vietnam Special Forces") are designated under the umbrella term " [combined arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_arms "Combined arms")" ( [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language "Vietnamese language"): _binh chủng hợp thành_) and directly administered by the [Ministry of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defence_(Vietnam) "Ministry of National Defence (Vietnam)"), under the command of the [CPV Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Military_Commission_of_the_Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "Central Military Commission of the Communist Party of Vietnam"), the [Minister of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_(Vietnam) "Minister of Defence (Vietnam)"), and the [General Staff of the Vietnam People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff_of_the_Vietnam_People's_Army "General Staff of the Vietnam People's Army").

During the [French Indochina War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War "First Indochina War") (1946–1954), the PAVN was often referred to as the [Việt Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%E1%BB%87t_Minh "Việt Minh"), or precisely as the coalition's armed wing. In the context of the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") (1955–1975), despite already using PAVN as its official designation, the army was referred to by its opposition forces as the **North Vietnamese Army** ( **NVA**; _Quân đội Bắc Việt_), serving as the mainline military force of the [Democratic Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Viet_Nam "Democratic Republic of Viet Nam"). This allowed writers, the [U.S. military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces"), and the general public, to distinguish northern communists from the [southern communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Revolutionary_Party_(Vietnam) "People's Revolutionary Party (Vietnam)"), known as the [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong "Viet Cong") (VC) and was formally established as the National Liberation Front (NLF). However, both groups ultimately worked under the same command structure, despite Viet Cong (VC/NLF) - also proclaimed the [Provisional Revolutionary Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Revolutionary_Government_of_the_Republic_of_South_Vietnam "Provisional Revolutionary Government of the Republic of South Vietnam") (PRG) - having its own nominal military forces being the [Liberation Army of South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Army_of_South_Vietnam "Liberation Army of South Vietnam") (LASV), which was implicitly and practically considered a component and proxy of the PAVN by the North Vietnamese.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Victory68-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-Victory68) In 1976, following the political reunification of Vietnam that institutionally unified the two governments of the [Democratic Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Viet_Nam "Democratic Republic of Viet Nam") (North) and the [Republic of South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Revolutionary_Government_of_the_Republic_of_South_Vietnam "Provisional Revolutionary Government of the Republic of South Vietnam") (PRG), LASV was officially disbanded and merged into the so-called NVA to form the existing incarnation of PAVN, serving as the national military of the unified state of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-16)

## Etymology and symbols

### Etymology

After multiple reorganisations during the [final days of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina_in_World_War_II "French Indochina in World War II") and subsequently [First Indochina War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War "First Indochina War"), [Viet Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Minh "Viet Minh")'s primary armed wing was formally named _Quân đội nhân dân Việt Nam_ (lit.'Military of the people of Vietnam') by [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Vietnam "President of Vietnam") [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh "Ho Chi Minh") and [his administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Vietnam "Government of Vietnam") in 1954, and since then, the name has become the sole official designation for the mainline military forces under the governments of the former [Democratic Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Viet_Nam "Democratic Republic of Viet Nam") and the contemporary [Socialist Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Viet_Nam "Socialist Republic of Viet Nam"). It is usually translated into English as the **People's Army of Vietnam** ( **PAVN**), however, there are more recent consensus and standardisation by official Vietnamese narratives to use **Vietnam People's Army** ( **VPA**) as the uniformed English designation. The name shares the common "People" theming with many [communist forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_revolution "Communist revolution"), and according to President Ho, this naming is to emphasise the military's [morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality "Morality") of being "from the People, fight for the People, and serve the People."[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-17)

The usage of the word " [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army "Army")" in English translations has caused confusion by making people mistakenly identifying the Vietnam People's Army to be the [land service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare "Land warfare") branch of the combined [Vietnam People's Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Armed_Forces "Vietnam People's Armed Forces"), just like how the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") is the principal land service of the [United States Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces"). While VPA itself is already the entired combined military force and is indeed a part of the People's Armed Forces, in Vietnam, the Armed Forces is the umbrella designation to call the national uniformed services that consist of the military (VPA), the police ( [PPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Public_Security_of_Vietnam "People's Public Security of Vietnam")), and the [militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Militia_and_Self-Defence_Force "Vietnam Militia and Self-Defence Force"). In other words, Vietnam's "Army" is equivalent to most of the nations' " [Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military "Military")" or " [Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Forces "Defence Forces")", and in practice being similar to "Armies" such as the Soviet's [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army"), the [Islamic Republic of Iran Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Army "Islamic Republic of Iran Army"), the [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army "People's Liberation Army") of China, and to some extent, the [Army of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Armed_Forces "Czech Armed Forces"). VPA's land warfare arms have not been organised under an actual "Ground Force" (_Lục quân_) service branch, which would be comparable to many countries' "Army" or land force component.

### Symbols and identification

According to codified regulations from the [Government of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Vietnam "Government of Vietnam"), the VPA emblem (_Quân hiệu_, lit.'Military emblem') is "circular, with a shaded [golden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tincture_(heraldry) "Tincture (heraldry)") star in the middle, surrounded by two golden rice ears on a bright red background; below the two rice ears is a half golden cogwheel, with a golden outer rim" and will be employed by all VPA service branches and components, except [Vietnam Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Coast_Guard "Vietnam Coast Guard") using slightly modified variants.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-18) There are different variants for different levels of usage from [combat uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_uniform "Combat uniform") to [service dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_dress_uniform "Service dress uniform") and [full ceremonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_dress_uniform "Full dress uniform"), as well as versions for [general officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_officer "General officer"), with all of them respecting the cited symbolism.

The VPA flag (_Quân kỳ_, lit.'Military flag') is the ["red banner with golden star" national flag of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Vietnam "Flag of Vietnam") defaced with the motto _Quyết thắng_ (Determination to win) added in yellow at the [canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_(flag) "Canton (flag)") (the top corner by the [flagpole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagpole "Flagpole")), having the unit's formal name or designation to be inscribed below the golden star in ceremonial occasions. Vietnam does not issue any distinctive [unit colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_colours,_standards_and_guidons "Military colours, standards and guidons"), [formation patches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_patch "Formation patch") or [shoulder sleeve insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_sleeve_insignia "Shoulder sleeve insignia") besides the units' inscribed military flags, shoulder patches indicating the service branches, as well as physical [collar tabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorget_patches "Gorget patches") indicating the service branches, combat arms and ranking.

Most VPA [armoured vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_fighting_vehicle "Armoured fighting vehicle") are decorated with " [roundels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundel "Roundel")", to be a red circular field with a central yellow five-pointed star, bordered by a thick yellow ring. Meanwhile, most [military aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aircraft "Military aircraft") are also designated with the similar roundels, however added with a pair of yellow-bordered red wing devices.

## History

### Before 1945

See also: [Military history of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Vietnam "Military history of Vietnam")

The first historical record of Vietnamese military history dates back to the era of [Hồng Bàng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%93ng_B%C3%A0ng "Hồng Bàng"), the first recorded state in ancient Vietnam to have assembled military force. Since then, military plays a crucial role in developing Vietnamese history due to its turbulent history of wars against China, [Champa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champa "Champa"), Cambodia, Laos and Thailand.

The [Southern expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_ti%E1%BA%BFn "Nam tiến") of Vietnam resulted in the destruction of Champa as an independent nation to a level that it did not exist anymore; total destruction of [Luang Prabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luang_Prabang "Luang Prabang");\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] the decline of Cambodia which resulted in Vietnam's annexation of the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta "Mekong Delta") and wars against Siam. In most of its history, the Royal Vietnamese Armed Forces was often regarded to be one of the most professional, battle-hardened and heavily trained armies in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") as well as Asia in a large extent.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Establishment

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Vo_Nguyen_Giap%2C_Vietminh_forces%2C_1944.jpg/500px-Vo_Nguyen_Giap%2C_Vietminh_forces%2C_1944.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Vo_Nguyen_Giap,_Vietminh_forces,_1944.jpg) General [Võ Nguyên Giáp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%B5_Nguy%C3%AAn_Gi%C3%A1p "Võ Nguyên Giáp") on the date of the PAVN's establishment in 1944. Chief of General Staff [Hoàng Văn Thái](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%C3%A0ng_V%C4%83n_Th%C3%A1i "Hoàng Văn Thái") wearing a [pith helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pith_helmet "Pith helmet") and holding the flag.

The PAVN was first conceived in September 1944 at the first Revolutionary Party Military Conference as the **Information, Communication and Education Unit of the Liberation Army** (alternatively translated as the **Vietnam Information, Communication and Education Liberation Army**, _Việt Nam Tuyên truyền Giải phóng Quân_) to educate, recruit and mobilise the Vietnamese to create a main force to drive the [French colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Vietnam#Colonization "History of Vietnam") and [Japanese occupiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina_in_World_War_II "French Indochina in World War II") from Vietnam.[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-:0) Under the guidelines of [Hồ Chí Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh "Ho Chi Minh"), [Võ Nguyên Giáp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%B5_Nguy%C3%AAn_Gi%C3%A1p "Võ Nguyên Giáp") was given the task of establishing the brigades and the Propaganda Unit of the Liberation Army came into existence on 22 December 1944. The first formation was made up of thirty-one men and three women, armed with two revolvers, seventeen rifles, one light machine gun, and fourteen breech-loading flintlocks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-21) It fought the PAVN's first ever engagement at the [Battles of Khai Phat and Na Ngan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Khai_Phat_and_Na_Ngan "Battles of Khai Phat and Na Ngan") against French soldiers in late 1944. The [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States")' [OSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Strategic_Services "Office of Strategic Services") agents, led by [Archimedes Patti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes_Patti "Archimedes Patti") – who was sometimes referred as the first instructor of the PAVN due to his role - had provided ammunitions as well as logistic intelligence and equipment. They also helped train these soldiers, who formed the backbone of the Vietnamese military to successfully fight the Japanese and other opponents. For instance, the PAVN's July 19, 1945 attack at Tam Dao internment camp in [Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonkin_(French_protectorate) "Tonkin (French protectorate)") saw 500 soldiers kill fifty Japanese soldiers and officials, freeing French civilian captives and escorting them to the Chinese border. The PAVN also fought the [Japanese 21st Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Division_(Imperial_Japanese_Army) "21st Division (Imperial Japanese Army)") in Thai Nguyen that year, and regularly raided rice storehouses to alleviate an [ongoing famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_famine_of_1944%E2%80%931945 "Vietnamese famine of 1944–1945").[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-22)

There was another separate communist army called the **National Salvation Army** ( _Cứu quốc quân_) which was founded and commanded by [Chu Văn Tấn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_V%C4%83n_T%E1%BA%A5n "Chu Văn Tấn") on 23 February 1941.

The Propaganda Liberation Army merged with the National Salvation Army into the **Vietnam Liberation Army** ( _Việt Nam Giải phóng Quân_) on 15 May 1945.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EarlyDays-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-EarlyDays) The [Democratic Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Vietnam "Democratic Republic of Vietnam") was proclaimed in [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi") by [Ho Chi Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh "Ho Chi Minh") and [Vietminh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietminh "Vietminh") on 2 September 1945. Then in September, the army was renamed the Vietnam **National Defence Force** ( _Việt Nam Vệ quốc Đoàn_).[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-24)[&#8202;](#ref-back-EarlyDays-1)[\[22\]](#refnote-EarlyDays) At this point, it had about 1,000 soldiers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-EarlyDays-2)[\[22\]](#refnote-EarlyDays) On 22 May 1946, the army was called the **National Army of Vietnam** ( _Quân đội Quốc gia Việt Nam_, not to be confused with the opposite [Vietnamese National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_National_Army "Vietnamese National Army") of the France-associated [State of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Vietnam "State of Vietnam") which had a synonymous English name and exactly the same Vietnamese name). Lastly, in 1950, it officially became the **People's Army of Vietnam** (or **Vietnam People's Army**, _Quân đội Nhân dân Việt Nam_).[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-:0)

Võ Nguyên Giáp went on to become the first full general of the PAVN on 28 May 1948, and famous for leading the PAVN in victory over French forces at the [Battle of Dien Bien Phu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dien_Bien_Phu "Battle of Dien Bien Phu") in 1954 and being in overall command against U.S. backed [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam") at the [Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon "Fall of Saigon") on 30 April 1975.

### French Indochina War

Main article: [First Indochina War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Indochina_War "First Indochina War")

On 7 January 1947, its first regiment, the 102nd 'Capital' Regiment, was created for operations around [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi").[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-25) Over the next two years, the first division, the [308th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/308_Division_(Vietnam) "308 Division (Vietnam)"), later well known as the Pioneer Division, was formed from the 88th Tu Vu Regiment and the 102nd Capital Regiment. By late 1950 the 308th Division had a full three infantry regiments, when it was supplemented by the 36th Regiment. At that time, the 308th Division was also backed by the 11th Battalion that later became the main force of the [312th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/312th_Division_(Vietnam) "312th Division (Vietnam)"). In late 1951, after launching three campaigns against three French strongpoints in the [Red River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Delta "Red River Delta"), the PAVN refocused on building up its ground forces further, with five new divisions, each of 10–15,000 men, created: the [304th Glory Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/304th_Division_(Vietnam) "304th Division (Vietnam)") at [Thanh Hóa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanh_H%C3%B3a_province "Thanh Hóa province"), the 312th Victory Division in Vinh Phuc, the [316th Bong Lau Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/316th_Division_(Vietnam) "316th Division (Vietnam)") in the northwest border region, the [320th Delta Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/320th_Division_(Vietnam) "320th Division (Vietnam)") in the north Red River Delta, the [325th Binh Tri Thien Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/325th_Division_(Vietnam) "325th Division (Vietnam)") in Binh Tri Thien province. Also in 1951, the first artillery Division, the [351st Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/351st_Artillery-Engineer_Division_(Vietnam) "351st Artillery-Engineer Division (Vietnam)") was formed, and later, before [Battle of Dien Bien Phu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dien_Bien_Phu "Battle of Dien Bien Phu") in 1954, for the first time in history, it was equipped with 24 captured 105mm US howitzers supplied by the Chinese [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army "People's Liberation Army"). The first six divisions (308th, 304th, 312th, 316th, 320th, 325th) became known as the original PAVN 'Steel and Iron' divisions. In 1954, four of these divisions (the 308th, 304th, 312th, 316th, supported by the 351st Division's captured US howitzers) defeated the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union "French Union") forces at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu, ending 83 years of French rule in Indochina.

The [French Foreign Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Foreign_Legion "French Foreign Legion") had been deployed to combat the Vietnamese insurgency during the First Indochina War. However, some of the legionnaires, such as [Stefan Kubiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Kubiak "Stefan Kubiak"), deserted after witnessing torture of Vietnamese peasants at the hands of French troops and began fighting for the [Việt Minh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Minh "Viet Minh"), volunteering to join the PAVN.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dantri-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-dantri)[&#8202;](#ref-back-naszahistoria-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-naszahistoria)[&#8202;](#ref-back-wp-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-wp)

### Vietnam War

Main article: [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/52/Pavnattack.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pavnattack.jpg) North Vietnamese troops in Vietnam War, 1967[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Nvamarch2.jpg/500px-Nvamarch2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nvamarch2.jpg) [Infiltrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infiltration_tactics "Infiltration tactics") on the move in Laos down the Ho Chi Minh trail.

Soon after the [1954 Geneva Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Geneva_Conference "1954 Geneva Conference"), the 330th and 338th Divisions were formed by southern Viet Minh members who had moved north in conformity with that agreement, and by 1955, six more divisions were formed: the 328th, 332nd and 350th in the north of the [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam "North Vietnam"), the 305th and the [324th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/324th_Division_(Vietnam) "324th Division (Vietnam)") near the [DMZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Demilitarized_Zone "Vietnamese Demilitarized Zone"), and the 335 Division of soldiers repatriated from [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"). In 1957, the theatres of the war with the French were reorganised as the first five military regions, and in the next two years, several divisions were reduced to brigade size to meet the manpower requirements of collective farms.

By 1958, it was becoming increasingly clear that the [South Vietnamese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam") was solidifying its position as an independent republic under [Ngô Đình Diệm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C3%B4_%C4%90%C3%ACnh_Di%E1%BB%87m "Ngô Đình Diệm"), who staunchly opposed the terms of the Geneva Accords, which required a national referendum on unification of north and south Vietnam under a single national government. North Vietnam prepared to settle the issue of unification by force.

In May 1959, the first major steps to prepare infiltration routes into [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam") were taken; [Group 559](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_559 "Group 559") was established, a logistical unit charged with establishing routes into the south via Laos and [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia"), which later became famous as the [Ho Chi Minh trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_trail "Ho Chi Minh trail"). At about the same time, Group 579 was created as its maritime counterpart to transport supplies into the South by sea. Most of the early infiltrators were members of the 338th Division, former southerners who had been settled at [Xuan Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuan_Mai?action=edit&redlink=1 "Xuan Mai") from 1954 onwards.

Regular formations were sent to South Vietnam from 1965 onwards; the 325th Division's 101B Regiment and the 66th Regiment of the 304th Division met U.S. forces on a large scale, a first for the PAVN, at the [Battle of Ia Drang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ia_Drang "Battle of Ia Drang") in November 1965. The 308th Division's 88A Regiment, the 312th Division's 141A, 141B, 165A, 209A, the 316th Division's 174A, the 325th Division's 95A, 95B, the 320A Division also faced the U.S. forces which included the [1st Cavalry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Cavalry_Division_(United_States) "1st Cavalry Division (United States)"), the [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division "101st Airborne Division"), the [173rd Airborne Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/173rd_Airborne_Brigade "173rd Airborne Brigade"), the [4th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Infantry_Division_(United_States) "4th Infantry Division (United States)"), the [1st Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Infantry_Division_(United_States) "1st Infantry Division (United States)") and the [25th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Infantry_Division_(United_States) "25th Infantry Division (United States)"). Many of those formations later became main forces of the [3rd Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Division_(Vietnam) "3rd Division (Vietnam)") (Yellow Star Division) in Binh Dinh (1965), the [5th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Infantry_Division_(Vietnam) "5th Infantry Division (Vietnam)") (1966) of 7th Military Zone (Capital Tactical Area of ARVN), the [7th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Division_(Vietnam) "7th Division (Vietnam)") (created by 141st and 209th Regiments originated in the 312th Division in 1966) and [9th Divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Division_(Vietnam) "9th Division (Vietnam)") (first Division of National Liberation Front of Vietnam in 1965 in Mekong Delta), the 10th Dakto Division in Dakto – [Central Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Highlands_(Vietnam) "Central Highlands (Vietnam)") in 1972.

On 20 December 1960, anti-government forces in South Vietnam joined to form a united front called [National Liberation Front of South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietcong "Vietcong") ( _Mặt trận Dân tộc Giải phóng Miền Nam Việt Nam_) or simply known as the Vietcong in the United States. On 15 December 1961, the NLF established its own military called [Liberation Army of South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Army_of_South_Vietnam "Liberation Army of South Vietnam") (LASV) to fight against the American supported [Army of the Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Republic_of_Vietnam "Army of the Republic of Vietnam"). The LASV was controlled and equipped by the PAVN.

General [Trần Văn Trà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%E1%BA%A7n_V%C4%83n_Tr%C3%A0 "Trần Văn Trà"), one-time commander of the [B2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COSVN "COSVN") Front (Saigon) HQ confirms that even though the PAVN and the LASV were confident in their ability to defeat the regular ARVN forces, U.S. intervention in Vietnam forced them to reconsider their operations. The decision was made to continue to pursue "main force" engagements even though "there were others in the South – they were not military people – who wanted to go back to guerrilla war," but the strategic aims were adjusted to meet the new reality.

> We had to change our plan and make it different from when we fought the Saigon regime, because we now had to fight two adversaries — the United States and South Vietnam. We understood that the U.S. Army was superior to our own logistically, in weapons and in all things. So strategically we did not hope to defeat the U.S. Army completely. Our intentions were to fight a long time and cause heavy casualties to the United States, so the United States would see that the war was unwinnable and would leave.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-29)

During the Vietnamese Lunar New Year [Tết holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%E1%BA%BFt "Tết") starting on 30 January 1968, the PAVN/VC launched a general offensive in more than 60 cities and towns throughout south of Vietnam against the US Army and [Army of the Republic of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Republic_of_Vietnam "Army of the Republic of Vietnam") (ARVN), beginning with operations in the border region to try to draw US forces and ARVN troops out of the major cities. In coordinated attacks, the [U.S. Embassy in Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tet_Offensive_attack_on_US_Embassy "Tet Offensive attack on US Embassy"), Presidential Palace, [Headquarters of the Joint General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tet_offensive_attack_on_Joint_General_Staff_Compound "Tet offensive attack on Joint General Staff Compound") and [Republic of Vietnam Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Vietnam_Navy "Republic of Vietnam Navy"), TV and Radio Stations, [Tan Son Nhat Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tet_Offensive_attack_on_Tan_Son_Nhut_Air_Base "Tet Offensive attack on Tan Son Nhut Air Base") in Saigon were attacked by commando forces known as "đặc công". This offensive became known as the " [Tet Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tet_Offensive "Tet Offensive")". The PAVN sustained heavy losses of its main forces in southern military zones. Some of its regular forces and command structure had to escape to Laos and Cambodia to avoid counterattacks from US forces and ARVN, while local guerrillas forces and political organisations in South Vietnam were exposed and had a hard time remaining within the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta "Mekong Delta") area due to the extensive use of the [Phoenix Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Program "Phoenix Program").

Although the PAVN lost militarily to the US forces and ARVN in the south, the political impact of the war in the United States was strong.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-30) Public demonstrations increased in ferocity and quantity after the Tet Offensive. During 1970, the 5th, 7th and 9th Divisions [fought in Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_campaign "Cambodian campaign") against U.S., ARVN, and Cambodian [Khmer National Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_National_Armed_Forces "Khmer National Armed Forces"). The PAVN gained new allies: the [Khmer Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge "Khmer Rouge") and guerrilla fighters supporting deposed Prime Minister [Sihanouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norodom_Sihanouk "Norodom Sihanouk"). In 1975 the PAVN were successful in aiding the Khmer Rouge in toppling [Lon Nol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lon_Nol "Lon Nol")'s U.S.-backed regime, despite heavy US bombing.

After the withdrawal of most U.S. combat forces from [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina "Indochina") because of the [Vietnamisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamization "Vietnamization") strategy, the PAVN launched the ill-fated [Easter Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Offensive "Easter Offensive") in 1972. Although successful at the beginning, the South Vietnamese repulsed the main assaults with U.S. air support. Still North Vietnam retained some South Vietnamese territory.

Nearly two years after the full U.S. withdrawal from Indochina in accordance with the terms of the 1973 [Paris Peace Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Accords "Paris Peace Accords"), the PAVN launched a [Spring offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_spring_offensive "1975 spring offensive") aimed at overthrowing the South Vietnamese government and uniting Vietnam under communist rule. Without direct support of the U.S., and suffering from stresses caused by dwindling aid, the ARVN was ill-prepared to confront the highly motivated PAVN, and despite the on paper superiority of the ARVN, the PAVN quickly secured victory within two months and [captured Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon "Fall of Saigon") on 30 April 1975, ending the 20 year Vietnam war.

After national reunification, the LASV was officially merged into PAVN on 2 July 1976.

### Sino-Vietnamese conflicts (1975–1991)

Main articles: [Cambodian–Vietnamese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War "Cambodian–Vietnamese War") and [Sino-Vietnamese conflicts (1979–1991)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_conflicts_(1979%E2%80%931991) "Sino-Vietnamese conflicts (1979–1991)")

Towards the second half of the 20th century the armed forces of Vietnam would participate in organised incursions to protect its citizens and allies against aggressive military factions in the neighbouring Indochinese countries of Laos and Cambodia, and the defensive border wars with China.

- The PAVN had forces in Laos to secure the Ho Chi Minh trail and to militarily support the [Pathet Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathet_Lao "Pathet Lao"). In 1975 the Pathet Lao and PAVN forces succeeded in toppling the [Royal Laotian regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Laos "Kingdom of Laos") and installing a new, and pro-Hanoi government, the Lao People's Democratic Republic,[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-31) that rules Laos to this day.
- Parts of Sihanouk's neutral Cambodia were occupied by troops as well. A pro US coup led by [Lon Nol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lon_Nol "Lon Nol") in 1970 led to the foundation pro-US [Khmer Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Republic "Khmer Republic") state. This marked the beginning of the [Cambodian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_Civil_War "Cambodian Civil War"). The PAVN aided [Khmer Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge "Khmer Rouge") forces in toppling Lon Nol's government in 1975. In 1978, along with the [FUNSK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FUNSK "FUNSK") Cambodian Salvation Front, the Vietnamese and Ex-Khmer Rouge forces succeeded in toppling [Pol Pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_Pot "Pol Pot")'s [Democratic Kampuchea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Kampuchea "Democratic Kampuchea") regime and installing a new government, the [People's Republic of Kampuchea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Kampuchea "People's Republic of Kampuchea").[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-32)
- During the [Sino-Vietnamese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_War "Sino-Vietnamese War") and the [Sino-Vietnamese conflicts (1979–1991)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_conflicts_(1979%E2%80%931991) "Sino-Vietnamese conflicts (1979–1991)"), Vietnamese forces would conduct cross-border raids into Chinese territory to destroy artillery ammunition. This greatly contributed to the outcome of the Sino-Vietnamese War, as the Chinese forces ran out of ammunition already at an early stage and had to call in reinforcements.
- While occupying Cambodia, Vietnam launched [several armed incursions into Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_border_raids_in_Thailand "Vietnamese border raids in Thailand") in pursuit of Cambodian guerrillas that had taken refuge on the Thai side of the border.

## Modern deployment

The PAVN has been actively involved in Vietnam's workforce to develop the [economy of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Vietnam "Economy of Vietnam") by co-ordinating national defence. It has regularly sent troops to aid with natural disasters such as flooding, landslides etc. The PAVN is also involved in such areas as industry, agriculture, [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry "Forestry"), [fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery "Fishery") and telecommunications. The PAVN has numerous small firms which have become quite profitable in recent years. However, recent decrees have effectively prohibited the commercialisation of the military. [Conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription") is in place for theoretically every male, age 18 to 27 years old, with the exception of the disabled and men who attended universities right after high school.

### International presence & operations

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/2025_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade_55.jpg/500px-2025_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade_55.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2025_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade_55.jpg) People's Army on Red Square in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") during the [Victory Day parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade "2025 Moscow Victory Day Parade") on 9 May 2025

The Foreign Relations Department of the Ministry of National Defence organises international operations of the PAVN.

Apart from its occupation of half of the disputed [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands "Spratly Islands"), which have been claimed as Vietnamese territory since the 17th century, Vietnam has not officially had forces stationed internationally since its withdrawal from [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Cambodia_(1989%E2%80%931993) "State of Cambodia (1989–1993)") and Laos in early 1990.

#### Vietnamese assistance for overseas leftist insurgencies

The effectiveness of the [People's Army of Vietnam Special Operation Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Army_of_Vietnam_Special_Forces "People's Army of Vietnam Special Forces") during the Vietnam War saw them instruct various other countries and [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist_revolution "Marxist revolution") rebel groups. From the 1970s to 1990s, they covertly provided training at the PAVN Sapper Training School in via Vietnamese sapper advisors assigned to the [Cuban Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Revolutionary_Army "Cuban Revolutionary Army")'s Sapper School in Cuba, and, during the 1980s, by a secret Vietnamese sapper training team stationed in [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua "Nicaragua").In addition to training [Cambodian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Kampuchea "People's Republic of Kampuchea"), [Laotian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), Soviet, and Cuban military personnel, their publications revealed that among the foreign revolutionary forces that received training in sapper tactics, bomb-making, and the use of weapons and explosives, were members of the Marxist El Salvadoran FMLN ( [Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farabundo_Mart%C3%AD_National_Liberation_Front "Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front")), the Chilean MIR ( [Revolutionary Left Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Left_Movement_(Chile) "Revolutionary Left Movement (Chile)")) fighting against the dictatorial regime of [Augusto Pinochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Pinochet "Augusto Pinochet"), as well as the Colombian FARC ( [Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Armed_Forces_of_Colombia "Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia")) movement, a Marxist guerilla group.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-33)

## Organisation

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/13/Vietnam_Ministry_of_National_Defence_structure.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Vietnam_Ministry_of_National_Defence_structure.jpg) PAVN's structure

The _de jure_ [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief "Commander-in-Chief") of the Armed Forces is the [President of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Vietnam "President of Vietnam"), though this position is virtually nominal and a majority of political power over the military is assumed by the [Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Military_Commission_of_the_Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "Central Military Commission of the Communist Party of Vietnam") of the ruling [Communist Party of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "Communist Party of Vietnam"). The [secretary of Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Central_Military_Commission_of_the_Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "Secretary of the Central Military Commission of the Communist Party of Vietnam") (usually the [General Secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "General Secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam")) is the _de facto_ supreme commander and now is [Tô Lâm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B4_L%C3%A2m "Tô Lâm").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Vietnam_Ministry_of_Defence_headquarters_2013.jpg/500px-Vietnam_Ministry_of_Defence_headquarters_2013.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Vietnam_Ministry_of_Defence_headquarters_2013.jpg) Headquarters of the Ministry of National Defence.

The [Minister of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_(Vietnam) "Minister of Defence (Vietnam)") oversees operations of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_(Vietnam) "Ministry of Defence (Vietnam)"), and the PAVN. He also oversees such agencies as the [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff "General Staff") and the General Department of Logistics. However, military policy is ultimately directed by the Central Military Commission of the ruling [Communist Party of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "Communist Party of Vietnam").

- **[Ministry of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_(Vietnam) "Ministry of Defence (Vietnam)")**: is the lead organisation, highest command and management of the Vietnam People's Army.
- **[General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff_(Vietnam_People's_Army) "General Staff (Vietnam People's Army)")**: is leading agency all levels of the Vietnam People's Army, command all of the armed forces, which functions to ensure combat readiness of the armed forces and manage all military activities in peace and war.
- **[General Political Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Political_Department_of_the_Vietnam_People's_Army "General Political Department of the Vietnam People's Army")**: is the agency in charge of Communist Party affairs – political work within PAVN, which operates under the direct leadership of the [Secretariat of the Communist Party of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Vietnam "Communist Party of Vietnam") and the Central Military Party Committee.
- **[General Department of Defence Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Department_of_Military_Intelligence "General Department of Military Intelligence")**: is an intelligence agency of the Vietnamese government and military.
- **General Department of Logistics**: is the agency in charge to ensure logistical support to units of the People's Army.
- **General Department of Technology**: is the agency in charge to ensure equipped technical means of war for the army and each unit.
- **[General Department of Defence Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Department_of_Defence_Industry "General Department of Defence Industry")** (commercially branded as the **[Vietnam Defence Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Defence_Industry "Vietnam Defence Industry")**): is the agency responsible for the development of the Vietnamese national [defence industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_industry "Arms industry") in support of the missions of the PAVN.

The People's Army of Vietnam composes of the standing (or regular) forces and the reserve forces. The standing forces include the main forces and the local forces. During peacetime, the standing forces are minimised in number, and kept combat-ready by regular physical and [weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon "Weapon") training, and stock maintenance.

#### Main forces

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/People%27s_Army_of_Vietnam_military_vehicles_roundel.svg/500px-People%27s_Army_of_Vietnam_military_vehicles_roundel.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:People's_Army_of_Vietnam_military_vehicles_roundel.svg) PAVN military vehicles roundel.[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/People%27s_Army_of_Vietnam_Ground_Forces_military_vehicles_roundel_%282025%29.svg/500px-People%27s_Army_of_Vietnam_Ground_Forces_military_vehicles_roundel_%282025%29.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:People's_Army_of_Vietnam_Ground_Forces_military_vehicles_roundel_(2025).svg) PAVN Ground Forces military vehicles roundel (Seen in the 80th National Day Military Parade)[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/People%27s_Army_Of_Vietnam_Ground_Forces_Badge_Vector_Version.svg/500px-People%27s_Army_Of_Vietnam_Ground_Forces_Badge_Vector_Version.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:People's_Army_Of_Vietnam_Ground_Forces_Badge_Vector_Version.svg) Badge on all personnel uniforms.[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Sleeve_Insignia_of_People%27s_Army_of_Vietnam_%282020s%29_Vector_Version.svg/500px-Sleeve_Insignia_of_People%27s_Army_of_Vietnam_%282020s%29_Vector_Version.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sleeve_Insignia_of_People's_Army_of_Vietnam_(2020s)_Vector_Version.svg) Patch on field uniform.

The Main Force of the PAVN and its People's Ground Forces consists of combat ready troops, as well as support units such as educational institutions for logistics, officer training, and technical training. In 1991, Conboy et al. stated that the PAVN Ground Force had four 'Strategic Army Corps' in the early 1990s, numbering 1–4, from north to south.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-53) [1st Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Corps_(Vietnam_People's_Army) "1st Corps (Vietnam People's Army)"), located in the [Red River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Delta "Red River Delta") region, consisted of the 308th (one of the six original 'Steel and Iron' divisions) and 312th Divisions, and the 309th Infantry Regiment. The other three corps, 2 SAC, 3 SAC, and 4 SAC, were further south, with [4th Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Corps_(Vietnam_People's_Army) "4th Corps (Vietnam People's Army)"), in Southern Vietnam, consisting of two former [LASV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Army_of_South_Vietnam "Liberation Army of South Vietnam") divisions, the 7th and 9th.

From 2014 to 2016, the [IISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies "International Institute for Strategic Studies") Military Balance attributed the Vietnamese ground forces with an estimated 412,000 personnel. [Formations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_(military) "Formation (military)"), according to the IISS, include 8 military regions, 4 corps headquarters, 1 special forces airborne brigade, 6 armoured brigades and 3 armoured regiments, two mechanised infantry divisions, and 23 active infantry divisions plus another 9 reserve ones.

Combat support formations include 13 artillery brigades and one artillery regiment, 11 air defence brigades, 10 engineers brigades, 1 electronic warfare unit, 3 signals brigades and 2 signals regiment.

Combat service support formations include 9 economic construction divisions, 1 logistical regiment, 1 medical unit and 1 training regiment. Ross wrote in 1984 that economic construction division "are composed of regular troops that are fully trained and armed, and reportedly they are subordinate to their own directorate in the Ministry of Defense. They have specific military missions; however, they are also entrusted with economic tasks such as food production or construction work. They are composed partially of older veterans."[&#8202;](#ref-back-FOOTNOTERoss198417-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-FOOTNOTERoss198417)
Ross also cited 1980s sources saying that economic construction divisions each had a strength of about 3,500.

In 2017, the listing was amended, with the addition of a single [Short-range ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-range_ballistic_missile "Short-range ballistic missile") brigade. The ground forces according to the IISS, hold Scud-B/C SRBMs.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-55)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Emblem_VPA.svg/60px-Emblem_VPA.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Emblem_VPA.svg)**[12th Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Corps_(Vietnam) "12th Corps (Vietnam)")**

First organised on 21 November 2023, the 12th Corps was created by merging all of the units from the former [1st Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Corps_(Vietnam) "1st Corps (Vietnam)") and the [2nd Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Corps_(Vietnam) "2nd Corps (Vietnam)"). It is stationed in [Tam Điệp District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tam_%C4%90i%E1%BB%87p_District "Tam Điệp District"), [Ninh Bình](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninh_B%C3%ACnh_province "Ninh Bình province").[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-56)[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-57)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Emblem_VPA.svg/60px-Emblem_VPA.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Emblem_VPA.svg)**[34th Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_Corps_(Vietnam) "34th Corps (Vietnam)")**

First organised on 10 December 2024, the 34th Corps was created by merging all of the units from the former [3rd Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Corps_(Vietnam) "3rd Corps (Vietnam)") and the [4th Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Corps_(Vietnam) "4th Corps (Vietnam)"). Stationed in [Pleiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleiku "Pleiku"), [Gia Lai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gia_Lai_province "Gia Lai province").[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-58)

#### Local forces

Local forces are an entity of the PAVN that, together with the [militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia "Militia") and "self-defence forces", act on the local level in protection of people and local governmental authorities. While the local forces are regular VPA forces, the people's militia consists of rural civilians, and the people's self-defence forces consist of civilians who live in urban areas and/or work in large groups, such as at construction sites or farming estates. The current number stands at 3–4million reservists and militia personnel combined who wear PAVN uniforms. They serve as force multipliers to the PAVN and Public Security during wartime and peacetime contingencies.

## Equipment

See also: [List of equipment of the Vietnam People's Ground Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Vietnam_People's_Ground_Forces "List of equipment of the Vietnam People's Ground Forces"), [List of equipment of the Vietnam People's Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Vietnam_People's_Navy "List of equipment of the Vietnam People's Navy"), and [List of equipment of the Vietnam People's Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Vietnam_People's_Air_Force "List of equipment of the Vietnam People's Air Force")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Duyet_binh.jpg/500px-Duyet_binh.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Duyet_binh.jpg) [People's Army of Vietnam Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Army_of_Vietnam_Special_Forces "People's Army of Vietnam Special Forces") parading in 2015 with Israeli-licence [ACE 31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IWI_Galil_ACE "IWI Galil ACE") rifles.

From the 1960s to 1975 the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), along with some smaller [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc") countries, was the main supplier of military hardware to North Vietnam. After the latter's victory in the war, it remained the main supplier of equipment to Vietnam. The United States had been the primary supplier of equipment to South Vietnam; much of the equipment left by the U.S. Army and the ARVN came under control of the re-unified Vietnamese government. The PAVN captured large numbers of ARVN weapons on 30 April 1975 after Saigon was captured.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Five_T-6C_Texan_II_%28601-605%29_for_Vietnam_People%27s_Air_Force.jpg/500px-Five_T-6C_Texan_II_%28601-605%29_for_Vietnam_People%27s_Air_Force.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Five_T-6C_Texan_II_(601-605)_for_Vietnam_People's_Air_Force.jpg) The [T-6C Texan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_T-6_Texan_II "Beechcraft T-6 Texan II") [trainer aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainer_aircraft "Trainer aircraft") are one of the first major assets that PAVN has procured from its former enemy [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in the modern era.

[Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") remains the largest arms-supplier for Vietnam; even after 1986, there were also increasing arms sales from other nations, notably from [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"). In 2016, [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") announced the lifting of the lethal weapons embargo on Vietnam, which has increased Vietnamese military equipment choices from other countries such as the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"), and other Western countries, which could enable a faster modernisation of the Vietnamese military. Since 2018, the United States has begun to provide warships for Vietnam Coast Guard as part of the military cooperation between two states, the first of these ships arrived in 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-60)

Despite Russia remaining Vietnam's largest weapon supplier, increasing cooperation with Israel has resulted in the development of Vietnamese weaponry with a strong mixture of Russian and Israeli weapons. For examples, the [STV rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STV_rifle "STV rifle") are three Vietnam-made indigenous service rifles modelled after the [Galil ACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galil_ACE "Galil ACE") of Israel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-soha-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-soha)
