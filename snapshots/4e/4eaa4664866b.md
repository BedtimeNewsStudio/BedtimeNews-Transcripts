**Isiaka Abiola Adeyemi Ajimobi**[//](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")[Play audio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f5/Yo-Isiaka_Abiola_Adeyemi_Ajimobi.ogg/Yo-Isiaka_Abiola_Adeyemi_Ajimobi.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Yo-Isiaka_Abiola_Adeyemi_Ajimobi.ogg "File:Yo-Isiaka Abiola Adeyemi Ajimobi.ogg") (16 December 1949 – 25 June 2020) was a Nigerian politician who served as [governor of Oyo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Oyo_State "Governor of Oyo State") from 2011 to 2019, he was the first person elected to the office twice.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)

He was formerly the Managing Director/Chief Executive Officer of the National Oil and Chemical Marketing Company, a subsidiary of [Shell Petroleum, Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Nigeria "Shell Nigeria"). He left the oil sector in 2002 after 26 years of meritorious service and was elected in 2003 as a Senator of the Republic of Nigeria representing Oyo South Senatorial District on the platform of the [Alliance for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Democracy_(Nigeria) "Alliance for Democracy (Nigeria)") (AD). After one term in the senate, he contested in 2007 for the governorship seat of Oyo state under the banner of the [All Nigeria People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Nigeria_People's_Party "All Nigeria People's Party"), a bid which he lost.[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) He contested again in the April 2011 gubernatorial elections under the [Action Congress of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_Congress_of_Nigeria "Action Congress of Nigeria") and was elected Governor of Oyo State in a closely contested vote.[&#8202;](#ref-back-r1-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-r1) In 2019, he was succeeded by [Engr. Oluwaseyi Makinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyi_Makinde "Seyi Makinde"). After leaving office, he contested the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Nigeria "Senate of Nigeria") seat of his senatorial district but lost. In June 2020, he was confirmed to have contacted [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") and was announced dead on 25 June.

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abiola_Ajimobi&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Isiaka Abiola Adeyemi Ajimobi was born on 16 December 1949 to the Ajimobi family of [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan "Ibadan") at Oja-Iba, Ibadan. Oyo State.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-4) He started his education at Saint Patricks Primary School, Oke-Padre in Ibadan. He completed his primary education at Ibadan City Council Primary School, Aperin. His secondary school education was at Lagelu Grammar School.[&#8202;](#ref-back-5-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-5)

Ajimobi's university education was at the [United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America "United States of America"), where he studied Business Administration and Finance at the [State University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York "State University of New York"), in Buffalo, New York graduating with a Bachelor of Science degree. His MBA was in Operations Research and Marketing with a concentration in Finance at [Governors State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_State_University "Governors State University"), [University Park, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Park,_Illinois "University Park, Illinois").[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-6)

## Political career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abiola_Ajimobi&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Political career")\]

In 2003, Abiola Adeyemi Ajimobi became a Senator of the Republic of Nigeria. Ajimobi was a principal officer in the Senate, serving as the Deputy Minority leader of the Senate. In 2007, Ajimobi contested the gubernatorial election under the umbrella of the [All Nigeria Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Nigeria_Peoples_Party "All Nigeria Peoples Party") but lost. Ajimobi contested again in 2011 under the [Action Congress of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_Congress_of_Nigeria "Action Congress of Nigeria") and won.[&#8202;](#ref-back-r1-1)[\[3\]](#refnote-r1)

Known as the Architect of the Modern [Oyo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_State "Oyo State"), Ajimobi contested for second term in an election that took place on 11 April 2015 under the [All Progressives Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Progressives_Congress "All Progressives Congress") for re-election and won. In the event of his re-election into office as the Governor, Ajimobi became the first person to occupy the seat twice and in succession.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-8) He was declared the winner of the Oyo State gubernatorial election by the [Independent National Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_National_Electoral_Commission_(Nigeria) "Independent National Electoral Commission (Nigeria)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

Ajimobi was elected as the All Progressivse Congress Oyo South senatorial candidate on 28 September 2018. He lost in the [2019 senatorial elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Nigerian_Senate_election_in_Oyo_State "2019 Nigerian Senate election in Oyo State") to Kola Balogun of Peoples Democratic Party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)

On 16 June 2020, he was appointed as the acting national chairman of the All Progressives Congress (APC) by the National Working Committee (NWC) of the party.[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

## Personal life and death

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Abiola_Ajimobi&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Personal life and death")\]

In 1980, Ajimobi married Florence Ajimobi; they had five children.

On 19 June 2020, Ajimobi was admitted to First Cardiologist and Cardiovascular Consultants Hospital in Lagos after slipping into a [coma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma "Coma") following [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") complications during the [COVID-19 pandemic in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Nigeria "COVID-19 pandemic in Nigeria"). He was declared dead on 25 June 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13)
