## Background

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Southern_Transitional_Council_takeover_of_Socotra&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")]

After the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring "Arab Spring") reached the capital of the island, [Hadibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadibu "Hadibu"), locals began to rise against the then-[President of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Yemen "President of Yemen"), [Ali Abdullah Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abdullah_Saleh "Ali Abdullah Saleh"). During this period of instability, the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates") (UAE) has sought to expand its presence in the region, framing its actions as humanitarian aid missions. This engagement has contributed to the increasing politicization and militarization of Socotra.[[2]](#cite_note-The_Conversation-2)

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Protests_%286408227511%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Protests_%286408227511%29_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Protests_(6408227511)_(cropped).jpg)

Protestors in [Hadibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadibu "Hadibu") during the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring "Arab Spring")

Protests in Socotra followed the pattern of protests in mainland Yemen, calling for "political reform" and "[the end of the regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash-shab_yurid_isqat_an-nizam "Ash-shab yurid isqat an-nizam") and its corruption". With the rise of [internet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_Yemen "Internet in Yemen"), locals divided into two camps: those calling for a Socotran governorate independent of the [Hadhramaut Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadhramaut_Governorate "Hadhramaut Governorate") of eastern mainland Yemen, and those demanding an [autonomous federal region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalization_of_Yemen "Federalization of Yemen"). In 2013, the Socotra Archipelago became the [Socotra Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate "Socotra Governorate").[[2]](#cite_note-The_Conversation-2)

A series of cyclones, [Chapala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Chapala "Cyclone Chapala") and [Megh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Megh "Cyclone Megh") in 2015 and [Mekunu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Mekunu "Cyclone Mekunu") in 2018, struck the archipelago[[3]](#cite_note-3)[[4]](#cite_note-4)[[5]](#cite_note-5) causing severe damage to the main island's infrastructure, homes, roads, and power. Due to the collective impacts of the cyclones, the UAE sent an aid ship and plane, carrying blankets, tents, and barrels of food.[[6]](#cite_note-6) The Emiratis also rebuilt the infrastructure and established the "[Sheikh Zayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayed_bin_Sultan_Al_Nahyan "Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan") City" and rebuilt the port of Hadiboh and the [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Airport "Socotra Airport"). The UAE's habit of operating and expanding infrastructures was not a new practice for the UAE, as they have done the same thing with the port cities of [Mokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokha "Mokha"), [Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden "Aden") and [Mukalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukalla "Mukalla"); in doing so, they established ties with [Southern Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Movement "Southern Movement")-related groups and militias, most notably the [Southern Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Transitional_Council "Southern Transitional Council") (STC).[[2]](#cite_note-The_Conversation-2)

In 2016, the UAE increased supplies delivered to Socotra, which had been neglected due to pressing mainland concerns during the [ongoing conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_crisis_(2011–present) "Yemeni crisis (2011–present)"). In October 2016, the 31st cargo aircraft landed in [Socotra Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Airport "Socotra Airport") carrying two tons of aid.[[1]](#cite_note-France_24-1)[[7]](#cite_note-7) At that time, the UAE also illegally established a military base on the island as a part of the [Saudi Arabian–led intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian–led_intervention_in_Yemen "Saudi Arabian–led intervention in Yemen").[[1]](#cite_note-France_24-1)

In 2017, Emirati troops were deployed to the island as part of the Saudi Arabian-led intervention. The UAE also provided more than $2 billion in aid to Yemen, with a significant portion of that funding directed toward Socotra. These actions sparked criticism and led to rumors suggesting the UAE attempted to occupy the island.[[8]](#cite_note-8)[[1]](#cite_note-France_24-1)[[9]](#cite_note-9) The UAE denied those claims.[[10]](#cite_note-The_New_Arab-10)

### 2018 Emirati occupation

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Southern_Transitional_Council_takeover_of_Socotra&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: 2018 Emirati occupation")]

Main article: [United Arab Emirates occupation of Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_occupation_of_Socotra "United Arab Emirates occupation of Socotra")

On 30 April 2018, the UAE deployed over a hundred troops with artillery and armored vehicles to the Yemeni [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago "Archipelago") of [Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Governorate "Socotra Governorate") in the [Guardafui Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardafui_Channel "Guardafui Channel") without prior coordination with the Yemeni government, causing the relations of the two countries to deteriorate. The initial deployment consisted of UAE military aircraft carrying over fifty UAE soldiers and two armored vehicles, followed by two more aircraft carrying additional soldiers, tanks, and other armored vehicles.[[11]](#cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-11)[[12]](#cite_note-12) Al Jazeera reported that shortly after landing, UAE forces dismissed Yemeni soldiers stationed at administrative installations such as [Socotra Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Airport "Socotra Airport") and seaports until further notice, and the [flag of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates "Flag of the United Arab Emirates") was raised above official government buildings in [Hadibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadibu "Hadibu").[[13]](#cite_note-AJ2-13) On 14 May an agreement was reached between the UAE and Yemen which saw Yemen regain administrative control and [Saudi Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia") troops also being deployed to the island.[[14]](#cite_note-14)

Since 2019, the UAE-backed STC [Security Belt Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Belt "Security Belt") has significantly expanded its presence on the island. The fighters involved primarily originate from Aden and the southwestern regions of Yemen.[[2]](#cite_note-The_Conversation-2)

## Takeover

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Southern_Transitional_Council_takeover_of_Socotra&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Takeover")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Southern_Transitional_Council_-_posters.jpg/250px-Southern_Transitional_Council_-_posters.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Southern_Transitional_Council_-_posters.jpg)

[Southern Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Transitional_Council "Southern Transitional Council") propaganda posters in [Qulensya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qulensya "Qulensya"), Socotra, with portraits of [Aidarus al-Zubaidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidarus_al-Zubaidi "Aidarus al-Zubaidi") (right) and [Raft al-Taqlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raft_al-Taqlee?action=edit&redlink=1 "Raft al-Taqlee (page does not exist)") (left) with a caption that reads "Socotra in a new era"

Between April and June of 2020, the UAE deployed hundreds of mercenaries to Socotra, taking control of key positions throughout the island, including the capital, [Hadiboh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadiboh "Hadiboh").[[15]](#cite_note-Le_Monde-15)

On June 21, 2020, the [Southern Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Transitional_Council "Southern Transitional Council") (STC) seized control of government facilities and military bases on Socotra Island following opposition from the island's governor, Ramzi Mahroos, to the establishment of a pro-Emirati local force. This action prompted a swift withdrawal of Saudi forces from the area. The takeover by the STC represented a significant shift in control, allowing the UAE to exercise indirect authority over Socotra. Reports indicate that the wages for Socotrans are allegedly paid by the UAE. Additionally, a unit from the local [Yemeni Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Coast_Guard "Yemeni Coast Guard") defected and aligned itself with the STC during this transition.[[10]](#cite_note-The_New_Arab-10)

The archipelago's governor later fled to [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman "Oman") and continued to govern the archipelago using [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp").[[15]](#cite_note-Le_Monde-15) According to *[Le Monde diplomatique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde_diplomatique "Le Monde diplomatique")*, "[Raft al-Taqlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raft_al-Taqlee?action=edit&redlink=1 "Raft al-Taqlee (page does not exist)") [...] is not really in command, but rather Khalfan al-Mazrouei, an Emirati who heads a welfare organization in his own country," and is behind the 2018 occupation and this coup.[[15]](#cite_note-Le_Monde-15)

## Aftermath

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Southern_Transitional_Council_takeover_of_Socotra&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Aftermath")]

The [Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdrabbuh_Mansur_Hadi "Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi")-led internationally recognized government of Yemen initially called the takeover "a full-fledged coup".[[1]](#cite_note-France_24-1)

A 2021 report by [Agence France-Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_France-Presse "Agence France-Presse") highlights that the flags of the STC are significantly smaller than the larger flags of the UAE that are seen at police checkpoints. Additionally, the report indicates that the newly established communication links connect directly to UAE networks instead of Yemen's official network, YemenNet.[[2]](#cite_note-The_Conversation-2)[[11]](#cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-11)

In 2023, the [Presidential Leadership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Leadership_Council "Presidential Leadership Council"), which integrated the STC into the government, appointed al-Taqlee as the *de jure* governor of the Archipelago. Under his rule, peaceful protests against the coup and the UAE's interference in Socotra were dispersed with violence, and journalists who criticized the coup were arrested and beaten.[[15]](#cite_note-Le_Monde-15)
