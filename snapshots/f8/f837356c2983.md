**Sir Shah Nawaz Bhutto**[\[a\]](#ref-2) (8 March 1888 – 19 November 1957) was a Pakistani politician and landowner who was the head of the [Bhutto family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutto_family "Bhutto family") and served as the last [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister "Prime minister") ( _[dewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewan "Dewan")_) of [Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junagadh_State "Junagadh State") from 1947 to 1948, until its [accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Junagadh "Annexation of Junagadh") to India. He represented [Sind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Division "Sind Division") in the [Bombay Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Legislative_Council "Bombay Legislative Council") from 1921 to [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Province_(1936%E2%80%931955) "Sind Province (1936–1955)"). He was the father of [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto "Zulfikar Ali Bhutto"), the fourth [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Pakistan "President of Pakistan") and ninth [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Pakistan "Prime minister of Pakistan") of Pakistan.

## Early life and education

Shah Nawaz Bhutto was born on 8 March 1888 in [Garhi Khuda Bakhsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garhi_Khuda_Bakhsh "Garhi Khuda Bakhsh") in [Ratodero Taleka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratodero_Tehsil "Ratodero Tehsil") in the [Larkana District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larkana_District "Larkana District") of the [Sind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Province_(1936%E2%80%931955) "Sind Province (1936–1955)") region, within the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency "Bombay Presidency") of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India "British India") (now in [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), Pakistan) into a [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Muslim "Shia Muslim") family of the [Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutto_(clan) "Bhutto (clan)") clan to father Ghulam Murtaza Bhutto. He was also the youngest brother of [Nawab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab "Nawab") [Nabi Bux Khan Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Bux_Khan_Bhutto "Nabi Bux Khan Bhutto").[\[2\]](#ref-EB)

The Bhutto family owned 250,000 acres of land spread across [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), in [Larkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larkana "Larkana"), [Sukkur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukkur "Sukkur") and [Jacobabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobabad "Jacobabad").[\[3\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEChitkara199617) Shah Nawaz got his early education and later at [St. Patrick's High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_High_School,_Karachi "St Patrick's High School, Karachi") in [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi"), and then at [Sindh Madressa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh_Madressa "Sindh Madressa"), also in Karachi.[\[1\]](#ref-paknetmag)

## Career and dewan of Junagadh

Bhutto entered the Legislative Council of the [Bombay Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Province "Bombay Province"), of which Sindh was a part, in 1921 at the age of 33. He continued till 1936. During this time, he received the honours of [CIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_the_Indian_Empire "Companion of the Order of the Indian Empire") followed by knighthood. In 1934, he became a minister in the Bombay government.[\[3\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEChitkara199617)

Bhutto attended the Round Table Conference in 1931 as a leader of Sindhi Muslims demanding separation of Sind from the Bombay province. This was eventually granted in the [Government of India Act 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1935 "Government of India Act 1935"), with Sind becoming a [separate province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_Province_(1936-55) "Sind Province (1936-55)") on 1 April 1936. Bhutto was appointed as a chief advisor to the Governor of Sind.[\[3\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEChitkara199617)

In preparation for the [provincial elections in 1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Sind_legislative_assembly_election "1937 Sind legislative assembly election"), the [Sind United Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_United_Party "Sind United Party") was formed by Haji [Abdullah Haroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Haroon "Abdullah Haroon") and Bhutto joined it as the vice-chairman of the party.[\[4\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEJalal199428) It was modeled after the [Punjab Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Unionist_Party "Punjab Unionist Party") and claimed to represent all Sindhis irrespective of religion. Nevertheless, Bhutto brought leading _pirs_ (Sufi saints) to influence the voters 'religiously' to cast their votes in his favour.[\[5\]](#ref-ans) The Sind United Party emerged as the largest party in the elections, winning 21 out of 60 seats. However, both Harron and Bhutto failed to get elected. The [Larkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larkana "Larkana") seat, which Bhutto had contested, was won by Sheikh Abdul Majid Sindhi. The Governor invited [Ghulam Hussain Hidayatullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghulam_Hussain_Hidayatullah "Ghulam Hussain Hidayatullah"), the leader of the Sind Muslim Party and a political rival of Bhutto in Sind, to form a government. Large scale defections took place in the Assembly, Bhutto resigned from the party and Haroon eventually merged his party into the [All-India Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League "All-India Muslim League").[\[4\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEJalal199428)[\[6\]](#ref-FOOTNOTESiddiqi201278)[\[7\]](#ref-pk) Bhutto returned to Bombay to become the Chairman of the Bombay-Sind Public Service Commission.[\[3\]](#ref-FOOTNOTEChitkara199617)
Early in 1947, Bhutto joined the council of ministers of [Muhammad Mahabat Khan III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Mahabat_Khan_III "Muhammad Mahabat Khan III") the [Nawab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab "Nawab") of [Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junagadh_State "Junagadh State") in the modern-day province of [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat"), becoming its [Dewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewan "Dewan"), or prime minister in May. At the time of the [independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India") in 1947, the princely states were asked by the British to decide whether to join the newly independent states of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") or [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") or to remain autonomous and outside them. The Constitutional Advisor to the Nawab, Nabi Baksh, indicated to [Lord Mountbatten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mountbatten "Lord Mountbatten") that he was recommending that the State should join India. However, the Nawab did not make a decision. Early in 1947, Bhutto was invited to join the Council of Ministers of the Nawab. In May, when the Dewan Abdul Khadir Muhammad Hussain went abroad for medical treatment, Bhutto was appointed as the Dewan. On 15 August 1947, the State announced that it had acceded to Pakistan. On 13 September 1947, the Government of Pakistan accepted the accession.[\[8\]](#ref-9)

However, the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") citizens of the State revolted which was followed by the [Indian annexation of Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_annexation_of_Junagadh "Indian annexation of Junagadh"), followed by a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite "Plebiscite"),[\[9\]](#ref-10) Nawab [Muhammad Mahabat Khan III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Mahabat_Khan_III "Muhammad Mahabat Khan III") of Junagadh (erstwhile Babi Nawab dynasty of Junagadh) fled to [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), Pakistan.[\[10\]](#ref-11)

Sir Shah Nawaz Bhutto moved to [Larkana District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larkana_District "Larkana District"), where his land-ownership made him among the wealthiest and most influential people in Sindh.[\[11\]](#ref-12) Bhutto was a good friend of [Governor General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Pakistan "Governor-General of Pakistan") (later President) [Iskander Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander_Mirza "Iskander Mirza"), who was a regular guest for the annual hunt in Larkana, staying at the Bhutto family home called _Al-Murtaza_. In the winter of 1955-1956, Mirza brought General [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan_(Field_Marshal) "Ayub Khan (Field Marshal)") with him to Larkana for the hunt.[\[12\]](#ref-Burki,_Shahid_Javed_1991)

## Personal life

Bhutto was a first cousin once removed of [Wahid Baksh Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahid_Baksh_Bhutto "Wahid Baksh Bhutto"), who in 1924 was made a [sardar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar "Sardar") and in 1926 was elected to the [Imperial Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Legislative_Assembly "Imperial Legislative Assembly") from [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), a constituency of the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency "Bombay Presidency"), becoming the first member of the Bhutto family to be elected to public office.

Shah Nawaz Bhutto was married to Khursheed Begum (born as Lakhi Bai), who was of a modest [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_people "Gujarati people") or [Kutchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutchi_people "Kutchi people") [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") family in [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat").[\[13\]](#ref-14) She converted from [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") to [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") before her marriage.[\[12\]](#ref-Burki,_Shahid_Javed_1991) Their children included their first son, Sikandar, who died from [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia") at the age of seven in 1914, their second child, Imdad Ali, died of [cirrhosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirrhosis "Cirrhosis") at the age of thirty-nine in 1953.[\[2\]](#ref-EB) Their third son, [Zulfikar Ali Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulfikar_Ali_Bhutto "Zulfikar Ali Bhutto"), was born in his parents' residence near [Larkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larkana "Larkana"), and later became the [Prime Minister of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Pakistan "Prime Minister of Pakistan"). Their fourth child, a daughter, Mumtaz Sahiba Bhutto, was married to [Brigadier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier "Brigadier") Muhammad Mustafa Khan Bahadur of the Sidi clan.[\[12\]](#ref-Burki,_Shahid_Javed_1991)

## Honours and legacy

The British imperial government awarded Bhutto the title of _[Khan Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Sahib "Khan Sahib")_, subsequently raising it to _[Khan Bahadur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Bahadur "Khan Bahadur")_. Bhutto was appointed an Officer of the [Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire "Order of the British Empire"), Civil Division (OBE) in the [1920 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_New_Year_Honours "1920 New Year Honours") list,[\[14\]](#ref-15) with a further appointment as a [Companion of the Order of the Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Indian_Empire "Order of the Indian Empire") (CIE) in the [1925 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1925_New_Year_Honours "1925 New Year Honours") list.[\[15\]](#ref-16) In the [1930 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_New_Year_Honours "1930 New Year Honours"), Bhutto was knighted,[\[16\]](#ref-17) and was invested with his knighthood on 27 February 1930 at [Viceroy's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy's_House "Viceroy's House") in New Delhi by the [Viceroy of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy_of_India "Viceroy of India"), [Lord Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Wood,_1st_Earl_of_Halifax "Edward Wood, 1st Earl of Halifax").[\[17\]](#ref-18)[\[18\]](#ref-19)

- He was a Delegate to the [Round Table Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Table_Conference "Round Table Conference") in London in 1930-31 as a leader of Sindhi Muslims demanding separation of Sindh from the Bombay Province[\[1\]](#ref-paknetmag)
- [Pakistan Postal Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Postal_Services "Pakistan Postal Services") issued a commemorative postage stamp in his honor in its 'Pioneers of Freedom' series.[\[1\]](#ref-paknetmag)
