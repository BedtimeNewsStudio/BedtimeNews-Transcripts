2I/Borisov

**2I/Borisov**, originally designated **C/2019 Q4 (Borisov)**,[&#8202;](#ref-back-MPEC2019-S72-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-MPEC2019-S72) is the first observed [rogue comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_comet "Rogue comet") and the second observed [interstellar interloper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_interloper "Interstellar interloper"), after [ʻOumuamua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BBOumuamua "ʻOumuamua").[&#8202;](#ref-back-EA-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-EA)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Grossman2019-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-Grossman2019) It was discovered by the [amateur astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_astronomy "Amateur astronomy") and telescope maker [Gennadiy Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennadiy_Borisov "Gennadiy Borisov") on 29August 2019 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC "UTC") (30August [local time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Time "Moscow Time")) in [MARGO Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_observatory_codes "List of observatory codes").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-20191207a-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-NYT-20191207a)[&#8202;](#ref-back-RIANovosti-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-RIANovosti)

2I/Borisov has a [heliocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliocentric "Heliocentric") [orbital eccentricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity "Orbital eccentricity") of 3.36 and is not bound to the Sun.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-NYT) The comet passed through the [ecliptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecliptic "Ecliptic") of the [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System "Solar System") at the end of October2019, and made its closest approach to the Sun at just over 2 [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit "Astronomical unit") on 8December 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jpldata-2)[\[3\]](#refnote-jpldata)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-20191207b-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-NYT-20191207b) The comet passed closest to Earth on 28 December 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guzik2019-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-Guzik2019) In November2019, astronomers from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") said that the comet's tail was 14times the size of Earth, and stated, "It's humbling to realize how small Earth is next to this visitor from another solar system."[&#8202;](#ref-back-YALE-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-YALE)

## Nomenclature

The comet is formally called "2I/Borisov" by the [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union "International Astronomical Union") (IAU), with "2I" or "2I/2019Q4" being its designation and "Borisov" being its name, but is sometimes referred to as "Comet Borisov", especially in the popular press.[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[c\]](#refnote-20) As the second observed interstellar interloper after [1I/ʻOumuamua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BBOumuamua "ʻOumuamua"), it was given the "2I" [designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_of_comets#Current_system "Naming of comets"), where "I" stands for interstellar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Christensen2019-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-Christensen2019)[&#8202;](#ref-back-MPEC2019-S72-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-MPEC2019-S72) The name Borisov follows the tradition of naming comets after their discoverers. Before final designation as 2I/Borisov, the object was referred to by other names:

- Early orbit solutions suggested that the comet could be a [near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object "Near-Earth object") and was thus listed on [IAU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union "International Astronomical Union")'s [Minor Planet Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center "Minor Planet Center")'s (MPC) [Near-Earth Object Confirmation Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_Object_Confirmation_Page "Near-Earth Object Confirmation Page") (NEOCP) as gb00234.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray-NEO-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-Gray-NEO)
- Further refinements after thirteen days of observation made clear the object was a hyperbolic comet, and it was given the designation C/2019Q4 (Borisov) by the Minor Planet Center on 11September 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MPECK19RA6-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-MPECK19RA6) A number of other astronomers including [Davide Farnocchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Farnocchia?action=edit&redlink=1 "Davide Farnocchia"), Bill Gray, and [David Tholen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Tholen "David Tholen") concluded that the comet was interstellar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MPECK19RA6-1)[\[20\]](#refnote-MPECK19RA6)
- On 24September 2019 the IAU announced that the [Working Group for Small Bodies Nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Group_for_Small_Bodies_Nomenclature "Working Group for Small Bodies Nomenclature") kept the name Borisov giving the comet the interstellar designation of 2I/Borisov, formally announcing the comet was indeed interstellar.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MPEC2019-S72-2)[\[9\]](#refnote-MPEC2019-S72)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Christensen2019-1)[\[18\]](#refnote-Christensen2019)

### Size and shape

Early estimates of nucleus 2I/Borisov diameter have ranged from 1.4 to 16km.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NASA-Initial-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-NASA-Initial)[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-28) 2I/Borisov has, unlike Solar System comets, noticeably shrunk during Solar System flyby, losing at least 0.4% of its mass before [perihelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis "Apsis").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hui2020-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-Hui2020) Also, the amplitude of non-gravitational acceleration places an upper limit of 0.4km on nucleus size, consistent with a previous [Hubble Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope "Hubble Space Telescope") upper limit of 0.5km.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jewitt2019b-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-Jewitt2019b) The comet did not come much closer to Earth than 300million kilometres (2.0AU), which prevents using radar to directly determine its size and shape. This could be done using the [occultation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occultation "Occultation") of a star by 2I/Borisov but an occultation would be difficult to predict, requiring a precise determination of its orbit, and the detection would necessitate a network of small telescopes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bamberger2019-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-Bamberger2019)

### Rotation

A study using observations from Hubble could not find a variation in the light curve. According to this study the rotational period must be larger than 10hours.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-30) A study with [CSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Space_Agency "Canadian Space Agency")'s [NEOSSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_Earth_Object_Surveillance_Satellite "Near Earth Object Surveillance Satellite") found a period of 13.2±0.2days, which is unlikely to be the nuclear spin.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-31) [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_method "Monte Carlo method") simulations based on the available orbit determinations suggest that the equatorial obliquity of 2I/Borisov could be about 59degrees or 90degrees; the latter is favored for the latest orbit determination.[&#8202;](#ref-back-rotationpole-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-rotationpole)

### Chemical makeup and nucleus structure

[David Jewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_C._Jewitt "David C. Jewitt") and [Jane Luu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Luu "Jane Luu") estimate from the size of its coma the comet is producing 2kg/s of dust and is losing 60kg/s of water. They extrapolate that it became active in June2019 when it was between 4 and 5AU from the Sun.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jewitt2019b-2)[\[7\]](#refnote-Jewitt2019b) A search of image archives found [precovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precovery "Precovery") observations of 2I/Borisov as early as 13December 2018, but not on 21November 2018, indicating it became active between these dates.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Ye_etal_precovery-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-Ye_etal_precovery)

2I/Borisov's composition appears uncommon yet not unseen in Solar System comets, being relatively depleted in water and [diatomic carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatomic_carbon "Diatomic carbon") (C2), but enriched in [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide "Carbon monoxide") and [amines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amines "Amines") (R-NH2).[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-34) The molar ratio of carbon monoxide to water in 2I/Borisov tail is 35–105%, resembling the unusual blue-tailed comet [C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C/2016_R2_(PanSTARRS) "C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS)") in contrast to the average ratio of 4% for Solar System comets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-35)

2I/Borisov has also produced a minor amount of neutral nickel emission attributed to an unknown volatile compound of nickel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-36) The nickel to iron abundance ratio is similar to Solar System comets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-37)

Interstellar velocity inbound (v∞{\\displaystyle v\_{\\infty }}![{\displaystyle v_{\infty }}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/26145027656115364f96e031bca0c0c11022986e))[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[d\]](#refnote-38)

As seen from Earth, the comet was in the northern sky from September until mid-November. It crossed the ecliptic plane on 26October near the star [Regulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulus "Regulus"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-41) and the [celestial equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_equator "Celestial equator") on 13November 2019, entering the southern sky. On 8December 2019, the comet reached [perihelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis "Apsis") (closest approach to the Sun)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jpldata-3)[\[3\]](#refnote-jpldata) and was near the inner edge of the asteroid belt. In late December, it made its closest approach to Earth,[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-20191207a-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-NYT-20191207a)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-20191207b-1)[\[15\]](#refnote-NYT-20191207b) 1.9AU, and had a solar elongation of about 80°.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray234-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-Gray234) Due to its 44° [orbital inclination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_inclination "Orbital inclination"), 2I/Borisov did not make any notable close approaches to the planets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jpldata-4)[\[3\]](#refnote-jpldata) 2I/Borisov entered the Solar System from the direction of [Cassiopeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiopeia_(constellation) "Cassiopeia (constellation)") near the border with [Perseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perseus_(constellation) "Perseus (constellation)"). This direction indicates that it originates from the [galactic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_plane "Galactic plane"), rather than from the [galactic halo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_halo "Galactic halo").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray-FAQ-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-Gray-FAQ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-deLeón2020-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-deLeón2020) It will leave the Solar System in the direction of [Telescopium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescopium "Telescopium").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray-FAQ-3)[\[6\]](#refnote-Gray-FAQ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-deLeón2020-1)[\[39\]](#refnote-deLeón2020) In interstellar space, 2I/Borisov takes roughly 9,000 years to travel a [light-year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year "Light-year") relative to the Sun.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[e\]](#refnote-44)

2I/Borisov's trajectory is extremely [hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_trajectory "Hyperbolic trajectory"), having an [orbital eccentricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity "Orbital eccentricity") of 3.36.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MPC-DB-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-MPC-DB)[&#8202;](#ref-back-jpldata-5)[\[3\]](#refnote-jpldata) This is much higher than the 300-plus known weakly [hyperbolic comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_comet "Hyperbolic comet"), with heliocentric eccentricities just over 1, and even ʻOumuamua with an eccentricity of 1.2.[&#8202;](#ref-back-JPL-Hyperbolic-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-JPL-Hyperbolic)[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[f\]](#refnote-46) 2I/Borisov also has a [hyperbolic excess velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_excess_velocity "Hyperbolic excess velocity") (v∞{\\displaystyle v\_{\\infty }}![{\displaystyle v_{\infty }}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/26145027656115364f96e031bca0c0c11022986e)) of 32km/s, much higher than what could be explained by perturbations, which could produce velocities when approaching an infinite distance from the Sun of less than a few km/s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Siegel2017-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-Siegel2017) These two parameters are important indicators of 2I/Borisov's interstellar origin.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray-FAQ-4)[\[6\]](#refnote-Gray-FAQ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Atel13100-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-Atel13100) For comparison, the _[Voyager 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_1 "Voyager 1")_ spacecraft, which is leaving the Solar System, is traveling at 16.9km/s (3.57AU/a).[&#8202;](#ref-back-Voyager-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-Voyager) 2I/Borisov has a much larger eccentricity than ʻOumuamua due to its higher excess velocity and its significantly higher perihelion distance. At this larger distance, the Sun's gravity is less able to alter its path as it passes through the Solar System.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray-FAQ-5)[\[6\]](#refnote-Gray-FAQ)

## Observation

### Discovery

The comet was discovered on 30August 2019 by [amateur astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_astronomer "Amateur astronomer") [Gennadiy Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennadiy_Borisov "Gennadiy Borisov") at his personal observatory [MARGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_observatory_codes#L51 "List of observatory codes") in Nauchnij, [Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea "Crimea"), using a 0.65meter telescope he designed and built himself.[&#8202;](#ref-back-S&T2019-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-S&T2019) The discovery has been compared to the discovery of [Pluto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto "Pluto") by [Clyde Tombaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Tombaugh "Clyde Tombaugh").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fontanka-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-Fontanka) Tombaugh was also an amateur astronomer at the time of his discovery, and had built his own telescopes, although he discovered Pluto using [Lowell Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_Observatory "Lowell Observatory")'s 13" [astrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrograph "Astrograph"). At discovery, 2I/Borisov was inbound 3AU from the Sun, 3.7AU from Earth, and had a [solar elongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_elongation "Solar elongation") of 38°.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray-prec-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-Gray-prec) Borisov described his discovery thus:[&#8202;](#ref-back-RIA-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-RIA)

> I observed it on August29, but it was August30 [Greenwich Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Mean_Time "Greenwich Mean Time").[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[g\]](#refnote-56) I saw a moving object in the frame, it moved in a direction that was slightly different from that of [main asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-belt "Main-belt").[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[h\]](#refnote-58) I measured its coordinates and consulted the [Minor Planet Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center "Minor Planet Center") database. Turned out, it was a new object. Then I measured the near-Earth object rating,[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[i\]](#refnote-60) it is calculated from various parameters, and it turned out to be 100%—in other words, [dangerous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentially_hazardous_object "Potentially hazardous object"). In such cases I must immediately post the parameters to the world webpage for confirmation of dangerous asteroids.[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[j\]](#refnote-62) I posted it and wrote that the object was diffuse and that it was not an asteroid, but a comet.

2I/Borisov's interstellar origin required a couple of weeks to confirm. Early orbital solutions based on initial observations included the possibility that the comet could be a near-Earth object 1.4AU from the Sun in an elliptical orbit with an orbital period of less than 1year.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray-NEO-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-Gray-NEO) Later using 151 observations over 12days, [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory "Jet Propulsion Laboratory")'s [Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentry_(monitoring_system)#Scout "Sentry (monitoring system)") gave an eccentricity range of 2.9–4.5.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Scout-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-Scout) But with an [observation arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_arc "Observation arc") of only 12days, there was still some doubt that it was interstellar because the observations were at a low solar elongation, which could introduce biases in the data such as [differential refraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_refraction "Atmospheric refraction"). Using large non-gravitational forces on the highly eccentric orbit, a solution could be generated with an eccentricity of about 1, an Earth [minimum orbit intersection distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_orbit_intersection_distance "Minimum orbit intersection distance") (MOID) of 0.34AU (51millionkm), and a perihelion at 0.90AU around 30December 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray35237-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-Gray35237) However, based on available observations, the orbit could only be parabolic if non-gravitational forces (thrust due to outgassing) affected its orbit more than any previous comets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Gray234c-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-Gray234c) Eventually with more observations the orbit converged to the hyperbolic solution that indicated an interstellar origin and non-gravitational forces could not explain the motion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MPECK19RA6-2)[\[20\]](#refnote-MPECK19RA6)

### Observation

Hubble started observations of 2I/Borisov in October2019, two months prior to the comet's closest approach to the Sun.

The last observations were in September 2020, nine months after perihelion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-jpldata-6)[\[3\]](#refnote-jpldata) Observation of 2I/Borisov was aided by the fact that the comet was detected while inbound towards the Solar System. ʻOumuamua had been discovered as it was leaving the system, and thus could only be observed for 80days before it was out of range. Because of its closest approach occurring near traditional year-end holidays, and the capability to have extended observations, some astronomers have called 2I/Borisov a "Christmas comet".[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-1)[\[14\]](#refnote-NYT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-2)[\[56\]](#refnote-NYT) Observations using the [Hubble Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope "Hubble Space Telescope") began on 12October, when the comet moved far enough from the Sun to be safely observed by the telescope.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hubble100719-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-Hubble100719) With its high angular resolution from above the Earth's atmosphere, Hubble could better separate the nucleus from the coma. The motion of jet-like structures in the coma indicate a 4.3-hour rotation period for the nucleus.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MNRAS-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-MNRAS)

### Comet chemistry

A preliminary (low-resolution) [visible spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_spectroscopy#Optical_spectroscopy "Astronomical spectroscopy") of 2I/Borisov indicated that it was similar to typical [Oort Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oort_Cloud "Oort Cloud") comets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-IAC-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-IAC)[&#8202;](#ref-back-deLeón2019-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-deLeón2019) Its [color indexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_index "Color index") resembled the Solar System's long period comets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jewitt2019b-3)[\[7\]](#refnote-Jewitt2019b) Emissions at 388 [nm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanometre "Nanometre") indicated the presence of [cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanide "Cyanide") ( [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_formula "Chemical formula") CN), which is typically the first molecule detected in Solar System comets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Fitzsimmons2019-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-Fitzsimmons2019) This was the first detection of gas emissions from an interstellar object.[&#8202;](#ref-back-CBAT4670-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-CBAT4670) The non-detection of [diatomic carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatomic_carbon "Diatomic carbon") C2 was reported in October 2019, with the ratio C2 to CN being less than 0.3.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kareta2019-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-Kareta2019)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Opitom2019-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-Opitom2019) Diatomic carbon was positively detected in November 2019, with a measured C2 to CN ratio of 0.2±0.1.[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-75) This resembled carbon-chain depleted comets[&#8202;](#ref-back-Kareta2019-1)[\[63\]](#refnote-Kareta2019) or rare blue-colored carbon monoxide comets like [C/2016 R2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C/2016_R2 "C/2016 R2").[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-76) By the end of November2019, C2 production had dramatically increased, and the C2 to CN ratio reached 0.61, coinciding with the appearance of bright [amine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amine "Amine") (NH2) [bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_band "Spectral band").[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-77) Estimates for the outgassing of water, based on the detection of [atomic oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_oxygen "Atomic oxygen"), indicated a rate similar to Solar System comets.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SA-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-SA)[&#8202;](#ref-back-ARX-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-ARX) In September 2019, neither water nor [hydroxyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxyl_radical "Hydroxyl radical") (OH) lines were directly detected.[&#8202;](#ref-back-80-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-80) The first unambiguous detection of OH lines was made on 1November 2019, and OH production peaked in early December 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-81-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-81)

### Suspected nucleus fragmentation

The comet came within about 2AU of the Sun, a distance at which many small comets have been found to disintegrate. The probability that a comet disintegrates strongly depends on the size of its nucleus; Guzik et al. estimated a probability of 10% that this would happen to 2I/Borisov.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Guzik2019-1)[\[16\]](#refnote-Guzik2019) Jewitt and Luu compared 2I/Borisov to [C/2019 J2 (Palomar)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C/2019_J2_(Palomar) "C/2019 J2 (Palomar)"), another comet of similar size that disintegrated in May2019 at a distance of 1.9AU from the Sun.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jewitt2019b-4)[\[7\]](#refnote-Jewitt2019b)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jewitt2019a-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-Jewitt2019a)

A severe outburst in February–March2020 led to suspected "ongoing nucleus fragmentation" from the comet by 12March.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AT-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-AT) The Hubble Space Telescope can be used to study the fragmentation of small comet nuclei,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Jewitt2019b-5)[\[7\]](#refnote-Jewitt2019b)[&#8202;](#ref-back-STScI-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-STScI) and images taken on 30March 2020 showed a non-stellar core indicating that 2I/Borisov has ejected sunward a large fragment.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AT-1)[\[75\]](#refnote-AT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-AT-2)[\[76\]](#refnote-AT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-SA-1)[\[77\]](#refnote-SA) The ejection is estimated to have begun around 7March,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Bolin_fragmentation-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-Bolin_fragmentation) and may have occurred during one of the outbursts that occurred near that time.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Drahus_outburst-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-Drahus_outburst)

A follow-up study, reported on 6April 2020, observed only a single object, and noted that the fragment had vanished.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AT-3)[\[80\]](#refnote-AT)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NYT-3)[\[81\]](#refnote-NYT) Later analysis of the event showed that the ejected dust and fragments had a combined mass of only about 0.1% of the total mass of the nucleus, making it a large outburst rather than a fragmentation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-92)

## Exploration

The high hyperbolic excess velocity of 2I/Borisov of 32km/s makes it hard for a spacecraft to reach the comet with existing technology: according to a team of the [Initiative for Interstellar Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiative_for_Interstellar_Studies "Initiative for Interstellar Studies"), a 202-kilogram (445lb) spacecraft could theoretically have been sent in July 2018 to intercept 2I/Borisov using a [Falcon Heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_Heavy "Falcon Heavy")-class launcher, or 765kg (1,687lb) on a [Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System "Space Launch System") (SLS)-class booster, but only if the object had been discovered much earlier than it was to meet the optimal launch date. Launches after the actual discovery date would eliminate the possibility to use Falcon Heavy-class rockets, requiring [Oberth maneuvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberth_effect "Oberth effect") near Jupiter and near the Sun and a larger launch vehicle. Even an SLS-class launcher would only have been able to deliver a 3kg (6.6lb) payload (such as a [CubeSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat "CubeSat")) into a trajectory that would intercept 2I/Borisov in 2045 at a relative speed of 34km/s.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Hibberd2019-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-Hibberd2019) According to congressional testimony, NASA may need at least five years of preparation to launch such an [intercepting mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_impact_avoidance#Deflection_efforts "Asteroid impact avoidance").[&#8202;](#ref-back-US-Congress-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-US-Congress)
