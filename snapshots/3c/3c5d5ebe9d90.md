2024 Salvadoran presidential election

Presidential elections were held in [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador "El Salvador") on 4 February 2024. Voters elected the country's [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_El_Salvador "President of El Salvador") and [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_El_Salvador "Vice President of El Salvador") to serve a five-year term. El Salvador used a [two-round system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-round_system "Two-round system"), and if no candidate reached an absolute majority, a second round would have occurred on 3 March 2024. The presidential election occurred concurrently with [legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Salvadoran_legislative_election "2024 Salvadoran legislative election").

Thirteen [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_El_Salvador "List of political parties in El Salvador") were registered with the [Supreme Electoral Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Electoral_Court_(El_Salvador) "Supreme Electoral Court (El Salvador)") (TSE) making them eligible to participate in the election. Of those, only 7 parties ran candidates, of which 6 were on the ballot. Incumbent president [Nayib Bukele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayib_Bukele "Nayib Bukele") ran for re-election with [Nuevas Ideas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevas_Ideas "Nuevas Ideas").[&#8202;](#ref-back-NI_support-0)[\[b\]](#refnote-NI_support) His candidacy was controversial as the [country's constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_El_Salvador "Constitution of El Salvador") prohibited immediate re-election, but in September 2021, the [Supreme Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Justice_of_El_Salvador "Supreme Court of Justice of El Salvador") ruled that the incumbent president could run for immediate re-election. Various lawyers, journalists, and opposition politicians criticized Bukele's re-election bid as [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism "Authoritarianism") and [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality "Constitutionality"), while most Salvadorans remained highly supportive of his campaign. The other five candidates were [Manuel Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Flores_(Salvadoran_politician) "Manuel Flores (Salvadoran politician)") of the [Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farabundo_Mart%C3%AD_National_Liberation_Front "Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front") (FMLN), Joel Sánchez of the [Nationalist Republican Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Republican_Alliance "Nationalist Republican Alliance") (ARENA), [Luis Parada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Parada_(lawyer) "Luis Parada (lawyer)") of [Nuestro Tiempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuestro_Tiempo_(El_Salvador) "Nuestro Tiempo (El Salvador)"), Javier Renderos of [Solidary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidary_Force "Solidary Force"), [Marina Murillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Murillo "Marina Murillo") of the [Salvadoran Patriotic Fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Patriotic_Fraternity "Salvadoran Patriotic Fraternity") (FPS).

On election night, Bukele declared himself the winner of the election. The TSE encountered technical difficulties in the counting process and had to conduct a recount. A few days after election night, the TSE confirmed Bukele's victory. Its final results showed that Bukele won 84.65 percent of the popular vote in a [landslide victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_victory "Landslide victory"). Flores finished in second with 6.40 percent, Sánchez came in third with 5.57 percent, and the remaining candidates each received less than 5 percent. Bukele and Ulloa were inaugurated on 1 June. Bukele was the first president of El Salvador to be re-elected since Brigadier General [Maximiliano Hernández Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximiliano_Hern%C3%A1ndez_Mart%C3%ADnez "Maximiliano Hernández Martínez") in [1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Salvadoran_presidential_election "1944 Salvadoran presidential election").

## Political background

### Presidency of Nayib Bukele

Further information: [2019 Salvadoran presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Salvadoran_presidential_election "2019 Salvadoran presidential election") and [Nayib Bukele §Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayib_Bukele#Presidency "Nayib Bukele")

[![Nayib Bukele standing and speaking at a podium](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/Discurso_de_Bukele_en_2019.jpg/500px-Discurso_de_Bukele_en_2019.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Discurso_de_Bukele_en_2019.jpg) [Nayib Bukele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayib_Bukele "Nayib Bukele") speaking on 1 June 2019, the day of his first inauguration

[Nayib Bukele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayib_Bukele "Nayib Bukele"), a former [mayor of San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_San_Salvador "Mayor of San Salvador"), won the [2019 Salvadoran presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Salvadoran_presidential_election "2019 Salvadoran presidential election") with 53 percent of the vote. He ran under the banner of the [Grand Alliance for National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Alliance_for_National_Unity "Grand Alliance for National Unity") (GANA), making him the first [president of El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_El_Salvador "President of El Salvador") since [José Napoleón Duarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Napole%C3%B3n_Duarte "José Napoleón Duarte") (1984–1989) to not be a member of one of the country's two largest political parties: the right-wing [Nationalist Republican Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Republican_Alliance "Nationalist Republican Alliance") (ARENA) and the left-wing [Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farabundo_Mart%C3%AD_National_Liberation_Front "Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front") (FMLN).[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-8)[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-9) Bukele's election broke the country's [two-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system "Two-party system").[&#8202;](#ref-back-4YDH-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-4YDH)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-11):76

Throughout Bukele's presidency, his government was described as [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism "Authoritarianism") and [autocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy "Autocracy"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-12) resulting in what many journalists described as [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding "Democratic backsliding").[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-13)[&#8202;](#ref-back-RBHZ-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-RBHZ):27 In February 2020, Bukele [sent 40 soldiers into the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Salvadoran_political_crisis "2020 Salvadoran political crisis") to pressure lawmakers to approve a US$109 million loan to fund the [Territorial Control Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Control_Plan "Territorial Control Plan"), a security program initiated by Bukele, in what critics described as an attempted [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_d'%C3%A9tat "Coup d'état").[&#8202;](#ref-back-RBHZ-1)[\[12\]](#refnote-RBHZ):27[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-15) In September 2020, the newspaper _[El Faro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Faro_(digital_newspaper) "El Faro (digital newspaper)")_ accused Bukele's government of negotiating with [criminal gangs in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_El_Salvador#Gangs "Crime in El Salvador"), notably [Mara Salvatrucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS-13 "MS-13") (MS-13) and the [18th Street gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Street_gang "18th Street gang"), to lower crime rates. Bukele and his government denied those accusations.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-16)[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-17) In the [2021 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Salvadoran_legislative_election "2021 Salvadoran legislative election"), [Nuevas Ideas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevas_Ideas "Nuevas Ideas"), the political party Bukele established, won [supermajorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermajority "Supermajority")[&#8202;](#ref-back-AQ-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-AQ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-19) in the [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_El_Salvador "Legislative Assembly of El Salvador"), [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_El_Salvador "Municipalities of El Salvador"), and the [Central American Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Parliament "Central American Parliament") (PARLACEN).[&#8202;](#ref-back-2021L-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-2021L)[&#8202;](#ref-back-2021M-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-2021M)[&#8202;](#ref-back-2021P-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-2021P) The [13th session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_XIII_Legislative_Assembly_of_El_Salvador "List of members of the XIII Legislative Assembly of El Salvador") of the Legislative Assembly assumed office on 1 May 2021[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-23) and Bukele's allies [voted to remove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Salvadoran_political_crisis "2021 Salvadoran political crisis") Attorney General [Raúl Melara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_Melara "Raúl Melara") and five justices from the Constitutional Court of the Supreme Court of Justice. Bukele later appointed a new Attorney General and new justices in what has since been described as a [self-coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-coup "Self-coup").[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-24)

Following a spike in murders in March 2022, Bukele's government began a [gang crackdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_gang_crackdown "Salvadoran gang crackdown"), referred to as a [state of exception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_exception "State of exception") and a war against gangs, which resulted in the reported arrests of over 75,163 alleged gang members by 11 January 2024[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-25) and 144 to 152 deaths in custody by 10 May 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-26) The crackdown, which had since been extended several times, was accused of engaging in [arbitrary arrests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrary_arrest_and_detention "Arbitrary arrest and detention"), [torture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torture "Torture"), and [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_El_Salvador "Human rights in El Salvador") by organizations such as [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International") and [Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch "Human Rights Watch").[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-27)[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-28) Opposition politicians described the crackdown as a political and electoral strategy to support the government and intimidate the opposition.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-29)

[Bukele remained extremely popular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_on_the_Nayib_Bukele_presidency "Opinion polling on the Nayib Bukele presidency") with approval ratings consistently hovering between 80 and 90percent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RBHZ-2)[\[12\]](#refnote-RBHZ):27[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-31) He was considered to be one of the most popular presidents in El Salvador's history[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-32) as well as one of the most popular incumbent [Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America "Latin America") heads of state.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-33)[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-34)

On 3 September 2021, the [Supreme Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Justice_of_El_Salvador "Supreme Court of Justice of El Salvador") ruled that the president of El Salvador is eligible to run for re-election in consecutive terms, discarding a 2014 ruling that required presidents to wait ten years before running for re-election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AJrel2021-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-AJrel2021) The court interpreted the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_El_Salvador "Constitution of El Salvador")'s ban on re-election as only applying to the president and not the candidates.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-37) Additionally, article 156 of the constitution prohibited anyone holding the presidency in the six months prior to the next presidential term from holding the office in the next term. The court interpreted it as permitting the president to seek re-election if they resigned or obtained a leave of office before those six months.[&#8202;](#ref-back-RBHZ-3)[\[12\]](#refnote-RBHZ):29[&#8202;](#ref-back-LPG6months-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-LPG6months) These rulings and interpretations made Bukele eligible to run for president in 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AJrel2021-1)[\[34\]](#refnote-AJrel2021) The [Supreme Electoral Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Electoral_Court_(El_Salvador) "Supreme Electoral Court (El Salvador)") (TSE) accepted the Supreme Court of Justice's ruling despite criticism from ARENA and the FMLN as well as U.S. _chargé d'affaires_ to El Salvador [Jean Elizabeth Manes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Elizabeth_Manes "Jean Elizabeth Manes").[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-39)

On 15 September 2022, Bukele announced his re-election campaign[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):357[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-41) despite having previously voiced his opposition to immediate re-election both before and during his presidency.[&#8202;](#ref-back-42-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-42) Bukele justified his re-election bid by arguing that most [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country "Developed country") allow re-election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-UniRel-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-UniRel) He also claimed that restrictions on re-election only exists in [third-world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World "Third World") countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-44) According to Bukele, a developed country criticizing his announcement would be hypocritical.[&#8202;](#ref-back-UniRel-1)[\[41\]](#refnote-UniRel) On 25 June 2023, Bukele registered his pre-candidacy for the presidency with Nuevas Ideas,[&#8202;](#ref-back-BULPG-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-BULPG) and on 9 July, Nuevas Ideas elected Bukele as its presidential candidate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-46)

[![Street protesters holding a white banner with red text reading "No to Re-Election" in Spanish](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/2023_Salvadoran_protests_%E2%80%93_%22No_a_la_Reelecci%C3%B3n%22_banner.png/500px-2023_Salvadoran_protests_%E2%80%93_%22No_a_la_Reelecci%C3%B3n%22_banner.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2023_Salvadoran_protests_%E2%80%93_%22No_a_la_Reelecci%C3%B3n%22_banner.png) Protesters holding a banner reading "No to Re-Election"

Bukele's announcement that he was running for re-election was criticized by constitutional lawyers who argued his re-election would be [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality "Constitutionality") and violate at least four articles of El Salvador's constitution.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-47)[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-48) [Manuel Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Flores_(Salvadoran_politician) "Manuel Flores (Salvadoran politician)"), then the FMLN's presidential pre-candidate, stated that "the law is the law \[&#8202;](#ref-back-MFrel-0)[and\] the law says that there is no re-election" ("_la ley es la ley \[y\] la ley dice que no hay reelección_"), adding that the constitution is "clear" ("_clara_") in prohibiting re-election.[\[47\]](#refnote-MFrel) [Protest marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Salvadoran_protests "2023 Salvadoran protests") against Bukele's government and against his re-election bid were held in San Salvador on 1 May and 15 September 2023. Flores, Joel Sánchez, and [Luis Parada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Parada_(lawyer) "Luis Parada (lawyer)") (the presidential candidates of the FMLN, ARENA, and [Nuestro Tiempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuestro_Tiempo_(El_Salvador) "Nuestro Tiempo (El Salvador)"), respectively) participated in the latter protest march.[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-50)[&#8202;](#ref-back-15pro-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-15pro)

In October and November 2023, Nuestro Tiempo, ARENA, and various NGOs filed ten requests with the TSE calling for the body to not register Bukele's presidential candidacy or void his candidacy after he initiated the registration process.[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-52) On 9 November, the TSE reaffirmed that Bukele's candidacy was legal and dismissed all the petitions to void his candidacy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-53)

On 30 November, to abide by article 156 of the constitution, Bukele was granted a leave of absence by the Legislative Assembly. [Claudia Rodríguez de Guevara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Rodr%C3%ADguez_de_Guevara "Claudia Rodríguez de Guevara"), Bukele's private secretary, was named as the [presidential designate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_El_Salvador#Succession "President of El Salvador").[&#8202;](#ref-back-LPG6months-1)[\[36\]](#refnote-LPG6months)[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[c\]](#refnote-55) She assumed office the following day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-56)[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-57)[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-58) Rodríguez was the first woman in Salvadoran history to hold presidential powers, although she never held the office of President.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-59)

## Electoral system

### Election procedure

A presidential election was held in El Salvador five years after the 2019 presidential election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-AQ-1)[\[16\]](#refnote-AQ)[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-60) The president and vice president were elected through a [popular vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_election "Direct election") and El Salvador's constitution mandated that the election was "free, direct, equal and [secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_ballot "Secret ballot")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-constitution-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-constitution) The election was held 4 February 2024, concurrent with [legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Salvadoran_legislative_election "2024 Salvadoran legislative election"). If a second round had been necessary, it would have occurred on 3 March.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ElectionDate-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-ElectionDate) To win the election, a candidate needed an [absolute majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority "Majority") (50%+1). If no candidate received an absolute majority, a [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-round_system "Two-round system") between the two candidates with the most valid votes would have occurred within 30 days of the first round.[&#8202;](#ref-back-constitution-1)[\[58\]](#refnote-constitution)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Nohlen-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-Nohlen):272–274 Presidential and vice presidential candidates had to be at least 30 years old and be Salvadoran citizens by birth.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Aspire-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-Aspire)

[Electronic voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voting "Electronic voting") for Salvadoran expatriates began at midnight on 6 January 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-ExpatriateVoters-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-ExpatriateVoters)[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[d\]](#refnote-68) [Expatriates were able to vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_Salvadoran_expatriates_to_vote "Right of Salvadoran expatriates to vote") at 81 voting centers in 60 of the [country's embassies and consulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_El_Salvador "List of diplomatic missions of El Salvador") in 30 countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-69)[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-70) Voting in El Salvador was held from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. [CST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Time_Zone "Central Time Zone").[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-71) A total of 1,595 voting centers were available across the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-72) Voting was not compulsory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-73) There were a total of 6,214,399 registered voters.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-1)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):364 Guillermo Wellman, a magistrate of the TSE, stated that individuals arrested during the country's gang crackdown were ineligible to vote.[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-74)

## Electoral campaign

### Nuevas Ideas

[![A crowd of people in front of the Legislative Assembly of El Salvador holding banners supporting Nayib Bukele and waving to a camera](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Supporters_of_Nayib_Bukele%27s_re-election_%28June_2023%29.jpg/500px-Supporters_of_Nayib_Bukele%27s_re-election_%28June_2023%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Supporters_of_Nayib_Bukele's_re-election_(June_2023).jpg) A crowd of Bukele supporters in front of the Legislative Assembly's meeting room

On 25 June 2023, Bukele and Ulloa registered their pre-candidacies with Nuevas Ideas for President and Vice President, respectively.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-92) On 9 July, Nuevas Ideas formalized Bukele and Ulloa's presidential and vice presidential campaigns.[&#8202;](#ref-back-93-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-93) They ran unopposed in the party's primary election and won 44,398 votes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-94) The TSE approved Bukele and Ulloa's candidacies on 3 November.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NBMF-2)[\[73\]](#refnote-NBMF)

Bukele called on his supporters to win a three-fourths majority for Nuevas Ideas in the concurrent Legislative Assembly election in order to allow him to maintain the gang crackdown during his second term.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BDNM-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-BDNM) Bukele's campaign relied heavily on social media promotion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-3)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):362 In a video message, Bukele claimed that if Nuevas Ideas lost one seat, the opposition would "liberate the gang members and use them to rise to power" ("_liberar a los pandilleros y ocuparlos para llegar al poder_"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-96) although opposition politicians have denied such claims.[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-97)[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-98) In a message posted on social media, Bukele claimed that people who continue to believe in and vote for the opposition "do not see, because they do not want to see; they will not change" ("_no ve, porque no quiere ver; no cambiará_").[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-99) Bukele stated that he was willing to work with the international community as long as they respected El Salvador as a partner rather than as subservient, adding that El Salvador could "change".[&#8202;](#ref-back-BDNM-1)[\[89\]](#refnote-BDNM) Bukele promised to intensify his security policies, improve the country's infrastructure, and promote tourism.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-4)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):360–361

### Nationalist Republican Alliance

Ahead of the ARENA's July 2023 primary elections, Carlos García Saade, the leader of ARENA, stated that the presidential pre-candidates' names would not be disclosed for their safety, claiming that they were threatened by persons in government and by governmental institutions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-100) On 16 July, ARENA elected Joel Sánchez, a businessman living in the United States, and Hilcia Bonilla, a businesswoman living in the United States, as its presidential and vice presidential candidates, respectively.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3candidates-1)[\[74\]](#refnote-3candidates)[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-101) Sánchez's candidacy was proposed by the Citizen Resistance civil movement the month prior.[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-102) Initially, Sánchez's running mate was Hardy García, the wife of [Salvadoran Independent Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Independent_Party "Salvadoran Independent Party") (PAIS) leader Roy García,[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-103) but she withdrew her candidacy because she had received "threats".[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-104) Sánchez's candidacy was independently proposed by the Citizen Resistance civil movement prior to being selected by ARENA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-105)

On 25 July 2023, Sánchez stated that he would be willing to renounce his presidential candidacy if it was what "our country and our population wanted" ("_deseo de nuestro país y de nuestra población_") in order for find a more "suitable" candidate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-106) Following the publication of a [Francisco Gavidia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Gavidia_University "Francisco Gavidia University") (UFG) opinion poll in late-August 2023 which listed Sánchez as having 4.3 percent support, Julia Evelyn Martínez, a former professor at the [Central American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_University,_San_Salvador "Central American University, San Salvador"), stated that Sánchez was considering withdrawing his candidacy due to his low polling figures.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-107) In September 2023, García Saade stated that he believed that Sánchez would be able to win the presidency in the second round.[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-108) On 9 November, the TSE approved Sánchez and Bonilla's candidacies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4cands-4)[\[75\]](#refnote-4cands)

Sánchez stated that the state of exception "has to be stopped" ("_tiene que ser cesado_"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-109) elaborating that it "opens the opportunity to carry out abuses, corruption, \[and\] [influence peddling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influence_peddling "Influence peddling")" ("_abre la oportunidad para llevar a cabo abusos, corrupción, tráfico de influencias_"). Sánchez promised to review economic, taxation, educational, and agricultural policies to determine which ones were succeeding and which were failing. He described the [adoption of bitcoin as legal tender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_in_El_Salvador "Bitcoin in El Salvador") as "the worst decision \[Bukele's\] government could have made" ("_fue la peor decisión que este gobierno pueda haber hecho_"), stating that it resulted in the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") refusing to support El Salvador and the government losing millions of dollars.[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-110) Sánchez and Bonilla stated that they would promote investment by "establishing a judicial system that works" ("_establecer nuestro sistema jurídico que funcione_"), and Sánchez claimed that he was already in talks with potential unspecified investors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-111) Sánchez supported restoring diplomatic relations with [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") as he believes that Taiwan was a "better ally" than [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), citing better exchange student programs and trade deals with the country before relations were severed in 2018. He opposed reducing the number of municipalities,[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[e\]](#refnote-114) arguing that it would result in parts of the country being neglected by mayors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-115)

### Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front

[![A photograph of an FMLN campaign rally](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/FMLN_election_campaign_rally_%282024%29.png/500px-FMLN_election_campaign_rally_%282024%29.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:FMLN_election_campaign_rally_(2024).png) A January 2024 FMLN rally

On 29 May 2023, Flores registered as a pre-candidate for the presidency and Werner Marroquín registered as his running-mate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-116-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-116) Flores was the party's only presidential pre-candidate to register,[&#8202;](#ref-back-117-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-117) although Jaime Zavaleta expressed interest in securing the party's nomination.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Zavaleta-1)[\[83\]](#refnote-Zavaleta) On 16 July 2023, Flores and Marroquín were elected as the FMLN's presidential and vice presidential candidates, respectively.[&#8202;](#ref-back-6primaries-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-6primaries) The TSE approved Flores and Marroquín's candidacies on 3 November.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MFWMreg-1)[\[76\]](#refnote-MFWMreg) FMLN leader [Óscar Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Ortiz_(Salvadoran_politician) "Óscar Ortiz (Salvadoran politician)") assured that the party's candidates represented the "hope and future of the country" ("_esperanza y el futuro del país_").[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-119)

In September 2023, Flores stated that, if elected, he would maintain the state of exception if the country's population supported it.[&#8202;](#ref-back-120-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-120) He also stated that all criminals would remain imprisoned and that his government would pursue others, countering claims that the party defended criminals. Regarding corruption, Flores stated that he would combat corruption and not select corrupt individuals to cabinet positions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-121)

Flores promised to invest US$30 million in the transportation sector and reduce the price of transportation fares for students by half, which would affect around 150,000 students in the country.[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-122) Flores promised to launch the "Zero Hunger" ("_Hambre Cero_") initiative to combat hunger by investing US$60 million into building three "zero-hunger supply centers" ("_centros de abastecimiento hambre cero_") which would distribute grain.[&#8202;](#ref-back-123-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-123) Flores also proposed raising the minimum pension from US$50 per day to US$70 per day.[&#8202;](#ref-back-124-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-124) He announced his "Prosperous Municipalities" ("_Municipios Prósperos_") plan to develop and invest in the country's municipalities.[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-125) Flores promised to launch investigations into all of [Bukele's government officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Nayib_Bukele "Cabinet of Nayib Bukele") if elected president.[&#8202;](#ref-back-126-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-126) In October 2023, Flores stated that he believed he would win the presidential election in the first round, making a second round irrelevant.[&#8202;](#ref-back-127-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-127)

### Nuestro Tiempo

On 31 May 2023, [Luis Parada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Parada_(lawyer) "Luis Parada (lawyer)"), a lawyer who resided in the United States,[&#8202;](#ref-back-128-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-128) announced that he was running for president. His candidacy was proposed by the Sumar por El Salvador political movement. [Celia Medrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celia_Medrano?action=edit&redlink=1 "Celia Medrano")\[ [es](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celia%20Medrano "es:Celia Medrano")\], a human rights lawyer, was Parada's running mate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-129-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-129) The following day, during an interview with [Telecorporación Salvadoreña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecorporaci%C3%B3n_Salvadore%C3%B1a "Telecorporación Salvadoreña")'s _Frente a Frente_, Failer confirmed that Parada and Medrano were both running under the banner of Nuestro Tiempo, adding that "it's to choose between democracy and dictatorship; if we unite ourselves behind this, we will change what is happening to our country" ("_es para elegir entre democracia y dictadura; si nos unimos detrás de eso, vamos a cambiar lo que está pasando en nuestro país_").[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-130) Parada had previously sought ARENA's 2019 presidential nomination.[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-131) On 9 November, the TSE approved Parada and Medrano's candidacies.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4cands-5)[\[75\]](#refnote-4cands)

In an interview with Channel 9 in July 2023, Parada stated that, if elected, he would review the necessity and constitutionality of the state of exception.[&#8202;](#ref-back-132-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-132)[&#8202;](#ref-back-133-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-133) In August 2023, Parada stated that he would suspend the state of exception, describing it as "nothing more than the current government's propaganda" ("_nada más la propaganda del Gobierno actual_"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-134-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-134) and an "illusion".[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-135) Parada promised to remove the justices on the Constitutional Court of the Supreme Court of Justice and restore the justices that the Legislative Assembly removed on 1 May 2021.[&#8202;](#ref-back-136-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-136) Nuestro Tiempo portrayed itself as an alternative to the country's traditional political parties.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-5)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):361

### Salvadoran Independent Party

Gerardo Awad, a former presidential pre-candidate for ARENA in 2019, sought to run a presidential campaign with the Salvadoran Independent Party, but PAIS refused to allow him to run.[&#8202;](#ref-back-137-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-137) Awad later announced that he would no longer seek to run for president with PAIS.[&#8202;](#ref-back-138-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-138) Awad invited [Mario Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Vega_(pastor) "Mario Vega (pastor)"), the senior pastor of [Misión Cristiana Elim Internacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misi%C3%B3n_Cristiana_Elim_Internacional "Misión Cristiana Elim Internacional"), to be his vice presidential candidate, but Vega declined Awad's offer and stated that he did not intend to seek any public office during the 2024 election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Vega-1)[\[85\]](#refnote-Vega) On 4 June 2023, Awad announced his withdrawal from the 2024 election as he did not believe that conditions were favorable for his campaign, but he stated that he would prepare for a new presidential campaign in 2029.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Awad-1)[\[82\]](#refnote-Awad)

On 28 August 2023, PAIS deputy secretary Carlos Molina announced that it selected José Cardoza, an agricultural engineer, as its presidential candidate and Irma Sosa as its vice presidential candidate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PAIScands-1)[\[80\]](#refnote-PAIScands) Roy García, the party's leader, stated on [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") that Molina's announcement was not legitimate and that all of the party's candidates were invalid. He added that the party should wait and reorganize for the [2027 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Salvadoran_legislative_election "2027 Salvadoran legislative election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-139-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-139) On 4 October, the TSE passed a resolution to deregister the party and bar it from participating in the 2024 election, rejecting its candidates in the process.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PAISrej-1)[\[81\]](#refnote-PAISrej) Cardoza denounced the resolution stating that it was undemocratic. Meanwhile, Roy García reiterated that PAIS should wait to participate in the 2027 legislative election as he believed that the party had no chance in winning the 2024 election and was in danger of falling into irrelevance.[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-140)

### Other parties

The [Salvadoran Patriotic Fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Patriotic_Fraternity "Salvadoran Patriotic Fraternity") (FPS) selected [Marina Murillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Murillo "Marina Murillo"), an architect, as its presidential candidate and Fausto Carranza as its vice presidential candidate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FPScand-1)[\[79\]](#refnote-FPScand) Murillo is the first woman to run for president of El Salvador since Rina Escalante ran with the Authentic Democratic Christian Movement in the [1994 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Salvadoran_general_election "1994 Salvadoran general election").[&#8202;](#ref-back-141-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-141) [Solidary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidary_Force "Solidary Force") announced that Javier Renderos, an [obstetrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetrics "Obstetrics"), would be its presidential candidate and that [Rafael Montalvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Montalvo_(politician) "Rafael Montalvo (politician)"), an [agriculturist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculturist "Agriculturist") and former ARENA deputy of the Legislative Assembly, would be its vice presidential candidate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FScand-1)[\[78\]](#refnote-FScand) Both parties' candidates were approved by the TSE on 9 November 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-4cands-6)[\[75\]](#refnote-4cands)

Prior to Bukele's registration as a presidential pre-candidate with Nuevas Ideas on 26 June 2023, some [Grand Alliance for National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Alliance_for_National_Unity "Grand Alliance for National Unity") (GANA) leaders believed that Bukele could seek re-election with GANA rather than Nuevas Ideas as he was initially elected in 2019 as a member of GANA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-142-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-142) In July 2022, [Guillermo Gallegos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Gallegos "Guillermo Gallegos"), a deputy of GANA in the Legislative Assembly, stated that the party would endorse Bukele's re-election campaign if he ran under the banner of another party instead of GANA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-143-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-143) On 16 June 2023, Gallegos stated that GANA would not participate in the presidential election unless Bukele was the party's candidate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-144-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-144) On 26 June, Gallegos confirmed that GANA would not run a presidential or vice presidential candidate, stating that the party supported Bukele's re-election campaign.[&#8202;](#ref-back-GANABRE-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-GANABRE)

On 5 March 2023, [Vamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vamos_(El_Salvador) "Vamos (El Salvador)") formally announced that it would not run a presidential candidate or compete in the PARLACEN elections.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Vel-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-Vel) [Claudia Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Ortiz_(politician) "Claudia Ortiz (politician)") announced her intention to be re-elected as a deputy of the Legislative Assembly and that she would not seek a presidential candidacy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Vno-0)[\[139\]](#refnote-Vno) She later added that winning control of the Legislative Assembly was more important that winning the presidency and that she would fight throughout the next presidential term.[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[140\]](#refnote-147)

In 2023, both the [Christian Democratic Party's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_(El_Salvador) "Christian Democratic Party (El Salvador)") (PDC) and [National Coalition Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_Party_(El_Salvador) "National Coalition Party (El Salvador)") (PCN) announced that neither would field presidential candidates.[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[141\]](#refnote-148)[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[142\]](#refnote-149) On 16 July 2023, the PDC declared its support for Bukele's presidential campaign,[&#8202;](#ref-back-PDCBRE-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-PDCBRE) and the PCN did the same on 30 January 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-PCNBE-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-PCNBE) [Democratic Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Change_(El_Salvador) "Democratic Change (El Salvador)") did not select a presidential candidate, instead endorsing Bukele's re-election campaign.[&#8202;](#ref-back-MGTV-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-MGTV) [Salvadoran Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Democracy "Salvadoran Democracy") did not participate in the 2024 election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-total_cand-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-total_cand)

## Opinion polls

Main article: [Opinion polling for the 2024 Salvadoran elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2024_Salvadoran_elections "Opinion polling for the 2024 Salvadoran elections")

Various groups such as CIESCA, Fundaungo, the Francisco Gavidia University, _[La Prensa Gráfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Prensa_Gr%C3%A1fica "La Prensa Gráfica")_, and TResearch conducted [opinion polling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_poll "Opinion poll") prior to the 2024 presidential election. Virtually every poll indicated significant leads for Bukele.[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-151) Additionally, opinion polling was conducted to ask whether Salvadorans supported Bukele's re-election; virtually every poll indicated that a large majority of Salvadorans would support Bukele's re-election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-152) Many [Salvadoran Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Americans "Salvadoran Americans") also supported Bukele's re-election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-153-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-153) Those who supported Bukele's re-election believed that his continuity in office was necessary to preserve the security policies he implemented. Conversely, opponents of re-election argued that re-election itself was a threat to democracy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-6)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):353

## Results

The TSE published the presidential election's final results on 9 February 2024 and formally ratified the results on 17 February; Bukele (NI) won with 84.65 percent of the vote[&#8202;](#ref-back-Final_Results_P-0)[\[147\]](#refnote-Final_Results_P)[&#8202;](#ref-back-155-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-155) accounting for around 43 percent of all registered voters.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-7)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):360 Bukele was the first presidential candidate in Salvadoran history to receive more than two million votes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-156-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-156) Among the other candidates, Flores (FMLN) received 6.40 percent of the vote, Sánchez (ARENA) received 5.57 percent, Parada (NT) received 2.04 percent, and Renderos (FS) and Murillo (FPS) each received less than 1 percent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Final_Results_P-1)[\[147\]](#refnote-Final_Results_P) Turnout was 52.60 percent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-8)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):366

## Aftermath

### Domestic reactions

[![A crowd of Nayib Bukele's supporters gathered in front of the National Palace awaiting for Bukele give a speech following his victory in the 2024 presidential election.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Nayib_Bukele%27s_supporters_in_front_of_the_National_Palace_%284_February_2024%29.png/500px-Nayib_Bukele%27s_supporters_in_front_of_the_National_Palace_%284_February_2024%29.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nayib_Bukele's_supporters_in_front_of_the_National_Palace_(4_February_2024).png) A crowd of Bukele's supporters in front of the [National Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Palace_(El_Salvador) "National Palace (El Salvador)") on election night

Shortly after polling stations closed but before the TSE officially announced the results of the election, Bukele declared on [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") that, "according to our numbers", he had won the presidential election with "more than 85% of the votes".[&#8202;](#ref-back-157-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-157) He also claimed that his electoral victory was "a record in the entire democratic history of the world"[&#8202;](#ref-back-158-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-158) and that the 2024 election was "the first time in history that El Salvador has democracy, and it's not me saying it, it's the people".[&#8202;](#ref-back-FTresults-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-FTresults) A crowd of Bukele's supporters gathered at the Plaza Gerardo Barrios in San Salvador to celebrate his victory. Bukele later addressed the crowd from the balcony of the [National Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Palace_(El_Salvador) "National Palace (El Salvador)") and celebrated his victory in a speech,[&#8202;](#ref-back-160-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-160)[&#8202;](#ref-back-161-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-161) during which he criticized journalists who themselves had criticized the gang crackdown and his concentration of power.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FTresults-1)[\[152\]](#refnote-FTresults)[&#8202;](#ref-back-162-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-162) Bukele also remarked that the election results was "the first time in a country that just one party exists in a completely democratic system", adding that "the entire opposition together was pulverized".[&#8202;](#ref-back-APresults-0)[\[156\]](#refnote-APresults)

Former FMLN Salvadoran president [Mauricio Funes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Funes "Mauricio Funes") stated that the opposition was defeated "without a doubt" ("_no cabe ninguna duda_").[&#8202;](#ref-back-LNSVMF-0)[\[157\]](#refnote-LNSVMF) In a press conference on 4 February 2024, Flores stated that the FMLN would not disappear as a political entity and that it was "not correct" ("_no es correcto_") for Bukele to declare himself the election's winner before the TSE made an official announcement. Óscar Ortiz thanked those who voted for Flores and stated that "history does not end here" ("_la historia no termina acá_").[&#8202;](#ref-back-165-0)[\[158\]](#refnote-165) Flores did not concede and later claimed that he had votes stolen from him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-FDNC-0)[\[159\]](#refnote-FDNC) On 5 February, Claudia Ortiz stated that "democratic spaces are closing in El Salvador, civil society is closing down and there is an environment of fear to speak out".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rres1-0)[\[160\]](#refnote-Rres1) On 7 February, Sánchez conceded the election and congratulated Bukele on his victory.[&#8202;](#ref-back-168-0)[\[161\]](#refnote-168)

[William H. Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Duncan "William H. On 5 February 2024, several foreign news publications called the presidential election for Bukele, describing it as a " [landslide victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_victory "Landslide victory")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-FTresults-2)[\[152\]](#refnote-FTresults)[&#8202;](#ref-back-180-0)[\[173\]](#refnote-180) Foreign news publications also described Nuevas Ideas' concurrent legislative victory, in which the party won 54 out of 60 seats, as creating a "_[de facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto "De facto")_ one-party state".[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rres1-1)[\[160\]](#refnote-Rres1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-WPres1-0)[\[174\]](#refnote-WPres1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-182-0)[\[175\]](#refnote-182) Tyler Mattiace, a researcher for [Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch "Human Rights Watch"), described the election as a "growing rejection of the basic principles of democracy and human rights, and support for authoritarian populism among people who feel that, concepts like democracy and human rights and due process have failed them".[&#8202;](#ref-back-WPres1-1)[\[174\]](#refnote-WPres1)

### Allegations of irregularities

Shortly after polling stations closed at 5 p.m. CST, Bukele accused the TSE and Indra Solutions of preventing expatriates from casting their vote by not allowing them to cast their vote after the stated time. He argued that people waiting in line before the polling stations closed still had to be allowed to vote and called upon Attorney General [Rodolfo Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Delgado_(lawyer) "Rodolfo Delgado (lawyer)") to launch an investigation. Delgado responded by stating that the office of the attorney general "will prosecute those who committed a crime".[&#8202;](#ref-back-183-0)[\[176\]](#refnote-183) The TSE announced that it would re-open voting at three voting centers in the United States that had not allowed Salvadorans to vote after 5 p.m. CST, however, the TSE later retraced its announcement following a petition made by ARENA.[&#8202;](#ref-back-184-0)[\[177\]](#refnote-184) Bukele responded on X to the TSE's retraction by stating "remember this, when the opposition asks you again for votes in the next elections".[&#8202;](#ref-back-185-0)[\[178\]](#refnote-185)

The TSE released the first preliminary results late on 4 February 2024 but stalled until the morning of 5 February with its website crashing shortly before midnight local time. The TSE attributed the delay to "multiple actions that have hampered the development of the transmission activities of preliminary results" and shortages of paper to print out vote tallies. It advised electoral boards at polling stations to resort to contingency measures such as tallying votes by hand and taking photographs or scans of manual tallies before sending them to the TSE.[&#8202;](#ref-back-APresults-1)[\[156\]](#refnote-APresults) Deputies from ARENA, the FMLN, Vamos, and Nuestro Tiempo criticized the TSE's counting delay.[&#8202;](#ref-back-186-0)[\[179\]](#refnote-186)Nuestro Tiempo claimed that there were inconsistencies between preliminary vote totals published by the TSE in published statements and on its website.[&#8202;](#ref-back-187-0)[\[180\]](#refnote-187) Óscar Picardo, the director of sciences at the Francisco Gavidia University, stated that "we've never seen a situation that affects electoral integrity like this ... it's obvious that Bukele has the most votes, as the polls say, but there are a lot of irregularities in the processing".[&#8202;](#ref-back-FTresults-3)[\[152\]](#refnote-FTresults)

ARENA, the FMLN, Vamos, and Nuestro Tiempo all called for the TSE to launch a [recount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_recount "Election recount").[&#8202;](#ref-back-188-0)[\[181\]](#refnote-188) On 5 February 2024, the TSE stated that it would conduct a recount of 29 percent of the presidential ballots at the [José Adolfo Pineda National Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimnasio_Nacional_Jos%C3%A9_Adolfo_Pineda "Gimnasio Nacional José Adolfo Pineda"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-189-0)[\[182\]](#refnote-189) and that the results would be finalized within 15 days.[&#8202;](#ref-back-190-0)[\[183\]](#refnote-190) The recount began on 7 February[&#8202;](#ref-back-191-0)[\[184\]](#refnote-191) and ended on 9 February.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Final_Results_P-2)[\[147\]](#refnote-Final_Results_P) A similar recount occurred for the concurrent legislative election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-192-0)[\[185\]](#refnote-192)

The [Organization of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_American_States "Organization of American States") (OAS) sent a election observation mission led by [Isabel Saint Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabel_Saint_Malo "Isabel Saint Malo").[&#8202;](#ref-back-193-0)[\[186\]](#refnote-193) Although the OAS voiced concern to the TSE during the recount process regarding the TSE leaving the recount process "in the hands of the representatives of the political parties",[&#8202;](#ref-back-APSPM-0)[\[187\]](#refnote-APSPM) the OAS stated that "the Mission did not observe actions that would indicate that the will of the citizenry expressed at the polls was altered" ("_la Misión no observó hechos que indicaran que se alteró la voluntad de la ciudadanía expresada en las urnas_").[&#8202;](#ref-back-195-0)[\[188\]](#refnote-195) The Interchange and Solidarity Center stated that it had observed irregularities at 69 voting centers both in El Salvador and in the United States.[&#8202;](#ref-back-196-0)[\[189\]](#refnote-196) Eduardo Escobar, a lawyer for the Citizen Action non-governmental organization, stated that there were "surely" irregularities and questioned how much of an impact they had on the final results.[&#8202;](#ref-back-APSPM-1)[\[187\]](#refnote-APSPM)

### Presidential transition

[![A photograph of Nayib Bukele and Gabriela Rodríguez waving from a balcony](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Nayib_Bukele_y_Gabriela_de_Bukele_en_Palacio_Nacional.jpg/500px-Nayib_Bukele_y_Gabriela_de_Bukele_en_Palacio_Nacional.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nayib_Bukele_y_Gabriela_de_Bukele_en_Palacio_Nacional.jpg) Bukele with his wife, [Gabriela Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Rodr%C3%ADguez_de_Bukele "Gabriela Rodríguez de Bukele"), at his second inauguration

The TSE ratified the results of the presidential election on 18 February 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-197-0)[\[190\]](#refnote-197) It granted Bukele and Ulloa their presidential and vice presidential credentials, respectively, on 29 February.[&#8202;](#ref-back-198-0)[\[191\]](#refnote-198) Bukele and Ulloa [were inaugurated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayib_Bukele#Inaugurations "Nayib Bukele") for a second term on 1 June at the Plaza Gerardo Barrios in [San Salvador's historic downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador_Historic_Downtown "San Salvador Historic Downtown").[&#8202;](#ref-back-199-0)[\[192\]](#refnote-199)

Alexis Henríquez, a writer for the Observatory of Political Reforms in Latin America, described the 2024 election as a "watershed" for the Salvadoran political party system due to reforms that enabled Bukele's re-election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Henriquez-9)[\[38\]](#refnote-Henriquez):355 Bukele is the first person since Brigadier General [Maximiliano Hernández Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximiliano_Hern%C3%A1ndez_Mart%C3%ADnez "Maximiliano Hernández Martínez") (in office 1931–1934 and 1935–1944) to be re-elected and serve multiple terms as president.[&#8202;](#ref-back-200-0)[\[193\]](#refnote-200)[&#8202;](#ref-back-201-0)[\[194\]](#refnote-201) Additionally, he was the first president since [Antonio Saca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Saca "Antonio Saca") (served 2004–2009) to seek re-election.[&#8202;](#ref-back-202-0)[\[195\]](#refnote-202)[&#8202;](#ref-back-204-0)[\[f\]](#refnote-204)

Immediately after Bukele's re-election victory, _[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")_ indicated that Bukele may potentially seek to change the constitution and run for a third presidential term in the [next presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Salvadoran_presidential_election "2027 Salvadoran presidential election"), as he had previously stated that the constitution did not "currently" allow for a third presidential term.[&#8202;](#ref-back-205-0)[\[197\]](#refnote-205) In a July 2024 interview with _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_, Bukele stated that he would not seek a third term in 2029.[&#8202;](#ref-back-206-0)[\[198\]](#refnote-206)[&#8202;](#ref-back-208-0)[\[g\]](#refnote-208)
