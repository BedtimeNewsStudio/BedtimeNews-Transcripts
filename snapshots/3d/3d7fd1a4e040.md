**[FSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_production_storage_and_offloading "Floating production storage and offloading") _Safer_** (pronounced "saffer" [/ˈsæfər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"))[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-:0) is a [floating oil storage and offloading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_production_storage_and_offloading "Floating production storage and offloading") vessel that is moored in the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea "Red Sea") north of the Yemeni city of [Al Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah "Al Hudaydah").

Prior to being emptied, the ship held more than 1.14million barrels of oil. Its structure has been left exposed to humidity and corrosion with little or no maintenance since [Yemen's civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_(2014%E2%80%93present) "Yemeni Civil War (2014–present)") started in 2015. An impasse exists between [Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthis "Houthis") groups and the [Saudi-backed government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Yemen "Republic of Yemen") over its ownership and responsibility. Inert gas[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2) on the ship that normally inhibits an explosion had dissipated, putting it at risk of exploding and causing an environmental disaster.[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) The ship is currently undergoing a [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations")-led salvage operation which has successfully removed the oil and will place it in much safer long-term storage.

## History

_Safer_ was built in 1976 by the [Hitachi Zosen Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitachi_Zosen_Corporation "Hitachi Zosen Corporation") in Japan as the oil tanker _Esso Japan_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-msi-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-msi) As built, her [gross tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_tonnage "Gross tonnage") was 192,679 and [deadweight tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadweight_tonnage "Deadweight tonnage") 406,640 tons. She measured 362 metres (1,188ft) in length and her beam was 70 metres (230ft).[&#8202;](#ref-back-msi-1)[\[4\]](#refnote-msi) She was powered by a single [steam turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_turbine "Steam turbine") that gave her a service speed of 15.5 knots (17.8mph).[&#8202;](#ref-back-msi-2)[\[4\]](#refnote-msi)

In 1987, _Esso Japan_ was converted into an unpropelled storage vessel and renamed _Safer_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-msi-3)[\[4\]](#refnote-msi) She was moored about 7 kilometres (4.3mi) off the coast of [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen "Yemen") in 1988 under the ownership of the Yemeni government via the [national oil company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_Oil_and_Gas_Corporation "Yemen Oil and Gas Corporation"), which used her to store and export oil from inland oil fields around [Ma'rib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'rib "Ma'rib").[&#8202;](#ref-back-wmn-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-wmn)[&#8202;](#ref-back-guardian-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-guardian) In her storage configuration, _Safer_ has a capacity of about three million [barrels of oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrels_of_oil "Barrels of oil").[&#8202;](#ref-back-guardian-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-guardian)

## Abandonment

In March 2015, in the early days of the [Yemeni Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_(2015%E2%80%93present) "Yemeni Civil War (2015–present)"), _Safer_ fell into the hands of [Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi "Houthi") forces when they took control of the coastline surrounding her mooring.[&#8202;](#ref-back-guardian-2)[\[6\]](#refnote-guardian) In the following years, her structural condition deteriorated significantly, leading to the risk of a catastrophic hull breach or explosion of oil vapors that would typically be suppressed by inert gas generated on board.[&#8202;](#ref-back-guardian-3)[\[6\]](#refnote-guardian)[&#8202;](#ref-back-wmn-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-wmn) The ship was estimated to contain about 1.14million barrels of oil valued at up to US$80million, which became a point of contention in negotiations between the Houthi rebels and Yemeni government, both of which asserted claims to the cargo and vessel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-aj-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-aj) In early December 2019, Al Jazeera reported that oil had begun to leak from _Safer_,[&#8202;](#ref-back-aj-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-aj) though subsequent satellite imagery showed that the report had been inaccurate and there was no sign of oil outflow from the ship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-9)

Following a leak in the cooling system, water entered the machine room, prompting the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council") to hold a special meeting about it in July 2020.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10)[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11)

On 15 July 2020, the United Nations warned that the FSO _Safer_ could spill four times as much oil as the [_Exxon Valdez_ oil spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exxon_Valdez_oil_spill "Exxon Valdez oil spill").[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

On 24 September 2020, [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia")'s ambassador to the United Nations wrote in a letter that experts had observed that "a pipeline attached to the vessel is suspected to have been separated from the stabilizers holding it to the bottom and is now floating on the surface of the sea."[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-13) In late November, the United Nations and Houthi leadership reached an agreement to allow a UN-led team access to _Safer_ by January 2021 for purposes of inspection and repair.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) The expedition was delayed indefinitely when the Houthis failed to provide a letter assuring the safety of the UN-led team.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15)

As of October 2021, it was being reported that the FSO _Safer_ was at imminent risk of sinking, fire or explosion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16) A massive spill would be disastrous, closing the ports of [Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudaydah "Hudaydah") and [As-Salif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Salif "As-Salif") for weeks, disrupting the food aid on which half the population of the country depends. This could also cause a lack of fuel, necessary for pumping or delivering water, and could disrupt desalination plants in the area. A spill would also shut down the fishing industry on which 1.7million people depend, and could disrupt world trade passing through the Red Sea and the Suez Canal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-10.1038/s41893-021-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-10.1038/s41893-021-00774)[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rahim-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-Rahim) The potential cost of damage has been estimated at $20billion.[&#8202;](#ref-back-f24-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-f24)

On 5 March 2022, [Mohammed al-Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_al-Houthi "Mohammed al-Houthi") signed an agreement with the United Nations to pump the oil still in the decaying tanker into another ship to prevent a potential natural disaster.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-21) The cost of the salvage operation has been estimated at $80–144million.[&#8202;](#ref-back-f24-1)[\[20\]](#refnote-f24) A conference held on 10 May 2022 in the Netherlands raised less than $40million toward the $80million cost of transferring the oil to a temporary storage ship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-22-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-22) The UN launched a crowdfunding campaign, asking members of the public for contributions as well.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)

In September 2022, the UN declared that it has raised sufficient money to carry out the first phase of the operation, and is looking to countries to provide the funds they pledged.[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)

## Salvage

In March 2023, in a statement from [Achim Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achim_Steiner "Achim Steiner"), it was announced that the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme "United Nations Development Programme") (UNDP) had purchased the _Safer_, along with a tanker undergoing refit in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") to begin the first phase of the operation to remove an estimated 1.14million barrels of crude oil. The whole operation is estimated to cost US$129million, of which US$75million had been received, and a further US$20million was pledged.

In April 2023, the [very large crude carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_tanker#VLCC_and_ULCC "Oil tanker") _Nautica_ left China to eventually store all oil offloaded from the _Safer_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26) In May 2023, a United Nations operation was initiated to extract 1.1 million barrels of oil. A specialized salvage vessel, crewed by a team of experts, successfully reached the FSO Safer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27)

On 12 June 2023, the UNDP announced that it had successfully obtained insurance coverage for the salvage operation,[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28) which was necessary in order for it to proceed. A month later, on 15 July 2023, the Netherlands' [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_(Netherlands) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Netherlands)"), which had previously contributed €15 million to the project,[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29) announced that Dutch salvage company [Boskalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boskalis "Boskalis")/ [SMIT Salvage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smit_International "Smit International") had been given the go-ahead to head for the Yemen coast to pump oil from the derelict tanker.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

Removal began around 24 July 2023,[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rahim-1)[\[19\]](#refnote-Rahim) and concluded on 11 August 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32) The operation has not fully concluded yet, since the ship still needs to be towed away and scrapped.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33)[&#8202;](#ref-back-34-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-34) Several companies have contacted the UNDP to express their interest in leading such a scrapping project, though none had been selected yet as of August 2023.[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35) Further work on salvaging the hull has been suspended as of January 2024 due to [Houthi attacks on shipping in the Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_crisis "Red Sea crisis").[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36)
