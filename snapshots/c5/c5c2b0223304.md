# Longest trains - Wikipedia

[Jump to content](#bodyContent)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

This article is about car count or vehicle length. For the trains with the longest routes, see [Longest train services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_train_services "Longest train services").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/SNIM_ore_train_Nouadhibou.jpg/250px-SNIM_ore_train_Nouadhibou.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SNIM_ore_train_Nouadhibou.jpg) [Mauritania Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania_Railway "Mauritania Railway") iron ore train at the station in [Nouadhibou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouadhibou "Nouadhibou").

The length of a [train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train "Train") may be measured in number of [wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_car "Railroad car") (commonly used for bulk commodities such as [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal") and [iron ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_ore "Iron ore")) or in [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metres "Metres") for general freight. On electrified railways, particularly those using lower-voltage systems such as [3 kV DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3000_V_DC "3000 V DC") and [1.5 kV DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1500_V_DC "1500 V DC"), train lengths and loads are often limited by traction and power supply constraints. Other limiting factors include [drawgear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawgear "Drawgear") (coupler) strength, [coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_coupling "Railway coupling") systems, track curvature, gradients, and the lengths of [crossing loops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_loop "Crossing loop") (passing sidings).

The development of [distributed power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_power "Distributed power")—where locomotives are placed mid-train or at the rear of the [consist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consist "Consist") and remotely controlled from the lead unit—has enabled the operation of very long freight trains, sometimes exceeding 6 kilometres (3.7mi; 20,000ft) in length. By distributing traction and braking forces more evenly throughout the train, this configuration allows for longer and heavier consists while reducing the risk of derailment, particularly on curves.

The longest train to date was operated by Transnet in South Africa in 2026 that was 7.602km (4.724mi) long and had 696 wagons.

- >5,000 metres (16,500ft) – [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") – [BNSF Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF_Railway "BNSF Railway") and [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad "Union Pacific Railroad") (UP) regularly operate [intermodal container](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_container "Intermodal container") trains exceeding 5,000 metres (16,500ft) in length on [main lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_line_(railway) "Main line (railway)") in the [western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States "Western United States"). On the UP, these trains can stretch to over 6,100 metres (20,000ft) with 5 locomotives and 280 [well cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_car "Well car").[&#8202;](#ref-back-TrainsMag20240624-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-TrainsMag20240624) Trains longer than 4,000 metres (13,200ft) accounted for approximately 6% of BNSF's and 10% of UP's total train volume as of December2024[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Longest_trains&action=edit). The practice has drawn criticism due to concerns about blocked road crossings, which can cause delays for motorists and impede emergency response times.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-14) Railroads have argued that longer trains improve efficiency, lower emissions, and reduce the number of crossing activations, the most likely time for a collisions between a train and vehicles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) According to the [Association of American Railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Railroads "Association of American Railroads"), fewer than 1% of trains in the United States exceed 4,300 metres (14,000ft) in length.[&#8202;](#ref-back-16-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-16)

Typical consist includes 2 or 3 locomotives, 14 passenger cars and more than 23 [autoracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autorack "Autorack") for transporting vehicles.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-19-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-20)
