Since 2023, many countries and subdivisions across the globe have passed or proposed laws to require [age verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_verification "Age verification") for parts of the internet, including [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") services, [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network "Social network"), devices, and other digital services with the intention of protecting young people from harmful and illegal content. Most of the passed laws or bills have the age verification process done through handing over a government-issued ID, a [facial scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facial_age_estimation "Facial age estimation") or [credit card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card "Credit card") over in order to prove that they are not underage, with the process done with third-party age verification providers.

These laws and proposals vary greatly, with some of them restricting access only to certain features or distinguishing between different users online, which could lead to companies requiring age verification to comply with such restrictions as the [Kids Online Safety Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_Online_Safety_Act "Kids Online Safety Act"), or banning users under a certain age, which is the approach of the [Online Safety Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Amendment_(Social_Media_Minimum_Age)_Act_2024 "Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024") in Australia which fully prohibits anyone under 16 years old from holding social media accounts. A few nations, such as [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), have proposed punishing those who allow minors to access these services.[&#8202;](#ref-back-1-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-2-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-2)[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:14-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-:14)

Despite these laws being introduced mostly for child protection reasons, they have received criticism and backlash from the general public[&#8202;](#ref-back-:22-0)[\[5\]](#refnote-:22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:23-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-:23) and organizations that focus on digital and civil rights for normalizing mass surveillance and restricting freedom of speech through the elimination of anonymous accounts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:24-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-:24) Other organizations such as [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") have opposed these laws on grounds that such laws may backfire, constitute violations of [freedom of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_speech "Freedom of speech") and [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privacy "Privacy") rights, and because there are better ways of protecting children online.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:25-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-:25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:26-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-:26)

These laws have also been bypassed by technologies such as [VPNs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_private_network "Virtual private network"), which have seen a significant amount of new users in countries where such laws are enforced. While these laws typically only apply in their respective countries, some online platforms have made their age verification policies global, such as [Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox "Roblox")[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-10) and [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord "Discord"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-11-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-11) which require age verification for certain features to close said loopholes to avoid receiving a lawsuit.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-12)

## Background

There were attempts to enact online age verification laws before [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia")'s [social media ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Amendment_(Social_Media_Minimum_Age)_Act_2024 "Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024"), such as the [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom")'s [Digital Economy Act 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Economy_Act_2017 "Digital Economy Act 2017"), which was passed on 27 April 2017, yet later went unenforced,[&#8202;](#ref-back-Waterson2019-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-Waterson2019) [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea")'s [shutdown law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shutdown_law "Shutdown law"), which was passed in 2011 and repealed in 2021,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:29-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-:29) and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia")'s [Online Safety Act 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2021 "Online Safety Act 2021"), where age verification was planned but later scrapped.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-15) All of these attempts failed due to free speech concerns, large protests and outcry.

On 26 October 2023, the [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom")'s [Online Safety Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023 "Online Safety Act 2023") was passed and went into effect on 25 July 2025. In 2024, [The Anxious Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Anxious_Generation "The Anxious Generation") was published by [Jonathan Haidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Haidt "Jonathan Haidt"). This book then became the catalyst for [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia")'s [Online Safety Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Amendment_(Social_Media_Minimum_Age)_Act_2024 "Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024"), which outlawed the usage of social media for individuals under 16.[&#8202;](#ref-back-SAProposal-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-SAProposal) As a result of these laws and proposals, age verification laws subsequently started being introduced in other countries.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:30-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-:30)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:31-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-:31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:32-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-:32)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:33-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-:33)

In the United States, legislation regarding social media age verification at the state level began in March 2023, when [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah "Utah") passed the [Utah Social Media Regulation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Social_Media_Regulation_Act "Utah Social Media Regulation Act"). The bills, which were challenged due to [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution") concerns, have not taken effect.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-:5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:0-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-:0)[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-23)[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-24)

## Laws about social media age restrictions

Many of the laws regarding social media age verification revolve around setting a minimum age to have an account on social media platforms, with the youngest proposed minimum age set at 12 by [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago "Trinidad and Tobago") (cf. the minimum age of the [COPPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COPPA "COPPA") law from the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), which is 13) and the oldest being 18 set by both the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe "Zimbabwe"). Neither of these examples gives an exception with parental consent, with most countries (such as the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates"), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey") and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece")) setting the minimum age at either 15 or (in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain")) at 16. Some of the proposed or passed laws that set a minimum age for social media allow exceptions with parental consent, such as those in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil") (set minimum age of 16 with parental consent exception), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark") (set minimum age of 15, parental consent from 13), [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal "Portugal") and the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") (set minimum age of 16 with parental consent from 13).

## Countries and subdivisions with laws in effect

### Australia

Main article: [Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Amendment_(Social_Media_Minimum_Age)_Act_2024 "Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024")

In November 2024, Australia's federal parliament passed the [_Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Amendment_(Social_Media_Minimum_Age)_Act_2024 "Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024"), which requires social media companies such as [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [Snapchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapchat "Snapchat"), [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") and [X (formerly Twitter)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") to take reasonable steps to prohibit minors under the age of 16 from owning accounts. [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") was originally excluded; however, it was later included and companies that do not comply can be fined up to 50million [AUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_dollar "Australian dollar") and is enforced by the [eSafety Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESafety_Commissioner "ESafety Commissioner"). The ban came into effect on 10 December 2025.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:5-1)[\[21\]](#refnote-:5)[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-26)[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-27) [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") then became the first country in the world to ban social media, banning platforms [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube"), [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok"), [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)"), [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram"), [Snapchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapchat "Snapchat"), [Twitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_(service) "Twitch (service)"), [Kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kick_(service) "Kick (service)"), [Threads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threads_(social_network) "Threads (social network)") and [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit") for individuals under 16. This meant that anyone over 16 must verify their age using methods such as facial scan or ID in order to create, log in to or continue using accounts by 10 December 2025.[&#8202;](#ref-back-28-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-28)

On 9 March 2026, Australia became the second country in the world to enforce age verification on social networks such as adult sites, search engines, online platforms like [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") that have allowed harmful content, online chats from [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox "Xbox"), [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation "PlayStation") and even on games such as [GTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto "Grand Theft Auto"). Apps rated 18+ and AI Chatbots that allow explicit material also became affected by this enforced age verification.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-29)

In March 2026, it was reported that over 310,000 accounts owned by children under 16 have been deactivated and blocked by the age filters.[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-30)

On 27 June 2026, the Australian government introduced amendments to strengthen enforcement of its under-16 social media ban, including increasing the maximum penalty for platforms that fail to take reasonable steps to exclude children to 99million [AUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_dollar "Australian dollar") and expanding the age verification to app stores. The proposed changes would also expand the powers of the [eSafety Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESafety_Commissioner "ESafety Commissioner") to compel documents and obtain information from platforms and third-party age verification providers. The amendments were referred to an eight-week Senate inquiry after opposition parties delayed their passage, meaning the increased penalties and expanded powers had not yet taken effect. The proposed changes followed reports that many children continued to access restricted platforms despite the ban, with eSafety reporting that 7 in 10 children still held accounts on restricted platforms when the ban began on services including Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-31)

In August 2026, [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") announced that gamers may have to verify their age to purchase video games on the [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox "Xbox") store rated [R18+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R18+ "R18+"), with the age verification process handled by [Yoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoti "Yoti").[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-32)

### Brazil

In September 2025, [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil") passed the [Digital Statute for Children and Adolescents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Statute_for_Children_and_Adolescents "Digital Statute for Children and Adolescents") that would require social media companies to implement age verification and link accounts under 16 with their parents and only allow children access to content that is intended for them. The law also requires parental consent to download apps for underage individuals aged between 12 and 18 years old and bans [lootboxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loot_box "Loot box") in video games similar to a law enacted in Belgium in 2018, with the exception that it also bans digital rewards, the digital receiving of either virtual or real money, and in-app purchases.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] If platforms do not comply, they can be fined up to 50 million [BRL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_real "Brazilian real").

The law came into effect on 17 March 2026, resulting in Brazil becoming the second country overall, and the first non-English-speaking and Latin American country to ban social media and all online platforms and social networking services, which includes various live-streaming and social media platforms such as [VK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VK_(company) "VK (company)"), [Telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegram_(software) "Telegram (software)"), [Bilibili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili "Bilibili"), among others, for individuals under 16 without parental consent, and forces them to verify their age through ID or facial scans. It also became the third country in the world to enforce age verification on social networks such as adult sites, search engines, online platforms like [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") which allow harmful content, online chats from [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox "Xbox"), [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation "PlayStation"), and even on games such as [GTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto "Grand Theft Auto") and mobile apps rated 18+.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:33-1)[\[20\]](#refnote-:33)[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-33) Due to the law banning lootboxes, digital rewards, in-app purchases, and the digital receiving of virtual or real money for those under 18, many video games and apps such as [Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox "Roblox"), [Minion Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despicable_Me "Despicable Me"), [Mario Kart Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Kart_Tour "Mario Kart Tour"), [EA Sports FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Sports_FC "EA Sports FC"), [Counter Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Strike "Counter-Strike"), [Genshin Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genshin_Impact "Genshin Impact"), [Fortnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite "Fortnite") have restricted in-app purchases or even entire games for non-verified users.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-:16)

China already has its own strict Internet censorship, such as the [Great Firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Firewall "Great Firewall") that blocks certain foreign websites including [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube"), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia "Wikipedia"), and [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google"), as well as the restriction of [VPNs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_private_network "Virtual private network") that bypass these blocks. Laws mandating ID and real-name verification have also been enacted in the country. These laws must be followed in order to create accounts on platforms such as [Bilibili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili "Bilibili"), [Douyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok"), and [Kuaishou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuaishou "Kuaishou").[&#8202;](#ref-back-35-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-35)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] Since 2 August 2023, China has adopted the Youth Anti-Addiction System, also known as [Minor Mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9D%92%E5%B0%91%E5%B9%B4%E6%A8%A1%E5%BC%8F?action=edit&redlink=1 "青少年模式")\[ [zh](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9D%92%E5%B0%91%E5%B9%B4%E6%A8%A1%E5%BC%8F "zh:青少年模式")\], which cuts off internet access for individuals under 18 between 22:00 and 6:00. Time limits include up to 40 minutes for those under 8, 1 hour for those aged 8–15, and 2 hours maximum for 16-17 year olds per day. Minor Mode came into force in January 2024.[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-36) Since September 2021, those under 18 will only be allowed to play online games on weekends and legal holidays between 20:00 and 21:00.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-37)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-38)

In compliance with the [EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union")'s [Digital Service Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Services_Act "Digital Services Act"), and the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland "Republic of Ireland")'s Online Safety Code, the social media platform [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") has taken steps to lock "certain types" of content behind age verification since 28 July 2025. These steps include the assurance of one's age based on extant indications such as account creation dates, legacy verification and prior acts of age verification. Alternatively, when these indications do not exist, email addresses and social connections are used to estimate age. The platform has also planned two user-involved "secure, privacy-respecting" options that will give one a choice of either taking a live selfie or submitting a government ID, both of which will estimate their age. These measures are expected to arrive in the coming weeks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-39-0)[\[39\]](#refnote-39) [Vimeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimeo "Vimeo") has also blocked users in the European Union from accessing certain features or types of content not suitable for younger audiences without age verification in September 2025, in order to protect these younger audiences from accessing "potentially harmful" content.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-40)

In January 2026, [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") announced plans to introduce age verification technology in the European Union within the coming weeks, with its system being based on the analysis of behavioural signals, profile information, and posted videos, in order to better comply with regional regulations and to better distinguish and ban accounts belonging to those under 13. Flagged accounts, rather than being spontaneously banned upon detection, will be reviewed by specialist moderators. Ban appeals will be done by an age-estimating facial scan, provided by digital identification service company [Yoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoti "Yoti"), as well as the presentation of one's credit card and government ID.[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-41)

In July 2026, [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit") blocked European users under the age of 18 from accessing posts and subreddits marked as [NSFW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_safe_for_work "Not safe for work"), as well as preventing users between the ages of 13 to 17 from using certain features such as profile visibility and followers. For those aged 13 to 15, restrictions on these aforementioned features are "locked to their most protective configuration" and cannot be changed. Those aged 16 to 17 have the same restrictions placed on them by default; however these individuals are able to manually modify these restrictions via account settings. Ad personalisation and chat are locked by default for every account that is in the age range of 13 to 17. If age cannot be defined via "existing signals", there are two ways for users to verify their age. One way involves the submission of a government ID or a self-taken photograph for age-estimation through [Persona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_(identity_verification_service) "Persona (identity verification service)"), while the other requires the confirmation of one's age through an [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") or [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") account.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] That same month, a [European Citizens' Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Citizens'_Initiative "European Citizens' Initiative") called “Stop Killing the Internet: No Digital ID & No Age Verification” was registered by the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission "European Commission"). The Initiative is similar to the [Stop Killing Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_Killing_Games "Stop Killing Games") movement, which has also criticised these age verification laws along with it,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-:13) and has demanded anonymous or pseudonymous age verification and the minimisation of data collection, as well as advocating for legislation for citizens to identity themselves only when absolutely necessary. Organisers of Stop Killing the Internet called for legislation that ensures “voluntary, privacy-friendly, and non-discriminatory” age verification and digital identification systems.[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-43)[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-44)

### Indonesia

In early 2025, the government of Indonesia had been looking into the idea of setting a minimum age to use social media as well as other protections that would be similar to Australia's. This proposal had been met with support and caution by the public.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-45)[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-46)[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-47)

On 28 March 2026, Indonesia banned social media for children under the age of 16, becoming the first non-Western, Asian and Muslim country and the third country in the world to enforce a social media ban. Platforms such as [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube"), [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok"), [X (formerly known as Twitter)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)"), [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram"), [Threads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threads_(social_network) "Threads (social network)"), [Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox "Roblox") and [Bigo Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bigo_Live "Bigo Live") were the first to be banned with measures similar to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"). Enforcement and account deactivation will be rolled out gradually, affecting all accounts that are reported to be under 16.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:30-1)[\[17\]](#refnote-:30)

As of July 2026, around 600,000 YouTube accounts and 4.1 million TikTok accounts, or a total of 4.7 million social media accounts combined that are reportedly owned by children under 16 have been terminated in Indonesia.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-48)

### Malaysia

In November 2025, the [Malaysian Communications Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Communications_(Malaysia) "Minister of Communications (Malaysia)") [Fahmi Fadzil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahmi_Fadzil "Fahmi Fadzil") announced that starting 1 January 2026, all social media platforms would have to ban all users under 16 years of age and implement age verification via eKYC, saying this was in line with the country's Online Safety Act, which was enacted in 2025 and took effect the same day the ban does. The effective date was later changed to 1 June 2026 from 1 January 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-49-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-49)[&#8202;](#ref-back-50-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-50)[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-51) [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia") then became the second Asian and Muslim country, and fourth country in the world to ban social media and online platforms that have more than 8 million users for individuals under 16 on 1 June 2026, with measures also similar to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"). Targeted platforms with over 8 million users included platforms such as [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube"), [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok"), [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") and [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram").[&#8202;](#ref-back-52-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-52)

### Republic of Ireland

The Online Safety Code is a law in force in the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland "Republic of Ireland"). The law addresses video content, which includes audiovisual programmes, user generated video, audiovisual commercial communications, and integral user-generated content, which include examples such as comments and comments associated with video content. The law also requires the protection of children from adult-only video content, which include pornography and excessive violence, video and audiovisual commercial content that can impair their physical, mental, and moral health, and audiovisual commercial content that can directly persuade children strongly to buy or hire a product or service via exploiting their inexperience and naïveté, encourage children to convince their parents or other adults to purchase advertised goods or services, exploit trust placed in parents, teachers, or others, and unreasonably show children in dangerous situations, and the general public to be protected from content that incites hatred and violence, encourages terrorism, racism, or xenophobia, disseminates illegal material such as child sexual abuse material, and promotes bullying, harassment, prejudice, and behaviour characterising eating or feeding disorders. The Online Safety Code was developed so that video-sharing platform service (VSPS) providers would take action to protect children and the general public, as well as in response to a report regarding the harm of video-sharing platform services, the PA Harms Report, with one of the measures including providing content rating, age verification and or parental control systems.[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-53)

### Singapore

On 1 April 2026, app stores in the country started being required by the [Infocomm Media Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infocomm_Media_Development_Authority "Infocomm Media Development Authority") ( [IMDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infocomm_Media_Development_Authority "Infocomm Media Development Authority")) to prevent users estimated to be under 18 years of age from downloading apps with inappropriate content. App stores do this by checking a user's government ID or using facial scans. This made Singapore the second country to enforce age verification on app stores, with targeted companies including [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc."), [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google"), [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft"), [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei "Huawei") and [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung "Samsung").[&#8202;](#ref-back-54-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-54)

### Turkey

Turkey's [Grand National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey "Grand National Assembly of Turkey"), along with the [country's president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkey "President of Turkey"), [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan "Recep Tayyip Erdoğan"), has been discussing a draft law that would ban social media for minors under 15 and provide parental controls for minors who are 15 to 17, as well as require social media platforms to implement age and identity verification to prevent minors under 15 from becoming users of social media. Along with this, the draft law proposes making it easier to remove problematic content and implementing measures to make bypassing the restrictions with a VPN harder. The draft law has already been accepted by the commission and now must pass the [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey "Grand National Assembly of Turkey"). The Grand National Assembly passed the law in April 2026,[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-55) and was published in the [Official Gazette of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resmi_Gazete "Resmi Gazete") on 1 May 2026, after which, it would take effect six months after publication.[&#8202;](#ref-back-56-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-56)[&#8202;](#ref-back-57-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-57)[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-58)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-:12)

The law took effect on 1 November.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:12-1)[\[59\]](#refnote-:12) Turkey then became the first country in the world to ban social media use for children under 15 and the third country in Asia to ban platforms such as YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and TikTok. Age verification is required for making new accounts after this date, while existing social media accounts are not affected until 31 March 2027.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-60) The age verification process is done with [e-Devlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-Devlet "E-Devlet").[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-61)

### United Kingdom

Main articles: [Online Safety Act 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023 "Online Safety Act 2023") and [Online age verification in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_age_verification_in_the_United_Kingdom "Online age verification in the United Kingdom")

The United Kingdom became the first country in the world on 25 July 2025 to enforce age verification under the [Online Safety Act 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023 "Online Safety Act 2023"), which although does not set an age limit for social media, requires providers of "user-to-user" services (which includes many popular social media services) to prevent access by children of content deemed harmful, including but not limited to pornographic content, self-harm, suicide, or eating disorders. Platforms including [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-62) [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-63) [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord "Discord"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-64) [Vimeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimeo "Vimeo"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-65) [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify "Spotify"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-66) and [Bluesky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluesky "Bluesky")[&#8202;](#ref-back-67-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-67) have implemented age verification for UK users for harmful and sensitive content as a result.

The UK became the first country in the world to expand age verification to social networking platforms and services such as adult sites, search engines, online platforms like [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") that have allowed inappropriate content, online chats from [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox "Xbox"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-68) [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation "PlayStation"),[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-69) and even online games such as [GTA Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_Online "Grand Theft Auto Online").[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-70) "Vile and illegal content created by AI" also became a target of this enforced age verification, which also included a ban on sexualised images without one's consent.[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-71)

On 17 June 2026, [Keir Starmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keir_Starmer "Keir Starmer") announced that the [Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Wellbeing_and_Schools_Act_2026 "Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026") has passed, which puts a ban on children under 16 having accounts and accessing social media apps such as [Snapchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapchat "Snapchat"), [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok"), [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram"), [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") and [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube"), effective from March 2027, due to these apps allowing underaged users to be exposed to sexual abuse, infinite scrolling, dangerous trends, blackmail, and bullying.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-72) This ban has been described as an “Australia Plus” ban, as it, just like its [Australian counterpart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Amendment_(Social_Media_Minimum_Age)_Act_2024 "Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024"), targets those under 16. A curfew was also proposed that voluntarily limits access to these affected social media platforms for those aged between 16 and 17 between 00:00 and 06:00, similar to the South Korean [shutdown law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shutdown_law "Shutdown law"). The ban also blocks strangers from talking to minors by default and blocks livestreams for those under 16, in order to "prevent a cliff-edge at 16," as said by the government.[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-73)[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-74)[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-75)

In September 2026, the communications regulator [Ofcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofcom "Ofcom") launched an investigation into [Aylo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aylo "Aylo"), the parent company of the adult video-sharing platform [Pornhub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornhub "Pornhub"), over whether or not the site has prevented children from viewing pornographic and adult content. Ofcom expressed concern that Aylo “may not have conducted sufficient due diligence and testing” before enforcing its age assurance process on Pornhub, which was implemented in May and is reliant on the age verification technology of [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc."). Ofcom has stated that it will not be investigating Apple on how it operates its age checks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-76)[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-77)

#### Bailiwick of Guernsey

In December 2025, the Committee for Home Affairs reviewed the application of age verification laws to locally accessed adult sites in the Crown Dependency. A spokesman for the Committee has stated that it was “reviewing the online safety bill and will be considering what elements might be appropriate to extend to Guernsey”. The President of the Committee for Home Affairs, Marc Leadbeater, has expressed support for banning the social media app [Snapchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapchat "Snapchat") for individuals under 16.[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-78)

In August 2026, campaigners from organisations within Guernsey and the rest of the United Kingdom proposed suggestions on how the Crown Dependency could safeguard children from accessing online content that is harmful. These suggestions included the simultaneous operation of two different networks, one network for adults and the other designated as “child-safe”, and not allowing children to have possession of mobile phones that are “internet-capable”. Deputy Sally Rochester has noted the lack of discussion regarding online safety laws in Guernsey, stating: “I find it interesting with the online protection we provide our young people with, that it is not more of a conversation in our community here in Guernsey”. Cyber security consultant Daniel Card expressed concerns that if online safety laws similar to those implemented in the UK were introduced to Guernsey, they could still be circumvented via the usage of [VPNs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_private_network "Virtual private network"). Emma Lawlor, a campaigner for the organisation “Smartphone Free Childhood Guernsey” has called for a “brick phone” (i.e. a phone that is not “internet-capable) policy as an alternative to age verification and expressed concerns over the lack of enforcement of online safety laws, saying: “We do not feel putting effort into age-restrictions, curfews or VPNs is really the way to go — with any of these things, enforcement is a massive issue”.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:11-0)[\[79\]](#refnote-:11)

#### Jersey

In November 2025, the [Economic Development Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers "Council of Ministers") of Jersey, [Kirsten Morel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Morel "Kirsten Morel"), announced that legislation allowing people of all ages to remove “harmful content” was “in progress” inside the government and being drafted, in response to a report that revealed that there was no age verification in the [Crown Dependency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependency "Crown Dependency"). A speech was made by deputy Catherine Curtis on 11 November, where she verified the lack of age verification laws in Jersey, stating: “we checked whether they were also in place in Jersey and they were not”.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-:7) As of September 2026, yet more information is yet to be provided on this proposed law.

### United States (state level)

There is no online age verification law that has been enacted at the federal level in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). Many states have enacted their own laws; however, most have been pending, have not yet, or have never taken effect. Notable exceptions to this are [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida"), [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi "Mississippi"), [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee"), and [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:18-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-:18)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-:1)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:19-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-:19)

Some platforms such as [Pornhub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornhub "Pornhub") and [Newgrounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newgrounds "Newgrounds") have blocked entire website access for states where age verification laws are enforced.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

[Florida's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida") law took effect on November 25, 2025, after the [11th Circuit Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Eleventh_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit") ruled 2-1 to overturn a preliminary injunction blocking its enforcement.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-:6) Florida's law bans social media for minors under 14 and require parental consent for minors aged 14 or 15. Florida would enforce its law against TikTok on June 15, 2026, after which TikTok would announce it would suspend accounts from users under 14 in the State of Florida. Florida then became the first state in United States to enforce a social media ban for minors.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:6-1)[\[85\]](#refnote-:6)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:17-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-:17)[&#8202;](#ref-back-87-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-87) In October 2026, the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals ruled in _[Computer & Communications Industry Association v. Uthmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_&_Communications_Industry_Association_v._Uthmeier "Computer & Communications Industry Association v. Uthmeier")_ that Florida's restrictions on minors' social media accounts likely satisfied [intermediate scrutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_scrutiny "Intermediate scrutiny") under the [First Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "First Amendment to the United States Constitution"). The court reversed a [preliminary injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injunction "Injunction") and remanded the case for further proceedings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-88)[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-89)

[Mississippi's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi "Mississippi") law took effect on July 17, 2025, after the [5th Circuit Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Fifth_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit") allowed the law to take effect pending appeal. However, an explanation was not given why. The law, known as the [Walker Montgomery Protecting Children Online Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_Montgomery_Protecting_Children_Online_Act "Walker Montgomery Protecting Children Online Act"), requires parental consent for users under 18 and requires platforms to prevent minors from view certain types of content. [Bluesky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluesky "Bluesky") announced in August 2025 that they would block users in Mississippi due to the law.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:3-1)[\[82\]](#refnote-:3)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:4-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-:4)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:2-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-:2)

[Ohio's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio") law, known as the [Social Media Parental Notification Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Media_Parental_Notification_Act "Social Media Parental Notification Act"), would take effect on June 18, 2026, after the [6th Circuit Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Sixth_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit") would rule 2-1 that the law was constitutional. The law requires parental consent for users under 16.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:19-1)[\[84\]](#refnote-:19)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:20-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-:20)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:21-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-:21)

The law in [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee"), known as the [Protecting Kids From Social Media Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protecting_Kids_From_Social_Media_Act "Protecting Kids From Social Media Act"), took effect on January 1, 2025. A judge for the [U.S District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Middle_District_of_Tennessee "United States District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee"), [Eli J. Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_J._Richardson "Eli J. Richardson"), would allow the law to take effect on June 18, 2025, because [NetChoice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NetChoice "NetChoice"), the ones challenging the law, did not show evidence that the law would be enforced against them. The law requires parental consent for users under 18.[&#8202;](#ref-back-wbir.com-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-wbir.com)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:1-1)[\[83\]](#refnote-:1)

A law in [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"), known as the App Store Accountability Act or SB 2420, came into effect on June 4, 2026 after the federal injunction that blocked it was lifted. This law requires age verification for creating [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") accounts after it came into effect, with the age verification done through methods such as handing over a government-issued ID or a credit card. [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas") then became the first state in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") to enforce age verification onto app stores.[&#8202;](#ref-back-95-0)[\[95\]](#refnote-95)

## Effect on platforms, companies and organizations

### Discord

Despite many lawsuits in recent years for failing to protect children from predators online as well as [moderation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_moderation "Content moderation") failures, since early 2024, [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord "Discord") began to blur out sensitive and harmful content. If anyone wants to unblur these types of content, they can click the eye icon to reveal said blurred content. However, since July 2025, Discord has locked the aforementioned sensitive and harmful content, including inappropriate material, behind age verification via ID for those residing in the [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") to comply with the [Online Safety Act 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023 "Online Safety Act 2023").[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[96\]](#refnote-96) This age verification measure then rolled out in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") by 10 December 2025, then in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil") by 17 March 2026, which included a ban for individuals under 16 without parental consent.

In late 2025, Discord had expanded age verification to include direct messages (DMs) from people who are not on one's friends list, which are automatically locked and sent to a separate "Message Requests" folder, of which this restriction cannot be disabled or moved to the main inbox without age verification and are restricted from speaking on Stage Channels in public servers without age verification.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In February 2026, Discord announced its teen safety protections, which would be rolled out to its user base worldwide. These protections make it impossible to bypass using bypassing mechanisms such as VPNs and will include [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube")-style automated age detection to flag user behaviour as "teen-appropriate", similar to methods employed by [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram"). Originally, these protections were supposed to come into force by March,[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[97\]](#refnote-97) but due to backlash over recent leaks of 70,000 user ID photos that happened in September 2025,[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[98\]](#refnote-98) it was delayed to the second half of 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-99-0)[\[99\]](#refnote-99) The teen safety protections went into effect on 23 September 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-100-0)[\[100\]](#refnote-100)

### Google

The Play Age Signals API for [Google Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Play "Google Play") started returning age signals for users in Brazil as a result of their age verification law taking effect on 17 March 2026. Age signals have also been returned to users in the U.S. state of [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"), more specifically to those "eligible users" who have created a Google Play account after May 28, 2026, in order to comply with SB 2420, also known as the App Store Accountability Act.[&#8202;](#ref-back-101-0)[\[101\]](#refnote-101)

### Linux distributions

[Garuda Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda_Linux "Garuda Linux"), in their March 2026 statement, condemned the Californian and Brazilian laws that mandate age verification on the [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system "Operating system") level. They asserted they would not implement OS-level age verification, claiming that their servers and donation fund repository are not based in jurisdictions that mandate OS-level age verification. TNE, a team member of Garuda Linux, further claimed that some of their maintainers had faced personal attacks for their contributions and could face the threats of either outstanding fines or jail time if they ignore court orders. [Arch Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_Linux "Arch Linux") restricted their Brazilian users' access in response to the _[Digital Statute for Children and Adolescents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Statute_for_Children_and_Adolescents "Digital Statute for Children and Adolescents")_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-102-0)[\[102\]](#refnote-102)

A Linux distribution known as [Ageless Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageless_Linux "Ageless Linux") was released that same month as a protest against online age verification laws in the United States. It added to the debate among Linux developers about the risks of the laws on privacy and data security.[&#8202;](#ref-back-103-0)[\[103\]](#refnote-103)

### Meta Platforms

In May 2026, [Meta Platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms"), which owns various social media services such as [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"), [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram"), [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp"), [Threads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threads_(social_network) "Threads (social network)") and [Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Messenger "Facebook Messenger"), announced plans to roll out age verification to users suspected too young to use services such as the previously mentioned Facebook and Instagram.[&#8202;](#ref-back-104-0)[\[104\]](#refnote-104) According to Secretary Henry Aguda of the Philippine [Department of Information and Communications Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Information_and_Communications_Technology "Department of Information and Communications Technology"), Meta promised to implement age and identity verification using [Philippine national identity card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_national_identity_card "Philippine national identity card") if the earlier implementation on Roblox worked. Aguda claimed they "understood" the "technical complexity" because it would be the first time the tech company would integrate a [sovereign national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_identity_card_policies_by_country "List of national identity card policies by country") as a prerequisite in using their services. Meta asserted that they might require same means of identity verification in 170 other jurisdictions. Aguda said the tech company would bring their "technical working team" in the country to study the proposal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-105-0)[\[105\]](#refnote-105)

### PEGI

On 12 March 2026, due to the passage of many laws across the globe regarding the age restriction of [lootboxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loot_box "Loot box"), in-app purchases and simulated gambling features, such as [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") raising the age rating for video games featuring lootboxes to have an M rating, in other words being only recommended to those 15 and older and assigning a R 18+ rating for video games featuring simulated gambling on 22 September 2024,[&#8202;](#ref-back-106-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-106) as well as [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil") passing the [Digital Statute for Children and Adolescents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Statute_for_Children_and_Adolescents "Digital Statute for Children and Adolescents"), also known as the Felca Law in September 2025, which outlaws lootboxes for individuals under 18,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:16-1)[\[34\]](#refnote-:16) the [Pan European Game Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGI "PEGI"), or PEGI for short, a video game rating system based in Europe, introduced "interactive risk categories", designed to address predatory monetization and engagement mechanics in video games, which raises the age rating of games to have a PEGI 16 rating for containing lootboxes or other "paid random items", such as card packs, [gacha systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gacha_game "Gacha game"), and prize wheels, and a PEGI 18 rating for having purchase systems involving [NFTs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-fungible_token "Non-fungible token") or other mechanisms associated with [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain "Blockchain") and "unrestricted communication features". Games will also receive a PEGI 12 rating for [FOMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_of_missing_out "Fear of missing out") mechanics and a PEGI 7 or 12 rating for engagement retention. This system came into force by June 2026 and will only apply to newer games like [EA Sports FC 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Sports_FC_27 "EA Sports FC 27"). After June 2026, older games that have already been rated before this change, such as [Fortnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite "Fortnite"), [Genshin Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genshin_Impact "Genshin Impact"), [Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox "Roblox"), and [Apex Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apex_Legends "Apex Legends"), will be initially excluded. However, if these games make certain changes, an example being the introduction of lootboxes, they may be subject to re-rating.[&#8202;](#ref-back-107-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-107)[&#8202;](#ref-back-108-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-108)

### Roblox

See also: [Child safety on Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_safety_on_Roblox "Child safety on Roblox")

Despite many lawsuits in recent years for failing to protect children from predators online as well as [moderation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_moderation "Content moderation") failures leading to numerous countries imposing restrictions or outright banning the platform, such as Turkey in August 2024,[&#8202;](#ref-back-109-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-109)[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-110) [Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox "Roblox") has recently added a plethora of safety features. However, despite these additions, countries are still pursuing investigations against it. On 19 November 2025, Roblox announced that it would require users to verify their age to be able to use the chat in-game. This system places users into age groups, and players can only chat with people in neighboring age groups. Roblox's age verification is done by scanning a user's face to estimate their age, or having them upload a picture of their ID. Both methods are done with [Persona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_(identity_verification_service) "Persona (identity verification service)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-111)[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-112)[&#8202;](#ref-back-113-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-113)

In June 2026, the platform introduced more age-based restrictions by dividing user accounts into different tiers: Roblox Kids, which is assigned to users under the age of 9, Roblox Select, which is assigned to users age 9 to 15, and standard Roblox, which is assigned to users 16 and above. If a user does not verify their age, they are restricted to only playing games with an age rating of 9+ or lower.[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-114)[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-115) It was also recently announced that, along with a number of other restrictions, age verification will be required to publish a game on the platform.

Citing high risk, [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") restricted the platform for users under 16 under its social media ban on 28 March 2026,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:10-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-:10) and [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil") restricted the platform for users under 16 on 17 March 2026. Formerly, Brazil restricted the platform to users under 18 due to the country's ban on [lootboxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loot_box "Loot box") for users under 18; however, after the platform announced it would regulate lootboxes, Brazil lowered the limit to 16.[&#8202;](#ref-back-117-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-117) Roblox has a deadline in October to implement the age verification system for the use of the platform in the Philippines through the use of the Philippine national identity card.[&#8202;](#ref-back-118-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-118)

### Wikimedia Foundation

With respect to the [Online Safety Act 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023 "Online Safety Act 2023") of the United Kingdom, the [Wikimedia Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation "Wikimedia Foundation") argued that age verification would "violate our commitment to collect minimal data about readers and contributors" and that they might pull out from the UK, disabling access from the UK readers in the process.[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[119\]](#refnote-119) The [High Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice "High Court of Justice") in 2025 rejected the foundation's legal challenge against the law. Nevertheless, Judge [Jeremy Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Johnson_(judge) "Jeremy Johnson (judge)") asserted that this rejection "does not give [Ofcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofcom "Ofcom") and the Secretary of State a green light to implement a regime that would significantly impede Wikipedia's operations".[&#8202;](#ref-back-120-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-120) The foundation narrowly escaped the "Category 1" service classification under the law on July 10, 2026, though the regulator asserted that Wikipedia remained on the "watch list", which the foundation claimed as "an existential threat".[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-121)

The organization also called for "balancing of legal measures" with regards to the _Digital Statute for Children and Adolescents_ of Brazil, asserting the need for "exceptions" to "online encyclopedias, educational services, and other public interest platforms". They claimed "mandatory age verification poses significant risks to human rights, including privacy, freedom of expression, and access to reliable information".[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-122)

## Africa

### Egypt

In January 2026, [Egypt's parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Egypt "Parliament of Egypt") debated legislation discussing children's use of social media. ### Gabon

On 8 April 2026, [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon "Gabon") published an ordinance regulating social media platforms. Among the requirements would be that social media platforms would have to implement age verification and set a digital age of majority of 16 and platforms could only allow minors under 16 to have an account with parental consent. The age verification process, unlike that of many other countries, would require any person who wants to join a platform to provide their name, address, and personal identification number; this goes further than the requirements of other countries, which only require obtaining the verified age of a person.[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-131)[&#8202;](#ref-back-132-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-132)[&#8202;](#ref-back-133-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-133)

### Kenya

In May 2025, the [Communication Authority of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_Authority_of_Kenya "Communications Authority of Kenya") published guidelines on online safety for children. One of the requirements of these guidelines was to require Application Service Providers (ASP) and Content Service Providers (CSP) to have age verification to restrict harmful content. Alongside this, the [Kenya Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Kenya "Parliament of Kenya") introduced the Kenya Information and Communications (Amendment) Bill, 2025, which requires age verification for social media services such as [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") and [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp "WhatsApp"). However, as of August 2025, the bill proposed by the Parliament has not passed and the status of the guidelines by the Communications Authority remains unclear.[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-135)[&#8202;](#ref-back-136-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-136)

On 17 February 2026, Kenyan members of parliament ruled out a ban on [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") due to the platform providing communication, creativity, and entrepreneurship among youth. The proposed ban also infringed on fundamental rights, specifically the freedom of speech, and stifled economic growth. This led to the country resorting to regulating the app instead, with two of its ministers expecting to report to the House on how to strengthen [age verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_verification "Age verification"), as well as [data localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_localization "Data localization") and [media literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_literacy "Media literacy") on privacy and responsible use of social media.[&#8202;](#ref-back-137-0)[\[137\]](#refnote-137)[&#8202;](#ref-back-138-0)[\[138\]](#refnote-138)

### Bahrain

Since January 2026, [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain "Bahrain") has been considering prohibiting social media accounts for users younger than 15 within the country and would require platforms to put certain safeguards in place for children between 15 and 17 years old to help limit exposure to harmful content. ### India

In 2025, the ZEP Foundation petitioned the [Supreme Court of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India "Supreme Court of India") for social media regulation. The court denied the request to ban children under 13 from social media saying it was something for the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India") to do and not the courts. However, the foundation's request to propose to the Central Government that it require age verification for social media and prohibit usage for those under 16 or 18 years old was granted and given an 8-week deadline; however as of September 2025, no laws have passed that resemble what the petition requested in India.[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-148)[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-149)[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-150)

On 31 January 2026, an ally of the [Indian Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India") [Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi "Narendra Modi"), lawmaker L.S.K. Devarayalu, proposed a bill to ban social media for children under 16. The bill is called the "Social Media (Age Restrictions and Online Safety) Bill", and states that no person under the age of 16 "shall be permitted to create, maintain, or hold" a social media account. Those persons in violation of this bill (i.e., those who have been discovered to have possession of an account) should have their accounts disabled. Devarayalu states that "Not only are our children becoming addicted to social media, but India is also one of the world's most largest producers of data for foreign platforms".[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-151)

#### Andhra Pradesh

The state's IT minister, [Nara Lokesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Lokesh "Nara Lokesh"), had been leading a group of ministers to oversee this draft, and has called for legislation that "balances digital access with child safety, creativity and mental well being".[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-152)

#### Goa

#### Karnataka

### Pakistan

In July 2025, a bill was introduced in the [Pakistan Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Pakistan "Senate of Pakistan") that would ban social media for minors under 16 and require platforms affected by the bill to implement age verification. ### Philippines

See also: [Social media use in the Philippines §Regulation proposals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media_use_in_the_Philippines#Regulation_proposals "Social media use in the Philippines")

Calls to introduce a social media ban for minors emerged after the deadly [2026 Tacloban school shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tacloban_school_shooting "2026 Tacloban school shooting"). Legislators also proposed mandatory age verification for "violent and mature-rated online games".[&#8202;](#ref-back-168-0)[\[168\]](#refnote-168) Renewed support came after the [2026 Ateneo de Zamboanga University shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ateneo_de_Zamboanga_University_shooting "2026 Ateneo de Zamboanga University shooting"), which resulted in the deaths of an underaged victim and the underaged suspect. There were also calls to ban children from online games and websites "that promote graphic, violent, and distressing crime-related content, including the [True Crime Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Crime_Community "True Crime Community")".[&#8202;](#ref-back-169-0)[\[169\]](#refnote-169)

#### Legislative measures

The [Senate of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Philippines "Senate of the Philippines") approved Senate Bill No. 2424 on October 8, 2026, also known as the _Safe Media Access and Responsible Technology for Kids in Digital Spaces (SMART KIDS) Act_. Sponsored by Senator [Robin Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Padilla "Robin Padilla"), it aims to prohibit everyone below the age of 18 from using social media. It also mandates all international social media platforms to cooperate with the government in protecting children from harmful content and to appoint a local representative to deal with these content. Senator [Joel Villanueva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Villanueva "Joel Villanueva") voiced his support for the bill, stating that "protection should extend to the screens, where a single click can expose the children to bullying, violence, and exploitation". Senator [Risa Hontiveros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risa_Hontiveros "Risa Hontiveros"), the only senator who voted against the bill, denounced the bill's very broad scope of age restrictions and suggested "tiered implementation for social media access". She argued that adolescents aged 13 and 15 "have different levels of development".[&#8202;](#ref-back-170-0)[\[170\]](#refnote-170)[&#8202;](#ref-back-171-0)[\[171\]](#refnote-171)

The [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_the_Philippines "House of Representatives of the Philippines") approved their counterpart measure, House Bill 11566 or the _Safe and Friendly Environment for Kids in Digital Spaces (SAFE KIDS) Act_, on October 12, 2026. This version prohibits everyone aged 12 and below from having social media and online gaming accounts while allowing the adolescents below 18 years old to maintain social media accounts but subject to restrictions. Restricted features not permitted for all adolescents include "harmful or prohibited content, behavioral profiling, unnecessary collection or sharing of personal information, purchases, gambling-like mechanisms, targeted advertising, recommendation algorithms, and endless scrolling". The bill proposes all covered online platforms to conduct "Child Safety Risk Assessments".[&#8202;](#ref-back-172-0)[\[172\]](#refnote-172)[&#8202;](#ref-back-173-0)[\[173\]](#refnote-173) The bill also covers application stores and operating systems, and demands the digital platforms to: establish a local office in the country and appoint a "compliance officer" for complaints involving content not appropriate for children.[&#8202;](#ref-back-174-0)[\[174\]](#refnote-174)

#### Related discourse

Senator [Panfilo Lacson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panfilo_Lacson "Panfilo Lacson") proposed the mandatory use of the [Philippine national identity card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_national_identity_card "Philippine national identity card") or "a backup ID" to verify the ages of social media users. DICT outlined "two non-negotiable demands" for Meta to continue their social media presence in the country: an actual local office in the country and mandating identity verification using the national identity card. ### South Korea

## Europe

### European Union (social media ban)

On 26 November 2025, the [EU Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Parliament "EU Parliament") voted on a non-legislative report by a vote of 483–92–86. The report would set a minimum age of 16 for social media, while allowing those aged 13 to 15 with parental consent. The report also bans things such as infinite scrolling and auto-play, and would protect minors from commercial exploitation. It would also prohibit access to services that do not comply with the law within the [EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union").[&#8202;](#ref-back-193-0)[\[193\]](#refnote-193)[&#8202;](#ref-back-194-0)[\[194\]](#refnote-194)[&#8202;](#ref-back-195-0)[\[195\]](#refnote-195)

On 13 July 2026, European Commission President [Ursula von der Leyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_von_der_Leyen "Ursula von der Leyen") officially pledged a bloc-wide push to limit children's access to social media, in which individuals under 13 will be restricted from using it, saying: "It is clear we need age-appropriate restrictions to platforms". A panel led by German psychiatrist Jörg Fregert and French epidemiologist Maria Melchor called for a delay of these restrictions to "social media plus", which refers to other online platforms using similar features, such as video games or AI chatbots. Two investigations against [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") and [Meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms"), the parent company of [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") and [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram"), highlighted several problematic features such as video autoplay, endless scrolling, and aggressive push notifications.[&#8202;](#ref-back-196-0)[\[196\]](#refnote-196)

On 13 September 2026, the [EU Kids Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Kids_Act "EU Kids Act") was introduced to the EU Parliament. The proposed regulation would outlaw the ownership of a personal account on video-sharing services, social media, online games, and AI chatbots for those under 13; accounts for these individuals are able to be created by their parents or guardians, who have full control over these accounts. For those aged 13 to 15, the creation of "introductory" accounts for those aforementioned services are possible. However, these accounts are subject to contact limits with strangers. Screen time and parental controls also apply to these "introductory" accounts. From those aged 15 onwards, children are able to start their own accounts. The regulation would also require age verification technology on video-sharing and social media platforms, in order to verify the ages of users. The EU Kids Act is to be enforced in a similar fashion to the [Digital Services Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Services_Act "Digital Services Act") and the [Artificial Intelligence Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Act "Artificial Intelligence Act"), where companies were to pay supervisory fees.[&#8202;](#ref-back-197-0)[\[197\]](#refnote-197)

## North America

### United States

#### Kids Online Safety Act

The [Kids Online Safety Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_Online_Safety_Act "Kids Online Safety Act"), also known as KOSA, is a bill in the [United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") that requires online platforms, not just social media companies, that are likely to be used by children to reduce [addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_addiction_disorder "Internet addiction disorder") and online harms to them through a duty of care. However, the bill has been criticized by the general public, as well as multiple civil rights groups such as the [ACLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union "American Civil Liberties Union") and [EFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation "Electronic Frontier Foundation"), for potentially leading to online censorship on services using age verification.[&#8202;](#ref-back-249-0)[\[249\]](#refnote-249)[&#8202;](#ref-back-250-0)[\[250\]](#refnote-250)[&#8202;](#ref-back-251-0)[\[251\]](#refnote-251)[&#8202;](#ref-back-252-0)[\[252\]](#refnote-252)

#### Kids Off Social Media Act

The [Kids Off Social Media Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_Off_Social_Media_Act "Kids Off Social Media Act") is a bill that was introduced originally in April 2024, but died before advancing. It was later reintroduced in January 2025 and advanced out of committee in February 2025; as of June 2025, the bill has been placed on the general calendar, meaning it can be taken up for a vote at any time. The bill bans anyone under 13 from having a social media account and bans certain [algorithmic recommendations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_recommendations "Algorithmic recommendations") for anyone under 17; those opposed to it, such as the [ACLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union "American Civil Liberties Union") and [Centers for Democracy and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Democracy_and_Technology "Center for Democracy and Technology"), have warned that the bill could lead social media platforms into doing age verification via government ID to avoid liability.[&#8202;](#ref-back-253-0)[\[253\]](#refnote-253)[&#8202;](#ref-back-254-0)[\[254\]](#refnote-254)[&#8202;](#ref-back-255-0)[\[255\]](#refnote-255)[&#8202;](#ref-back-256-0)[\[256\]](#refnote-256)[&#8202;](#ref-back-257-0)[\[257\]](#refnote-257)

#### Protecting Kids on Social Media Act

In April 2023, the Protecting Kids on Social Media Act was introduced, and it, like the Kids Off Social Media Act, bans social media services for children under 13, and also bans [algorithmic recommendations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_recommendations "Algorithmic recommendations"); but instead of banning it for those under 17, it bans it for all minors under 18 and requires parental consent to use social media if the person creating the account is 13–17 years old. The bill was opposed by the Electronic Frontier Foundation because the age verification part of the bill would invade privacy rights and would make it easier for the government to identify people and because the parental consent provision could be problematic for older minors; the bill has since died and has not been reintroduced.[&#8202;](#ref-back-258-0)[\[258\]](#refnote-258)[&#8202;](#ref-back-259-0)[\[259\]](#refnote-259)[&#8202;](#ref-back-260-0)[\[260\]](#refnote-260)

#### Kids Internet and Digital Safety Act

In March 2026, the Kids Internet and Digital Safety Act, also known as the [KIDS Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KIDS_Act "KIDS Act"), was introduced.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-0)[\[261\]](#refnote-:15) It includes parts of legislation such as the [Kids Online Safety Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_Online_Safety_Act "Kids Online Safety Act"), the SCREEN Act, SAFE BOTS Act, [COPPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Online_Privacy_Protection_Act "Children's Online Privacy Protection Act") 2.0, the SPY Kids Act, and more.[&#8202;](#ref-back-262-0)[\[262\]](#refnote-262) Notably, it removed provisions from the Kids Online Safety Act that required a [duty of care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty_of_care "Duty of care") system, but would still require large-scale age verification across social platforms.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:15-1)[\[261\]](#refnote-:15)

The bill passed the House of Representatives on June 29, 2026, with 267 in support and 117 against. Though it is meant to ensure [child safety on social networking websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_safety_on_social_networking_websites "Child safety on social networking websites"), advocates such as the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation "Electronic Frontier Foundation") argue that systems' reliance on [government-issued identification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_ID "Government ID") or biometric age estimation could chill anonymous speech by discouraging users from sharing sensitive information online.[&#8202;](#ref-back-263-0)[\[263\]](#refnote-263)

### New Zealand

In inspiration of Australia's Online Safety Amendment, the [National Party of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_National_Party "New Zealand National Party"), along with the [Prime Minister of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_New_Zealand "Prime Minister of New Zealand"), [Christopher Luxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Luxon "Christopher Luxon"), introduced the [Social Media (Age-Restricted Users) Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Media_(Age-Restricted_Users)_Bill "Social Media (Age-Restricted Users) Bill") in May 2025, which, just like its Australian counterpart, bans social media accounts for children under 16, with the maximum fine that can be imposed being 2 million [NZD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_dollar "New Zealand dollar").[&#8202;](#ref-back-284-0)[\[284\]](#refnote-284)[&#8202;](#ref-back-285-0)[\[285\]](#refnote-285)[&#8202;](#ref-back-286-0)[\[286\]](#refnote-286) This bill has been placed on a pause following consideration of wide regulation change.[&#8202;](#ref-back-287-0)[\[287\]](#refnote-287)

On 24 August 2026, Christopher Luxon announced that social media use for children under 16 would be banned. Alongside this, the Online Safety (Minimum Age and Child Safety Risk Assessment) Bill was introduced to the House of Representatives the same day. Non-compliance penalties would total up to 10% of a company's global revenue. However, it has faced opposition from [ACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_New_Zealand "ACT New Zealand") and [New Zealand First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_First "New Zealand First").[&#8202;](#ref-back-288-0)[\[288\]](#refnote-288)

## Countries that have rejected online age verification laws

### Asia

#### Hong Kong

In July 2026, the [Secretary for Innovation, Technology and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_for_Innovation,_Technology_and_Industry "Secretary for Innovation, Technology and Industry"), [Dong Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Sun "Dong Sun"), announced that the government currently does not have any plans to forbid the usage of social media by underaged individuals via law, stating that any action implemented to restrict access to the internet or content online would need to consider multiple aspects, which include the impact of said content and access on the social and psychological development of young persons, the local ecosystem of innovation and technology, and public expectations regarding information's free flow, and that any unconditional restriction or ban would also need to take into account the feasibility of enforcing it. He further said that even when online safety laws regarding children are in effect, the implementation of said laws remains difficult due to factors such as cross-border jurisdiction and age verification, and that matters such as this require deep public discussion and a general accord before the drafting of specific restrictions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-289-0)[\[289\]](#refnote-289)

### Europe

#### Croatia

In March 2026, the [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Parliament "Croatian Parliament") club [We Can!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Can!_(Croatia) "We Can! (Croatia)") requested that access to social networks be restricted for those under the age of 15. According to their proposal, access to social media would be allowed to those under the age of 15 only with the consent of a parent or guardian, although their access would still be restricted between 10 p.m. and 8 a.m. The [Minister of Justice, Public Administration and Digital Transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice,_Public_Administration_and_Digital_Transformation_(Croatia) "Ministry of Justice, Public Administration and Digital Transformation (Croatia)"), [Damir Habijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damir_Habijan "Damir Habijan"), rejected the proposal, saying that the issue of the technical implementation of such a ban has not yet been resolved.[&#8202;](#ref-back-290-0)[\[290\]](#refnote-290)

#### France

On 29 June 2023, [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") passed a law which would require parental consent for anyone under 15 to use social media; however, the bill passed without a clear effective date, stating that they would apply it as soon as possible. If the bill does go into effect, then companies would be fined up to one percent of their revenue if they do not comply with the law.[&#8202;](#ref-back-291-0)[\[291\]](#refnote-291)[&#8202;](#ref-back-292-0)[\[292\]](#refnote-292)[&#8202;](#ref-back-293-0)[\[293\]](#refnote-293) Since this law has passed, the [President of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_France "President of France"), [Emmanuel Macron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Macron "Emmanuel Macron"), has said he could look into a complete ban for anyone under 15 to use social media.[&#8202;](#ref-back-294-0)[\[294\]](#refnote-294)[&#8202;](#ref-back-295-0)[\[295\]](#refnote-295)

In September 2025, during the policy development, some lawmakers discussed implementing "digital negligence," which would fine parents if they fail to supervise their children online, making France the second country to propose criminalising access to social media for children after the failure of a similar proposal in Pakistan.[&#8202;](#ref-back-296-0)[\[296\]](#refnote-296)

In January 2026, the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_(France) "National Assembly (France)") of France approved a government-backed bill banning the use of social media by children under the age of 15. The legislation, supported by President Macron's administration, was adopted by 116 votes to 23 and aims to "protect minors' mental health by mitigating risks such as online harassment and psychological harm". The law requires social media platforms to implement age-verification mechanisms, while allowing exemptions for educational resources such as online encyclopedias.[&#8202;](#ref-back-297-0)[\[297\]](#refnote-297)[&#8202;](#ref-back-298-0)[\[298\]](#refnote-298)[&#8202;](#ref-back-299-0)[\[299\]](#refnote-299)[&#8202;](#ref-back-300-0)[\[300\]](#refnote-300)[&#8202;](#ref-back-301-0)[\[301\]](#refnote-301) This proposed legislation was championed by President Macron and was passed by the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_(France) "Senate (France)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-:9-0)[\[302\]](#refnote-:9)

In July 2026, the [French Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Parliament "French Parliament") approved the law, and the ban would have taken effect in two stages, with the first stage requiring age verification for creating new accounts and taking effect in September 2026, whilst the second stage would have applied age verification to existing social media accounts and would have taken effect in January 2027.[&#8202;](#ref-back-303-0)[\[303\]](#refnote-303)

On 14 August 2026, this law was [struck down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_2026-911_(France) "Decision 2026-911 (France)") by the [Constitutional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Council_(France) "Constitutional Council (France)") because it "constitutes a restriction that is not appropriate, necessary or proportionate".[&#8202;](#ref-back-304-0)[\[304\]](#refnote-304)

#### Estonia

However, she does not support the idea of implementing age verification, saying that it could affect everyone and could easily be bypassed by young people; instead, she supports enforcing existing EU Laws and better education for young people and parents.[&#8202;](#ref-back-305-0)[\[305\]](#refnote-305)[&#8202;](#ref-back-306-0)[\[306\]](#refnote-306)[&#8202;](#ref-back-307-0)[\[307\]](#refnote-307)

#### Italy

The [Italian Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Italy "Prime Minister of Italy"), [Giorgia Meloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgia_Meloni "Giorgia Meloni"), has said that her government would not introduce a social media ban for children, specifically for those under the age of 16, saying that such bans "can be easily circumvented", further stating a social media ban may risk "to transfer the problem on families" and can be ineffective unless governments pressure social media platforms to intervene and "take their responsibilities".[&#8202;](#ref-back-308-0)[\[308\]](#refnote-308)

## Reception

### Support

A survey conducted by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") between May 26 and June 1, 2026 showed 78% of US adults favored mandating users of all ages to verify their ages before using social media. It increased from 71% in 2023. Support was highest among 50- to 64-year-old US adults, and lowest among 18- to 29-year-old US adults, at 84% and 66%, respectively. Among US adults, 83% of the parents of a child under 18 support age verification measures, compared to 76% of US adults with no children aged under 18.[&#8202;](#ref-back-309-0)[\[309\]](#refnote-309) An opinion poll conducted among adults in the United Kingdom by [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov "YouGov") in 2025 found that 39% strongly support, 35% somewhat support and 19% oppose banning social media use for children under the age of 16.[&#8202;](#ref-back-310-0)[\[310\]](#refnote-310) In a poll conducted by [Léger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9ger_(company) "Léger (company)"), 83% of Canadians showed concern about social media’s potentially negative impact, while 81% were concerned about AI chatbots such as [Claude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_(AI) "Claude (AI)"), [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT"), and [Copilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Copilot "Microsoft Copilot"). Regarding a ban on social media, 32% of Canadians said that social media should be banned for children under 16, while 29% said that social media should be banned for those under 17. Only 5% said that social media should not be age-restricted. Seven out of ten, or 70% of people in Canada, supported a ban on social media for those under 16, which included 44% who strongly support a ban of this type. Banning AI chatbots garnered a similar level of support, with 69% of Canadians supporting a ban, which included 41% who strongly support such a ban.[&#8202;](#ref-back-311-0)[\[311\]](#refnote-311)

Politicians on the [Crown Dependency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependencies "Crown Dependencies") of [Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey "Jersey") in the United Kingdom have expressed concern for the safety of children after a review revealed that there was no age verification on adult sites in the Crown Dependency. The review, conducted by the Children, Education and Home Affairs Scrutiny Panel, found that claims that regulations in the UK would protect children indirectly were not entirely certain, meaning that children on the island “may now face fewer barriers to accessing inappropriate content than their UK counterparts”. Deputy Catherine Curtis stated that evidence in the review demonstrated that children at a young age will acquire usage of said inappropriate content due to their curiosity, further stating that one in ten children would have accessed pornographic sites for adults by the time they reach the age of nine.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:7-1)[\[80\]](#refnote-:7)

The Philippine Council for the Welfare of Children asserted that it is crucial to implement age verification checks to restrict minors from social media platforms, since they must be shielded from harmful online content "during a stage where children are still developing judgment and resilience". They added that it is a "shared responsibility" to protect them while they navigate the digital space.[&#8202;](#ref-back-312-0)[\[312\]](#refnote-312) A supportive editorial by the Philippine newspaper _[Manila Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Standard "Manila Standard")_ asserted the age verification proposals "shift responsibility away from overwhelmed parents and toward the corporations that profit from children's engagement" and "restore authority to parents and guardians who have long been outpaced by the speed and opacity of tech platforms". It claimed the self-regulation approach being practised by social media platforms has "repeatedly failed to protect minors".[&#8202;](#ref-back-313-0)[\[313\]](#refnote-313)

Philippine cybersecurity watchdog Scam Watch Pilipinas urged the formation of an " [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") Big Tech Accountability Framework" as a "united front" for the [ASEAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN "ASEAN") region that would compel the [Big Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Tech "Big Tech") to enforce accountability by "addressing harmful content and scams and strengthening protection for children and vulnerable users". The coalition's lead convenor Jocel de Guzman cited the European Union's success in compelling the online platforms to comply with regional laws through both the [Digital Services Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Services_Act "Digital Services Act") and the [Digital Markets Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Markets_Act "Digital Markets Act"). He claimed that if the platforms wanted access to "nearly 700 million ASEAN consumers", the regional bloc "must also have the power to demand accountability from them".[&#8202;](#ref-back-314-0)[\[314\]](#refnote-314)

### Criticism and concerns

The online age verification laws and proposals have received criticism and severe backlash from the general public,[&#8202;](#ref-back-:22-1)[\[5\]](#refnote-:22)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:23-1)[\[6\]](#refnote-:23) as well as organisations that focus on digital and civil rights (such as the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation "Electronic Frontier Foundation"), [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union "American Civil Liberties Union") and [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House "Freedom House") in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States")) for normalising mass surveillance and restricting freedom of speech through the elimination of anonymous accounts.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:24-1)[\[7\]](#refnote-:24)[&#8202;](#ref-back-315-0)[\[315\]](#refnote-315) The New Zealand Council for Civil Liberties, [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF"), and the [Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe_Commissioner_for_Human_Rights "Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights") have also opposed such laws on the basis that they constitute violations of the rights to [freedom of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_speech "Freedom of speech") and [privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_privacy "Right to privacy"), that such laws may backfire, and that there are better ways of protecting children online.[&#8202;](#ref-back-316-0)[\[316\]](#refnote-316)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:25-1)[\[8\]](#refnote-:25)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:26-1)[\[9\]](#refnote-:26)[&#8202;](#ref-back-:27-0)[\[317\]](#refnote-:27)

These laws can also be bypassed by loopholes with technologies such as [VPNs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_private_network "Virtual private network"), which have seen a significant number of new users in countries where these laws are enforced. Despite the laws being mainly used to verify the ages of users on social media services, critics believe that these laws, which verify users' ages with invasive methods (such as facial scans, handing over [government IDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_document "Identity document") and presenting credit cards), would be silently expanded beyond social media to include apps, games, website and devices to create a realm of digital censorship.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:28-0)[\[318\]](#refnote-:28)

In March 2026, [Ross Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Scott "Ross Scott"), the founder of consumer movement [Stop Killing Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_Killing_Games "Stop Killing Games") affirmed the movement's support for the opposition of age verification laws in a statement on [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)"). In a lengthened statement posted on [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit"), Stop Killing Games criticised laws such as California's [Digital Age Assurance Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Digital_Age_Assurance_Act "California Digital Age Assurance Act") and the UK's [Online Safety Act 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023 "Online Safety Act 2023"). The movement referenced [Urban Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Dead "Urban Dead"), a web game that was forced to shut down on 14 March 2025 after the game's creator, Kevan Davis, found the implementation of required measures in the Online Safety Act as "unfeasible".[&#8202;](#ref-back-:13-1)[\[42\]](#refnote-:13)

In May 2026, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia "Wikipedia") founder [Jimmy Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wales "Jimmy Wales") called an Australia-inspired online age verification law "an unmitigated disaster and an embarrassment". He further added that compelling adults to submit "personally identifying information" just to prove their ages "is really unsafe". Instead of online age verification laws, he suggested the governments should educate the adults on the existence of the parental controls on their children's phones.[&#8202;](#ref-back-319-0)[\[319\]](#refnote-319) The developer of the [Firefox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefox "Firefox") browser, [Mozilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla "Mozilla"), suggested "holding online platforms accountable, encouraging the responsible use of parental controls, and investing in digital skills and a whole-society approach to digital wellbeing" instead of age verification.[&#8202;](#ref-back-320-0)[\[320\]](#refnote-320)

A study conducted by the [University of Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Newcastle_(Australia) "University of Newcastle (Australia)") in June 2026 found that 85% of Australian children ranging between 12 and 17 years old were still using social media despite legislation barring them from doing so. More than half of these children utilize their own accounts. The study made the conclusion that a ban on social media would perhaps be more effective when applied to children under the age of eight instead of adolescents who already utilize it.[&#8202;](#ref-back-321-0)[\[321\]](#refnote-321)

James Baker, the free expression manager at the [Open Rights Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Rights_Group "Open Rights Group"), a digital rights group based in the UK, stated that disadvantages to age checks include the privacy of individuals, as well as the risk of transferred data being breached, saying: “One of the downsides we have seen is in terms of people’s privacy. If they hand that data over to a company, then the data can be at risk of being breached”.[&#8202;](#ref-back-:11-1)[\[79\]](#refnote-:11)

Ann Cuisia, CEO of Philippine fintech organization TraXion Tech, asserted that banning minors from social media "may reduce exposure" but they could "find ways around it". She also said that stricter measures mean "platforms start collecting more personal information". She suggested a "layered approach" instead, which included "platform redesign, smarter regulation, parental guidance, and digital literacy".[&#8202;](#ref-back-322-0)[\[322\]](#refnote-322) Former Philippine Senator [Manny Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Villar "Manny Villar") opined about the concerns on privacy and data security, the ability of some children to bypass restrictions using fake identities, and a "widening inequality" for families whose only resources of information and creativity are online.[&#8202;](#ref-back-323-0)[\[323\]](#refnote-323) Philippine IT organisation [Computer Professionals' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Professionals'_Union "Computer Professionals' Union") (CPU) also criticised the proposed online age and identity verification measures and instead suggested the following alternate measures: "accessible mental health services, comprehensive digital literacy programs integrated into educational systems, and stricter gun controls".[&#8202;](#ref-back-324-0)[\[324\]](#refnote-324)
