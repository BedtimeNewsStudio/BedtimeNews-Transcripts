Death of Jeffrey Epstein

On August 10, 2019, [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein "Jeffrey Epstein"), an American [financier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financier "Financier") and a [child sex offender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_sex_offender "Child sex offender"), was found unresponsive in his jail cell at 6:30a.m, at the [Metropolitan Correctional Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Correctional_Center,_New_York "Metropolitan Correctional Center, New York") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), hanging off the side of his cell's bed, where he was awaiting trial on [sex trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_trafficking "Sex trafficking") charges. After prison guards reportedly performed [CPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPR "CPR"), he was transported in [cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest "Cardiac arrest") to the [New York Downtown Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Downtown_Hospital "New York Downtown Hospital"), where he was pronounced dead at 6:39am. The [New York City medical examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_medical_examiner "New York City medical examiner") and the [Justice Department Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Department_Inspector_General "Justice Department Inspector General") ruled that Epstein's death was caused by [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide") by means of hanging. Epstein's lawyers challenged the medical examiner's conclusion and opened their own investigation, hiring pathologist [Michael Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Baden "Michael Baden").

After initially expressing suspicion, Attorney General [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr "William Barr") described Epstein's death as "a perfect storm of screw-ups". Both the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") (FBI) and the [Department of Justice's Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice's_Inspector_General "Department of Justice's Inspector General") conducted investigations into the circumstances of his death. The guards on duty were later charged with multiple counts of record falsification.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NPR-charge-0)[\[1\]](#refnote-NPR-charge) Many public figures accused the [Federal Bureau of Prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Prisons "Federal Bureau of Prisons") (FBOP) of negligence; several lawmakers called for reforms to the federal prison system. In response, Barr removed the director of the FBOP.

As a result of Epstein's death, all charges against him were dismissed, and ongoing sex-trafficking investigations shifted attention to his alleged associates, notably [Ghislaine Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghislaine_Maxwell "Ghislaine Maxwell"), who was arrested and indicted in July 2020 and convicted on five sex trafficking-related counts on December 29, 2021. Another associate, [Jean-Luc Brunel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Luc_Brunel "Jean-Luc Brunel"), was arrested by French authorities in 2020 and later died by suicide.

Due to jail procedures on the night of Epstein's death, and his proximity to high-profile figures, his death generated speculation and [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theories "Conspiracy theories") about the possibility that he was murdered.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Epstein_Conspiracy_Theories-0)[\[2\]](#refnote-Epstein_Conspiracy_Theories)[&#8202;](#ref-back-3-0)[\[3\]](#refnote-3) Other theories claimed his death was [staged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faked_death "Faked death"). In November 2019, the contested nature of his death spawned the " [Epstein didn't kill himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_didn't_kill_himself "Epstein didn't kill himself")" meme. Public opinion polls suggest that only a minority of Americans believe that Epstein died by suicide; one such poll saw 16% of respondents saying they believed Epstein died by suicide, 45% believing he was murdered, and 39% being unsure.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Insider_poll_-_Yahoo_News-0)[\[4\]](#refnote-Insider_poll_-_Yahoo_News)

## Arrest and imprisonment

### Apprehension and indictment

Further information: [Jeffrey Epstein §Legal issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein#Legal_issues "Jeffrey Epstein")

[![Photograph of the multi-story Metropolitan Correctional Center in New York](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/MCC_New_York_jeh.JPG/250px-MCC_New_York_jeh.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MCC_New_York_jeh.JPG) [The Metropolitan Correctional Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Correctional_Center,_New_York "Metropolitan Correctional Center, New York"), where Epstein was held after his 2019 arrest

On July6, 2019, Jeffrey Epstein was arrested in New York City on multiple charges, including [sex trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_trafficking "Sex trafficking"), and held at the Metropolitan Correctional Center[&#8202;](#ref-back-6-0)[\[note 1\]](#refnote-6) in [Lower Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Manhattan "Lower Manhattan").[&#8202;](#ref-back-7-0)[\[6\]](#refnote-7)[&#8202;](#ref-back-8-0)[\[7\]](#refnote-8) He pleaded not guilty.[&#8202;](#ref-back-9-0)[\[8\]](#refnote-9)[&#8202;](#ref-back-10-0)[\[9\]](#refnote-10) He faced similar charges in Florida in 2008, but accepted a plea bargain in exchange for immunity from federal charges. Under its provisions, he pleaded guilty to two state felony charges, paid restitution to three dozen victims identified by the FBI, and registered as a sex offender in both Florida and New York.[&#8202;](#ref-back-deal-0)[\[10\]](#refnote-deal)

On July18, 2019, Epstein was denied bail after offering $600,000 to be able to wear an [ankle tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_tagging "Electronic tagging") at [his New York City townhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_N._Straus_House "Herbert N. Straus House"). He was viewed as a potential [flight risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bail_in_the_United_States#Federal_law "Bail in the United States") because of the 20 international flights he had taken in the previous 18 months.[&#8202;](#ref-back-12-0)[\[11\]](#refnote-12)[&#8202;](#ref-back-13-0)[\[12\]](#refnote-13) Epstein appealed the bail denial decision to the [United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Second_Circuit "United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit"); at the time of his death, the appeal was still pending.[&#8202;](#ref-back-14-0)[\[13\]](#refnote-14)

### Initial incident and final weeks

At 1:27a.m. on July23, 2019, Epstein was found semiconscious in his cell with injuries to his neck. Prison officials questioned his cellmate, multiple murder and drug conspiracy suspect [Nicholas Tartaglione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Tartaglione "Nicholas Tartaglione"). He denied harming Epstein.[&#8202;](#ref-back-15-0)[\[14\]](#refnote-15) An internal prison investigation cleared Tartaglione of any connection to the event.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cleared-0)[\[15\]](#refnote-cleared) One unnamed source claimed Epstein told his lawyers Tartaglione had inflicted the injuries on him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-17-0)[\[16\]](#refnote-17) Another unnamed source claimed Epstein staged the incident in order to be transferred.[&#8202;](#ref-back-18-0)[\[17\]](#refnote-18) [Spencer Kuvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Kuvin "Spencer Kuvin"), a lawyer who represented three of Epstein's alleged victims, said in July 2019, after Epstein's first apparent suicide attempt, that he believed it was an attack on his life, and that there was a high probability that he would be murdered in prison.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Palm-0)[\[18\]](#refnote-Palm)[&#8202;](#ref-back-20-0)[\[19\]](#refnote-20)

[Play video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jeffrey_Epstein's_cell_block_CCTV_footage_(10hr)_released_by_the_US_Department_of_Justice_in_July_2025.webm "Play video")CCTV footage released in July 2025 showing part of a common area within the Metropolitan Correctional Center where Epstein was being held. A minute is missing from the footage, where the clock jumps from 11:58:58 to 12:00:00. This was explained as a re-construction of footage, though this is unverified.[&#8202;](#ref-back-21-0)[\[20\]](#refnote-21)

As a result of the incident, Epstein was placed on [suicide watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_watch "Suicide watch").[&#8202;](#ref-back-Williams-0)[\[21\]](#refnote-Williams) He was held in an observation cell surrounded by windows, where lights were left on and any devices that he could use to kill himself were not permitted.[&#8202;](#ref-back-23-0)[\[22\]](#refnote-23) After six days, psychological staff removed Epstein from suicide watch following a psychiatric examination.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Williams-1)[\[21\]](#refnote-Williams)[&#8202;](#ref-back-24-0)[\[23\]](#refnote-24) He then returned to the Security Housing Unit (SHU), where he was to have a cellmate and be checked on every 30 minutes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-25-0)[\[24\]](#refnote-25)

During his final days in his cell, Epstein allegedly wrote two notes. One said investigators had "FOUND NOTHING!!!" and that "It is a treat to be able to choose one's time to say goodbye"; a court made the note public in 2026.[&#8202;](#ref-back-26-0)[\[25\]](#refnote-26)[&#8202;](#ref-back-27-0)[\[26\]](#refnote-27) The other, released by _60Minutes_ in 2020, included a complaint about large bugs crawling on his body, guard Tova Noel giving him burnt food, and a guard intentionally locking him in a shower cell without clothes for an hour.[&#8202;](#ref-back-29-0)[\[note 2\]](#refnote-29)[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-0)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-30-0)[\[28\]](#refnote-30) Both notes contained the phrase "NO FUN" in all caps and underlined.

On August8, Epstein signed his [last will and testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_and_testament "Will and testament"), witnessed by two attorneys who knew him. The will named two longtime employees as executors, and immediately gifted all his assets, and any assets remaining in [his estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_of_Jeffrey_Epstein "Estate of Jeffrey Epstein"), to a trust.[&#8202;](#ref-back-31-0)[\[29\]](#refnote-31)[&#8202;](#ref-back-32-0)[\[30\]](#refnote-32) According to the _New York Times_, Epstein's estate was valued at around $600million at the time of his death.[&#8202;](#ref-back-33-0)[\[31\]](#refnote-33)

## Death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/The_Noose_Jeffrey_Epstein_Died_By.png/250px-The_Noose_Jeffrey_Epstein_Died_By.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Noose_Jeffrey_Epstein_Died_By.png) The noose Jeffrey Epstein allegedly used to hang himself.

When Epstein was placed in the [Security Housing Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Housing_Unit "Security Housing Unit") (SHU), the jail informed the Justice Department that he would have a cellmate and that a guard would look into the cell every 30 minutes. These procedures were not followed on the night he died.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Benner-0)[\[32\]](#refnote-Benner) On August9, Epstein's cellmate was transferred, and no replacement was brought in. The evening of his death, Epstein met with his lawyers, who described him as "upbeat" before being escorted back to the SHU at 7:49p.m. by guard Tova Noel.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-1)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes) CCTV footage shows that the two guards failed to perform the required institutional count at 10:00p.m. and recorded Noel briefly walking by Epstein's cell at 10:30p.m., the last time the guards entered the tier where his cell was located.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn.com-0)[\[33\]](#refnote-cnn.com) Through the night, in violation of the jail's normal procedure, Epstein was not checked every 30 minutes.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Benner-1)[\[32\]](#refnote-Benner) The two guards assigned to check his cell overnight, Noel and Michael Thomas, [fell asleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeping_on_the_job "Sleeping on the job") at their desk[&#8202;](#ref-back-36-0)[\[note 3\]](#refnote-36) for about three hours and later falsified related records.[&#8202;](#ref-back-37-0)[\[34\]](#refnote-37)[&#8202;](#ref-back-38-0)[\[35\]](#refnote-38) Two cameras in front of Epstein's cell malfunctioned that night.[&#8202;](#ref-back-broken_cameras-0)[\[36\]](#refnote-broken_cameras) Another camera had footage that was "unusable".[&#8202;](#ref-back-broken_cameras-1)[\[36\]](#refnote-broken_cameras)

### Discovery

[Play video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Epstein_Body_Moved_From_New_York_Hospital_to_Medical_Examiner's_Office.webm "Play video")Epstein's body being moved to the medical examiner's office

On the morning of August 10, Noel typed "latest on Epstein in jail" into a search engine at 5:42a.m. and 5:52a.m.[&#8202;](#ref-back-40-0)[\[37\]](#refnote-40)[&#8202;](#ref-back-41-0)[\[38\]](#refnote-41) As the guards were distributing breakfast shortly after 6:30a.m., Epstein was found unresponsive in cardiac arrest in his cell.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn.com-1)[\[33\]](#refnote-cnn.com) He was found in a kneeling position with a strip of bedsheet[&#8202;](#ref-back-43-0)[\[note 4\]](#refnote-43) wrapped around his neck.[&#8202;](#ref-back-44-0)[\[40\]](#refnote-44) The sheet was tied to the top of his bunk.[&#8202;](#ref-back-45-0)[\[41\]](#refnote-45) The guards performed [CPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPR "CPR") on Epstein, and other prisoners heard them yell "Breathe, Epstein, breathe."[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-2)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes) At 6:33a.m., the guards pulled an alarm, notifying their supervisor, to whom Noel said, "Epstein hung\[ _[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_\] himself."[&#8202;](#ref-back-cnn.com-2)[\[33\]](#refnote-cnn.com) He was rushed to the [New York Downtown Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Downtown_Hospital "New York Downtown Hospital") where he was pronounced dead at 6:39a.m.[&#8202;](#ref-back-46-0)[\[42\]](#refnote-46) His body was transported to the medical examiner's office soon after.[&#8202;](#ref-back-47-0)[\[43\]](#refnote-47) The forensic pathologist hired by Epstein's family said he had likely died two hours before guards found him.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-3)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes)

The removal of Epstein's body from his cell was a violation of protocol, as the [Bureau of Prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Prisons "Bureau of Prisons") (BOP) mandates that a suicide scene be treated with the "same level of protection as any crime scene in which a death has occurred".[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-4)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes) Consequently, prison personnel also failed to photograph Epstein's body as it was found.[&#8202;](#ref-back-48-0)[\[44\]](#refnote-48)

### Controversy

Epstein's death was the first death ruled a suicide at the MCC in 21 years.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-0)[\[45\]](#refnote-auto) [Michael Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Baden "Michael Baden") and _[60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes "60 Minutes")_ questioned whether Epstein, who was almost 6ft (1.83m) tall and weighed 185 pounds (84kg), could have been able to hang himself from the lower bunk. Photos taken after the death also show bottles and medicine standing upright on the top bunk.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-5)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes) Baden also questioned why Epstein did not use other materials available in his cell as a ligature, such as wires and tubing from a [sleep apnea machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_apnea "Sleep apnea"), which were stronger and longer.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-6)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes)

### Possible motivations

A psychological reconstruction report of Epstein's death compiled by Bureau of Prisons five weeks after his death suggests that Epstein's identity "appeared to be based on his wealth, power and association with other high-profile individuals."[&#8202;](#ref-back-Weiser_et_al._2021-0)[\[46\]](#refnote-Weiser_et_al._2021) The report further attributes his suicide to lack of significant interpersonal connections, loss of status, and the idea of potentially spending the rest of his life in prison.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Weiser_et_al._2021-1)[\[46\]](#refnote-Weiser_et_al._2021)

## Autopsy and forensic evidence

### Autopsy

New York City's Chief Medical Examiner [Barbara Sampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Sampson "Barbara Sampson") conducted a four-hour autopsy on Epstein's body on August11\. Epstein's lawyers sent pathologist [Michael Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Baden "Michael Baden") to observe the autopsy.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-7)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes)[&#8202;](#ref-back-51-0)[\[47\]](#refnote-51) Following the autopsy, the medical examiner's office reported that Epstein had hanged himself with a sheet from his bed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Sisak-0)[\[48\]](#refnote-Sisak) The last death ruled a suicide at the MCC was in 1998.[&#8202;](#ref-back-auto-1)[\[45\]](#refnote-auto) Epstein's lawyers released a joint statement after the medical examiner's report, expressing dissatisfaction with it, challenging its conclusions and saying a more complete response was forthcoming. They said that the defense team fully intended to continue its own investigation into the circumstances and cause of Epstein's death,[&#8202;](#ref-back-53-0)[\[49\]](#refnote-53) including taking legal action to view the camera footage near his cell during the night of his death.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Frias-0)[\[50\]](#refnote-Frias) They later said that the evidence concerning Epstein's death was "far more consistent" with murder than suicide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-55-0)[\[51\]](#refnote-55)

### Autopsy report and criticism

[![Sketch of a human skeleton, with the hyoid bone highlighted in red](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Hyoid_bone_-_animation.gif/250px-Hyoid_bone_-_animation.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hyoid_bone_-_animation.gif) Epstein's [hyoid bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyoid_bone "Hyoid bone"), among others, was broken. It is disputed whether this is more indicative of hanging or strangulation.

On August16, Sampson announced that Epstein's death had been ruled a suicide via hanging.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailybeaster-0)[\[52\]](#refnote-dailybeaster) Later, conflicting reports said the injuries Epstein sustained were as consistent, if not more so, with strangulation or homicide as with suicide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-broken_bones-0)[\[53\]](#refnote-broken_bones)[&#8202;](#ref-back-58-0)[\[54\]](#refnote-58) Sampson's report found there was no [foul play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime "Crime") in his death.[&#8202;](#ref-back-59-0)[\[55\]](#refnote-59)[&#8202;](#ref-back-60-0)[\[56\]](#refnote-60)[&#8202;](#ref-back-61-0)[\[57\]](#refnote-61) Three of Epstein's lawyers expressed their dissatisfaction with Sampson's conclusion, having hired forensic pathologist Michael Baden to observe the autopsy; they said they would initiate their own investigation and provide a more detailed statement in the future.[&#8202;](#ref-back-62-0)[\[58\]](#refnote-62)[&#8202;](#ref-back-63-0)[\[59\]](#refnote-63) Baden observed Sampson's autopsy when it was conducted; after the autopsy he said he could not comment because of gag orders filed by Sampson's office and Epstein's estate.[&#8202;](#ref-back-dailybeaster-1)[\[52\]](#refnote-dailybeaster)

On October30, 2019, Baden issued a report stating that Epstein's neck injuries were much more consistent with "homicidal strangulation" than suicide. He stated that Epstein "had two fractures on the left and right sides of his [larynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larynx "Larynx"), specifically the thyroid cartilage or [Adam's apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam's_apple "Adam's apple"), as well as one fracture on the left hyoid bone above the Adam's apple". In particular, Baden claimed that Epstein's [hyoid bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyoid_bone "Hyoid bone") was [broken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyoid_bone_fracture "Hyoid bone fracture") in a way indicative of strangulation from behind.[&#8202;](#ref-back-64-0)[\[60\]](#refnote-64) Later that day, Sampson rejected Baden's claims, saying, "I stand firmly behind our determination of the cause and manner of death for Mr. Epstein. The cause is hanging, the manner is suicide."[&#8202;](#ref-back-65-0)[\[61\]](#refnote-65) Baden later said, "Going over a thousand jail hangings, suicides in the New York City state prisons over the past 40–50 years, no one had three fractures."[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-8)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes)

Neurosurgeon and [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") medical correspondent [Sanjay Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjay_Gupta "Sanjay Gupta") asserted Epstein could have easily broken his hyoid bone in a hanging because of the bone's weakening and loss of flexibility with age. Gupta also suggested that multiple broken neck bones were more characteristic of hanging. Gerald Rodts, chief of spinal surgery at [Emory University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University_Hospital "Emory University Hospital"), also stated that multiple broken neck bones are consistent with hanging.[&#8202;](#ref-back-66-0)[\[62\]](#refnote-66) A professor of [forensic science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_science "Forensic science") at [John Jay College of Criminal Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jay_College_of_Criminal_Justice "John Jay College of Criminal Justice"), Lawrence Kobilinsky, said the hyoid bone can indeed break from hanging, noting that it is a weak bone. According to their analysis, a broken hyoid bone is not sufficient proof of homicide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Levenson-0)[\[63\]](#refnote-Levenson)
According to Baden, the neck wound was in the center of Epstein's neck, not under his mandibles as in a typical hanging.[&#8202;](#ref-back-60minutes-9)[\[27\]](#refnote-60minutes) Baden said this is more common when a victim is strangled by a wire or cord. Baden also said that the wound was much thinner than the strip of bedsheet, and although there was blood on Epstein's neck, it was absent on the bed-sheet ligature.[&#8202;](#ref-back-68-0)[\[64\]](#refnote-68) In response to Baden's claims, Sampson stood by the conclusion that Epstein's death was suicide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Levenson-1)[\[63\]](#refnote-Levenson) In June 2023, the [Justice Department Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Department_Inspector_General "Justice Department Inspector General") affirmed that Epstein died by suicide. The report stated that investigators interviewed dozens of witnesses and reviewed 100,000 documents, and found nothing contradicting the FBI's investigation, which also ruled the death a suicide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-69-0)[\[65\]](#refnote-69)\[ _[further explanation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_\]

### Burial

Following the autopsy, Epstein's body was claimed by an "unidentified associate", later revealed to be his brother, [Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Epstein_(property_developer) "Mark Epstein (property developer)").[&#8202;](#ref-back-70-0)[\[66\]](#refnote-70)[&#8202;](#ref-back-71-0)[\[67\]](#refnote-71) On September5, the body was buried in an unmarked tomb next to those of his parents at the IJ Morris Star of David mausoleum in [Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach,_Florida "Palm Beach, Florida"), Florida. His parents' names were removed from their tombstone to prevent vandalism.[&#8202;](#ref-back-72-0)[\[68\]](#refnote-72)

The first time the public knew about Epstein's death was in the form of a [4chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4chan "4chan") post, where the poster speculated Epstein was switched out,[&#8202;](#ref-back-73-0)[\[69\]](#refnote-73) made thirty-eight minutes before the first mainstream news articles were published about it. The IP address associated with the poster was subpoenaed by the Department of Justice.[&#8202;](#ref-back-74-0)[\[70\]](#refnote-74) A few hours after Epstein's death was announced, President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") responded by [retweeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retweet "Retweet") a post related to the " [Clinton body count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_body_count_conspiracy_theory "Clinton body count conspiracy theory")", a [conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory "Conspiracy theory") purporting to link Epstein's death to former president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") and his wife, the former secretary of state [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton").[&#8202;](#ref-back-75-0)[\[71\]](#refnote-75) At a speech in [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania") three days later, Trump told reporters he wanted "a full investigation".[&#8202;](#ref-back-76-0)[\[72\]](#refnote-76) Attorney General [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr "William Barr") said he was "appalled" by Epstein's death in federal custody and that it "raises serious questions that must be answered". He ordered an investigation by the Justice Department's [Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Inspector_General_(United_States) "Office of Inspector General (United States)") in addition to the FBI investigation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-77-0)[\[73\]](#refnote-77) Senator [Ben Sasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Sasse "Ben Sasse"), chairman of the United States Senate Judiciary oversight subcommittee, later wrote Barr a letter saying, "The Department of Justice failed." He added, "Given Epstein's previous attempted suicide, he should have been locked in a padded room under unbroken, 24/7, constant surveillance. Obviously, heads must roll."[&#8202;](#ref-back-78-0)[\[74\]](#refnote-78) Representative [Matt Gaetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Gaetz "Matt Gaetz"), who sat on the [United States House Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_the_Judiciary "United States House Committee on the Judiciary"), called on chairman [Jerry Nadler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Nadler "Jerry Nadler") to prioritize investigating the circumstances around Epstein's death over other probes the committee was carrying out.[&#8202;](#ref-back-79-0)[\[75\]](#refnote-79) Representative [Lois Frankel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lois_Frankel "Lois Frankel") called for a congressional investigation into Epstein's 2008 plea deal.[&#8202;](#ref-back-democrats_call-0)[\[76\]](#refnote-democrats_call) Senators [Kirsten Gillibrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Gillibrand "Kirsten Gillibrand") and [Rick Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Scott "Rick Scott") called for an investigation and expressed their dissatisfaction that Epstein's victims would not find closure in a trial.[&#8202;](#ref-back-The_Hill-0)[\[77\]](#refnote-The_Hill)[&#8202;](#ref-back-82-0)[\[78\]](#refnote-82)

"The apparent suicide of this high-profile and—if allegations are proven to be accurate—particularly reprehensible individual while in the federal government's custody demonstrates severe miscarriages of or deficiencies in inmate protocol and has allowed the deceased to ultimately evade facing justice", they wrote. "Any victims of Mr. Epstein's actions will forever be denied proper recourse and the scintilla of recompense our justice system can provide in the face of such alleged atrocities; the competency and rigor of our criminal justice system has been marred by this apparent oversight".[&#8202;](#ref-back-84-0)[\[80\]](#refnote-84) On August13, Nebraska senator Ben Sasse, the chair of the [Senate Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_the_Judiciary "United States Senate Committee on the Judiciary"), wrote a letter to Attorney General Barr urging him to "rip up" the 2008 non-prosecution deal for Epstein and his co-conspirators. Sasse argued that the Justice Department must bring Epstein's co-conspirators to justice, despite his death, and added, "This crooked deal cannot stand."[&#8202;](#ref-back-85-0)[\[81\]](#refnote-85) On August19, Barr replaced the BOP director with former director [Kathleen Hawk Sawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Hawk_Sawyer "Kathleen Hawk Sawyer").[&#8202;](#ref-back-86-0)[\[82\]](#refnote-86)[&#8202;](#ref-back-sawyer-0)[\[83\]](#refnote-sawyer) Sasse praised the move.[&#8202;](#ref-back-sawyer-1)[\[83\]](#refnote-sawyer)

"For them to pull him off suicide watch is shocking", Cameron Lindsay, a former warden at three federal facilities, told [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News "NBC News"). "For someone this high-profile, with these allegations and this many victims, who has had a suicide attempt in the last few weeks, you can take absolutely no chances. You leave him on suicide watch until he's out of there."[&#8202;](#ref-back-88-0)[\[84\]](#refnote-88) Federal prosecutors in the case called Epstein's death "disturbing" and emphasized they would continue to seek justice for his accusers even after the financier's death. [Geoffrey Berman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Berman "Geoffrey Berman"), the [U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Attorney_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York "U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York"), said in a statement, "To those brave young women who have already come forward and to the many others who have yet to do so, let me reiterate that we remain committed to standing for you, and our investigation of the conduct charged in the indictment—which included a conspiracy count—remains ongoing."[&#8202;](#ref-back-89-0)[\[85\]](#refnote-89)

### Investigations

[![Wikimedia Commons logo](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/4/4a/Commons-logo.svg/40px-Commons-logo.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Commons-logo.svg)

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Epstein Files: First Phase](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Epstein%20Files%20Phase%201 "commons:Category:Epstein Files Phase 1").

[![William Barr speaking at a podium](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/William_Barr_delivers_remarks_at_the_Correctional_Workers_Week_Memorial_Service.jpg/250px-William_Barr_delivers_remarks_at_the_Correctional_Workers_Week_Memorial_Service.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:William_Barr_delivers_remarks_at_the_Correctional_Workers_Week_Memorial_Service.jpg) Attorney General [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr "William Barr") later described Epstein's death as "a perfect storm of screw-ups".

The DOJ launched several investigations into the circumstances of Epstein's death,[&#8202;](#ref-back-90-0)[\[86\]](#refnote-90)[&#8202;](#ref-back-91-0)[\[87\]](#refnote-91) with Attorney General [William Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barr "William Barr") saying that Justice Department officials would thoroughly investigate "serious irregularities" at the Metropolitan Correctional Center.[&#8202;](#ref-back-92-0)[\[88\]](#refnote-92) As Barr received more information surrounding Epstein's death, however, he came to believe it was only "a perfect storm of screw-ups".[&#8202;](#ref-back-screw-0)[\[89\]](#refnote-screw) In November 2019, BOP director Sawyer told the Senate Judiciary Committee that the FBI was investigating whether a "criminal enterprise" was involved in Epstein's death. She added there is "no indication, from anything I know" that his death "was anything other than a suicide". A spokesperson later explained that she had used the phrase "criminal enterprise" because that was what Senator [Lindsey Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsey_Graham "Lindsey Graham") had asked her about, and that "she was referring to looking into possible criminal conduct by staff".[&#8202;](#ref-back-94-0)[\[90\]](#refnote-94)

On August 13, 2020, Attorney General Barr ordered the BOP to reassign the warden of the MCC, Lamine N'Diaye, temporarily, while the FBI and Justice Department inspector general investigated the circumstances surrounding Epstein's death. James Petrucci, the warden of [FCI Otisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Correctional_Institution,_Otisville "Federal Correctional Institution, Otisville"), was named acting warden.[&#8202;](#ref-back-warden_reassigned-0)[\[91\]](#refnote-warden_reassigned) Two staff members assigned to Epstein's unit were placed on administrative leave.[&#8202;](#ref-back-warden_reassigned-1)[\[91\]](#refnote-warden_reassigned)[&#8202;](#ref-back-96-0)[\[92\]](#refnote-96) On August14, Manhattan federal court judge Richard Berman, who was overseeing Epstein's criminal case, wrote to N'Diaye asking whether an investigation into Epstein's death would include a probe into his prior (July 23) injuries. Judge Berman wrote that, to his knowledge, it had never been definitely explained what they concluded about the incident.[&#8202;](#ref-back-97-0)[\[93\]](#refnote-97) In November 2019, Berman called for widespread prison reforms in an open letter to Barr in _The New York Times_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-98-0)[\[94\]](#refnote-98)

### Subsequent criminal trials and developments

On November19, 2019, federal prosecutors in New York indicted MCC guards Michael Thomas and Tova Noel, charging Noel with 5 counts of falsifying records, Thomas 3 counts, and both 1 count of conspiracy to falsify records.[&#8202;](#ref-back-110-0)[\[106\]](#refnote-110)[&#8202;](#ref-back-NPR-charge-1)[\[1\]](#refnote-NPR-charge)[&#8202;](#ref-back-111-0)[\[107\]](#refnote-111) The charges were based on video footage obtained by prosecutors, showing that the guards did not check on Epstein for eight hours. Instead, they made personal searches on their computers and slept. According to Geoffrey Berman, the [U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Attorney_for_the_Southern_District_of_New_York "U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York"), during that time they repeatedly signed records affirming they had performed the required checks.[&#8202;](#ref-back-NPR-charge-2)[\[1\]](#refnote-NPR-charge) Their bail was set at $100,000.[&#8202;](#ref-back-112-0)[\[108\]](#refnote-112) They claimed they were " [scapegoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scapegoat "Scapegoat")" for larger issues within the federal prison system.[&#8202;](#ref-back-113-0)[\[109\]](#refnote-113)[&#8202;](#ref-back-114-0)[\[110\]](#refnote-114) In May 2021, a judge approved a deferred prosecution deal, through which the guards can avoid a conviction and sentence if they comply with specific terms.[&#8202;](#ref-back-115-0)[\[111\]](#refnote-115)

In a December 2019 court proceeding against Epstein's cellmate Nick Tartaglione, federal prosecutors reported that the surveillance footage from outside Epstein's cell during the July23 incident had disappeared. Tartaglione's defense, who claimed that the footage showed Tartaglione saving Epstein's life, hoped that it would illustrate Tartaglione's character. His lawyer had requested that the footage be kept on July25, two days after the incident.[&#8202;](#ref-back-116-0)[\[112\]](#refnote-116) Judge [Kenneth Karas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_M._Karas "Kenneth M. Karas") requested that the government determine what had happened to the footage.[&#8202;](#ref-back-117-0)[\[113\]](#refnote-117) Several days later, federal prosecutors reversed their statement, claiming they had found the missing footage. But in January 2020, they admitted the footage had been permanently deleted because of a "clerical error".[&#8202;](#ref-back-118-0)[\[114\]](#refnote-118)[&#8202;](#ref-back-119-0)[\[115\]](#refnote-119)

On July2, 2020, almost a year after Epstein was arrested, the FBI arrested and charged Maxwell in [Bradford, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford,_New_Hampshire "Bradford, New Hampshire"). The charges included "enticement and conspiracy to entice minors to travel to engage in illegal sex acts, transportation and conspiracy to transport minors with intent to engage in criminal sexual activity, and two counts of perjury".[&#8202;](#ref-back-120-0)[\[116\]](#refnote-120)[&#8202;](#ref-back-121-0)[\[117\]](#refnote-121) Federal authorities have expressed concerns she might also attempt suicide in custody and have reportedly implemented additional safety protocols.[&#8202;](#ref-back-122-0)[\[118\]](#refnote-122) In an August 2020 interview, President Trump said that he "wish\[&#8202;](#ref-back-123-0)[ed\] her well", and again questioned whether Epstein's death was a suicide or homicide.[\[119\]](#refnote-123)

In 2020, associate [Jean-Luc Brunel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Luc_Brunel "Jean-Luc Brunel") was arrested and [charged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_charge "Criminal charge") with the [rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape_in_France "Rape in France") of minors by French [prosecutors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecutor "Prosecutor").[&#8202;](#ref-back-nbc-1)[\[97\]](#refnote-nbc) He later died from an apparent [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide") before his trial could proceed.[&#8202;](#ref-back-124-0)[\[120\]](#refnote-124) On July 17, 2025, White House press secretary [Karoline Leavitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoline_Leavitt "Karoline Leavitt") said: "The president would not recommend a special prosecutor in the Epstein case. That's how he feels."[&#8202;](#ref-back-125-0)[\[121\]](#refnote-125) In February 2026, newly released Department of Justice (DOJ) documents and surveillance‑video observation logs raised additional questions about activity near Jeffrey Epstein's cell on the night before his death. Investigators reviewing footage from the Metropolitan Correctional Center (MCC) noted an orange‑colored figure moving up the stairway toward Epstein's locked housing tier at approximately 10:39p.m. on August 9, 2019.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cbsvidlog-0)[\[122\]](#refnote-cbsvidlog) An internal FBI memorandum described the figure as "possibly an inmate," while a separate review by the DOJ Office of Inspector General (OIG) concluded it may have been a corrections officer carrying orange linen or bedding. The OIG's final report stated that an unidentified corrections officer appeared on the stairway at 10:39p.m. and re‑entered camera view at 10:41p.m.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cbsvidlog-1)[\[122\]](#refnote-cbsvidlog) Independent video analysts consulted by CBS News said the movement appeared more consistent with an inmate or someone wearing an orange prison uniform, though this interpretation was not adopted in official findings.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cbsvidlog-2)[\[122\]](#refnote-cbsvidlog) Official reviews of Epstein's death have not previously mentioned this figure, and authorities, including then–Attorney General William Barr, had stated that no one entered Epstein's housing tier during the night. The newly released logs appear to contradict those earlier assertions.[&#8202;](#ref-back-cbsvidlog-3)[\[122\]](#refnote-cbsvidlog)

## Homicide suspicions and conspiracy theories

See also: [Epstein didn't kill himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_didn't_kill_himself "Epstein didn't kill himself")

Epstein's death has spawned skepticism and multiple conspiracy theories. A [Rasmussen Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmussen_Reports "Rasmussen Reports") poll conducted shortly after his death in August 2019 found that only 29% of U.S. adults believed Epstein actually died by suicide, while 42% thought he was murdered to prevent him from testifying against powerful people with whom he associated, and 29% of people were undecided.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rasmussen_August_2019-0)[\[123\]](#refnote-Rasmussen_August_2019) By November 2019, a _[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider "Business Insider")_ poll found that those who believed Epstein was murdered outnumbered suicide proponents three to one.[&#8202;](#ref-back-BI_November_2019-0)[\[124\]](#refnote-BI_November_2019) In a 2020 poll, Rasmussen found a majority of Americans believed Epstein was murdered, with just 21% believing that he died by suicide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Rasmussen_January_2020-0)[\[125\]](#refnote-Rasmussen_January_2020) [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") professor Eric Oliver, a conspiracy theory expert, labeled [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist "Populist") sentiment and a [mistrust of the political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-establishment#United_States "Anti-establishment") as major contributors to the widespread rejection of the official narrative.[&#8202;](#ref-back-130-0)[\[126\]](#refnote-130)

At an August27 hearing, Epstein defense attorney Reid Weingarten expressed "significant doubts" that Epstein's death was due to suicide. According to Weingarten, when attorneys met with their client shortly before his death, "we did not see a despairing, despondent, suicidal person".[&#8202;](#ref-back-131-0)[\[127\]](#refnote-131) Epstein's brother, Mark, has rejected the possibility of Jeffrey's suicide, claiming, "I could see if he got a life sentence, I could then see him taking himself out, but he had a bail hearing coming up."[&#8202;](#ref-back-132-0)[\[128\]](#refnote-132) He also claimed his "life may also be in danger", if Epstein was indeed murdered.[&#8202;](#ref-back-133-0)[\[129\]](#refnote-133) Epstein's butler of 18 years also believed that he did not kill himself.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Tel8825-0)[\[130\]](#refnote-Tel8825) In a press conference about two months after Epstein's death, de Blasio declined to endorse chief medical examiner Sampson's conclusions, saying, "Something doesn't fit here. It just doesn't make sense that the highest-profile prisoner in America—you know, someone forgot to guard him."[&#8202;](#ref-back-135-0)[\[131\]](#refnote-135) Former US Attorney and Senate Judiciary Committee counsel [Brett Tolman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Tolman "Brett Tolman") said the death was "more than coincidental", considering Epstein's "many connections to powerful people".[&#8202;](#ref-back-136-0)[\[132\]](#refnote-136)

Immediately after his death, online conspiracy theorists falsely claimed that the images of Epstein in transport to the hospital were actually that of a body double. They relied on purported differences between the body's ear and Epstein's ear in a juxtaposed photo. Fact-checker _[Snopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snopes "Snopes")_ pointed out that the image of Epstein was 15 years old and that it was "more plausible that these discrepancies were the result of aging".[&#8202;](#ref-back-137-0)[\[133\]](#refnote-137) In October 2020, over a year after his death, similar false theories emerged that claimed that Epstein had [faked his death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faked_death "Faked death") and was living at [Zorro Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorro_Ranch "Zorro Ranch"), his residence in [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico "New Mexico").[&#8202;](#ref-back-138-0)[\[134\]](#refnote-138)[&#8202;](#ref-back-139-0)[\[135\]](#refnote-139)

In a 2025 interview with [Todd Blanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Blanche "Todd Blanche"), Ghislaine Maxwell said she did not believe Epstein's death was a suicide.[&#8202;](#ref-back-140-0)[\[136\]](#refnote-140)

## In popular culture

[![Graffito on a bridge over a highway](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Epstein_Bridge_Nov2019_2_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Epstein_Bridge_Nov2019_2_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Epstein_Bridge_Nov2019_2_(cropped).jpg)

[![A simple white sticker reading, "Epstein Didn't Kill Himself."](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Epstein_Didn%27t_Kill_Himself_Sticker.png/250px-Epstein_Didn%27t_Kill_Himself_Sticker.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Epstein_Didn't_Kill_Himself_Sticker.png)

The meme appeared in a variety of forms and places, such as this graffito on an Ohio interstate overpass or this sticker at a bus stop in Canada.

As more information surrounding Epstein's death became public in November 2019, his death and the possibility of murder became a popular [Internet meme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_meme "Internet meme"), particularly in the form of the phrase " [Epstein didn't kill himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_didn't_kill_himself "Epstein didn't kill himself")". The meme gained prominence as it was interjected at the end of live interviews, such as by a [Navy SEAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs "United States Navy SEALs") on [Fox News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News "Fox News"), or later by a [University of Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alabama "University of Alabama") student on [MSNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC "MSNBC").[&#8202;](#ref-back-147-0)[\[143\]](#refnote-147)[&#8202;](#ref-back-148-0)[\[144\]](#refnote-148) The meme often appears as a [non-sequitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non_sequitur_(literary_device) "Non sequitur (literary device)") after a body of text or within an image.[&#8202;](#ref-back-149-0)[\[145\]](#refnote-149) [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR")'s [Scott Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Simon "Scott Simon") likened the bait-and-switch aspect of the meme to [Rickrolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickrolling "Rickrolling"). Simon also worried that media attention to the meme could spread misinformation.[&#8202;](#ref-back-150-0)[\[146\]](#refnote-150) The [Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") observed that "\[&#8202;](#ref-back-151-0)[t\]he phrase 'Jeffrey Epstein didn't kill himself' has taken on a life of its own—sometimes more as a pop culture catchphrase than an actual belief."[\[147\]](#refnote-151)

Celebrities have comedically referenced Epstein's death. [Ricky Gervais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Gervais "Ricky Gervais") remarked at the [77th Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77th_Golden_Globe_Awards "77th Golden Globe Awards") that Epstein did not take his own life, and [Adam Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Driver "Adam Driver") played a murdered Epstein in hell on _[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")_.[&#8202;](#ref-back-152-0)[\[148\]](#refnote-152) U.S. Representative [Paul Gosar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gosar "Paul Gosar") posted 23 tweets in which the first letter of each tweet spelled out the meme's phrase.[&#8202;](#ref-back-153-0)[\[149\]](#refnote-153) Holiday-themed merchandise (like [Christmas sweaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_jumper "Christmas jumper")) that prominently featured the phrase also became available for sale through several online retailers.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Slate-0)[\[150\]](#refnote-Slate)

In an interview with _[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_(magazine) "Slate (magazine)")_, independent merchandisers indicated that the Christmas/Epstein product lines were selling comparatively well, citing the mashup's [dark humor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy "Black comedy") as the reason for their popularity.[&#8202;](#ref-back-Slate-1)[\[150\]](#refnote-Slate) According to _[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")_, the Christmas-themed paraphernalia was outselling _[Game of Thrones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_Thrones "Game of Thrones")_ merchandise.[&#8202;](#ref-back-155-0)[\[151\]](#refnote-155) In the Season2 finale of the streaming television series _[The Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boys_(TV_series) "The Boys (TV series)")_, it was revealed that [Stormfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormfront_(character) "Stormfront (character)") secretly killed Epstein in its alternate timeline.[&#8202;](#ref-back-156-0)[\[152\]](#refnote-156)[&#8202;](#ref-back-157-0)[\[153\]](#refnote-157) Several television documentaries were created because of increased public interest after his death. In May 2020, [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") released _[Jeffrey Epstein: Filthy Rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein:_Filthy_Rich "Jeffrey Epstein: Filthy Rich")_. As of October 2019,[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Death_of_Jeffrey_Epstein&action=edit) [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO"), [SonyTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SonyTV "SonyTV"), and [Lifetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifetime_(TV_network) "Lifetime (TV network)") also have similar works in progress.[&#8202;](#ref-back-158-0)[\[154\]](#refnote-158)[&#8202;](#ref-back-159-0)[\[155\]](#refnote-159)
