# Most of the Jane Wu estate's suit against Northwestern to proceed - Evanston RoundTable

[Skip to content](https://evanstonroundtable.com/2026/07/16/judge-denies-northwestern-motion-jane-wu/#main)

![](https://evanstonroundtable.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/1_Jane-Wu.jpg)Prof. Jane Wu, a researcher at Northwestern’s Feinberg School of Medicine, died by suicide in July 2024 after the university shut down her lab. Her daughter called it “a grave injustice.” Credit: Courtesy of Elizabeth Rao

In a hearing Thursday, a Cook County Circuit Court judge largely denied Northwestern University’s motion to dismiss a suit from the estate of former professor Jane Wu, who died by suicide in July 2024.

Wu was a Northwestern University Feinberg School of Medicine professor. The **[suit from her estate](https://evanstonroundtable.com/2025/08/24/jane-wu-northwestern-discrimination-lawsuit/)**, filed last summer, claims that Northwestern discriminated against Wu on the basis of gender, race, national origin or disability. The suit also accuses the Northwestern and Chicago police departments of assault, battery and false imprisonment. Northwestern had asked the court to dismiss the suit.

Representatives for Northwestern did not return a request for comment before publication.

#### Case likely to proceed despite requested refiling

At Thursday’s hearing, Judge Jonathan Clark Green denied the motion from Northwestern to dismiss the suit on eight of the nine counts listed in the suit, including false imprisonment, medical malpractice and discrimination on the basis of national origin.

Green asked Thomas Geoghegan, the lawyer for Wu’s estate, to file an amendment to the suit on the count charging that Northwestern breached Wu’s contract by denying her tenure. Geoghegan will file the amended complaint by July 30. This will be the second amended complaint submitted to the court. (Geoghegan declined to comment for this piece.)

Green ordered Northwestern to file an amended response to the complaint by Aug. 20.

Since Green requested the amended complaint to address just one of the nine counts listed, and said he denied the motion to dismiss on the other eight counts, the suit is likely to move forward into discovery. The judge scheduled a status hearing Aug. 27, saying he hoped a discussion of discovery would begin on that date.

#### **NIH investigation and aftermath**

Wu was born in China and became a naturalized U.S. citizen in 2000. But in 2019, the National Institutes of Health (NIH), which had awarded millions of dollars in grant funding to Wu’s projects over the years, launched an administrative investigation into her ties to China. The investigation was part of the first Trump administration’s “China Initiative,” according to the suit.

The lawsuit claims that no charges were ever brought against Wu, and that the NIH closed its investigation in 2023. In the same letter announcing she was no longer under NIH scrutiny, Feinberg leadership “cut her salary for lack of funding of her research during the NIH investigation,” added additional requirements for her to apply for future grants and “gave her only a limited chance” to meet those requirements, the suit says.

In February 2024, the lawsuit claims, Northwestern closed part of Wu’s lab space and “took away” members of her research team.

The suit claims that in spite of this, Wu notified Northwestern administrators that she was in the process of applying for new NIH grants to continue her research. On May 2, 2024, according to the lawsuit, administrators “misleadingly” told Wu that she would be allowed to apply for NIH funding. Just days later, though, the university announced the “pending” closure of the rest of her lab.

Wu died by suicide two years ago, on July 10, 2024.

Wu published 176 peer-reviewed articles and three books over the course of her career, according to the complaint. She held both a medical degree from Shanghai Medical University and a doctorate in cancer biology from Stanford University. She joined the faculty at Northwestern in 2005, after two years at Vanderbilt University.

#### Support for the suit

More than **[1,000 academics signed a letter](https://www.aasforum.org/2026/01/14/signatories-letter-to-support-justice-and-honor-the-memory-of-dr-jane-ying-wu/)** in February sponsored by the Asian American Scholar Forum and the Federation of Asian Professor Associations calling on Northwestern University to apologize for its “unjust treatment” of Wu.

The **[Asian American Scholar Forum (AASF)](https://www.aasforum.org/about-us/aasf-origins-founders/)**, one of the nonprofits that co-sponsored the letter, promotes belonging and academic freedom for Asian American academics. According to Gisela Perez Kusakawa, the executive director of the AASF, signatories came from 44 U.S. states and Washington, D.C., and more than 300 academic institutions.

[**In a RoundTable interview in February**,](https://evanstonroundtable.com/2026/02/12/asian-american-scholar-forum-sends-letter-to-northwestern-condemning-death-of-jane-wu/) Kusakawa described Wu’s death as both a “private tragedy” but also something that concerned the Asian American academic community at large.

“Today’s hearing underscores the importance of ensuring that universities respond appropriately to allegations and protect their faculty from discrimination, Kusakawa wrote in a statement. “AASF stands with Elizabeth Rao and her family as they continue to seek justice and accountability from Northwestern University.”

Elizabeth Rao, Wu’s daughter, is an independent filmmaker. She is also the executor of her mother’s estate.

“My grief and anguish at Northwestern University and Northwestern Memorial Hospital’s treatment of my mother continues. They behaved outrageously towards my mother. We are proceeding with the case,” she wrote in an statement to the RoundTable.
